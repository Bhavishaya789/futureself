Evidence and theories of evolution, immunity and common diseases.

**Key formulas and rules**
- **Miller and Urey**: simulated early earth conditions to produce amino acids
- **Homologous organs**: show divergent evolution
- **Analogous organs**: show convergent evolution
- **Darwin**: proposed natural selection
- **Lamarck**: proposed use and disuse of organs
- **Hardy-Weinberg principle**: states allele frequencies remain constant in the absence of evolutionary forces

**Facts to remember**
- Miller and Urey simulated early earth conditions to produce amino acids.
- Homologous organs show divergent evolution.
- Analogous organs show convergent evolution.
- Darwin proposed natural selection.
- Lamarck proposed use and disuse of organs.
- Hardy-Weinberg principle states allele frequencies remain constant in the absence of evolutionary forces.
- Genetic drift is a random change in allele frequency.
- Industrial melanism in peppered moth supports natural selection.
- Australopithecus was an early hominid.
- Homo erectus had a brain size of about 900 cc.
- Neanderthal man had a brain size of about 1400 cc.
- Innate immunity is non-specific.
- Acquired immunity is specific and has memory.
- Antibody has two heavy and two light chains.

**Exam approach.** Write the formula first, substitute with units, then check the order of magnitude. In NEET, speed comes from recognising the standard pattern of each chapter, so practise the numerical types below until they are automatic. Questions in this chapter repeat a small number of patterns with changed numbers or wording, so after two or three sets you should recognise each pattern at a glance. Keep a short error log of units, signs and exceptions, and read it again the day before the test.
