High-yield NCERT facts for NEET Biology: vitamins and deficiency diseases, endocrine glands and hormones, cell organelles and their functions, and Mendelian genetics with worked crosses (monohybrid 3:1 and 1:2:1, dihybrid 9:3:3:1, test cross 1:1, incomplete dominance, ABO blood groups).
## Deep dive: vitamins and deficiency diseases
Vitamins are organic compounds needed in small amounts. Fat-soluble vitamins are A, D, E and K (stored in the body); water-soluble ones are B-complex and C (not stored, need regular intake). Vitamin A (retinol) deficiency causes night blindness and xerophthalmia; B1 (thiamine) beri-beri; B2 (riboflavin) cheilosis and glossitis; B3 (niacin) pellagra; B6 (pyridoxine) anaemia and convulsions; B9 (folic acid) megaloblastic anaemia and neural tube defects; B12 (cyanocobalamin) pernicious anaemia (needs intrinsic factor from the stomach); C (ascorbic acid) scurvy; D rickets in children and osteomalacia in adults; E infertility and fragile RBCs; K delayed blood clotting. Provitamin forms: beta-carotene gives vitamin A; 7-dehydrocholesterol gives vitamin D in skin with sunlight.
## Deep dive: endocrine system
Hormones are chemical messengers secreted by endocrine glands into the blood. The hypothalamus controls the pituitary: anterior pituitary (adenohypophysis) secretes growth hormone, prolactin, TSH, ACTH, FSH and LH; the posterior pituitary (neurohypophysis) stores and releases oxytocin and ADH made in the hypothalamus. Thyroid: thyroxine (T4, T3) controls basal metabolic rate (deficiency causes goitre, cretinism in children, myxoedema in adults; excess causes Graves disease); calcitonin lowers blood calcium. Parathyroid: PTH raises blood calcium. Pancreas islets: insulin (beta cells) lowers glucose, glucagon (alpha cells) raises it; insulin deficiency causes diabetes mellitus. Adrenal medulla: adrenaline and noradrenaline (emergency hormones); adrenal cortex: cortisol and aldosterone. Gonads: testosterone (Leydig cells), oestrogen and progesterone. Gastrointestinal hormones: gastrin, secretin, CCK and GIP.
## Deep dive: Mendelian genetics
Mendel studied pea plants (Pisum sativum) and gave three laws: dominance, segregation (law of purity of gametes) and independent assortment. Monohybrid cross Tt x Tt gives phenotypic ratio 3:1 and genotypic ratio 1:2:1 (TT : Tt : tt). A test cross (unknown x homozygous recessive) gives 1:1 if the unknown is heterozygous and all dominant if homozygous. A dihybrid cross RrYy x RrYy gives 9:3:3:1 with 16 combinations. Incomplete dominance (snapdragon, Mirabilis) gives 1:2:1 for both genotype and phenotype in F2; co-dominance (ABO blood group IA IB) shows both alleles. ABO: alleles IA, IB (dominant over i), genotypes: A = IAIA or IAi, B = IBIB or IBi, AB = IAIB, O = ii. A heterozygote makes two types of gametes; an individual heterozygous for n genes makes 2^n gametes. Sex determination in humans: XX female and XY male (XO in grasshoppers, ZW in birds with female heterogametic).
