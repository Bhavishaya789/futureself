# The Living World and Biological Classification

Taxonomy arranges organisms in ranks from species to kingdom. Whittaker's five kingdom system groups life by cell type, body organisation and nutrition.

**Key formulas and rules**
- **Binomial nomenclature**: was introduced by Carolus Linnaeus
- **Taxonomic hierarchy**: runs species, genus, family, order, class, phylum, kingdom
- **Species**: is the basic unit of classification
- **Five kingdom classification**: was proposed by R.H. Whittaker in 1969
- **Monera**: includes prokaryotes such as bacteria and cyanobacteria
- **Protista**: includes unicellular eukaryotes like Amoeba and Paramecium

**Facts to remember**
- Binomial nomenclature was introduced by Carolus Linnaeus.
- Taxonomic hierarchy runs species, genus, family, order, class, phylum, kingdom.
- Species is the basic unit of classification.
- Five kingdom classification was proposed by R.H. Whittaker in 1969.
- Monera includes prokaryotes such as bacteria and cyanobacteria.
- Protista includes unicellular eukaryotes like Amoeba and Paramecium.
- Fungi have chitin in their cell walls and are heterotrophic.
- Plantae are multicellular autotrophs with cellulose cell walls.
- Animalia are multicellular heterotrophs without cell walls.
- Viruses are non-cellular and contain either DNA or RNA.
- Viroids are free RNA without a protein coat.
- Prions are infectious proteins causing mad cow disease.
- Archaebacteria live in extreme habitats such as hot springs and salt marshes.
- Mycoplasma are bacteria without a cell wall.
