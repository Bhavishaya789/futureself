# Morphology of Flowering Plants
minutes: 40
yt: NEET Morphology of Flowering Plants
@study
Root, stem, leaf, inflorescence, flower, fruit and seed are the organs of a flowering plant.

**Key formulas and rules**
- **Tap root system**: is found in dicots
- **Fibrous root system**: is found in monocots
- **Adventitious roots**: arise from parts other than the radicle
- **Pneumatophores**: are breathing roots of mangroves like Rhizophora
- **Prop roots**: are found in banyan
- **Stilt roots**: are found in maize and sugarcane

**Facts to remember**
- Tap root system is found in dicots.
- Fibrous root system is found in monocots.
- Adventitious roots arise from parts other than the radicle.
- Pneumatophores are breathing roots of mangroves like Rhizophora.
- Prop roots are found in banyan.
- Stilt roots are found in maize and sugarcane.
- Rhizome is a modified stem as in ginger.
- Tuber is a modified underground stem as in potato.
- Phyllode is a flattened petiole as in Australian acacia.
- Phyllotaxy is the arrangement of leaves on the stem.
- Venation in dicots is reticulate.
- Venation in monocots is parallel.
- Racemose inflorescence has the main axis continuing to grow.
- Cymose inflorescence ends in a flower at the main axis.
