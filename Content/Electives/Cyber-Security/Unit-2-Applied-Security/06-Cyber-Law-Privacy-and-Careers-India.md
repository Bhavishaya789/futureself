# Cyber law, privacy and careers (India)
minutes: 20

@study
**India's legal framework** (verify the latest text before relying on it): the **Information Technology Act, 2000** covers hacking, identity theft, cheating by impersonation and cyber offences; the **Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023** governs handling of personal data. The **Indian Computer Emergency Response Team (CERT-In)** handles incident reporting and advisories.
**Reporting a cyber crime:** use the national portal **cybercrime.gov.in** or the helpline **1930** for financial fraud, report quickly so money can be frozen, and keep screenshots and transaction details. Never share OTPs, PINs or card details, and be wary of fake "KYC" or "customer care" calls.
**Ethics:** only test systems you own or have written permission to test. Unauthorised access is a crime.
**Careers and certifications:** SOC analyst, penetration tester, security engineer, forensics analyst, GRC; certifications such as CompTIA Security+, CEH, CISSP; practice legally on platforms such as TryHackMe and Hack The Box, and learn Linux, networking and Python.

@qa
Q: Where can you report cyber crime in India?
A: The national portal cybercrime.gov.in or the helpline 1930 for financial fraud.
Q: Which Indian law covers cyber offences?
A: The Information Technology Act, 2000 (with the DPDP Act, 2023 for personal data).
Q: Should you share an OTP with a "bank officer" who calls you?
A: Never; banks do not ask for OTPs or PINs.
Q: Is it legal to test a website you do not own?
A: No, not without written permission from the owner.
