**Key formulas and rules**
- **Subject verb agreement**: requires a singular verb for a singular subject
- **Neither nor**: takes a verb that agrees with the nearer subject
- **Present perfect**: is used for past actions with present relevance
- **Past perfect**: shows the earlier of two past actions
- **Passive voice**: uses be plus the past participle
- **Reported speech**: shifts tense one step back

**Facts to remember**
- Subject verb agreement requires a singular verb for a singular subject.
- Neither nor takes a verb that agrees with the nearer subject.
- Present perfect is used for past actions with present relevance.
- Past perfect shows the earlier of two past actions.
- Passive voice uses be plus the past participle.
- Reported speech shifts tense one step back.
- Articles use a before consonant sounds and an before vowel sounds.
- Preposition after different is from in standard usage.
- Comparative with than requires the same kind of comparison.
- Conditional sentences use if with a matching main clause.
- Each and every take singular verbs.
- Modal verbs include can, may, must and should.
- Gerund is the ing form used as a noun.
- Infinitive is to plus the base verb.
