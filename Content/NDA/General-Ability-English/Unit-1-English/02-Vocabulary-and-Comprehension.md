Word power and reading skills.

**Key formulas and rules**
- **Synonym**: means a word of similar meaning
- **Antonym**: means a word of opposite meaning
- **Idiom**: has a figurative meaning
- **Ubiquitous**: means present everywhere
- **Benevolent**: means kind and generous
- **Candid**: means frank and honest

**Facts to remember**
- Synonym means a word of similar meaning.
- Antonym means a word of opposite meaning.
- Idiom has a figurative meaning.
- Ubiquitous means present everywhere.
- Benevolent means kind and generous.
- Candid means frank and honest.
- Ephemeral means short lived.
- Meticulous means very careful.
- Ambiguous means open to more than one meaning.
- Prudent means wise and cautious.
- Reading comprehension is solved by reading questions first.
- One word substitution replaces a phrase by a word.
- Homonyms are words with the same spelling but different meanings.
- Spelling errors are common in commonly confused words.

**Exam approach.** Write the formula first, substitute with units, then check the order of magnitude. In NDA, speed comes from recognising the standard pattern of each chapter, so practise the numerical types below until they are automatic. Revise this chapter in three passes: the formula sheet, the facts above, then the generated numerical questions with a timer.

**How to revise.** Read the formula list and the facts once, then close the page and write them from memory. Attempt the generated questions in sets of ten, mark each answer, and note the reason for every miss. Questions in this chapter repeat a small number of patterns with changed numbers or wording, so after two or three sets you should recognise each pattern at a glance. Keep a short error log of units, signs and exceptions, and read it again the day before the test.
