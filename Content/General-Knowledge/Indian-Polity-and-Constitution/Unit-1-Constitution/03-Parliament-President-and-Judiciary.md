# Parliament, President and Judiciary | संसद, राष्ट्रपति और न्यायपालिका
minutes: 15
yt: parliament president judiciary india polity
@study
- **Parliament** = **President + Lok Sabha + Rajya Sabha**. Lok Sabha: maximum strength **550** (currently **543** elected), 5-year term, minimum age **25**. Rajya Sabha: maximum **250** (238 elected + 12 nominated), a **permanent house**; **one-third retires every two years**; members serve 6 years, minimum age 30.
- **Voting age 18** (61st Amendment, 1988). **Money Bills** begin only in the Lok Sabha.
- **President:** head of State, elected indirectly by an electoral college, term **5 years**, minimum age **35**. **Vice-President** is the ex-officio chairman of the Rajya Sabha. The **Prime Minister** heads the Council of Ministers, which is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha (Art. 75).
- **Emergency provisions:** Art. **352** (national), **356** (President's rule in a state), **360** (financial).
- **Amendment:** Article **368**. **Basic structure doctrine:** *Kesavananda Bharati case, 1973*.
- **Judiciary:** Supreme Court (established **28 January 1950**), High Courts, subordinate courts. Retirement age: Supreme Court judges **65**, High Court judges **62**. Election Commission: Art. **324**. Panchayati Raj: **73rd Amendment (1992)**; urban local bodies: **74th**.

## Advanced
P: Explain the basic structure doctrine.
A: In Kesavananda Bharati (1973) the Supreme Court held that Parliament can amend the Constitution under Art. 368 but cannot alter its basic structure, such as democracy, federalism and judicial review.
