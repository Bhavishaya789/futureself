# Fundamental Rights, Directive Principles and Duties | मौलिक अधिकार, नीति निदेशक तत्व और कर्तव्य
minutes: 15
yt: fundamental rights directive principles duties india
@study
- **Fundamental Rights (Part III, Articles 12-35):** six rights. **Equality (Art. 14-18)**, **Freedom (Art. 19-22)**, **Against Exploitation (Art. 23-24)**, **Freedom of Religion (Art. 25-28)**, **Cultural and Educational Rights (Art. 29-30)** and **Constitutional Remedies (Art. 32)**. Dr Ambedkar called Art. 32 the "heart and soul" of the Constitution.
- The **right to property** was removed from Part III by the **44th Amendment (1978)** and is now a legal right under **Art. 300A**.
- **Article 21A:** free and compulsory education for ages **6-14** (86th Amendment, 2002).
- **Directive Principles (Part IV, Art. 36-51):** guidelines for the State, **not enforceable in court**; aim at a welfare state.
- **Fundamental Duties (Art. 51A, Part IVA):** added by the 42nd Amendment (1976) on the Swaran Singh Committee's advice; now **11 duties** (the 11th, parents' duty to educate children aged 6-14, added in 2002).

## Moderate

A: Equality, Freedom, Against Exploitation, Freedom of Religion, Cultural and Educational Rights, Constitutional Remedies.

## High
P: Distinguish Fundamental Rights from Directive Principles.
A: Rights (Part III) are justiciable and enforceable by courts; Directive Principles (Part IV) are non-justiciable guidelines for governance.
R: 2 | Rights
R: 2 | Directive Principles
## Advanced
P: Why is Article 32 called the "heart and soul" of the Constitution?
A: It lets citizens go directly to the Supreme Court to enforce their Fundamental Rights through writs, making all other rights effective.
