A short biography of Albert Einstein (1879 to 1955). He was born in Ulm, Germany, was slow to speak, hated rote learning and was fascinated by a compass and geometry. He was a pacifist who left Germany when the Nazis came to power, settled in the USA, won the Nobel Prize in 1921 for the photoelectric effect and developed the theory of relativity. Later he regretted that his equation led to the atom bomb.
## Deep dive: childhood and education
Albert Einstein was born in Ulm, Germany, on 14 March 1879. His family later moved to Munich. His father had an electrical business and his mother taught him the violin. He was slow to speak, and some thought him dull, but he was very curious. At five his father showed him a compass, and he was amazed that the needle pointed north without being touched. He disliked rote learning and strict schooling. He loved Euclid's geometry. He left school in Germany at fifteen and went to Switzerland to finish school and study at the Zurich Polytechnic.
## Deep dive: career and achievements
He worked as a clerk in the patent office in Bern and, in 1905, published papers on the photoelectric effect, Brownian motion and the special theory of relativity. These made him famous. He later developed the general theory of relativity. For his work on the photoelectric effect he received the Nobel Prize for Physics in 1921. He became a professor and travelled widely.
## Deep dive: peace and later life
Einstein was a pacifist. When the Nazis took power in Germany in 1933 he left for the United States and worked at Princeton. He wrote to President Roosevelt warning that Germany might build an atom bomb, but later regretted that his work led to nuclear weapons. He supported world peace, civil rights and humanitarian causes, declined the presidency of Israel and died on 18 April 1955. The chapter shows that a truly beautiful mind combines great intelligence with kindness and courage.
