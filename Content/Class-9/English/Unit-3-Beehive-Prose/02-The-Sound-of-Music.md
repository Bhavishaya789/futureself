# The sound of music
minutes: 35
yt: class 9 english the sound of music
@study
The chapter has two parts. Part I: Evelyn Glennie, a Scottish percussionist who became deaf by the age of eleven yet became a world-famous solo percussionist; she hears music by feeling vibrations, often performing barefoot. Part II: Ustad Bismillah Khan, the shehnai maestro from Dumraon in Bihar, who raised the shehnai to the concert stage and played at the Red Fort on 15 August 1947.
## Deep dive: Evelyn Glennie
Evelyn Glennie was born in Scotland. She gradually lost her hearing and was almost deaf by eleven. Her parents and teachers encouraged her. Her teacher Ron Forbes asked her to place her hands on the wall of the room while he beat the drum, and she learned to feel the sound; he taught her that low notes could be felt in the legs and feet and high notes in the face or upper body. She chose percussion and was admitted to the Royal Academy of Music in London despite her deafness. She became the first full-time solo percussionist in the world, playing barefoot to feel the music.
## Deep dive: Bismillah Khan
Long ago the pungi, a reed instrument, was played by snake charmers, and it was forbidden in the royal courts because its sound was shrill. A barber from a family of professional musicians who had access to the court improved it: he made a pipe with a natural hollow stem with seven holes, and it produced a soft sound and came to be called the shehnai. Bismillah Khan, born in Dumraon in Bihar, became its greatest player. He played at the Red Fort on 15 August 1947 at the first Independence Day. He loved Varanasi and the Ganga and refused to settle abroad. He was given the Bharat Ratna in 2001.
## Deep dive: themes
Both parts show that talent, hard work and love for music matter more than difficulty. The chapter links music to dedication, humility and national pride.
