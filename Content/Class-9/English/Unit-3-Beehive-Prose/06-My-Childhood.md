# My childhood
minutes: 30
yt: class 9 english my childhood
@study
An extract from the autobiography Wings of Fire by Dr A. P. J. Abdul Kalam. He describes his childhood in Rameswaram, his loving but strict family, his friends Ramanadha Sastry, Aravindan and Sivaprakasan, a teacher who refused to let a Hindu boy sit with a Muslim, and how his science teacher Sivasubramania Iyer invited him home. He also worked for his cousin Samsuddin to deliver newspapers and earned his first wages.
## Deep dive: family and friends
A. P. J. Abdul Kalam was born into a middle-class Muslim family in Rameswaram. His father, Jainulabdeen, had little formal education or wealth, but he was generous and wise and taught him honesty, self-discipline and simplicity; his mother Ashiamma fed many people daily. Kalam had three close friends, Ramanadha Sastry, Aravindan and Sivaprakasan, all Hindus, and no one in the group felt any difference of religion. Ahmed Jallaluddin, his friend and later his brother-in-law, spoke to him about spiritual matters.
## Deep dive: the teacher and the lesson
When Kalam was in the fifth class a new teacher saw him sitting with Ramanadha Sastry, the son of the temple's chief priest, and sent him to the back bench. The two boys were hurt and told their parents. Ramanadha's father, Lakshmana Sastry, summoned the teacher and said that he must not spread social inequality and communal intolerance, or else leave the village. The teacher apologised and changed. Another teacher, Sivasubramania Iyer, invited Kalam to his home for a meal even though his wife refused at first to serve a Muslim boy; he served Kalam himself and sat beside him. Kalam later returned and found the same wife serving him with her own hands.
## Deep dive: first earnings
During the war the demand for newspapers rose. As the trains stopped at Rameswaram, the bundles of papers were thrown from the moving train and Kalam's cousin Samsuddin needed help catching them. Kalam did so and earned his first wages. The chapter shows how childhood experiences shaped his belief in unity and service.
