# Clauses
minutes: 35
yt: class 9 english clauses
@study
A clause is a group of words with its own subject and verb. A main (independent) clause makes complete sense; a subordinate (dependent) clause does not. Subordinate clauses are of three kinds: noun clauses (do the work of a noun, often begin with that, what, whether), adjective clauses (describe a noun, begin with who, which, that, whose) and adverb clauses (tell time, place, reason, condition, purpose, result, concession or comparison).
## Deep dive: phrase and clause
A phrase is a group of words without a subject and verb (in the garden). A clause has a subject and a finite verb. A sentence with one clause is simple; a sentence with a main clause and subordinate clause(s) is complex; two or more main clauses joined by and, but, or, so form a compound sentence. A main clause: The boy won. A subordinate clause: who studied hard.
## Deep dive: noun clauses
A noun clause does the work of a noun. It can be the subject (What he said is true), object (I know that he is honest), complement (The truth is that he lied) or object of a preposition (I am sure of what I saw). It starts with that, what, whether, if, who, why, how, when, where. Test: replace it with something or it and the sentence still makes sense.
## Deep dive: adjective clauses
An adjective (relative) clause describes a noun or pronoun and follows it directly. Relative pronouns: who (people), whom, whose, which (things), that (people and things); relative adverbs: where, when, why. Example: The boy who won the prize is my cousin. Defining clauses are essential to the meaning; non-defining ones add extra information within commas: My brother, who lives in Pune, is a doctor.
## Deep dive: adverb clauses
An adverb clause tells how, when, where, why, or on what condition the action takes place. Time: when, while, before, after, until, as soon as. Place: where, wherever. Reason: because, since, as. Condition: if, unless, provided that. Purpose: so that, in order that. Result: so...that, such...that. Concession: although, though, even if. Comparison: than, as...as. Example: She was absent because she was ill. Do not use a comma before because clauses that follow the main clause.
