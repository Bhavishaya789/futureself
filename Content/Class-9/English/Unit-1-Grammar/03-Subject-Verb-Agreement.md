# Subject-verb agreement
minutes: 30
yt: class 9 english subject verb agreement
@study
The verb must agree with its subject in number and person. A singular subject takes a singular verb (She plays), a plural subject takes a plural verb (They play). Either, neither, each, every, everyone, someone and nobody take singular verbs. Two subjects joined by and take a plural verb unless they are one unit. With either-or and neither-nor the verb agrees with the nearer subject. Words such as news, mathematics and physics are singular.
## Deep dive: the basic rule
A singular subject takes a singular verb and a plural subject takes a plural verb. Singular present verbs end in s (He runs); plural verbs do not (They run). Be forms: I am, he/she/it is, we/you/they are; was for I/he/she/it and were for we/you/they. Have forms: has with he, she, it; have with I, we, you, they. Do not be misled by words between the subject and the verb: The box of apples is heavy; the real subject is box.
## Deep dive: singular subjects
Each, every, either, neither, one, everyone, everybody, someone, anybody, nobody and no one take a singular verb: Each of the girls has finished. Many a + singular noun takes a singular verb. Subjects ending in s but singular: news, mathematics, physics, economics, mumps, measles, the United States, and book titles such as Gulliver's Travels. Amounts of money, distance, time and weight taken as one unit are singular: Ten kilometres is far. A number of takes a plural verb; The number of takes a singular verb.
## Deep dive: plural subjects and joined subjects
Scissors, trousers, spectacles, glasses, clothes, police, cattle, people and the poor are plural: The police are here. Two subjects joined by and take a plural verb: Ram and Shyam are friends; but when the two form one idea the verb is singular: Bread and butter is my breakfast. With or, nor, either-or and neither-nor the verb agrees with the nearer subject: Neither the teacher nor the students were present. With as well as, along with, together with, and with, the verb agrees with the first subject: The captain, with his players, is arriving.
## Deep dive: collective nouns and there is
A collective noun (committee, jury, team, class, family) takes a singular verb when it acts as one unit (The committee has decided) and a plural verb when members act separately (The committee are arguing). In sentences beginning with there, the verb agrees with the noun that follows: There are three books on the shelf; There is a pen and a book on the table (nearer noun). In questions, find the real subject: Where are my glasses?
