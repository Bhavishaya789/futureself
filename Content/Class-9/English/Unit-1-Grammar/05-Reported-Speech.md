# Reported speech
minutes: 35
yt: class 9 english reported speech
@study
In direct speech we give the exact words of the speaker inside quotation marks; in indirect (reported) speech we report the meaning using that, if, whether or to. Changes: the reporting verb in the past tense moves the tense back (am/is to was; do to did; will to would; can to could; have to had), pronouns change by sense, and words of time and place change (now to then; today to that day; tomorrow to the next day; here to there; this to that). Questions use if or whether (yes/no) or the wh-word with normal order; commands use to + verb.
## Deep dive: statements
Direct: Meena said, "I like mangoes." Indirect: Meena said that she liked mangoes. The conjunction that joins the clauses, the pronoun changes from I to she according to the speaker, and simple present changes to simple past. When the reporting verb is in the present or future (She says, She will say) or when the statement is a universal truth, the tense does not change: He said that the earth moves round the sun.
## Deep dive: tense changes
Simple present becomes simple past; present continuous becomes past continuous; present perfect and simple past become past perfect; will becomes would; can becomes could; may becomes might; must stays must or becomes had to. Past continuous and past perfect usually stay unchanged or shift to past perfect continuous. Time and place words shift: now to then, today to that day, tonight to that night, yesterday to the day before, tomorrow to the next day, ago to before, here to there, this to that, these to those, come to go.
## Deep dive: questions, commands and exclamations
Yes/no questions: use if or whether, and the order becomes subject then verb with no question mark: He asked me, "Are you tired?" becomes He asked me if I was tired. Wh-questions keep the wh-word: She asked, "Where do you live?" becomes She asked where I lived. Commands and requests use to + verb: The teacher said, "Open your books" becomes The teacher told us to open our books; a negative command uses not to. Exclamations use exclaimed with joy or sorrow: She said, "Hurray! We won" becomes She exclaimed with joy that they had won.
## Deep dive: reporting verbs and pronoun rules
Use said (statement), told (with an object), asked (question), ordered, advised, requested, suggested, warned, promised, exclaimed, replied. Pronoun rule: first person pronouns follow the subject of the reporting verb, second person pronouns follow its object, and third person pronouns do not change. So He said to me, "You are late" becomes He told me that I was late. Note: say to becomes tell; said to in questions becomes asked.
