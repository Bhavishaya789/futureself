# Determiners
minutes: 30
yt: class 9 english determiners
@study
A determiner is a word placed before a noun to show which one or how many. Types: articles (a, an, the), demonstratives (this, that, these, those), possessives (my, your, his, her, its, our, their), quantifiers (some, any, much, many, few, little, all, enough, several), distributives (each, every, either, neither) and numbers. Use a before consonant sounds and an before vowel sounds. Use much with uncountable nouns and many with countable nouns.
## Deep dive: articles
The indefinite articles a and an are used for a single, non-specific countable noun: a pen, an egg. The choice depends on sound, not spelling: a university (y sound), a one-rupee note (w sound), an hour (silent h), an honest man, an MBA. The definite article the is used for something known or unique (the sun, the moon), the same thing mentioned earlier, rivers, seas, mountain ranges, holy books, musical instruments, superlatives (the best), ordinals (the first) and the only of its kind. Do not use the before names of people, most countries, languages, meals, subjects, or plural nouns used in general: I like music.
## Deep dive: demonstratives and possessives
This and these point to near things; that and those point to distant things: this book, that book, these books, those books. Possessive determiners show ownership and come before a noun: my, your, his, her, its, our, their; they differ from possessive pronouns (mine, yours, his, hers, ours, theirs), which stand alone: This is my pen; the pen is mine.
## Deep dive: quantifiers
Some is used in positive sentences and offers (some milk), any in negatives and questions (I do not have any money). Much goes with uncountable nouns (much time), many with countable (many friends); both are mainly used in negatives and questions, while a lot of is common in positive sentences. Few means not many (almost none); a few means some. Little means not much; a little means some. Fewer is for countable nouns and less for uncountable. Enough, all, most, several, both, half and plenty of also quantify nouns.
## Deep dive: distributives and numbers
Each means every one of two or more taken separately; every means all, seen as a group (every student). Either means one or the other of two; neither means not one and not the other of two. All of these take singular verbs. Numbers: cardinal (one, two) and ordinal (first, second). Other determiners include which, what, whose, another and other: Which book do you want?
