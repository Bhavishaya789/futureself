@study
Modal verbs (can, could, may, might, shall, should, will, would, must, ought to, need, dare) help the main verb to show ability, permission, possibility, obligation, advice and requests. They are followed by the base form of the verb, have no s in the third person and do not take do or does in questions. Can shows ability; may shows permission or possibility; must shows strong obligation; should shows advice; would is the past of will and is used for polite requests.
## Deep dive: what modals are
Modals are helping verbs that add meaning to the main verb: can, could, may, might, shall, should, will, would, must, ought to, need and dare. They do not change form for the subject (no s or ing), are followed by the bare infinitive (the base form without to, except ought to), and form questions by moving the modal before the subject: Can you help? Negatives add not: cannot, should not, must not.
## Deep dive: meanings and uses
Can: ability (She can sing), permission (informal) and possibility. Could: past ability (I could swim at six), polite request (Could you help?) and weak possibility. May: permission (May I go?) and possibility (It may rain). Might: weaker possibility. Must: strong obligation (You must wear a helmet), logical certainty and, in the negative, prohibition (You must not smoke). Should and ought to: advice and duty. Shall: offers and suggestions with I and we (Shall I open the window?). Will: promises, predictions and decisions. Would: polite request and habits in the past.
## Deep dive: need, dare and have to
Need and dare can act as modals in negatives and questions (He need not go; Dare you say that?). Need not means no obligation (you do not have to), while must not means prohibition. Have to shows external obligation (I have to wake up early), and had to is its past; must has no past form. Used to describes past habits. Modal perfect forms: should have + V3 (a past duty not done), could have + V3, might have + V3 and must have + V3 (certainty about the past).
## Deep dive: common errors
Do not add to after a modal (except ought to): wrong "She can to swim". Do not use a modal after another modal: wrong "He will can come". Avoid the s form: wrong "He musts go". Do not use did with could or should. Use "may I" for formal permission and "can I" for casual speech. Choose must for the speaker's own sense of duty and have to for rules from outside.

## Uses of modals
Ability | can, could | I can swim
Permission | may, can, could | May I come in?
Possibility | may, might, could | It may rain
Obligation | must, have to, should | You must obey
Advice | should, ought to | You should rest
Request | could, would, can | Could you help?
Prohibition | must not, cannot | You must not smoke
## Structure
Modal sentence | subject + modal + V1 | 
Question | modal + subject + V1 | 
Negative | modal + not + V1 |
