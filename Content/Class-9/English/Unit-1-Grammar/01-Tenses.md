# Tenses

@study
A tense shows the time of an action: present, past or future. Each has four forms: simple, continuous, perfect and perfect continuous, making 12 tenses. Simple present: V1 or V1+s for habits and facts. Present continuous: am/is/are + V-ing. Present perfect: has/have + V3. Simple past: V2. Past continuous: was/were + V-ing. Past perfect: had + V3. Simple future: will + V1. Always match the verb with the subject.
## Deep dive: present tenses
Simple present: habits, general truths and fixed facts: The sun rises in the east. He plays cricket. With he, she, it add s or es to the verb. Present continuous: action happening now or around now: She is reading. Present perfect: action finished but linked to the present, or experience: I have seen the film. It uses since (point of time) and for (period of time). Present perfect continuous: action started in the past and still going on: He has been studying since morning.
## Deep dive: past tenses
Simple past: finished action at a definite past time: They went to Agra last year. Past continuous: action in progress at a past moment: I was sleeping when you called. Past perfect: the earlier of two past actions: The train had left before we reached. Past perfect continuous: continuous action before a past moment: She had been waiting for an hour when he came.
## Deep dive: future tenses
Simple future: will/shall + V1 for promises and predictions: I will help you. Future continuous: will be + V-ing, an action in progress at a future time: This time tomorrow I will be flying. Future perfect: will have + V3, completed before a future time: By June I will have finished the course. Future perfect continuous: will have been + V-ing: By May she will have been working here for ten years.
## Deep dive: signal words and common errors
Signal words: always, usually, every day (simple present); now, at present, look! (present continuous); already, just, yet, ever, never, since, for (present perfect); yesterday, ago, last week (simple past); tomorrow, next week (future). Never use will after if or when in the condition: If it rains, we will stay (not will rain). Use did + V1 in questions and negatives of the simple past. Verbs of state (know, like, want, belong) are not normally used in continuous form.
