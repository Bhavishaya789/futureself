# Notice and message writing
minutes: 30
yt: class 9 english notice and message writing
@study
A notice gives public information about an event, change or request, in a box with the name of the issuer, the heading NOTICE, a date, a title, the body in 50 words or fewer, and the name, designation and signature of the writer. A message is a short note for a person who is absent, with the date, time, a salutation, the body and the sender. Both are brief and clear, give the 5Ws (what, who, when, where, why) and use polite words.
## Deep dive: notice writing
A notice is a formal written announcement for a group of people on a notice board. Format: (1) the name of the school or organisation at the top; (2) the word NOTICE as the heading; (3) the date; (4) a short title that states the subject in capital letters; (5) the body in the third person; (6) the name and designation of the writer, with signature. Write within a box in about 50 words. Use the passive voice and a formal tone: Students are informed that... Include the 5Ws: what event, who may attend, when, where, and whom to contact.
## Deep dive: types of notices
Common notices are of events (annual day, sports day, debate, quiz), of lost and found items, of meetings, of changes in timing, of cancellations, of admissions, of sale, and of public warnings. A lost-and-found notice gives a description of the item, where and when it was lost, and a contact number. An invitation to an event states the programme, chief guest, date and venue. A cancellation notice gives the reason and a new date if any.
## Deep dive: message writing
A message is a short note left for a person who is not available. Format: date and time at the top, salutation (Dear Sir, Hi Riya), the body in two or three lines, and the sender's name at the end. Write in the first person for the sender and refer to the receiver as you. Include only the essential details: who called, what is to be done, by when, and a contact number. A telephone message begins with While you were out, or a similar line and uses simple words.
## Deep dive: tips for good writing
Keep the language simple and direct. Do not add extra details or personal feelings in a notice. Check the date, time and venue; repeated details cause confusion. Use correct spelling and punctuation, begin sentences with capitals and use the present or future tense for events. Respect the word limit; examiners check content, format, accuracy and fluency.
