# Reading comprehension and vocabulary
minutes: 40
yt: class 9 english reading comprehension and vocabulary
@study
Reading comprehension is understanding a passage: read it once for the gist, then read the questions, find the answers in the text and write in your own words. Types of passage: factual, discursive and literary. Skills: skimming (quick overview), scanning (finding facts), inferring (reading between the lines) and finding the main idea. Vocabulary: synonyms, antonyms, one-word substitutes, idioms, phrasal verbs and the meaning of words in context.
## Deep dive: reading strategies
Skimming means reading quickly to get the main idea. Scanning means searching for a particular word, name or number. Inferring means using clues to understand what is not stated directly. Look at the title, the first and last sentences of paragraphs and the connectors. Underline key words. Answer in complete sentences using your own words; avoid copying large chunks. Multiple-choice items need the best answer from the text, not general knowledge.
## Deep dive: kinds of passages and questions
Factual passages (news, reports, notices, advertisements) give information; discursive passages (articles, opinion) give views and arguments; literary passages (stories, poems) express feelings and use figures of speech. Questions ask for the main idea, details, reasons, the writer's attitude, the meaning of a word in the passage, references (what does it refer to), and inferences. For vocabulary-in-context questions, read the sentence around the word and replace the word with each option.
## Deep dive: building vocabulary
A synonym is a word of similar meaning (happy and glad); an antonym has an opposite meaning (happy and sad). One-word substitutes replace a phrase (a person who loves books is a bibliophile). Idioms are phrases whose meaning is not the sum of the words (a piece of cake means very easy). Phrasal verbs combine a verb with a particle (give up means stop). Prefixes (un-, dis-, re-) and suffixes (-ness, -ful, -less) change the meaning of root words. Learn words in context and use a dictionary.
## Deep dive: comprehension practice
Time yourself, mark the lines where answers appear and check spelling and grammar. For summary questions, write in the present tense, in your own words, and cover only the main points. For poetry, identify the speaker, theme, tone, imagery and poetic devices such as simile, metaphor, personification, alliteration and rhyme. Revise regularly with unseen passages.
