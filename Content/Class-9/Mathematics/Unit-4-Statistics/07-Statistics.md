# Statistics
minutes: 30
yt: class 9 statistics
@study
Raw data is organised into a frequency distribution table, grouped into classes of equal width for large data. It can be shown as a bar graph, histogram or frequency polygon. The mean is the sum of observations divided by their number, the median is the middle value of the ordered data, and the mode is the most frequent value.
## Deep dive: collecting and presenting data
Data collected first-hand is primary data; from other sources, secondary data. Ungrouped data is arranged in a frequency table with tally marks. For large data, group into continuous classes of equal width (class size = upper limit - lower limit). Graphs: a bar graph has bars of equal width with gaps; a histogram has no gaps and bars in a continuous class; a frequency polygon joins the mid-points of tops of histogram bars (or class marks vs frequency).
## Deep dive: mean, median and mode
Mean x = (sum of all observations)/(number of observations). For a frequency table, x = sum (f x)/sum f. Median: arrange in order; for n odd it is the ((n+1)/2)th value, for n even the average of the (n/2)th and (n/2 + 1)th values. Mode: the observation with the highest frequency. Mean = sum/ n so adding a constant to every value adds it to the mean; multiplying multiplies the mean.
## Deep dive: choosing the measure
The mean uses all values and is affected by extreme values; the median is not affected by extreme values and is best for skewed data; the mode is best for the most popular size or choice (such as shoe size).
