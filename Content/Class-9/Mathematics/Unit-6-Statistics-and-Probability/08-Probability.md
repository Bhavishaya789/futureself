# Probability
minutes: 40
yt: class 9 maths probability
@study
Probability measures how likely an event is to occur. In the experimental or empirical approach, the probability of an event E is P(E) = (number of trials in which E happens) / (total number of trials). The probability of a sure event is 1 and that of an impossible event is 0, so 0 ≤ P(E) ≤ 1. The probabilities of all elementary events of an experiment add up to 1. P(E) + P(not E) = 1. Equally likely outcomes are assumed in simple examples such as coins, dice and cards.
## Deep dive: experimental probability
Repeat an experiment many times and record how often the event occurs. P(E) = (trials with E)/(total trials). For a coin tossed 100 times with 55 heads, P(head) = 55/100 = 0.55. As the trials increase the value approaches 1/2.
## Deep dive: properties
0 ≤ P(E) ≤ 1; P(sure event) = 1; P(impossible event) = 0; the sum of probabilities of all elementary events is 1; P(not E) = 1 - P(E).
## Deep dive: coins, dice and cards
Sample space for one coin: {H, T}; for a die: {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6}; two coins: {HH, HT, TH, TT}. A pack has 52 cards in four suits of 13. P(even number on a die) = 3/6 = 1/2; P(ace) = 4/52 = 1/13.
