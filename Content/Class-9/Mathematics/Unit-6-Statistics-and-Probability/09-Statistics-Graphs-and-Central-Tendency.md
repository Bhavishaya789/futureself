# Statistics: Graphs and Central Tendency
minutes: 40
yt: class 9 maths statistics: graphs and central tendency
@study
Statistics deals with collecting, organising, presenting and interpreting data. Data can be presented as a frequency distribution table, a bar graph, a histogram (with continuous classes and no gaps) or a frequency polygon (joining the mid-points of class intervals). The measures of central tendency are mean, median and mode. Mean = sum of observations / number of observations, median is the middle value of ordered data, and mode is the most frequent observation. Range = highest - lowest.
## Deep dive: presenting data
Raw data is organised into a frequency table using tally marks. Discrete data is shown with a bar graph (equal-width bars with gaps). Continuous data is grouped into classes (class size = upper limit - lower limit) and shown with a histogram (no gaps). A frequency polygon joins the mid-points (class marks) of the tops of the bars.
## Deep dive: mean, median and mode
Mean = Σx/n or Σfx/Σf. Median: arrange in order and take the middle value (or the average of the two middle values). Mode: the most frequent value. Range = maximum - minimum.
## Deep dive: unequal classes
For classes of different widths, adjusted frequency = (frequency × minimum class width) ÷ class width, so the area of each bar is proportional to its frequency.
