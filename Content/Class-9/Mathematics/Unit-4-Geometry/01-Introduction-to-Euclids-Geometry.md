# Introduction to Euclid's Geometry
minutes: 30
yt: class 9 maths euclid's geometry
@study
Euclid, a Greek mathematician of Alexandria (about 300 BC), wrote the Elements in 13 books and built geometry from a few definitions, five postulates and some common notions (axioms). Axioms are universal truths; postulates are assumptions specific to geometry. The fifth postulate led to non-Euclidean geometries.
## Deep dive: definitions, axioms and postulates
Euclid's Elements opens with 23 definitions such as a point being that which has no part and a line being breadthless length. Because some terms must remain undefined, modern geometry takes point, line and plane as undefined terms. Axioms (common notions) are general truths used throughout mathematics, while postulates are assumptions specific to geometry. Seven axioms are listed in the NCERT text.
## Deep dive: the five postulates
Postulate 1: a line may be drawn from any point to any other point. Postulate 2: a terminated line can be extended indefinitely. Postulate 3: a circle can be drawn with any centre and radius. Postulate 4: all right angles are equal. Postulate 5: if a line meets two lines so that the interior angles on one side total less than two right angles, the two lines meet on that side when extended. Postulate 5 is equivalent to Playfair's axiom about a unique parallel through an external point.
## Deep dive: consequences
Two distinct lines cannot have more than one common point, and through two points exactly one line passes. Euclid built theorems on these ideas. Doubts about the fifth postulate led mathematicians in the nineteenth century to non-Euclidean geometries, which describe curved surfaces such as the sphere.
