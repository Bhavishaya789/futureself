# Heron's Formula
minutes: 40
yt: class 9 maths heron's formula
@study
Heron's formula gives the area of a triangle when the lengths of all three sides a, b and c are known: Area = √[s(s - a)(s - b)(s - c)], where s = (a + b + c)/2 is the semi-perimeter. It is useful when the height is not known. The area of an equilateral triangle of side a is (√3/4)a², of an isosceles triangle with equal sides a and base b is (b/4)√(4a² - b²). Heron's formula can be used for quadrilaterals by dividing them into two triangles.
## Deep dive: the formula
Heron's formula finds the area of a triangle from its three sides. First compute s = (a + b + c)/2; then area = √[s(s - a)(s - b)(s - c)]. Example: for sides 13, 14, 15, s = 21 and area = √(21 × 8 × 7 × 6) = √7056 = 84.
## Deep dive: special triangles
Equilateral: area = (√3/4)a². Isosceles with equal sides a and base b: area = (b/4)√(4a² - b²). Right triangle: area = (1/2) × product of legs; also Heron's formula agrees with it.
## Deep dive: quadrilaterals
Split a quadrilateral by a diagonal into two triangles and add their areas. Parallelogram: base × height. Rhombus: (1/2) d1 d2. Trapezium: (1/2)(a + b)h.
## Deep dive: word problems
In real-life problems (parks, signboards, cost of painting) compute the area first, then multiply by the rate per unit area. Keep units consistent.
