# Number Systems
minutes: 40
yt: class 9 maths number systems
@study
Numbers are classified as natural, whole, integers, rational and irrational; together rational and irrational numbers form the real numbers. A rational number is p/q with q not zero and has a terminating or repeating decimal; an irrational number has a non-terminating non-repeating decimal. Real numbers fill the number line, and laws of exponents work for rational powers.
## Deep dive: classification of numbers
Natural numbers N = {1, 2, 3, ...}; whole numbers W = N and 0; integers Z = {..., -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, ...}; rational numbers Q = {p/q : p, q in Z, q not 0}; irrational numbers are numbers that are not rational; real numbers R are the union of Q and the irrationals. Every real number has a unique point on the number line, and every point is a real number. There are infinitely many rational numbers between any two rational numbers, and infinitely many irrationals too.
## Deep dive: decimal expansions
A rational number has a terminating decimal or a non-terminating repeating (recurring) decimal. p/q (lowest terms) terminates exactly when q = 2^m x 5^n. To convert 0.333... to a fraction put x = 0.333..., then 10x - x = 3, so x = 1/3. For two repeating digits use 100x - x. An irrational number has a decimal that neither ends nor repeats, such as sqrt 2 = 1.41421356... or pi = 3.14159265...
## Deep dive: operations and exponents
Sum, difference, product and quotient of a non-zero rational and an irrational are irrational; but two irrationals can combine to give a rational. Rationalising: 1/(a + sqrt b) = (a - sqrt b)/(a^2 - b). Laws: a^m x a^n = a^(m+n), a^m/a^n = a^(m-n), (a^m)^n = a^(mn), a^m b^m = (ab)^m, a^0 = 1, a^(-n) = 1/a^n, a^(m/n) = (nth root of a)^m. The square root of a non-perfect-square natural number is irrational and can be located on the number line by the square-root spiral.

Irrational | not p / q | non-terminating, non-repeating

## Exponents

a^m / a^n | a^(m - n) |
