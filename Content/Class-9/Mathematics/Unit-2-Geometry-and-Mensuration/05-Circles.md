Equal chords of a circle subtend equal angles at the centre; the perpendicular from the centre bisects a chord. Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral are supplementary.
## Deep dive: chords and the centre
Equal chords of a circle (or of congruent circles) subtend equal angles at the centre, and conversely. The perpendicular from the centre to a chord bisects the chord, and the line joining the centre to the mid-point of a chord is perpendicular to it. Equal chords are equidistant from the centre and conversely. There is one and only one circle through three non-collinear points.
## Deep dive: angles in a circle
The angle subtended by an arc at the centre is double the angle subtended at any point on the remaining part of the circle. Angles in the same segment are equal. The angle in a semicircle is a right angle. If a line segment joining two points subtends equal angles at two other points on the same side of it, the four points are concyclic.
## Deep dive: cyclic quadrilaterals
A quadrilateral whose four vertices lie on a circle is cyclic. Its opposite angles are supplementary (sum 180 degrees); an exterior angle equals the interior opposite angle. Conversely, if the sum of a pair of opposite angles is 180 degrees the quadrilateral is cyclic. Distance of a chord of length 2c from the centre of a circle of radius r: d = sqrt (r^2 - c^2).
