# Pastoralists in the Modern World
minutes: 40
yt: class 9 history pastoralists in the modern world
@study
Pastoralists are people who move with their herds in search of pasture. In India the Gujjar Bakarwals, Gaddis, Gollas, Kurumas, Dhangars, Banjaras and Raikas, and in Africa the Maasai, lived by herding. Colonial laws, forest reservations, taxes and loss of pastures changed their lives, forcing many to adapt, settle or move.
## Deep dive: pastoralists in India
In the mountains of J&K, Gujjar Bakarwals move between winter lowlands (bhabar) and summer meadows (bugyal). Gaddis of Himachal do the same. Dhangars of Maharashtra rear sheep; Gollas, Kurumas and Kurubas of the south herd cattle and sheep. Raikas of Rajasthan combine farming with camel herding. Banjaras trade grain and cattle.
## Deep dive: colonial effects
The Waste Land Rules turned pastures into farms. The Forest Acts barred grazing in reserved forests. The Criminal Tribes Act (1871) controlled wandering groups. Tax was levied on every animal. As pasture shrank, herders cut herd size, took labour jobs or settled.
## Deep dive: the Maasai
Maasailand in East Africa was divided between British Kenya and German Tanganyika in 1885. Maasai lost much pasture to farms and game reserves like the Masai Mara and Serengeti. Droughts caused severe losses. Social change gave power to chiefs and traders, and young warriors lost their role.
