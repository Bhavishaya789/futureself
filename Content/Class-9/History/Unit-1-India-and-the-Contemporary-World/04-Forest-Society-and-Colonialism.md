# Forest Society and Colonialism
minutes: 40
yt: class 9 history forest society and colonialism
@study
Forests were exploited by colonial rulers for railways, shipbuilding and plantations. The Forest Acts restricted the rights of people who lived in forests, leading to protests such as the Bastar rebellion of 1910 in India and the Samin movement in Java. Scientific forestry replaced diverse forests with commercial species like teak and sal.
## Deep dive: why forests were exploited
The British needed timber for the navy, railways and construction, and land for plantations of tea, coffee and rubber. Forest cover shrank as agriculture and plantations expanded. The government declared forests state property and started the Imperial Forest Service under Dietrich Brandis. The Forest Acts of 1865, 1878 and 1927 classified forests into reserved, protected and village forests.
## Deep dive: effects on people
Villagers lost access to firewood, grazing, fruits, honey and hunting. Shifting cultivation was banned because it was unfit for timber growing. Many communities were forced to work in forests. Some got jobs as labour and as forest guards. Tribal rebellions grew.
## Deep dive: revolts
In Bastar the Dhurwas rose in 1910 under Gunda Dhur after two-thirds of the forest was reserved. In Java, the Kalangs resisted the Dutch and the Samin movement refused to pay taxes. The Dutch enforced Blandongdiensten and later forest laws. Scientific forestry replaced diverse forests with a few species, which harmed biodiversity.
