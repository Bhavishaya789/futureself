# Socialism in Europe and the Russian Revolution
minutes: 40
yt: class 9 history socialism in europe and the russian revolution
@study
The Russian Revolution of 1917 overthrew the Tsar and led to the first socialist state. Russia was an autocracy under Tsar Nicholas II with a poor peasantry and an industrial working class. The February Revolution of 1917 ended Tsarist rule, and the Bolsheviks under Lenin took power in the October Revolution. Stalin later built a planned socialist economy with collectivisation and Five Year Plans.
## Deep dive: Russia before 1914
Russia was an empire stretching over Eurasia ruled by Tsar Nicholas II. Peasants formed most of the population and lived in poverty; landlords held the land. Industry grew late, with long working hours in factories. Socialists organised workers; the Russian Social Democratic Party split into Bolsheviks and Mensheviks. In 1905 Bloody Sunday sparked a revolution, and the Tsar allowed a Duma but soon curbed it.
## Deep dive: 1917
World War I brought heavy losses, food and fuel shortages and anger. In February 1917 workers and women in Petrograd rose, the Tsar abdicated and a Provisional Government took charge. Lenin returned and issued the April Theses. In October 1917 the Bolsheviks seized power. The new government ended the war by the Treaty of Brest-Litovsk, nationalised land and banks and fought a civil war against the Whites.
## Deep dive: after the revolution
The USSR was formed in 1922. Under Stalin, farms were collectivised and Five Year Plans built heavy industry; critics and kulaks were harshly treated. The USSR became a major power and inspired socialist movements worldwide.
