# Poverty as a challenge
minutes: 35
yt: class 9 economics poverty as a challenge
@study
Poverty means being unable to meet basic needs of food, clothing, shelter, health and education. The poverty line separates the poor from the non-poor using the money needed for a minimum calorie intake: 2400 calories a day in rural areas and 2100 in urban areas. Causes: low growth, population, inequality, illiteracy and unemployment. Vulnerability, social exclusion and inequality worsen poverty. Anti-poverty measures include MGNREGA, PMRY, SGSY and the Antyodaya Anna Yojana.
## Deep dive: what poverty means
Poverty is a multi-dimensional problem: hunger, lack of shelter, no safe water, illiteracy, poor health, child labour, no job security and a feeling of helplessness. Poor people live in overcrowded huts, often have no land, work as casual or bonded labour and cannot send children to school. Social scientists look at poverty through the poverty line, social exclusion (being kept out of facilities enjoyed by others, such as through caste), vulnerability (the greater chance of becoming or staying poor in bad times) and inequality.
## Deep dive: the poverty line
The poverty line is a common measure. It is based on the money needed to buy food for a minimum calorie intake (2400 calories per person per day for rural areas and 2100 for urban areas) plus basic clothing, shoes, fuel, light, education and health. Rural people need more calories as they do heavier work, but the money value of the line is higher in towns because of higher prices. The line is updated by the government using the Consumer Price Index; for 2011-12 it was about Rs 816 per person per month in rural and Rs 1000 in urban areas. The Planning Commission earlier, and now NITI Aayog and the National Sample Survey Organisation (NSSO), measure it from household surveys.
## Deep dive: who is poor and why
Poverty is highest among scheduled tribe and scheduled caste households and among casual agricultural labour and urban casual labour households. Poor households are larger, with more children, and women, children and the elderly suffer most. States such as Bihar, Odisha, Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh and Uttar Pradesh have more poor people; Kerala, Punjab, Haryana, Himachal Pradesh and Gujarat have reduced poverty through land reform, growth and food distribution. The proportion of poor in India fell from about 55 percent in 1973 to about 22 percent in 2011-12, but absolute numbers remain large. Globally, poverty fell sharply in China and Southeast Asia, while it remained high in sub-Saharan Africa.
## Deep dive: causes and anti-poverty measures
Causes: colonial rule that destroyed handicrafts, slow growth and population increase, unequal land distribution, unequal income, illiteracy, unemployment, social customs (spending on marriages and festivals leading to debt) and low productivity. Measures: economic growth with jobs, and targeted programmes. MGNREGA 2005 gives 100 days of guaranteed wage employment per rural household. PMRY (1993) helps educated unemployed youth to start businesses, REGP and SGSY support self-employment, PMGSY builds rural roads, and the Antyodaya Anna Yojana gives cheap grain to the poorest. The Midday meal, ICDS and health schemes support the poor. Future challenge: educated, healthy and well-paid for all.
