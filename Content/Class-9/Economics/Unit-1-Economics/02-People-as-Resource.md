# People as resource
minutes: 35
yt: class 9 economics people as resource
@study
People are a resource when they are educated, healthy and skilled; this is human capital formation. Economic activities are primary (agriculture, fishing), secondary (manufacturing) and tertiary (services). Education and health raise productivity and income. Unemployment is when people willing to work at the going wage cannot find jobs: seasonal, disguised and educated unemployment. Government programmes include Sarva Shiksha Abhiyan and the mid-day meal.
## Deep dive: population as an asset
A large population becomes an asset when invested in education, training and health, turning people into human capital. The story of Sakal (a farmer) and Vilas shows how an educated and skilled person earns more than an unskilled one. Land and physical capital are productive only when human beings use them. Japan has few natural resources but invested in people and became rich. Human capital adds more value than land or physical capital because it can use them well and create new knowledge.
## Deep dive: economic activities
Economic activities add value to the national income and are of three sectors: primary (agriculture, dairy, fishing, forestry, poultry farming), secondary (manufacturing of goods by factories and small units) and tertiary or services (trade, transport, communication, banking, education, health, tourism, insurance). Non-economic activities are done for self-satisfaction without pay (cooking for the family). Market activities are done for pay or profit; non-market activities include production for self-consumption. Women's household work is often not counted though it is valuable.
## Deep dive: education, health and training
Education raises skills and income. Literacy rate in India was about 74 percent in the 2011 census, with a gap between men and women and between states. Schemes: Sarva Shiksha Abhiyan (universal elementary education, 2001), mid-day meal (to raise enrolment and nutrition) and National Education Policy. Health includes the ability to work to full potential: measured by life expectancy, infant mortality rate and nutrition. Training makes workers skilled, and migration for better jobs also forms human capital when people earn more.
## Deep dive: unemployment
Unemployment exists when people willing to work at the going wage cannot find work. The working age is 15 to 59 years. Seasonal unemployment occurs in months when there is no farm work. Disguised unemployment occurs when more people work on a farm than needed; removing some does not reduce output. Educated unemployment affects matriculates, graduates and postgraduates who cannot find suitable jobs. Unemployment wastes human resources, lowers income and increases poverty and dependence. Employment generation, skill training and growth of the service sector help to reduce it.
