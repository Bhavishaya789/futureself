# Democratic Rights
minutes: 35
yt: class 9 democratic politics democratic rights
@study
Rights are claims of a person that are recognised by society and the law. The Indian Constitution gives six Fundamental Rights: Equality, Freedom, Against Exploitation, Freedom of Religion, Cultural and Educational Rights and Constitutional Remedies. Citizens can approach courts through writs. The NHRC and the Right to Information also protect rights.
## Deep dive: why rights
Rights are reasonable claims of a person recognised by society and sanctioned by law. In a democracy they protect citizens from the state and from the majority. Examples like Guantanamo Bay (prisoners held without trial), Kosovo (persecution of Albanians) and Saudi Arabia (limited political rights) show what happens when rights are denied.
## Deep dive: Fundamental Rights
Equality (Articles 14 to 18) bans discrimination and untouchability; Freedom (19 to 22) protects speech, assembly, association, movement, residence, profession and life and liberty; Right against Exploitation (23 and 24) bans trafficking, forced labour and child labour; Freedom of Religion (25 to 28) ensures religious freedom; Cultural and Educational Rights (29 and 30) protect minorities; Constitutional Remedies (32) lets citizens approach the Supreme Court and High Courts.
## Deep dive: enforcement
Courts issue writs: habeas corpus, mandamus, prohibition, certiorari and quo warranto. The NHRC (1993) investigates violations, and the Right to Information Act (2005) enables transparency. Education for children aged 6 to 14 became a right under Article 21A. Rights are subject to reasonable restrictions.
