# Electoral Politics
minutes: 35
yt: class 9 democratic politics electoral politics
@study
Elections are the mechanism by which people choose their representatives. India holds elections regularly to Lok Sabha, state assemblies and local bodies. The Election Commission conducts them independently. The chapter explains why elections are needed, what makes them democratic, the electoral constituency, voters list, nomination, campaign, polling, counting and the Model Code of Conduct.
## Deep dive: why elections
In a large democracy people cannot rule directly, so they elect representatives. Elections allow people to choose who will make laws, form the government and decide policies. Voters can also remove those who did not perform. Elections need competition. For a democratic election four conditions are essential: everyone can choose, there is a real choice, the elections are held regularly, and the candidate preferred by the people is elected.
## Deep dive: the election system
India is divided into 543 Lok Sabha constituencies, some reserved for SC and ST. The voters list is prepared by the Election Commission, and everyone aged 18 or above can vote. Candidates file nomination papers with an affidavit about assets and cases. Parties campaign for about two weeks under the Model Code of Conduct. Polling uses EVMs and counting declares the candidate with the most votes the winner.
## Deep dive: free and fair
The Election Commission, headed by the Chief Election Commissioner appointed by the President, works independently. Problems include money power, muscle power and criminals. Courts and the Commission have introduced affidavits and strict rules to improve fairness.
