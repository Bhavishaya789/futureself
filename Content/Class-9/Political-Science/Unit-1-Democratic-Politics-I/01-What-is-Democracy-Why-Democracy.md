Democracy is a form of government in which the rulers are elected by the people. Its key features are elected rulers who take decisions, free and fair elections, one person one vote one value, and rule of law with rights. The chapter uses Chile, Poland and Zimbabwe to show how democracy is gained and how it can be misused, and gives reasons why democracy is the preferred form of government.
## Deep dive: what is democracy
Democracy comes from the Greek words demos (people) and kratos (rule). A democracy is a form of government in which the rulers are elected by the people. Four features define it: the rulers are elected; elections are free and fair with real choice; each adult has one vote, and each vote has one value; and the government works within rule of law, respecting citizens rights. Examples help: Chile under Pinochet (1973 to 1988) was a dictatorship that ended after a referendum; Poland had a one-party rule until Solidarity under Lech Walesa; Zimbabwe holds elections but not free and fair ones; Saudi Arabia denies women the vote.
## Deep dive: why democracy
Democracy is accountable to citizens, improves the quality of decisions through consultation, provides a method to deal with differences and conflicts, enhances the dignity of citizens by treating them as equals, and allows mistakes to be corrected. Critics say it brings instability and delay; supporters reply that these costs are worth the benefits.
## Deep dive: broader meaning
Democracy is not only about elections; it also means equal rights and opportunities in social and economic life. Many countries are still working on becoming more democratic.
