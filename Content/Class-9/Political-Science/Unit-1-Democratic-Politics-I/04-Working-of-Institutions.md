# Working of Institutions
minutes: 35
yt: class 9 democratic politics working of institutions
@study
Democracies run through institutions: the Legislature (Parliament), Executive (Prime Minister, Council of Ministers, civil servants) and Judiciary (courts). The Mandal Commission case shows how they work together. Parliament makes laws, the executive implements them and the judiciary interprets them and protects the Constitution.
## Deep dive: the three organs
Parliament, with the Lok Sabha and the Rajya Sabha, makes laws, controls the government and its money, and discusses national policy. The executive, led by the Prime Minister and the Council of Ministers, implements laws and policies and is assisted by civil servants. The judiciary interprets the Constitution and laws and settles disputes.
## Deep dive: Parliament and executive
The Lok Sabha is directly elected for five years and can remove the government by a no-confidence motion; it has the final say on money bills. The Rajya Sabha is elected by MLAs and has 6-year members. The President is the nominal head of state and acts on the advice of the Council of Ministers, led by the Prime Minister. Political executives take decisions; permanent executives (civil servants) carry them out.
## Deep dive: judiciary and Mandal
The Supreme Court is the highest court; judges are appointed by the President in consultation with the Chief Justice, and the judiciary is independent. Through judicial review it can strike down laws against the Constitution. The Mandal case: the 1979 Commission recommended 27 percent OBC reservation; in 1990 Prime Minister V. P. Singh announced it; the Supreme Court in 1992 upheld it, excluding the creamy layer.
