# Natural vegetation and wildlife
minutes: 35
yt: class 9 geography natural vegetation and wildlife
@study
Natural vegetation depends on temperature, rainfall, soil and relief. India has tropical evergreen forests, tropical deciduous forests, thorny scrubs, montane forests and mangrove forests. India has about 8 percent of the world's species. Projects and reserves like Project Tiger and biosphere reserves protect wildlife.
## Deep dive: forest types
Tropical evergreen forests grow where rainfall exceeds 200 cm with a short dry season, in the Western Ghats, Lakshadweep, Andaman and Nicobar, and the north-east. They are dense with tall trees such as rosewood, mahogany and ebony. Tropical deciduous (monsoon) forests are the most widespread, in 70 to 200 cm rainfall, with teak, sal, shisham, sandalwood and khair; they shed leaves in the dry season. Thorny forests, in less than 70 cm rainfall, have acacia, palms, euphorbia and cacti with long roots and thorns. Montane forests change with height: wet temperate, then coniferous such as pine, deodar and fir, and alpine. Mangroves grow on tidal coasts, with the Sundari tree in the Sundarbans.
## Deep dive: wildlife
India has about 8.1 percent of the world's species: about 47000 species of plants and 89000 species of animals. The elephant lives in Assam, Karnataka and Kerala; the one-horned rhino in Assam and West Bengal; the Asiatic lion in Gir; the tiger in many forests including the Sundarbans; the camel in the Thar; and the yak, snow leopard and bear in the Himalayas. Vultures and other species are declining.
## Deep dive: conservation
The Wildlife Protection Act (1972), Project Tiger (1973), Project Elephant (1992), 18 biosphere reserves, national parks and sanctuaries conserve nature. Deforestation, poaching and pollution are the main threats.
