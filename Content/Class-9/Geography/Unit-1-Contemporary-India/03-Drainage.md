# Drainage
minutes: 35
yt: class 9 geography drainage
@study
Drainage means the river system of an area. India has the Himalayan rivers (Indus, Ganga, Brahmaputra), which are perennial and fed by rain and snow, and the Peninsular rivers (Narmada, Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri), which are seasonal and rain-fed. The Ganga is the longest river of India. Rivers form deltas, lakes and waterfalls.
## Deep dive: Himalayan rivers
The Indus rises near Mansarovar in Tibet, flows west through Ladakh and Pakistan and falls in the Arabian Sea, length about 2900 km. The Ganga rises at Gangotri as the Bhagirathi, meets the Alaknanda at Devprayag, enters the plains at Haridwar and flows 2525 km to the Bay of Bengal, forming the Sundarbans delta with the Brahmaputra. The Brahmaputra rises in Tibet as the Tsangpo, enters India as the Dihang, forms the Majuli island and floods Assam and Bangladesh.
## Deep dive: peninsular rivers
The Narmada and Tapi flow west in rift valleys and form estuaries. The Godavari (1465 km, Dakshin Ganga), Krishna, Kaveri and Mahanadi flow east and form deltas. Peninsular rivers are seasonal, have shorter and shallower courses and flow in fixed beds.
## Deep dive: lakes and river pollution
Lakes are formed by glaciers, wind, river action and humans. Wular (Kashmir) is the largest freshwater lake, Dal is in Srinagar, Sambhar is a salt water lake, and Chilika is a lagoon on the Odisha coast. Rivers are polluted by sewage and industrial waste. The Ganga Action Plan (1985) and the National River Conservation Plan (1995) aim to clean them.
