**Law of conservation of mass** and **law of constant proportions** (water is always 1:8 by mass of hydrogen to oxygen). Atom: smallest unit of an element. **Mole** = 6.022 x 10^23 particles. Molecular mass of water = 2 x 1 + 16 = **18 u**.
**Atomic structure:** protons (+), neutrons (0) in the nucleus, electrons (-) in shells 2, 8, 8. Atomic number = protons; mass number = protons + neutrons. Example: sodium (Z = 11) is 2, 8, 1. **Isotopes** have the same Z but different mass numbers (C-12 and C-14).
**Cell:** basic unit of life. Plant cells have a cell wall and chloroplasts; animal cells do not. Tissues: epithelial, connective, muscular, nervous.
## Deep dive: matter, atoms and molecules
**Laws of chemical combination.** Conservation of mass: mass is neither created nor destroyed in a reaction. Constant proportions: a compound always has the same elements in the same ratio by mass (water: 1 part hydrogen to 8 parts oxygen). Dalton's atomic theory: matter is made of indivisible atoms; atoms of an element are identical; atoms combine in simple whole-number ratios; atoms are neither created nor destroyed in a reaction.
**Symbols and formulae.** Elements have symbols (H, O, Na from natrium, K from kalium, Fe from ferrum). Valency is the combining capacity: Na 1, Mg 2, Al 3, C 4, O 2, Cl 1. Write the symbols, put the valencies crosswise and simplify: Al and O give Al2O3. Atomic mass is in u (1 u = 1/12 of the mass of a carbon-12 atom). Molecular mass is the sum of atomic masses: H2O = 18 u, CO2 = 44 u, NaCl = 58.5 u, H2SO4 = 98 u.
**The mole.** One mole is 6.022 x 10^23 particles (Avogadro number). Moles = given mass / molar mass = number of particles / 6.022 x 10^23. 18 g of water is one mole.
## Deep dive: structure of the atom
**Subatomic particles.** Electron (-1, negligible mass, J. J. Thomson), proton (+1, mass 1 u, Goldstein), neutron (0, mass 1 u, Chadwick). Rutherford's gold foil experiment showed a small dense positive nucleus with electrons around it; Bohr added fixed orbits (shells) K, L, M, N holding at most 2, 8, 18, 32 electrons (the outermost shell holds at most 8).
**Numbers.** Atomic number Z = number of protons = number of electrons in a neutral atom. Mass number A = protons + neutrons. Isotopes have the same Z but different A (C-12, C-14; Cl-35, Cl-37); isobars have the same A but different Z (Ar-40, Ca-40). Valence electrons decide valency. Electronic configuration: Na (11) 2, 8, 1; Cl (17) 2, 8, 7; Ar (18) 2, 8, 8.
## Deep dive: the cell
**Cell theory.** All living things are made of cells; the cell is the basic unit of structure and function; cells come from pre-existing cells (Schleiden, Schwann, Virchow). Cells were first seen by Robert Hooke in cork.
**Parts.** Cell membrane (selective, controls entry and exit). Cell wall (cellulose; plants only). Nucleus (chromosomes, DNA, controls the cell). Cytoplasm (jelly where reactions occur). Mitochondria (powerhouse; respiration, ATP). Endoplasmic reticulum (transport, synthesis; rough has ribosomes). Golgi apparatus (packaging and secretion). Lysosomes (digestion; suicide bags). Ribosomes (protein synthesis). Plastids: chloroplasts (photosynthesis), chromoplasts, leucoplasts. Vacuoles (large in plants, small in animals).
**Prokaryotes and eukaryotes.** Bacteria have no true nucleus (a nucleoid) and no membrane-bound organelles; eukaryotes do. Osmosis is the movement of water through a semipermeable membrane towards the higher solute concentration; diffusion is movement from high to low concentration.
**Tissues.** Plant: meristematic (growth) and permanent (parenchyma, collenchyma, sclerenchyma, xylem, phloem). Animal: epithelial (covering), connective (blood, bone, cartilage, tendons), muscular (striated, smooth, cardiac) and nervous (neurons).
