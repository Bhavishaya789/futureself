@study
A pure substance has a single kind of particle: elements and compounds. A mixture has two or more substances in any proportion: homogeneous (solution) or heterogeneous (suspension, colloid). Concentration of a solution: mass percent = (mass of solute/mass of solution) x 100; volume percent = (volume of solute/volume of solution) x 100. Separation methods: filtration, evaporation, centrifugation, distillation, fractional distillation, chromatography, sublimation and crystallisation.
## Deep dive: pure substances and mixtures
A pure substance has constant composition and properties throughout. Elements (hydrogen, iron, oxygen) cannot be broken down further by chemical means; compounds (water, sodium chloride) are formed by the chemical combination of two or more elements in a fixed ratio, with properties different from those of the elements. Mixtures are formed by physical mixing in any ratio; components keep their properties and can be separated by physical methods. Homogeneous mixtures (solutions, alloys, air) have uniform composition; heterogeneous mixtures (sand and water, oil and water, chalk powder in water) do not.
## Deep dive: solutions, suspensions and colloids
A solution is a homogeneous mixture of solute and solvent: particles are below 1 nm, the path of light is not visible, it is stable and passes through filter paper (sugar in water). A suspension is heterogeneous: particles larger than 100 nm are visible, scatter light, settle on standing and can be filtered (chalk in water). A colloid has particles of size 1 to 100 nm; it appears homogeneous but scatters light (Tyndall effect), is stable, does not settle and cannot be filtered (milk, fog, smoke, ink). Concentration of a solution = amount of solute in a given amount of solution: mass by mass percentage = (mass of solute/mass of solution) x 100; mass by volume percent; volume by volume percent. A saturated solution cannot dissolve more solute at that temperature; solubility generally rises with temperature.
## Deep dive: separation techniques and changes
Filtration: insoluble solid from liquid. Evaporation: a soluble solid from its solution (salt from sea water). Centrifugation: cream from milk. Separating funnel: two immiscible liquids (oil and water). Sublimation: ammonium chloride from salt. Chromatography: dyes in ink; Rf = distance by solute/distance by solvent. Distillation: miscible liquids with boiling points differing by more than 25 K (acetone and water); fractional distillation for closer boiling points (petroleum fractions, gases of air). Crystallisation: purifying copper sulphate or alum from impure samples. A physical change affects the form but not the composition (melting, freezing); a chemical change forms new substances (burning, rusting, digestion). Air is a mixture: about 78% nitrogen, 21% oxygen, 0.9% argon and 0.04% carbon dioxide.
