# Natural Resources
minutes: 40
yt: class 9 science natural resources
@study
Natural resources include air, water and soil. The atmosphere is a blanket of air that regulates temperature and protects life; it contains nitrogen (78%), oxygen (21%), carbon dioxide (0.03%) and other gases. The greenhouse effect warms the Earth. The ozone layer shields us from UV rays. Water, carbon, nitrogen and oxygen move through biogeochemical cycles between living and non-living components. Pollution (acid rain, smog, water and soil pollution) harms these resources. Soil forms by weathering of rocks and is affected by erosion.
## Deep dive: air
The atmosphere is mainly nitrogen (78%) and oxygen (21%) with small amounts of argon, carbon dioxide and water vapour. It regulates temperature, supports respiration and photosynthesis and shields us from UV rays. The greenhouse effect keeps the Earth warm, but excess CO2 and methane cause global warming. Ozone (O3) in the stratosphere absorbs UV; CFCs damage it. Burning fuels releases SO2 and nitrogen oxides, causing acid rain and smog.
## Deep dive: water
About 71% of the Earth is covered with water. The water cycle includes evaporation, transpiration, condensation and precipitation. Groundwater, rivers, lakes and glaciers are sources of fresh water. Sewage, industrial waste and chemicals pollute water.
## Deep dive: soil
Soil forms by weathering of rocks aided by sun, wind, water and organisms; humus makes it fertile. Soil erosion is checked by trees and terrace farming.
## Deep dive: biogeochemical cycles
Nitrogen is fixed by Rhizobium and lightning, converted by Nitrosomonas and Nitrobacter to nitrate, absorbed by plants, and returned to the air by denitrification (Pseudomonas). Carbon moves by photosynthesis, respiration, combustion and decay. Oxygen moves between photosynthesis and respiration.

Q: What is the atmosphere?
A: The blanket of air around the Earth.
Q: Name the main gases of air and their percentages.
A: Nitrogen 78%, oxygen 21%, argon 0.9%, carbon dioxide 0.03%.
Q: Name the layers of atmosphere.
A: Troposphere, stratosphere, mesosphere and thermosphere.
Q: What is the role of the atmosphere in climate?
A: It keeps the average temperature steady during day and night.
Q: What is wind?
A: Movement of air from high pressure to low pressure areas.
Q: What causes land and sea breezes?
A: Unequal heating of land and water.
Q: What is rain?
A: Water that falls after condensation of water vapour.
Q: What is the greenhouse effect?
A: Warming of Earth because gases trap heat.
Q: Name greenhouse gases.
A: Carbon dioxide, methane, nitrous oxide and water vapour.
Q: What is global warming?
A: Rise in the Earth's average temperature due to increased greenhouse gases.
Q: What is the ozone layer?
A: A layer of ozone in the stratosphere that absorbs harmful UV rays.
Q: What is the chemical formula of ozone?
A: O3.
Q: How is ozone formed?
A: High-energy UV splits O2 into O atoms which combine with O2 to form O3.
Q: What harms the ozone layer?
A: Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs).
Q: What is acid rain?
A: Rain with sulphuric and nitric acids formed from SO2 and nitrogen oxides.
Q: What causes smog?
A: Smoke plus fog and pollutants.
Q: What is hydrosphere?
A: All water bodies on the Earth.
Q: How much of Earth's surface is water?
A: About 71%.
Q: Name the sources of fresh water.
A: Rivers, lakes, glaciers and groundwater.
Q: What is the water cycle?
A: Continuous movement of water between oceans, atmosphere and land.
Q: What is evaporation?
A: Change of water into vapour by heat.
Q: What is transpiration?
A: Loss of water vapour from plant leaves.
Q: What is precipitation?
A: Falling of water as rain, snow or hail.
Q: What is groundwater?
A: Water stored below the ground.
Q: What is water pollution?
A: Contamination of water by harmful substances.
Q: Name causes of water pollution.
A: Sewage, industrial waste, pesticides and fertilisers.
Q: What is a bio-indicator?
A: A species whose presence shows the state of the environment.
Q: What is soil?
A: The upper layer of the Earth formed by weathering of rocks and decay of organic matter.
Q: How is soil formed?
A: By weathering of rocks due to sun, water, wind and living organisms.
Q: What is humus?
A: Decayed organic matter that improves soil fertility.
Q: What is soil erosion?
A: Removal of top soil by wind and water.
Q: How can soil erosion be prevented?
A: By planting trees, terrace farming and crop cover.
Q: What is the biosphere?
A: The part of Earth where life exists: air, water and soil.
Q: What is a biogeochemical cycle?
A: Movement of nutrients between living organisms and the environment.
Q: What is nitrogen fixation?
A: Conversion of atmospheric nitrogen into usable compounds.
Q: Which bacteria fix nitrogen in root nodules?
A: Rhizobium.
Q: How is nitrogen fixed in the atmosphere?
A: By lightning which converts nitrogen into nitrogen oxides.
Q: Which bacteria convert ammonia to nitrite?
A: Nitrosomonas.
Q: Which bacteria convert nitrite to nitrate?
A: Nitrobacter.
Q: What is denitrification?
A: Conversion of nitrates back to nitrogen gas by bacteria like Pseudomonas.
Q: What is the carbon cycle?
A: Movement of carbon between atmosphere, living things and fossil fuels.
Q: How is carbon dioxide added to the air?
A: Respiration, burning of fuels and decomposition.
Q: How is carbon dioxide removed from the air?
A: Photosynthesis and dissolving in oceans.
Q: What is the oxygen cycle?
A: Balance between oxygen release by photosynthesis and use in respiration and burning.
Q: What are the main forms of carbon on Earth?
A: Carbon dioxide, carbonates, glucose and fossil fuels.
Q: Why is the water cycle important?
A: It supplies fresh water and regulates climate.
Q: Why is nitrogen essential?
A: For proteins, nucleic acids and chlorophyll.
