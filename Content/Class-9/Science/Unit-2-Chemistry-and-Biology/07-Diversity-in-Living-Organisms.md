# Diversity in Living Organisms
minutes: 40
yt: class 9 science diversity in living organisms
@study
Living organisms are classified on the basis of cell structure, body organisation, mode of nutrition and reproduction. R. H. Whittaker proposed the five-kingdom classification in 1969: Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae and Animalia. Plants are divided into Thallophyta, Bryophyta, Pteridophyta, Gymnosperms and Angiosperms. Animals are divided into phyla from Porifera to Chordata. Carolus Linnaeus gave the binomial system of naming. The hierarchy is kingdom, phylum or division, class, order, family, genus and species.
## Deep dive: need for classification
Classification makes the study of the huge diversity of life easier. Organisms are grouped by characteristics: cell type (prokaryotic or eukaryotic), body organisation (unicellular or multicellular), mode of nutrition (autotrophic or heterotrophic) and reproduction. Characteristics that appeared earlier in evolution are more basic.
## Deep dive: five kingdoms
Monera: prokaryotes (bacteria, cyanobacteria). Protista: unicellular eukaryotes (Amoeba, Euglena). Fungi: heterotrophic, chitin cell wall (yeast, mushroom). Plantae: multicellular autotrophs with cell walls. Animalia: multicellular heterotrophs without cell walls.
## Deep dive: plant kingdom
Thallophyta (algae), Bryophyta (moss), Pteridophyta (ferns), Gymnosperms (pine, Cycas) and Angiosperms (flowering plants, monocots and dicots).
## Deep dive: animal kingdom
Porifera, Coelenterata, Platyhelminthes, Nematoda, Annelida, Arthropoda, Mollusca, Echinodermata, Protochordata and Vertebrata. Vertebrates: Pisces, Amphibia, Reptilia, Aves and Mammalia. Nomenclature: Genus species, e.g. Homo sapiens.

Q: What is classification?
A: Grouping organisms according to similarities and differences.
Q: Who proposed the five-kingdom classification?
A: R. H. Whittaker in 1969.
Q: Name the five kingdoms.
A: Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae and Animalia.
Q: What are the basis of five-kingdom classification?
A: Cell structure, body organisation, mode of nutrition and reproduction, and phylogenetic relationship.
Q: Which kingdom has prokaryotic cells?
A: Monera.
Q: Give examples of Monera.
A: Bacteria and blue-green algae (cyanobacteria).
Q: Which kingdom has unicellular eukaryotes?
A: Protista.
Q: Give examples of Protista.
A: Amoeba, Paramecium, Euglena and diatoms.
Q: Which kingdom has heterotrophic decomposers with chitin cell walls?
A: Fungi.
Q: Give examples of Fungi.
A: Yeast, Mushroom and Aspergillus.
Q: What is the cell wall of fungi made of?
A: Chitin.
Q: What is a symbiotic association of fungi with algae called?
A: Lichens.
Q: Which kingdom has multicellular autotrophs with cell walls?
A: Plantae.
Q: Which kingdom has multicellular heterotrophs without cell walls?
A: Animalia.
Q: Who gave the binomial nomenclature?
A: Carolus Linnaeus.
Q: What is binomial nomenclature?
A: A system of naming organisms with a genus name and a species name.
Q: Write the binomial name of humans.
A: Homo sapiens.
Q: Write the binomial name of mango.
A: Mangifera indica.
Q: Write the binomial name of the tiger.
A: Panthera tigris.
Q: Write the binomial name of the lion.
A: Panthera leo.
Q: Write the binomial name of the dog.
A: Canis familiaris.
Q: How is a scientific name written?
A: Genus capitalised first, species lower case, italicised or underlined.
Q: What are the levels of classification?
A: Kingdom, phylum/division, class, order, family, genus and species.
Q: What is a species?
A: A group of organisms that can interbreed to produce fertile offspring.
Q: Name the five divisions of plants.
A: Thallophyta, Bryophyta, Pteridophyta, Gymnosperms and Angiosperms.
Q: Which plant group has no differentiated body?
A: Thallophyta, like algae.
Q: Which plant group is called amphibians of the plant kingdom?
A: Bryophyta.
Q: Give examples of Bryophyta.
A: Moss and Marchantia.
Q: Which plant group has vascular tissues but no flowers or seeds?
A: Pteridophyta.
Q: Give examples of Pteridophyta.
A: Marsilea, ferns and horsetails.
Q: Which plants have naked seeds?
A: Gymnosperms.
Q: Give examples of Gymnosperms.
A: Pine and Cycas.
Q: Which plants have seeds enclosed in fruits?
A: Angiosperms.
Q: What are cotyledons?
A: Seed leaves in the embryo of a seed.
Q: What are monocots?
A: Angiosperms with seeds having one cotyledon.
Q: What are dicots?
A: Angiosperms with seeds having two cotyledons.
Q: Name the phyla of animal kingdom.
A: Porifera, Coelenterata, Platyhelminthes, Nematoda, Annelida, Arthropoda, Mollusca, Echinodermata, Protochordata and Vertebrata.
Q: What are porifera?
A: Pore-bearing animals such as sponges.
Q: What are coelenterates?
A: Animals with a body cavity and stinging cells, like Hydra and jellyfish.
Q: What are platyhelminthes?
A: Flatworms like tapeworm and Planaria.
Q: What are nematodes?
A: Roundworms with a tubular body, such as Ascaris.
Q: What are annelids?
A: Segmented worms like earthworm and leech.
Q: What are arthropods?
A: Animals with jointed legs, like prawn, butterfly, spider and scorpion.
Q: What is the largest phylum of animals?
A: Arthropoda.
Q: What are molluscs?
A: Soft-bodied animals with a shell, like snail and octopus.
Q: What are echinoderms?
A: Spiny-skinned marine animals like starfish and sea urchin.
Q: Name the classes of vertebrates.
A: Pisces, Amphibia, Reptilia, Aves and Mammalia.
Q: Which vertebrates have a two-chambered heart?
A: Pisces (fishes).
Q: Which vertebrates have a three-chambered heart?
A: Amphibia and most Reptilia.
Q: Which vertebrates have a four-chambered heart?
A: Aves and Mammalia (and crocodiles).
Q: Which vertebrates are warm-blooded?
A: Aves and Mammalia.
Q: Which vertebrates lay eggs and have feathers?
A: Aves (birds).
Q: Which vertebrates have mammary glands?
A: Mammalia.
Q: Name egg-laying mammals.
A: Platypus and echidna.
