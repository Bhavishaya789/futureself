# Why Do We Fall Ill
minutes: 40
yt: class 9 science why do we fall ill
@study
Health is a state of physical, mental and social well-being. Diseases may be acute or chronic and may be infectious (caused by viruses, bacteria, fungi, protozoa or worms) or non-infectious. Immediate causes are the pathogens, while contributory causes include poor hygiene, poverty and poor nutrition. Infectious diseases spread through air, water, vectors and contact. Treatment kills the cause and relieves symptoms. Prevention is general (clean water, food, vector control) and specific (vaccination). Edward Jenner developed the smallpox vaccine.
## Deep dive: health and disease
Health depends on physical, mental and social well-being, which in turn depend on clean surroundings, food, income and social equality. Disease is a disturbance of body functions. Acute diseases (cold, cough) are short-lived; chronic diseases (TB, elephantiasis) are long-lasting and affect general health.
## Deep dive: causes and spread
Infectious diseases have immediate causes (pathogens) and contributory causes (poverty, bad hygiene). Pathogens spread through air (cold, TB), water (cholera, typhoid), vectors (mosquitoes for malaria and dengue) and contact (AIDS). Viruses cause cold, dengue, AIDS and polio; bacteria cause typhoid, cholera and TB; fungi cause ringworm; protozoa cause malaria and kala-azar; worms cause elephantiasis.
## Deep dive: treatment and prevention
Treatment relieves symptoms and kills the cause. Antibiotics kill bacteria but not viruses. Prevention includes general measures (clean water and surroundings, nutrition) and specific measures (vaccination). Jenner introduced smallpox vaccination in 1796; vaccines exist for DPT, polio, measles, BCG, hepatitis and others.
## Deep dive: public health
Community health is protected by sanitation, safe water, vector control and immunisation programmes. Early diagnosis and good nutrition help the immune system resist infection.

Q: What is health?
A: A state of complete physical, mental and social well-being.
Q: What is disease?
A: A condition in which normal functioning of the body is disturbed.
Q: What are acute diseases?
A: Diseases lasting for a short time, like common cold.
Q: What are chronic diseases?
A: Diseases lasting a long time, like tuberculosis and elephantiasis.
Q: Why do chronic diseases affect health more?
A: They drain the body over a long period.
Q: Name the immediate cause of infectious disease.
A: A pathogen such as virus or bacteria.
Q: What are contributory causes?
A: Poverty, poor hygiene, malnutrition and overcrowding that make people susceptible.
Q: What are non-infectious diseases?
A: Diseases not spread from person to person, like cancer or hypertension.
Q: What are infectious diseases?
A: Diseases caused by organisms that spread from one person to another.
Q: Name the pathogens causing infectious diseases.
A: Viruses, bacteria, fungi, protozoa and worms.
Q: Name diseases caused by viruses.
A: Common cold, influenza, dengue, AIDS, polio and hepatitis.
Q: Name diseases caused by bacteria.
A: Typhoid, cholera, tuberculosis, anthrax and tetanus.
Q: Name diseases caused by fungi.
A: Skin infections like ringworm.
Q: Name diseases caused by protozoa.
A: Malaria and kala-azar.
Q: Name diseases caused by worms.
A: Elephantiasis and intestinal worm infections.
Q: How are diseases spread through air?
A: By droplets from coughing and sneezing, as in common cold and tuberculosis.
Q: How are diseases spread through water?
A: By drinking contaminated water, as in cholera.
Q: Which disease spread by sexual contact is caused by a virus?
A: AIDS (HIV).
Q: What is a vector?
A: An animal that carries a pathogen from one host to another, such as a mosquito.
Q: Which mosquito spreads malaria?
A: Female Anopheles.
Q: Which organism causes malaria?
A: Plasmodium.
Q: Which mosquito spreads dengue?
A: Aedes.
Q: What causes cholera?
A: Vibrio cholerae.
Q: What causes typhoid?
A: Salmonella typhi.
Q: What causes tuberculosis?
A: Mycobacterium tuberculosis.
Q: What causes kala-azar?
A: Leishmania.
Q: What causes AIDS?
A: Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV).
Q: Which organ does HIV affect?
A: The immune system.
Q: What does hepatitis affect?
A: The liver.
Q: Which type of hepatitis spreads through contaminated water?
A: Hepatitis A.
Q: Who discovered penicillin?
A: Alexander Fleming in 1928.
Q: What are antibiotics?
A: Medicines that block the biochemical pathways of bacteria and kill them.
Q: Do antibiotics work against viruses?
A: No.
Q: Why can we not easily kill viruses?
A: They have few biochemical pathways of their own.
Q: What are the two principles of treatment?
A: Reduce symptoms and kill the cause of the disease.
Q: What are the two types of prevention?
A: General and specific.
Q: Give general ways of preventing disease.
A: Clean water, clean food, hygienic surroundings, vector control and good nutrition.
Q: What is immunisation?
A: Giving vaccines to build immunity against a disease.
Q: Who developed the smallpox vaccine?
A: Edward Jenner in 1796.
Q: Which disease has been eradicated globally by vaccination?
A: Smallpox.
Q: Name some diseases for which vaccines are available.
A: Tetanus, diphtheria, whooping cough, measles, polio, tuberculosis and hepatitis.
Q: What is the DPT vaccine against?
A: Diphtheria, pertussis (whooping cough) and tetanus.
Q: What is the BCG vaccine against?
A: Tuberculosis.
Q: What is the immune system?
A: Body cells that fight and remember pathogens.
Q: Which blood cells fight infection?
A: White blood cells.
Q: What is incubation period?
A: The time between infection and appearance of symptoms.
Q: What are symptoms?
A: Signs of disease felt by the patient, like fever.
Q: What are signs of disease?
A: Clinical findings noted by the doctor, like a rash.
Q: What does a high WBC count suggest?
A: Possible infection.
Q: Why is mosquito control important?
A: To prevent malaria, dengue and other vector-borne diseases.
