# Structure of the atom

@study
An atom has a nucleus of protons and neutrons with electrons in shells. Atomic number Z = number of protons; mass number A = protons + neutrons. Electrons fill shells K, L, M, N with a maximum of 2n^2 (2, 8, 18, 32), with the outermost shell holding at most 8 electrons. Valency is the number of electrons lost, gained or shared to complete the octet. Isotopes have the same Z but different A; isobars the same A but different Z.
## Deep dive: models of the atom
Thomson proposed the plum-pudding model: a positively charged sphere with electrons embedded in it. Rutherford bombarded a thin gold foil with alpha particles: most passed straight through (the atom is mostly empty space), a few were deflected by small angles, and very few (about 1 in 12000) bounced back (a tiny, dense, positively charged nucleus). His model has a central nucleus with electrons revolving around it, but could not explain why the atom is stable. Bohr proposed that electrons revolve only in certain special orbits (shells) without radiating energy; each shell has fixed energy and is labelled K, L, M, N or n = 1, 2, 3, 4. Chadwick discovered the neutron in 1932; Goldstein discovered the canal rays (protons) and J. J. Thomson the electron.
## Deep dive: subatomic particles and electronic configuration
Electron: charge -1, mass about 1/1837 of a proton, found outside the nucleus. Proton: charge +1, mass 1 u. Neutron: no charge, mass 1 u. Atomic number Z = protons = electrons in a neutral atom; mass number A = protons + neutrons, so neutrons = A - Z. The Bohr-Bury scheme: the maximum number of electrons in the nth shell is 2n^2 (K = 2, L = 8, M = 18, N = 32); the outermost shell can hold at most 8 electrons (2 for the first shell); electrons fill a new shell only after the previous inner shell is full. Valency: elements with 1, 2, 3 electrons in the outermost shell have valency 1, 2, 3 (lose electrons); with 5, 6, 7 electrons the valency is 3, 2, 1 (gain electrons); with 4 electrons valency 4 (share); noble gases have valency 0 as their shells are complete.
## Deep dive: isotopes, isobars and atomic mass
Isotopes are atoms of the same element with the same atomic number but different mass numbers (hydrogen: protium 1, deuterium 2, tritium 3; chlorine-35 and chlorine-37; carbon-12 and carbon-14). They have the same chemical properties but different physical properties. Applications: uranium-235 in nuclear reactors, cobalt-60 in cancer treatment, iodine-131 for goitre, carbon-14 dating. Isobars have the same mass number but different atomic numbers (argon-40 and calcium-40). Average atomic mass = sum of (isotope mass x abundance). For chlorine: 35 x 0.75 + 37 x 0.25 = 35.5 u. Isotones have the same number of neutrons.
