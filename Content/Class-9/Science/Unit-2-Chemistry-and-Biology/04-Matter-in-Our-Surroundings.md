# Matter in our surroundings

@study
Matter has mass and occupies space; it exists as solid, liquid and gas, made of tiny particles with spaces between them that attract each other and are always moving. Melting point of ice is 273 K, boiling point of water is 373 K; K = C + 273. Latent heat of fusion and vaporisation hide heat during change of state. Evaporation cools; humidity and wind speed affect its rate.
## Deep dive: particle nature of matter
Matter is made of very small particles (the particle size is about 10^-9 m or less); a few crystals of potassium permanganate colour a whole beaker of water, showing how tiny and numerous the particles are. Particles have spaces between them (sugar dissolves in water without the level rising much), are continuously moving (diffusion of ink in water, smell of perfume spreading, faster in hot water) and attract each other (the force of attraction is strong in solids and weak in gases; an iron nail is hard to break while a gas spreads freely). Rate of diffusion is greater in gases than in liquids, and rises with temperature.
## Deep dive: states of matter and changes
Solid: definite shape and volume, negligible compressibility, particles tightly packed in fixed positions. Liquid: definite volume but no definite shape, small compressibility, particles slide past each other. Gas: no definite shape or volume, highly compressible (LPG, CNG), particles far apart moving randomly at high speed. Plasma and Bose-Einstein condensate are other states. Melting point is the temperature at which a solid changes to liquid at atmospheric pressure (ice 273.15 K); boiling point is where a liquid changes to gas (water 373 K). Latent heat of fusion is the heat needed to convert 1 kg of solid to liquid at its melting point without raising temperature (ice: 3.34 x 10^5 J/kg); latent heat of vaporisation is for liquid to gas at the boiling point (water: 22.5 x 10^5 J/kg), so steam at 373 K has more energy than water at the same temperature. Sublimation: solid to gas directly (camphor, naphthalene, ammonium chloride, dry ice).
## Deep dive: evaporation and temperature scales
Evaporation is a surface phenomenon: particles near the surface with enough kinetic energy escape. Factors raising its rate: larger surface area, higher temperature, lower humidity and higher wind speed. It causes cooling (sweating cools the body, water in an earthen pot or desert cooler stays cool, cotton clothes in summer absorb sweat). Boiling is a bulk phenomenon at a fixed temperature. Temperature conversion: K = C + 273 and C = K - 273; Fahrenheit F = 9C/5 + 32. Pressure also affects the state: increasing pressure and decreasing temperature can liquefy gases; solid CO2 is called dry ice because it sublimes on reducing pressure to 1 atm.
