@study
Sound is a longitudinal mechanical wave that needs a medium. Speed v = f lambda; in air about 343 m/s at 20 C. Frequency is heard as pitch and amplitude as loudness. Audible range is 20 Hz to 20 000 Hz. Echo needs a reflector at least 17 m away; reflection of sound follows the same laws as light. Ultrasound (above 20 kHz) is used in sonar and medical imaging.
## Deep dive: production and propagation of sound
Sound is produced by vibrating bodies (vocal cords, a tuning fork, a stretched string). It travels as a longitudinal wave: particles of the medium vibrate parallel to the direction of the wave, forming alternate compressions (high pressure) and rarefactions (low pressure). It needs a material medium - solid, liquid or gas - and cannot travel through a vacuum (the bell-jar experiment). The speed of sound is greatest in solids and least in gases (about 5000 m/s in iron, 1500 m/s in water, 343 m/s in air at 20 C) and increases with temperature.
## Deep dive: characteristics of sound
Wavelength lambda is the distance between two consecutive compressions or rarefactions; frequency f is the number of vibrations per second (hertz); time period T = 1/f; amplitude is the maximum displacement. Speed v = f x lambda. Pitch depends on frequency (a high frequency gives a shrill sound); loudness depends on amplitude and is measured in decibels; quality or timbre distinguishes two sounds of the same pitch and loudness. Intensity is the sound energy passing per second per unit area. Humans hear 20 Hz to 20 000 Hz; infrasound (below 20 Hz) is produced by earthquakes and heard by elephants and whales; ultrasound (above 20 000 Hz) is heard by dogs, bats and dolphins.
## Deep dive: reflection, echo, reverberation and applications
Sound reflects from hard surfaces and obeys the laws of reflection (angle of incidence equals angle of reflection, all in one plane). An echo is the repetition of sound heard after reflection; the persistence of hearing is 0.1 second, so for an echo the minimum distance to the reflector is d = v t/2 = 343 x 0.1/2 = about 17 m. Repeated reflections give reverberation, reduced in halls by absorbing materials, curved ceilings and soundboards. Applications: megaphones, stethoscopes, SONAR (distance = v t/2), ultrasound scanning, cleaning of delicate parts, and flaw detection in metals. The human ear has the outer ear (pinna), middle ear (three ossicles: hammer, anvil, stirrup) and the inner ear with the cochlea that converts vibrations to electrical signals sent to the brain.
