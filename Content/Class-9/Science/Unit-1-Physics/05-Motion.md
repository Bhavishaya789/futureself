# Motion
minutes: 40
yt: class 9 science motion
@study
Motion is the change of position of an object with time. Distance is the total path length (scalar); displacement is the shortest straight-line distance with direction (vector). Speed is distance per unit time; velocity is displacement per unit time. Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity. For uniform acceleration the three equations of motion are v = u + at, s = ut + (1/2)at² and v² = u² + 2as. Motion can be shown on distance-time and velocity-time graphs; the slope of a distance-time graph gives speed, the slope of a velocity-time graph gives acceleration and the area under it gives distance.
## Deep dive: describing motion
Position is described relative to a reference point. Distance is a scalar and displacement is a vector. A car travelling round a circular track and returning to the start has zero displacement but non-zero distance. Speed = distance/time, velocity = displacement/time and acceleration = (v - u)/t.
## Deep dive: equations of motion
For uniform acceleration: v = u + at; s = ut + (1/2)at²; v² = u² + 2as. They are derived from the velocity-time graph: the slope gives a and the area under the graph gives s. For a body starting from rest u = 0.
## Deep dive: graphs
A distance-time graph with a straight line shows uniform speed, a horizontal line shows rest, and a curve shows changing speed. In a velocity-time graph a horizontal line shows uniform velocity and a sloped line shows uniform acceleration; the area under it is the distance travelled.
## Deep dive: circular motion
In uniform circular motion speed is constant but velocity changes continuously because direction changes, so the motion is accelerated. Speed v = 2πr/T.
