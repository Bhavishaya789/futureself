# Gravitation
minutes: 40
yt: class 9 science gravitation
@study
Every object in the universe attracts every other object with a force called gravitational force. Newton's universal law of gravitation states that this force is directly proportional to the product of the masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them: F = G M m / r². G is the universal gravitational constant, 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N m²/kg². Near the Earth the acceleration due to gravity is g = GM/R² = 9.8 m/s². Weight W = mg. Archimedes' principle gives the buoyant force on a body immersed in a fluid, and explains floatation. Pressure is force per unit area.
## Deep dive: universal law
Newton realised that the force that pulls an apple to the ground also holds the Moon in orbit. The force between two masses M and m separated by r is F = GMm/r². The force is attractive, acts along the line joining the masses, and obeys the inverse-square law. Doubling the distance reduces the force to one-fourth.
## Deep dive: g and weight
Near Earth, g = GM/R² = 9.8 m/s². g is independent of the falling mass. It varies slightly with latitude and falls with height. Weight W = mg; on the Moon g is about one-sixth, so weight is one-sixth, while mass is the same.
## Deep dive: free fall equations
For dropped bodies u = 0, v = gt, s = (1/2)gt² and v² = 2gs. For an upward throw, time to the top is u/g and the maximum height is u²/2g.
## Deep dive: pressure and buoyancy
Pressure = thrust/area. Fluids exert buoyant force equal to the weight of fluid displaced (Archimedes). A body floats if its density is less than that of the fluid, sinks if more. Relative density = density of substance/density of water.
