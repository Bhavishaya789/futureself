# Tissues: Animal Tissues
minutes: 40
yt: class 9 science tissues: animal tissues
@study
Animal tissues are of four types: epithelial, connective, muscular and nervous. Epithelial tissue covers and lines organs. Connective tissue binds and supports and includes blood, bone, cartilage, ligament, tendon, areolar and adipose tissue. Muscular tissue (striated, smooth and cardiac) brings about movement. Nervous tissue, made of neurons, receives and transmits impulses.
## Deep dive: epithelial tissue
Epithelia protect and separate. Squamous epithelium lines the mouth and oesophagus; stratified squamous forms the skin. Cuboidal epithelium forms kidney tubules and glands; columnar lines the intestine; ciliated columnar lines the trachea and helps move mucus. Glandular epithelium secretes enzymes and hormones.
## Deep dive: connective tissue
Blood has plasma, RBCs, WBCs and platelets. Bone has a hard matrix rich in calcium and phosphorus. Cartilage smooths joints. Ligaments join bone to bone; tendons join muscle to bone. Areolar tissue fills spaces, and adipose tissue stores fat and insulates.
## Deep dive: muscular and nervous tissue
Skeletal muscle is voluntary and striated with multinucleate cells. Smooth muscle is involuntary and spindle-shaped. Cardiac muscle is involuntary, branched and striated. A neuron has a cell body, dendrites and an axon; impulses travel from dendrites to cell body to axon.

Q: How many types of animal tissues are there?
A: Four: epithelial, connective, muscular and nervous.
Q: What is epithelial tissue?
A: Tissue that covers or lines the surfaces of organs and body cavities.
Q: How are epithelial cells arranged?
A: Tightly packed with very little intercellular space.
Q: What is squamous epithelium?
A: Epithelium of flat cells, as in the skin and lining of mouth and oesophagus.
Q: Where is stratified squamous epithelium found?
A: In the skin, to prevent wear and tear.
Q: What is cuboidal epithelium?
A: Epithelium of cube-shaped cells, as in kidney tubules and salivary gland ducts.
Q: What is columnar epithelium?
A: Epithelium of tall pillar-like cells, as in the lining of the intestine.
Q: What is ciliated epithelium?
A: Columnar epithelium with cilia, as in the respiratory tract.
Q: What is glandular epithelium?
A: Epithelium that secretes substances.
Q: What is connective tissue?
A: Tissue with scattered cells in a matrix that binds and supports organs.
Q: Name the connective tissues.
A: Blood, bone, cartilage, ligament, tendon, areolar tissue and adipose tissue.
Q: What is the matrix of blood called?
A: Plasma.
Q: What are the components of blood?
A: Plasma, red blood cells, white blood cells and platelets.
Q: What does blood transport?
A: Gases, digested food, hormones and wastes.
Q: What is the matrix of bone made of?
A: Calcium and phosphorus compounds.
Q: What is the function of bone?
A: Supports the body, protects organs and anchors muscles.
Q: What is cartilage?
A: Connective tissue with widely spaced cells and a solid matrix.
Q: Where is cartilage found?
A: In the nose, ear, trachea, larynx and at joints.
Q: What is a ligament?
A: Strong flexible tissue connecting bone to bone.
Q: What is a tendon?
A: Fibrous tissue connecting muscle to bone.
Q: What is areolar tissue?
A: Loose connective tissue that fills spaces, supports organs and helps repair tissues.
Q: What is adipose tissue?
A: Fat-storing tissue below the skin and around organs.
Q: What are the functions of adipose tissue?
A: Stores fat and acts as an insulator.
Q: What is muscular tissue?
A: Tissue made of elongated cells (muscle fibres) that cause movement.
Q: What are the three types of muscle?
A: Skeletal (striated), smooth (unstriated) and cardiac.
Q: What is a voluntary muscle?
A: A muscle that moves under our control, such as skeletal muscle.
Q: What are involuntary muscles?
A: Muscles that work without our control, such as smooth and cardiac muscles.
Q: Describe skeletal muscle cells.
A: Long, cylindrical, unbranched and multinucleate with stripes.
Q: Describe smooth muscle cells.
A: Spindle-shaped, unbranched, uninucleate without stripes.
Q: Describe cardiac muscle cells.
A: Cylindrical, branched, uninucleate, striated and involuntary.
Q: Where are smooth muscles found?
A: In the iris, urinary bladder, blood vessels, alimentary canal and ureters.
Q: What is a neuron?
A: The structural and functional unit of nervous tissue.
Q: What are the parts of a neuron?
A: Cell body, dendrites and axon.
Q: What does the axon do?
A: Carries the nerve impulse away from the cell body.
Q: What do dendrites do?
A: Receive impulses and carry them to the cell body.
Q: What is a nerve impulse?
A: A signal carried along neurons.
Q: Which is the longest cell in the human body?
A: The neuron.
Q: What is an organ?
A: A group of tissues working together for a specific function.
Q: What is an organ system?
A: A group of organs working together.
