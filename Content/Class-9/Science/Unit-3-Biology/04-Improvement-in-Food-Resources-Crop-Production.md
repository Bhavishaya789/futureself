# Improvement in Food Resources: Crop Production
minutes: 40
yt: class 9 science improvement in food resources: crop production
@study
Improvement in crop yield involves crop variety improvement, crop production management and crop protection. Crops are grown in kharif (June to October) and rabi (November to April) seasons. Plants need 16 nutrients, of which 13 come from soil. Manure and fertilisers supply nutrients. Irrigation, cropping patterns (mixed cropping, inter-cropping, crop rotation), weed and pest control and safe storage help increase production.
## Deep dive: crop variety and nutrients
Variety improvement uses hybridisation (intervarietal, interspecific, intergeneric) and genetic modification to get higher yield, better quality, resistance and adaptability. Plants need 16 nutrients: carbon and oxygen from air, hydrogen from water, and 13 from soil; six are macronutrients (N, P, K, Ca, Mg, S) and seven are micronutrients (Fe, Mn, B, Zn, Cu, Mo, Cl).
## Deep dive: manure and fertilisers
Manure, compost, vermicompost and green manure supply organic matter and nutrients and improve soil structure. Fertilisers give nutrients quickly but harm soil and water if overused. Organic farming reduces chemical use.
## Deep dive: cropping systems and irrigation
Mixed cropping lowers risk; inter-cropping reduces pest spread; crop rotation maintains fertility. Irrigation comes from wells, canals, rivers and tanks.
## Deep dive: protection and storage
Weeds like Xanthium and Parthenium compete with crops. Pests attack by cutting, sucking and boring. Pesticides and herbicides must be used with care. Grain storage losses come from insects, rodents, fungi, moisture and temperature; cleaning, drying and fumigation protect stored grain.

Q: Why is crop improvement required?
A: To feed a growing population from limited land.
Q: What is the Green Revolution?
A: The large increase in food-grain production, especially wheat and rice, through high-yielding varieties and better inputs.
Q: What are the main activities in improvement of crop yield?
A: Crop variety improvement, crop production improvement and crop protection management.
Q: What are kharif crops?
A: Crops grown in the rainy season from June to October.
Q: Name some kharif crops.
A: Paddy, soybean, pigeon pea, maize, cotton, green gram and black gram.
Q: What are rabi crops?
A: Crops grown in winter from November to April.
Q: Name some rabi crops.
A: Wheat, gram, peas, mustard and linseed.
Q: What is hybridisation?
A: Crossing between genetically dissimilar plants.
Q: Name three types of hybridisation.
A: Intervarietal, interspecific and intergeneric.
Q: What is a genetically modified crop?
A: A crop in which a gene from another organism has been introduced.
Q: What are the desired traits in variety improvement?
A: Higher yield, improved quality, biotic and abiotic resistance, change in maturity duration, wider adaptability and desirable agronomic traits.
Q: What is meant by agronomic traits?
A: Traits such as tallness in fodder crops or dwarfness in cereals.
Q: How many nutrients do plants need?
A: Sixteen.
Q: From where do plants get carbon and oxygen?
A: From air.
Q: From where do plants get hydrogen?
A: From water.
Q: How many nutrients come from the soil?
A: Thirteen.
Q: What are macronutrients?
A: Nutrients needed in large quantities: nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium, calcium, magnesium and sulphur.
Q: Name the micronutrients.
A: Iron, manganese, boron, zinc, copper, molybdenum and chlorine.
Q: What is manure?
A: Organic material from decomposed animal excreta and plant waste that adds nutrients and humus.
Q: What is compost?
A: Manure made by decomposing farm waste in pits.
Q: What is vermi-compost?
A: Compost prepared using earthworms.
Q: What is green manure?
A: Plants like sunn hemp or guar ploughed back into soil before sowing.
Q: What are fertilisers?
A: Commercially produced inorganic plant nutrients.
Q: Why should fertiliser use be careful?
A: Excess causes soil and water pollution and loss of fertility.
Q: What is organic farming?
A: Farming with minimal or no use of chemicals and with organic manure and biological control.
Q: What is irrigation?
A: Artificial supply of water to crops.
Q: Name some sources of irrigation.
A: Wells, tube wells, canals, rivers and tanks.
Q: What is mixed cropping?
A: Growing two or more crops simultaneously on the same land.
Q: What is inter-cropping?
A: Growing two or more crops in definite row patterns.
Q: What is crop rotation?
A: Growing different crops on the same land in a planned succession.
Q: What are weeds?
A: Unwanted plants growing in the crop field.
Q: Name some weeds.
A: Xanthium, Parthenium (gajar ghas) and Cyperus rotundus (motha).
Q: What are pests?
A: Organisms that damage crops.
Q: Name some ways in which pests attack plants.
A: Cutting roots, stems and leaves, sucking cell sap and boring into stems and fruits.
Q: Which are biotic factors in grain storage loss?
A: Insects, rodents, fungi, mites and bacteria.
Q: Which are abiotic factors in grain storage loss?
A: Inappropriate moisture and temperature.
Q: How can grains be protected in storage?
A: By cleaning, drying, fumigation and proper treatment with chemicals.
Q: What is fumigation?
A: Treatment of grain with gaseous chemicals to kill pests.
Q: What is the role of preventive methods?
A: To avoid pest attack through proper seed bed, resistant varieties and crop rotation.
