# Tissues: Plant Tissues
minutes: 40
yt: class 9 science tissues: plant tissues
@study
A tissue is a group of cells with similar structure and function. Plant tissues are of two main types. Meristematic tissue (apical, lateral and intercalary) has dividing cells and causes growth. Permanent tissue is formed when cells stop dividing and differentiate; it may be simple (parenchyma, collenchyma, sclerenchyma, epidermis, cork) or complex (xylem and phloem). Xylem carries water and minerals; phloem carries food.
## Deep dive: meristematic tissue
Meristematic cells divide continuously. Apical meristem lengthens roots and shoots, lateral meristem (cambium) thickens the stem and intercalary meristem at the base of leaves and internodes helps regrowth of grasses. Cells are small, thin-walled, with dense cytoplasm and prominent nuclei.
## Deep dive: simple permanent tissues
Parenchyma stores food, and chlorenchyma and aerenchyma are its special forms. Collenchyma gives flexibility with pectin thickening. Sclerenchyma is dead with lignin and gives hardness, for example the coconut husk. Epidermis protects; stomata with guard cells allow gas exchange; the cuticle reduces water loss. Cork replaces epidermis in older stems.
## Deep dive: complex tissues
Xylem consists of tracheids, vessels, parenchyma and fibres; it carries water and minerals upward. Phloem consists of sieve tubes, companion cells, parenchyma and fibres and carries food (translocation). Xylem is mainly dead, phloem mainly living.

Q: What is a tissue?
A: A group of cells with similar structure that perform a specific function together.
Q: How many main types of plant tissues are there?
A: Two: meristematic and permanent.
Q: Where is apical meristem found?
A: At the tips of roots and shoots.
Q: What does apical meristem do?
A: Increases the length of the plant.
Q: Where is lateral meristem found?
A: On the sides of stems and roots, such as cambium.
Q: What does lateral meristem do?
A: Increases the girth (thickness) of the stem or root.
Q: Where is intercalary meristem found?
A: At the base of leaves or internodes, as in grasses.
Q: What is differentiation?
A: Taking up of a permanent shape, size and function by cells.
Q: What kind of cells do meristematic tissues have?
A: Thin-walled cells with dense cytoplasm and prominent nuclei, with little or no vacuole.
Q: What are the two types of permanent tissues?
A: Simple and complex permanent tissues.
Q: What is parenchyma?
A: Simple tissue of loosely packed, thin-walled living cells.
Q: What is the function of parenchyma?
A: Storage of food and support.
Q: What is chlorenchyma?
A: Parenchyma with chloroplasts that carries out photosynthesis.
Q: What is aerenchyma?
A: Parenchyma with large air cavities that helps aquatic plants float.
Q: What is collenchyma?
A: Living tissue with cells thickened at the corners with pectin.
Q: What is the function of collenchyma?
A: Provides flexibility and mechanical support, as in leaf stalks.
Q: What is sclerenchyma?
A: Dead tissue with thick, lignified walls.
Q: What is the function of sclerenchyma?
A: Makes plant hard and stiff, as in husk of coconut.
Q: What substance makes sclerenchyma walls hard?
A: Lignin.
Q: What is epidermis?
A: The outermost protective layer of cells in plants.
Q: What is the function of stomata?
A: Exchange of gases and transpiration.
Q: What are guard cells?
A: Bean-shaped cells that open and close the stomata.
Q: What is cuticle?
A: A waxy water-resistant layer on the epidermis.
Q: What is cork?
A: Dead protective tissue of the bark with suberin in walls.
Q: Why is cork impervious to gases and water?
A: Suberin in the cell walls makes it so.
Q: What are complex tissues?
A: Tissues made of more than one type of cells working together.
Q: Name the two complex tissues.
A: Xylem and phloem.
Q: What are the components of xylem?
A: Tracheids, vessels, xylem parenchyma and xylem fibres.
Q: Which xylem components are dead?
A: Tracheids, vessels and fibres.
Q: What are the components of phloem?
A: Sieve tubes, companion cells, phloem parenchyma and phloem fibres.
Q: What is the function of companion cells?
A: They help sieve tubes in conduction of food.
Q: What is translocation?
A: Transport of food from leaves to other parts through phloem.
Q: Which phloem component is dead?
A: Phloem fibres.
Q: What is the role of root hairs?
A: To absorb water and minerals from the soil.
Q: What is the epidermis like in desert plants?
A: It has a thick waxy coating to reduce water loss.
Q: Which tissue is responsible for secondary growth?
A: Lateral meristem (cambium).

P: What is the function of xylem and phloem?
A: Xylem conducts water and minerals from roots to leaves; phloem conducts food from leaves to other parts.
