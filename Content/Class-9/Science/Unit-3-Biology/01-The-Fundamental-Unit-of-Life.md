# The Fundamental Unit of Life (Cell)
minutes: 40
yt: class 9 science the fundamental unit of life (cell)
@study
The cell is the structural and functional unit of life. Robert Hooke first saw cells in cork in 1665. Cells may be prokaryotic (no membrane-bound nucleus or organelles) or eukaryotic (true nucleus and organelles). A cell has a plasma membrane, cytoplasm and organelles such as the nucleus, endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi apparatus, lysosomes, mitochondria, plastids and vacuoles. Plant cells also have a cell wall. The plasma membrane is selectively permeable and controls diffusion and osmosis.
## Deep dive: discovery and cell theory
Robert Hooke (1665) examined thin slices of cork under a self-designed microscope and named the box-like compartments cells. Leeuwenhoek (1674) saw free-living cells in pond water. Robert Brown (1831) discovered the nucleus, and Purkinje (1839) named the cell substance protoplasm. Schleiden and Schwann presented the cell theory in 1838-39 and Virchow (1855) added that new cells come from pre-existing cells.
## Deep dive: plasma membrane and transport
The plasma membrane, made of lipids and proteins, is selectively permeable. Small molecules such as oxygen and carbon dioxide move by diffusion. Water moves by osmosis. In a hypotonic medium the cell gains water, in a hypertonic medium it loses water, and in an isotonic medium there is no net movement. Plant cells survive hypotonic media because of the cell wall. Amoeba takes in food by endocytosis.
## Deep dive: organelles
The nucleus has a double membrane with pores, chromatin, and a nucleolus. Rough ER carries ribosomes and makes proteins, while smooth ER makes lipids and detoxifies. The Golgi apparatus modifies, packs and dispatches materials. Lysosomes digest waste and are called suicide bags. Mitochondria produce ATP and have their own DNA. Plastids include chloroplasts with chlorophyll, chromoplasts with pigments and leucoplasts for storage. Plant cells have a large central vacuole.
## Deep dive: prokaryotes versus eukaryotes
Prokaryotes (bacteria, blue-green algae) have a nucleoid, no membrane-bound organelles and small ribosomes. Eukaryotes (plants, animals, fungi, protists) have a true nucleus and membrane-bound organelles.

Q: Who discovered cells and when?
A: Robert Hooke in 1665, in a thin slice of cork.
Q: Who first saw free-living cells?
A: Anton van Leeuwenhoek in 1674.
Q: Who discovered the nucleus?
A: Robert Brown in 1831.
Q: Who coined the term protoplasm?
A: Purkinje in 1839.
Q: Who proposed the cell theory?
A: Schleiden and Schwann (1838-39).
Q: Who added that all cells arise from pre-existing cells?
A: Rudolf Virchow in 1855.
Q: What does the cell theory state?
A: All living things are made of cells, and the cell is the basic unit of life.
Q: Which is the largest cell?
A: The egg of an ostrich.
Q: Which is the smallest cell?
A: Mycoplasma, about 0.1 micrometre.
Q: Which is the longest cell in the human body?
A: The nerve cell (neuron).
Q: What is a prokaryotic cell?
A: A cell without a well-defined nucleus and without membrane-bound organelles.
Q: What is a eukaryotic cell?
A: A cell with a true nucleus enclosed by a nuclear membrane and with membrane-bound organelles.
Q: What is the nucleoid?
A: The region of a prokaryotic cell that contains DNA, not enclosed by a membrane.
Q: Name two examples of prokaryotes.
A: Bacteria and blue-green algae.
Q: What is the plasma membrane made of?
A: Lipids and proteins.
Q: What is meant by selectively permeable membrane?
A: A membrane that allows only some substances to pass through.
Q: What is diffusion?
A: Movement of a substance from a region of higher concentration to lower concentration.
Q: What is osmosis?
A: Movement of water across a selectively permeable membrane from higher water concentration to lower water concentration.
Q: What happens to a cell in a hypotonic solution?
A: Water enters and the cell swells.
Q: What happens to a cell in a hypertonic solution?
A: Water leaves and the cell shrinks.
Q: What happens to a cell in an isotonic solution?
A: There is no net movement of water, so the cell stays the same size.
Q: What is plasmolysis?
A: Shrinking of the cell contents away from the cell wall in a hypertonic solution.
Q: What is the cell wall made of in plants?
A: Cellulose.
Q: Why does a plant cell not burst in a hypotonic medium?
A: The rigid cell wall resists swelling.
Q: What is endocytosis?
A: Engulfing of food and materials from outside by the plasma membrane, as in Amoeba.
Q: What does the nucleus contain?
A: Chromosomes made of DNA and protein.
Q: What is chromatin?
A: Thread-like material in the nucleus that condenses into chromosomes during division.
Q: What is the function of the nucleolus?
A: It makes ribosomes.
Q: What is the function of the nucleus?
A: It controls cell activities and carries hereditary information.
Q: What is cytoplasm?
A: The fluid content inside the plasma membrane that holds the organelles.
Q: What is the full form of ER?
A: Endoplasmic reticulum.
Q: What does rough ER do?
A: It carries ribosomes and makes proteins.
Q: What does smooth ER do?
A: It makes lipids and helps detoxify poisons and drugs.
Q: Who discovered the Golgi apparatus?
A: Camillo Golgi in 1898.
Q: What is the function of the Golgi apparatus?
A: Storage, modification and packaging of products and formation of lysosomes.
Q: Why are lysosomes called suicide bags?
A: They can digest the cell itself when it is damaged or dead.
Q: Why are mitochondria called the powerhouse of the cell?
A: They release energy as ATP.
Q: What is ATP?
A: Adenosine triphosphate, the energy currency of the cell.
Q: Which organelle has its own DNA and ribosomes?
A: Mitochondria.
Q: Which cell organelles are double-membraned?
A: Mitochondria and plastids.
Q: Name the three types of plastids.
A: Chloroplasts, chromoplasts and leucoplasts.
Q: Which pigment gives chloroplasts their green colour?
A: Chlorophyll.
Q: What is the function of leucoplasts?
A: Storage of starch, oils and proteins.
Q: What is the function of vacuoles?
A: Storage of water, food, wastes and maintaining turgidity.
Q: Which cells have large central vacuoles?
A: Plant cells.
Q: What are ribosomes?
A: Tiny structures where proteins are made.
Q: What is biogenesis of membrane?
A: Making of membrane lipids and proteins, done by the ER.
Q: How many cells does an organism like Amoeba have?
A: One.
Q: What is a unicellular organism?
A: An organism made of a single cell.
Q: What is a multicellular organism?
A: An organism made of many cells.
Q: Name two differences between plant and animal cells.
A: Plant cells have a cell wall and plastids; animal cells do not.
