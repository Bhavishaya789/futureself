# Communication skills
minutes: 30
yt: class 9 communication skills
@study
Communication is the exchange of information, ideas or feelings between a sender and a receiver. The process has a sender, message, encoding, channel, receiver, decoding and feedback; noise can block it. It can be verbal (spoken or written) or non-verbal (body language, gestures, eye contact). The 7 Cs are clear, concise, correct, complete, courteous, concrete and considerate. Good listening, clear writing and polite behaviour make communication effective.
## Deep dive: the communication process
Communication needs a sender who has an idea, a message put into words or signs (encoding), a channel (speech, phone, e-mail, letter), a receiver who decodes the message and feedback that tells the sender it was understood. Noise (a loud room, poor network, unclear words) can distort the message at any step. Two-way communication has feedback; one-way communication (a TV broadcast) does not.
## Deep dive: verbal and non-verbal communication
Verbal communication uses words: oral (face to face, phone call, meeting) or written (letter, e-mail, report, text message). Non-verbal communication uses body language: posture, facial expression, eye contact, gestures, tone of voice and personal space. Formal communication follows rules and a chain of command (office memo); informal communication is casual (chat with friends). Communication also flows upward, downward and sideways in an organisation.
## Deep dive: the 7 Cs and barriers
Clear: one idea in simple words. Concise: short without losing meaning. Correct: right facts and grammar. Complete: all needed information. Courteous: polite and respectful. Concrete: specific facts and figures. Considerate: keeps the receiver in mind. Barriers are physical (noise, distance), language (jargon, accents), emotional (anger, fear), cultural (different customs), and attitude (not listening). They are overcome by active listening, simple language, checking understanding and respecting differences.
## Deep dive: listening, speaking and writing
Active listening means giving full attention, not interrupting, nodding, asking questions and summarising what was said. Good speaking uses clear voice, correct pace, and eye contact. Good writing has a greeting, a clear body in short paragraphs and a polite closing; an e-mail also needs a meaningful subject line. A telephone call should begin with a greeting and name, speak clearly and end politely.
