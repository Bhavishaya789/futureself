**Indian Knowledge System (IKS)** is the body of knowledge developed in the Indian subcontinent over thousands of years, covering philosophy, mathematics, astronomy, medicine, language, arts, architecture, metallurgy, agriculture, water management and ethics, passed down through texts, teachers (guru–shishya parampara) and practice.

## Why study it?
- To understand the roots of science and culture.
- To connect traditional ideas with modern disciplines (for example Panini's grammar and computer science).
- To value sustainability and community knowledge.
- The National Education Policy (NEP) 2020 encourages including IKS in higher education; AICTE has an IKS Division and the Ministry of Education supports IKS research.
Study critically: separate documented evidence from legend, and acknowledge sources.

## Sources of knowledge (pramanas)
Indian philosophy recognises means of valid knowledge:
- **Pratyaksha:** direct perception.
- **Anumana:** inference.
- **Shabda:** reliable testimony (texts, experts).
- Others used by some schools: upamana (analogy), arthapatti, anupalabdhi.
This resembles observation, logical inference and evidence in modern science.

## The literature
- **Vedas:** Rigveda, Yajurveda, Samaveda, Atharvaveda; each has Samhita, Brahmana, Aranyaka and Upanishad parts.
- **Vedangas** (auxiliary sciences): Shiksha (phonetics), Chandas (metre), Vyakarana (grammar), Nirukta (etymology), Kalpa (rituals), Jyotisha (astronomy).
- **Upanishads, Itihasa (Ramayana, Mahabharata), Puranas, Smritis, Arthashastra.**
- Sutra style: very short, memorable aphorisms, supported by commentaries (bhashya).
- Preservation: oral transmission with strict recitation methods, then manuscripts on palm leaf and birch bark; Sanskrit, Pali, Prakrit, Tamil and other languages.

## Knowledge traditions
Chatur-Vidya, 14 vidyas and 64 kalas (arts) in classical lists; Ayurveda, Yoga, Jyotisha, Ganita (mathematics), Vastu, Natya, Sangita, Krishi.

## Features of the Indian approach
Holistic (body–mind–nature), emphasis on dialogue and debate (shastrartha), practical application, and respect for diversity of schools of thought.

## Time periods (rough)
Indus (Harappan) civilisation (about 3300–1300 BCE), Vedic period, Mauryan and Gupta ages (classical science), medieval Kerala school, Mughal–Rajput era observatories, and modern scientists such as Ramanujan, C. V. Raman, J. C. Bose.

> IKS is a living heritage: learn it with curiosity and evidence.

Q: Name the four Vedas.
A: Rigveda, Yajurveda, Samaveda and Atharvaveda.
Q: What are the Vedangas?
A: Six auxiliary disciplines: Shiksha, Chandas, Vyakarana, Nirukta, Kalpa and Jyotisha.

A: The accepted means of valid knowledge, mainly perception (pratyaksha), inference (anumana) and testimony (shabda).
Q: Why is IKS included in modern curricula?
A: To connect students with India's intellectual heritage, encourage interdisciplinary and sustainable thinking, and follow NEP 2020's vision.

## Moderate
P: What is meant by Indian Knowledge Systems (IKS)? Name five areas in which India has a traditional knowledge heritage.
A: IKS is the body of knowledge, practices and ways of learning developed in the Indian subcontinent over thousands of years and passed on through texts, teachers and traditions. Areas: mathematics and astronomy, medicine (Ayurveda) and yoga, language and grammar (Panini), philosophy and logic, metallurgy, architecture, water management, and arts such as music and drama.

## High
P: Name three ancient Indian texts or scholars and the field of each. Why is it important for engineering students to study IKS?
A: Examples: Aryabhatiya (mathematics and astronomy), Sushruta Samhita (surgery), Ashtadhyayi by Panini (grammar), Arthashastra (administration and economics), Natyashastra (performing arts), Charaka Samhita (internal medicine).
IKS builds cultural roots, shows sustainable and low-resource solutions, inspires innovation (for example water harvesting, medicine, algorithms) and gives a broader view of knowledge beyond a single tradition.

## Advanced
P: Explain how knowledge was preserved and passed on in the Gurukul tradition. How does it compare with modern education, and what does NEP 2020 say about IKS?
A: Knowledge was passed on orally and through manuscripts: students lived with the teacher, learned by listening, memorising, repeating and practising (shruti and smriti), with personal mentoring. Modern education is institutional, written, examination-based and scalable, but it can lose personal mentoring and practice. NEP 2020 encourages integrating Indian knowledge, languages and traditions into curricula in a scientific, relevant way.
