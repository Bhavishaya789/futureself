## Ayurveda
"Science of life" (ayus = life, veda = knowledge). Classical texts:
- **Charaka Samhita:** internal medicine, diagnosis, diet, ethics of the physician.
- **Sushruta Samhita:** surgery (shalya tantra), instruments, anatomy, training by practice on models; the traditions of reconstructive (rhinoplasty) surgery are described.
- **Ashtanga Hridaya (Vagbhata).**
These texts were compiled over centuries (dates are debated; roughly from the first millennium BCE to early centuries CE).

## Core concepts
- **Tridosha:** vata, pitta, kapha; health is balance and disease is imbalance.
- **Dhatus** (tissues), **agni** (digestion/metabolism), **prakriti** (constitution).
- Emphasis on **diet, daily routine (dinacharya), seasonal routine, sleep, exercise and mental health**.
- Treatment: herbs and formulations, panchakarma cleansing, diet, yoga.

## Plants and medicines
Examples: turmeric (curcumin: studied for anti-inflammatory effects), neem, tulsi, ashwagandha, amla, giloy. Many modern drugs derive from plants (for example aspirin from salicin in willow, reserpine from Rauvolfia serpentina used in Indian practice). Evidence levels differ: some remedies have trials, many need more research, and some preparations have contained harmful metals, so quality control matters.

## Other Indian systems (AYUSH)
**A**yurveda, **Y**oga and naturopathy, **U**nani, **S**iddha, **H**omoeopathy. The Ministry of AYUSH regulates education, research and standards. Patients should not replace modern emergency or serious-disease treatment with unproven remedies; integrative approaches work with qualified doctors.

## Protecting traditional knowledge
**Biopiracy** is patenting traditional knowledge without credit.
- **Turmeric patent (US, 1995):** granted for wound healing; revoked in 1997 after India (CSIR) showed ancient texts.
- **Neem patent (European Patent Office):** revoked in 2005 after challenge by India and others.
- **Traditional Knowledge Digital Library (TKDL):** India's database of traditional formulations in several languages, used by patent examiners worldwide.
- **GI (Geographical Indication) tags:** Darjeeling tea was India's first GI-tagged product (registered in the early 2000s); others include Basmati, Kanchipuram silk and Mysore silk.

## Medicine and technology
IoT wearables measure pulse and sleep; digital Ayurveda records; AI-based herb identification; telemedicine (e-Sanjeevani).

## Public health traditions
Yoga and pranayama for stress, community nutrition (millets), and hygiene customs.

> Respect the tradition, test it with evidence, protect it from misuse.

## Moderate
P: Name the three doshas of Ayurveda and the main quality of each.
A: Vata (air and space; movement, dryness, lightness), Pitta (fire and water; heat, digestion, metabolism) and Kapha (earth and water; structure, stability, lubrication). Ayurveda aims at balance among them.

## High
P: List the eight limbs of Ashtanga yoga in order. Who compiled the Yoga Sutras and why was 21 June chosen as the International Day of Yoga?
A: Yama, Niyama, Asana, Pranayama, Pratyahara, Dharana, Dhyana, Samadhi. The Yoga Sutras were compiled by Patanjali. The International Day of Yoga is on 21 June, the summer solstice, the longest day in the northern hemisphere; it was adopted by the UN General Assembly in 2014 and first observed in 2015.

## Advanced
P: Compare the Charaka Samhita and the Sushruta Samhita. What is TKDL and why was it created (turmeric patent example)?
A: Charaka Samhita is mainly about internal medicine, diagnosis, diet and physician ethics; Sushruta Samhita is mainly about surgery, instruments, anatomy and training.
TKDL (Traditional Knowledge Digital Library) is an Indian database of traditional medicine knowledge in languages and formats patent examiners understand. It was created to prevent wrong patents on traditional knowledge, as in the turmeric wound-healing patent granted in the US in the 1990s and revoked after India proved prior knowledge.
