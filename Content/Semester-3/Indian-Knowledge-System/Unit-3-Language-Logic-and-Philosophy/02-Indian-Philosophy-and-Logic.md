## Darshanas (viewpoints)
**Astika** (accept the authority of the Vedas): Nyaya, Vaisheshika, Samkhya, Yoga, Purva Mimamsa, Uttara Mimamsa (Vedanta).
**Nastika** (do not): Buddhism, Jainism, Charvaka (Lokayata, materialist).

| School | Core idea |
| --- | --- |
| Nyaya | Logic and epistemology; four pramanas |
| Vaisheshika | Atomism (Kanada): matter made of paramanu; categories of reality |
| Samkhya | Dualism of purusha (consciousness) and prakriti (nature) |
| Yoga | Discipline of body and mind (Patanjali's Yoga Sutras, eight limbs) |
| Mimamsa | Ritual duty and interpretation of Vedic texts |
| Vedanta | Nature of Brahman and self (Advaita by Shankara, Vishishtadvaita, Dvaita) |
| Buddhism | Four noble truths, eight-fold path, impermanence |
| Jainism | Ahimsa, **anekantavada** (many-sidedness), syadvada |
| Charvaka | Perception as the only valid knowledge |

## Nyaya logic
A syllogism with five members (**pancha-avayava**): pratijna (claim), hetu (reason), udaharana (example), upanaya (application), nigamana (conclusion). Example: "There is fire on the hill (claim), because there is smoke (reason); wherever smoke is, there is fire, as in a kitchen (example); this hill has smoke (application); therefore it has fire (conclusion)." Compare with modus ponens and the Western syllogism, which uses three parts. Navya-Nyaya (14th century onwards) developed a precise formal language for relations and qualifiers.
**Jain syadvada** lists seven predications for a statement ("in some respects it is…"), related to many-valued logic. **Buddhist logic** (Dignaga, Dharmakirti) refined theories of inference.

## Atomism
Kanada's Vaisheshika Sutra describes indivisible atoms (paramanu) that combine to make matter, with qualities such as weight, motion and contact.

## Yoga
Patanjali's eight limbs (ashtanga): yama, niyama, asana, pranayama, pratyahara, dharana, dhyana, samadhi. International Yoga Day is observed on 21 June (declared by the UN in 2014). Modern studies show benefits for stress and flexibility, but yoga is not a substitute for medical treatment.

## Ethics and values
Ahimsa (non-violence), satya (truth), dharma (duty), karma (action and consequence), seva (service), vasudhaiva kutumbakam (the world is one family), aparigraha (non-possessiveness). Gandhi applied ahimsa and satyagraha to politics.

## Education and debate
Shastrartha (formal debate) trained reasoning. **Takshashila**, **Nalanda** (5th–12th century CE) and **Vikramashila** were great centres of learning attracting students from across Asia.

## Relevance today
Logic (AI reasoning), ethics (responsible technology), mindfulness (well-being), plural thinking (anekantavada supports tolerance in teams).

> Indian philosophy valued reasoned debate; logic was a tool to test claims.

## High
P: Name the four pramanas (means of valid knowledge) in Nyaya with an example of each.
A: Pratyaksha (perception): seeing a tree. Anumana (inference): seeing smoke and inferring fire. Upamana (comparison): recognising a wild ox because it resembles a cow. Shabda (testimony): trusting a reliable expert or text.

## Advanced
P: Write the five steps of a Nyaya syllogism for "the hill has fire". How does it compare with Western logic, and what is the Jain idea of syadvada?
A: 1) Pratijna (proposition): the hill has fire. 2) Hetu (reason): because it has smoke. 3) Udaharana (example): wherever there is smoke there is fire, as in a kitchen. 4) Upanaya (application): this hill has smoke. 5) Nigamana (conclusion): therefore the hill has fire. It includes a concrete example, unlike the three-step Aristotelian syllogism.
Syadvada (saptabhangi): a statement can be true from one standpoint, so judgements are qualified with "in some sense" (seven-fold predication), supporting non-absolutism.
