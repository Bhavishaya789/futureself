## Natyashastra
Attributed to **Bharata Muni** (traditionally dated between about 200 BCE and 200 CE), it is a treatise on performing arts covering drama, dance, music, stage design and aesthetics.
**Rasa theory:** the aesthetic experience of an audience. Nine rasas (navarasa): shringara (love), hasya (laughter), karuna (compassion), raudra (anger), veera (heroism), bhayanaka (fear), bibhatsa (disgust), adbhuta (wonder), shanta (peace). Bhava (emotion) shown by the performer becomes rasa in the audience.

## Classical dance forms (recognised by the Sangeet Natak Akademi)
Bharatanatyam, Kathak, Kathakali, Kuchipudi, Odissi, Manipuri, Mohiniyattam, Sattriya.

## Music
Two main systems: **Hindustani** (north) and **Carnatic** (south). Core ideas: **swara** (notes: sa re ga ma pa dha ni), **raga** (melodic framework with rules, mood and time of day), **tala** (rhythmic cycle, e.g. teentaal has 16 beats), improvisation. The seven notes relate to a scale; microtones are called **shruti**.
Instruments: sitar, sarod, tabla, veena, flute (bansuri), mridangam, violin. Mathematics in music: tala cycles (16 beats = 4 + 4 + 4 + 4) and permutations of rhythm.

## Folk and visual arts
Madhubani, Warli, Pattachitra, Kalamkari, Gond; textiles (Banarasi, Pochampally ikat), crafts (Channapatna toys), and regional theatre (Nautanki, Yakshagana, Therukoothu).

## Gurukul and apprenticeship
Learning by listening, imitation, practice and personal guidance: a model modern internships and mentoring echo.

## IKS and the modern world
- **Technology:** the data of Ayurveda or astronomy tables in digital libraries; AI for manuscripts (OCR for palm leaf), Bhashini.
- **Management:** *Arthashastra* on administration and strategy; Bhagavad Gita on duty, balance and decision-making under pressure (karma yoga: act without anxiety about results).
- **Sustainability:** traditional crop and water practices.
- **Wellness:** yoga, meditation, dietary diversity.
- **Entrepreneurship:** GI-tagged crafts, handloom, AYUSH start-ups.
- **Heritage protection:** UNESCO sites, manuscripts mission (National Mission for Manuscripts).

## Critical thinking about IKS
Good scholarship: cite sources and dates, avoid exaggerated or unverified claims ("everything was invented in ancient India"), compare with other civilisations fairly, and be open to testing traditional ideas scientifically. IKS and modern science can complement each other.

## Project ideas for students
Document a local craft or oral tradition with interviews; build an app map of stepwells; digitise a family recipe book; analyse a raga with audio software; measure the Sun's shadow to find latitude.

@qa
Q: What is rasa theory?
A: A theory from the Natyashastra explaining how the emotions (bhava) portrayed by performers evoke aesthetic experiences (rasa) such as love, humour or compassion in the audience.
Q: Name the nine rasas.
A: Shringara, hasya, karuna, raudra, veera, bhayanaka, bibhatsa, adbhuta and shanta.
Q: Difference between Hindustani and Carnatic music?
A: Hindustani is the north Indian tradition with more emphasis on elaboration of ragas and often Persian-influenced forms; Carnatic is the south Indian tradition with a strong compositional repertoire (kritis). Both use raga and tala.
Q: What is a tala?
A: A rhythmic cycle of beats that structures a piece of music, such as teentaal of 16 beats.
Q: Why is critical thinking needed when studying IKS?
A: To distinguish documented evidence from legend, avoid exaggeration and ensure that claims are supported by sources and, where possible, tested.

## High
P: Name the seven swaras of Indian music, define raga and tala, and show how a 16-beat tala (teental) is divided.
A: Sa, Re, Ga, Ma, Pa, Dha, Ni. A raga is a melodic framework with rules for notes and phrases that expresses a mood (and traditionally a time of day). A tala is a rhythmic cycle of beats. Teental has 16 beats in 4 groups of 4 (4 + 4 + 4 + 4).
