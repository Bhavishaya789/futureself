Follow your department's official **report format** first. The structure below is a common one.

## Report structure
1. **Title page:** title, name, roll number, programme, semester, mentors, host organisation, session.
2. **Certificates:** host organisation completion certificate; mentor certificate.
3. **Declaration and acknowledgements.**
4. **Abstract** (150–250 words): problem, what you did, results.
5. **Table of contents, list of figures.**
6. **Chapter 1 Introduction:** organisation profile, problem, objectives (SMART).
7. **Chapter 2 Methodology:** needs assessment, plan, activities, tools.
8. **Chapter 3 Work done:** week-wise activities, diary summary.
9. **Chapter 4 Results and impact:** data, charts, feedback, testimonials.
10. **Chapter 5 Learning and reflection:** skills, challenges, ethical issues.
11. **Conclusion and recommendations.**
12. **References and Appendices:** survey forms, photos with consent, attendance sheets.

## Writing tips
- Use clear, simple, past-tense English (or the required language).
- Show numbers: "28 of 30 participants (93%) passed the post-test".
- Include charts and tables with captions.
- Be honest about limits: low attendance, small sample.
- Give credit; do not copy text from other reports (plagiarism).
- Proofread and use consistent formatting.

pre = [4, 5, 3, 6, 5]

Average score gain 3 points; total score rose 65.2% from pre to post test (23 to 38 points).

## Presentation / viva
- 8–10 slides: problem, objective, plan, work done, results, learning, thanks.
- One idea per slide, big fonts, photos and charts, little text.
- Practise timing; prepare for questions such as "What was your role?", "What was the biggest challenge?", "What would you do differently?", "How is it sustainable?"
- Speak honestly about your own contribution if you worked in a team.

## After the internship
Send a thank-you message; share the final report with the host; add the experience to your resume and LinkedIn; keep volunteering if you can.

Q: What should an internship abstract contain?
A: A brief statement of the problem, the work done, the main results and the learning, in about 150 to 250 words.
Q: How do you show impact in a report?
A: With measurable evidence such as pre- and post-test scores, participation numbers, feedback ratings and testimonials, displayed in tables and charts.
Q: Why is honesty about limitations important?
A: It makes the report credible and shows maturity; it also helps others improve future projects.
Q: What questions may come in the viva?
A: Your role, challenges, results, ethical issues, sustainability of the project and what you would do differently.
Q: What is plagiarism and why avoid it?
A: Copying someone else's work without credit; it is unethical and can lead to penalties.
