# Understanding the social internship
minutes: 25

@study
A **social internship** is a structured period in which a student works with a community, NGO, school, government body or social enterprise to understand and help solve a real social problem. Your course lists Social Internship as a credit-bearing activity: the university sets the hours, mentor and format, so follow your official guidelines and notices first. This module is a **practical guide**, not a replacement for them.

## Learning outcomes
- Apply technical skills for social good (for example a digital literacy class, a simple website for an NGO, an IoT water-level sensor for a village tank).
- Develop empathy, communication, teamwork and leadership.
- Understand social problems and policy: education, health, environment, livelihoods, digital inclusion.
- Practise professional skills: planning, documentation, reporting.
- Connect to the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs).

## Typical steps
1. **Get guidelines** from your department: duration, credits, evaluation, report format, mentor.
2. **Choose a theme** and organisation.
3. **Get approval** from your internal mentor and an acceptance letter from the host.
4. **Plan** the work with objectives and a timeline.
5. **Do the work** and keep a daily/weekly diary.
6. **Prepare the report and presentation**; get a completion certificate from the host.
7. **Reflect** on learning.

## Possible themes for engineering students
| Theme | Example activities |
| --- | --- |
| Digital literacy | Teach UPI safety, smartphone basics, DigiLocker, cyber awareness |
| Education | Free coding or maths classes for school students |
| Environment | Waste segregation drive, tree plantation, air-quality sensors |
| Water and agriculture | Simple soil-moisture or water-tank IoT prototype |
| Health | Awareness camps with a hospital NGO, BMI/hygiene sessions |
| Accessibility | Help NGOs for the visually impaired use screen readers |
| Livelihood | Website or social-media page for local artisans |

## Choosing a good project
Is the need real (talk to the community)? Can you finish it in the time? Do you have the skills and permission? Can the NGO continue it after you leave? Does it avoid harm?

## Ethics and safety
Respect dignity and consent; take photos only with permission; protect personal data; do not accept money from beneficiaries; for work with children follow child-safety rules; take a teammate on field visits; keep your family and mentor informed; avoid unsafe locations; follow the host's rules.

## Roles
Student, internal mentor (faculty), external mentor (NGO/community), and beneficiaries. Communicate regularly with your mentor.

> Social work starts with listening, not with solutions.
@qa
Q: What is a social internship?
A: A structured, supervised period of working with a community or organisation to address a social need while learning practical and professional skills.
Q: How can engineering students contribute in a social internship?
A: By using technical skills for digital literacy classes, websites for NGOs, simple IoT or software tools, environmental drives and awareness sessions.
Q: Why should you listen to the community before choosing a project?
A: To make sure the project addresses a real need, builds trust and avoids imposing unwanted solutions.
Q: List ethical rules during a social internship.
A: Respect dignity and consent, protect privacy and data, follow child-safety rules, avoid taking money from beneficiaries, and work safely under supervision.
Q: Who are the mentors in a social internship?
A: An internal mentor from the university and an external mentor from the host organisation.

## Moderate
P: What is a social internship and what are its three main objectives?
A: A social internship is a structured experience in which a student works with a community, NGO or social organisation to understand and help address a real social issue. Objectives: develop empathy and social responsibility, apply classroom knowledge to real problems, and build skills such as communication, teamwork and reporting.
R: 2 | Definition
R: 2 | Three objectives
## High
P: Write a short proposal (5 to 6 lines) for a 4-week social internship on digital literacy for senior citizens: problem, goal, activities and expected outcome.
A: Problem: many senior citizens in the neighbourhood cannot use UPI, video calls or government portals safely. Goal: help 20 senior citizens use these services confidently in 4 weeks. Activities: a survey of needs in week 1, weekly two-hour hands-on sessions in weeks 2 and 3, practice with a helper in week 4. Outcome: at least 15 of the 20 can complete a UPI payment and a video call on their own, shown by a short test.

R: 2 | Activities by week
R: 1 | Measurable outcome
## Advanced
P: Identify four ethical and safety points to follow during a social internship and show how a SMART goal is written for it.
A: Ethics and safety: take informed consent before photos or surveys; protect privacy (do not share personal details); respect local culture and do not impose; work with a supervisor and follow safety rules. SMART goal: Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound, for example "teach 20 seniors to make a UPI payment within 4 weeks, measured by a practical test".
