# Consumer Rights
minutes: 35
yt: class 10 economics consumer rights
@study
Consumers need protection because producers and traders can cheat through adulteration, short weights, high prices and hidden risks. The consumer movement in India grew from hoarding, black marketing and adulteration. The Consumer Protection Act (COPRA) 1986, amended in 2019, set up a three-tier redressal system: District Consumer Commission, State Commission and National Commission. Consumer rights include the right to safety, to be informed, to choose, to be heard, to seek redressal and to consumer education. Agencies such as the Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS) give the ISI mark, Agmark certifies agricultural goods, and Hallmark certifies gold purity. The Right to Information Act 2005 lets citizens seek government information. Consumer International is a global body. Consumers should check labels, MRP, expiry date, ISI or Agmark and always take a cash memo. The consumer movement grows through consumer forums and awareness: 24 December is National Consumer Day.
## Deep dive: why protection is needed
Individual consumers are weak against large producers. Adulteration, short weights, hoarding and false advertising exploit them, so laws and consumer organisations are required.
## Deep dive: rights
Safety, information, choice, representation, redressal and consumer education. These are protected by COPRA 1986 and its 2019 update, which covers e-commerce.
## Deep dive: redressal
District, State and National Commissions hear cases by value. The consumer can seek refund, replacement or compensation. RTI 2005 gives access to government information.
## Deep dive: being a smart consumer
Check MRP, expiry date, quantity, ISI, Agmark and Hallmark; keep the bill; compare prices; complain if cheated; and join consumer forums.
