# Credit, Loans and Self-Help Groups
minutes: 35
yt: class 10 economics credit, loans and self-help groups
@study
Credit (loan) is an agreement where the lender supplies money, goods or services in return for a promise of future payment. Credit helps people earn more when used productively, as with Salim who borrowed to produce shoes, but it can also push borrowers into a debt trap if the crop fails or income falls. Terms of credit include interest rate, collateral, documentation and mode of repayment. Collateral is an asset the borrower owns, such as land or a house, pledged as security. Sources are formal (banks and cooperatives, supervised by the RBI, lower interest) and informal (moneylenders, traders, relatives, employers, with higher rates and no records). Poor households depend more on informal credit. Formal credit should expand. Self-Help Groups (SHGs) of 15 to 20 members, usually women, save regularly and give small loans at reasonable interest; after some time they get bank loans. The Grameen Bank of Bangladesh founded by Muhammad Yunus is an example of group lending without collateral.
## Deep dive: credit and its terms
Credit lets people spend now and pay later. Terms include interest rate, collateral, documentation and mode of repayment. Credit can raise income if used productively (Salim's shoe order) but can create a debt trap when income falls (Swapna the farmer).
## Deep dive: sources
Formal credit from banks and cooperatives is supervised by the RBI and cheaper. Informal credit from moneylenders is costly. The poor depend on informal sources; formal credit must expand.
## Deep dive: SHGs
Self-Help Groups of 15-20 members, mostly women, save regularly, lend small amounts, keep records and build credit history for bank loans without collateral. Grameen Bank is a famous example.

Q: What is credit?
A: An agreement in which the lender provides money or goods in return for future payment.
Q: What is a loan?
A: Money borrowed with a promise to repay.
Q: Who is a lender?
A: The person or institution that gives credit.
Q: Who is a borrower?
A: The person who takes credit.
Q: How can credit be useful?
A: It helps in production and increases earnings.
Q: What is a debt trap?
A: A situation where the borrower cannot repay and falls deeper into debt.
Q: What are the terms of credit?
A: Interest rate, collateral, documentation requirements and mode of repayment.
Q: What is interest rate?
A: The cost of borrowing, a percentage of the loan.
Q: What is collateral?
A: An asset pledged as security for a loan.
Q: Give examples of collateral.
A: Land, building, vehicle and livestock.
Q: Why do lenders ask for collateral?
A: To recover money if the borrower fails to repay.
Q: What is documentation?
A: Papers required to get a loan.
Q: What is mode of repayment?
A: The way and time in which a loan is repaid.
Q: What are formal sources of credit?
A: Banks and cooperatives.
Q: What are informal sources of credit?
A: Moneylenders, traders, employers, relatives and friends.
Q: Who supervises formal lenders?
A: The Reserve Bank of India.
Q: Who supervises informal lenders?
A: No one; there is no supervision.
Q: Which source charges higher interest?
A: Informal sources.
Q: Why does the RBI supervise banks?
A: To ensure they lend to all and keep reserves.
Q: How does the RBI monitor banks?
A: By requiring them to submit information on how much they lend and to whom.
Q: Why should formal credit be expanded?
A: So that poor people avoid high-interest informal loans.
Q: Which sector depends more on informal credit?
A: The poorer households.
Q: What is cheap and affordable credit?
A: Loans at low interest rates.
Q: What is a self-help group?
A: A small group of 15 to 20 members who pool savings and lend.
Q: Who usually forms SHGs?
A: Women from the same neighbourhood.
Q: What do SHG members do?
A: Save regularly and give small loans to members.
Q: What is an advantage of SHGs?
A: Loans without collateral at reasonable interest.
Q: What do SHGs do after some time?
A: Get loans from banks.
Q: Which bank in Bangladesh gives loans to poor women?
A: Grameen Bank.
Q: Who founded Grameen Bank?
A: Muhammad Yunus.
Q: What is micro-credit?
A: Small loans to the poor.
Q: What is a cooperative society?
A: A group of people who pool resources for mutual benefit.
Q: Why do the poor depend on moneylenders?
A: Banks may require collateral and documents.
Q: What does NABARD do?
A: Supports agriculture and rural credit.
Q: What is a Kisan Credit Card?
A: A card giving farmers easy access to credit.
Q: What is the effect of a high interest rate?
A: It lowers earnings and raises the risk of a debt trap.
Q: Why is credit a double-edged sword?
A: It can raise income or push people into debt.
Q: What is the difference between formal and informal credit?
A: Formal is regulated and cheaper; informal is unregulated and costly.
Q: What is the credit rating?
A: A measure of a borrower's ability to repay.
Q: What is an overdraft?
A: Withdrawing more than the balance in an account.
