# Money
minutes: 35
yt: class 10 economics money
@study
Money is a medium of exchange that removes the problem of the double coincidence of wants in barter. Modern forms of money are currency (paper notes and coins) and demand deposits in banks. Currency is issued by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI); in India no one can refuse a payment in rupees, which is called legal tender, and the rupee is accepted because the law guarantees it. Coins and notes: the Government of India mints coins, but RBI issues notes. Demand deposits can be withdrawn on demand by cheque, ATM or net banking; cheques are not money but payment orders. Banks hold only a fraction of deposits as cash and lend the rest to earn interest, as they know that depositors will not all withdraw at once. Money should be safe, accepted and hold value over time.
## Deep dive: from barter to money
Barter needs a double coincidence of wants. Money as a medium of exchange removes this difficulty, making trade easy and allowing specialisation.
## Deep dive: forms of money
Currency (notes issued by the RBI, coins minted by the government) is legal tender. Demand deposits with banks are also accepted as money because payments can be made by cheque, ATM, UPI or net banking.
## Deep dive: banks and deposits
Banks accept deposits, pay interest, keep a fraction as cash reserve and lend the rest at higher interest. The difference is their income.
## Deep dive: features of money
Good money is widely accepted, durable, divisible and stable in value. Inflation reduces its purchasing power.
