# Organised and Unorganised Sectors, Ownership
minutes: 35
yt: class 10 economics organised and unorganised sectors, ownership
@study
In the organised sector, jobs are regular with fixed hours, job security, benefits such as provident fund, pensions, paid leave, and the enterprise is registered and follows rules like the Factories Act, Minimum Wages Act, Payment of Gratuity Act and Shops and Establishments Act. In the unorganised sector, jobs are irregular, wages low, there is no security or benefits, and rules are not followed. About 90 per cent of India's workers are in the unorganised sector; most farmers, casual labourers, and street vendors belong here. Workers need protection in wages, safe work conditions and health care. By ownership, the public sector (government owns assets and provides services: railways, post, BSNL) and private sector (individuals or companies own: Tata Steel, Reliance). The government must provide basic services such as roads, schools, hospitals, electricity and irrigation and support the weak.
## Deep dive: organised vs unorganised
The organised sector has regular, registered, rule-governed jobs with benefits like provident fund, gratuity, pension and paid leave. The unorganised sector has irregular jobs, low wages, no benefits and little regulation; about 90% of workers are there.
## Deep dive: protecting workers
Governments protect workers through the Minimum Wages Act, safety rules and support for small farmers and traders; unions and cooperatives help workers bargain.
## Deep dive: ownership
Public sector enterprises are government-owned and aim at public welfare; private sector firms aim at profit. The government must provide essential services and support farmers with MSP and procurement.
