# Sectors of Economic Activity
minutes: 35
yt: class 10 economics sectors of economic activity
@study
Economic activities are grouped into three sectors: primary (agriculture, dairy, fishing, forestry, mining), secondary (manufacturing from natural products) and tertiary or service sector (transport, trade, banking, IT, education). The tertiary sector supports the other two. Gross Domestic Product (GDP) is the total value of all final goods and services produced within a country in a year; only final goods are counted to avoid double counting. In India, production shifted from the primary to the secondary and then the tertiary sector, so by the 2000s the service sector contributed the largest share of GDP, while the majority of workers remained in agriculture. This leads to underemployment (disguised unemployment) in agriculture. Solutions include irrigation, agro-based industries, cold storage, and creating more non-farm jobs.
## Deep dive: classification of sectors
Primary uses natural resources (farming, fishing, mining), secondary processes these (sugar, cloth, steel), and tertiary supports both (transport, storage, banking). Sectors are interdependent.
## Deep dive: GDP
GDP sums the value of final goods and services produced in a year. Counting only final goods avoids double counting; for example, the value of wheat is included in biscuits, not counted separately.
## Deep dive: structural change
Over time the share of the primary sector in GDP fell and the tertiary sector rose to be largest, but employment stayed mostly in agriculture, causing underemployment.
## Deep dive: creating jobs
Irrigation, credit, agro-processing, cold storage, tourism, IT and the MGNREGA 2005 job guarantee help create employment.

Q: What are the three sectors of the economy?
A: Primary, secondary and tertiary.
Q: What is the primary sector?
A: The sector producing goods by exploiting natural resources, like agriculture.
Q: Why is the primary sector also called the agriculture and related sector?
A: Most of its products come from agriculture, dairy, fishing and forestry.
Q: What is the secondary sector?
A: The sector converting natural products into other forms by manufacturing.
Q: What is another name for the secondary sector?
A: The industrial sector.
Q: What is the tertiary sector?
A: The sector providing services that help the other sectors.
Q: Give examples of tertiary activities.
A: Transport, banking, trade, communication, teaching and IT.
Q: Why are the sectors interdependent?
A: Each depends on goods or services of the others.
Q: What is GDP?
A: The value of all final goods and services produced in a country in a year.
Q: Why are only final goods counted in GDP?
A: To avoid double counting of intermediate goods.
Q: What are intermediate goods?
A: Goods used up in producing final goods.
Q: What are final goods?
A: Goods that reach the consumer.
Q: Which organisation measures GDP in India?
A: The Central Government ministry through the NSO.
Q: Which sector was the largest in GDP in India in the early 20th century?
A: The primary sector.
Q: Which sector is the largest contributor to India's GDP today?
A: The tertiary sector.
Q: What is the historical change in sectors?
A: Primary dominated first, then secondary, now tertiary.
Q: Which sector employs most people in India?
A: The primary sector.
Q: What is disguised unemployment?
A: More workers are engaged than required, so some add nothing to output.
Q: What is another name for disguised unemployment?
A: Underemployment.
Q: Why does agriculture have underemployment?
A: Small land holdings and large families work on the same land.
Q: Give a measure to reduce underemployment.
A: Irrigation and cheap credit for farmers.
Q: What is the role of agro-based industries?
A: They create non-farm jobs in rural areas.
Q: Which service sectors grew rapidly recently?
A: IT, communication, finance and trade.
Q: Why did the tertiary sector grow?
A: Increased demand for services and the rise of IT.
Q: Why are the services sector jobs not equally spread?
A: Only a small number of skilled workers get high-paying jobs.
Q: What are the other jobs in services?
A: Cobblers, barbers and domestic helpers with low pay.
Q: What is the Right to Work scheme?
A: MGNREGA 2005 guaranteeing 100 days of work in rural areas.
Q: What does MGNREGA stand for?
A: Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act.
Q: What are non-economic activities?
A: Activities not done for earning, like household work.
Q: What is double counting?
A: Counting the value of the same product more than once.
Q: What is the primary sector's main problem?
A: Low productivity and underemployment.
Q: What are Cold storage and warehouses examples of?
A: Infrastructure supporting agriculture.
Q: What is value added?
A: Final value minus the value of intermediate goods.
Q: What is a measure of economic activity?
A: GDP.
Q: What is a service?
A: An activity that helps production but does not produce a tangible good.
Q: Why is the tertiary sector important?
A: It supports production and creates jobs and income.
