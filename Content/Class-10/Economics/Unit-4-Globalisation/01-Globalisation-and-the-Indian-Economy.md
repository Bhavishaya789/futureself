# Globalisation and the Indian Economy
minutes: 35
yt: class 10 economics globalisation and the indian economy
@study
Globalisation is the rapid integration among countries through foreign trade and foreign investment. Multinational corporations (MNCs) own or control production in more than one country; they set up offices and factories where costs are low. Foreign trade links markets of different countries, giving consumers more choice and prices. Foreign investment is the money spent by MNCs on assets such as land, buildings and machines; investment in a foreign country is Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) when it builds factories. Technology (transport, information and communication technology) and liberalisation (removal of trade barriers such as tariffs and quotas) in 1991 sped it up. The World Trade Organisation (WTO) sets rules for trade. India set up Special Economic Zones (SEZs) with world-class facilities to attract investment. The impact has been unequal: producers in a few sectors benefited (cars, IT, services) while small producers suffered competition. Fair globalisation needs fair rules, labour protection and WTO reform.
## Deep dive: what globalisation is
Globalisation links countries through trade, investment and movement of ideas. It is driven by MNCs, technology and liberalisation of trade and investment policies.
## Deep dive: MNCs and production
MNCs spread production across countries to cut costs: design in one, manufacture in another, call centres elsewhere. They invest in or tie up with local firms through joint ventures and subcontracting.
## Deep dive: 1991 liberalisation and the WTO
India removed trade barriers (tariffs and quotas) in 1991. The WTO frames trade rules, but richer nations have greater influence. SEZs attract foreign investment.
## Deep dive: impact
Consumers gained choice; some Indian firms became MNCs; but small producers, workers in the unorganised sector and domestic industries faced competition and insecurity. Fair globalisation needs fair rules, worker protection and support to small producers.
