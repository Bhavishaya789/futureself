# AI Project Cycle and Ethics
minutes: 35
yt: class 10 ai and it ai project cycle and ethics
@study
The AI project cycle has five stages: Problem Scoping, Data Acquisition, Data Exploration, Modelling and Evaluation, followed by Deployment. Problem scoping uses the 4Ws canvas (Who, What, Where, Why) and a problem statement template. Data features, training and testing data, and visualisation guide modelling. AI models are rule-based or learning-based (supervised, unsupervised, reinforcement). AI ethics demands fairness, transparency, privacy, accountability, safety and the avoidance of bias; AI bias arises from skewed data or design choices. The three domains of AI are Data, Computer Vision and NLP.
## Deep dive: problem scoping
Use the 4Ws canvas: Who (stakeholders), What (nature of problem), Where (context), Why (benefit of solving). Summarise it in a problem statement template. Choose problems linked to the SDGs.
## Deep dive: data and exploration
Acquire data from surveys, sensors, cameras, web scraping and APIs. Split it into training and testing sets. Visualise with charts to find patterns and relationships among features.
## Deep dive: modelling approaches
Rule-based models follow developer-written rules; learning-based models learn from data. Learning types are supervised (labelled), unsupervised (unlabelled) and reinforcement (rewards). Deep learning uses multi-layer neural networks.
## Deep dive: ethics
Bias in data leads to unfair outcomes. Ethical AI needs fairness, privacy, transparency, accountability and access for all. Developers must test for bias and protect personal data.

Q: What are the stages of the AI project cycle?
A: Problem scoping, data acquisition, data exploration, modelling and evaluation.
Q: What is problem scoping?
A: Identifying and clearly defining the problem to be solved.
Q: What are the 4Ws of problem scoping?
A: Who, What, Where and Why.
Q: What does the Who canvas identify?
A: The stakeholders affected by the problem.
Q: What does the What canvas describe?
A: The nature of the problem and evidence for it.
Q: What does the Where canvas describe?
A: The context or situation in which the problem occurs.
Q: What does the Why canvas describe?
A: The benefits to stakeholders if the problem is solved.
Q: What is a problem statement template?
A: A summary that states the problem, stakeholders and the ideal solution.
Q: What are the UN Sustainable Development Goals used for in AI projects?
A: To choose meaningful real-world problems to solve.
Q: What is data acquisition?
A: Collecting reliable data for the project.
Q: What are the two types of data?
A: Training data and testing data.
Q: What is training data?
A: Data used to teach the model.
Q: What is testing data?
A: Data used to check how well the model works.
Q: What is a data feature?
A: A type of data that affects the problem, such as age or temperature.
Q: What is the system map?
A: A diagram showing relationships among system elements.
Q: What are sources of data?
A: Surveys, web scraping, sensors, cameras, observations and APIs.
Q: What is web scraping?
A: Collecting data automatically from websites.
Q: What is an API?
A: An interface that lets programs exchange data.
Q: What is data exploration?
A: Visualising and understanding data to find patterns.
Q: Name some data visualisation tools.
A: Bar charts, line graphs, scatter plots and pie charts.
Q: What is modelling?
A: Choosing and training an algorithm to learn patterns.
Q: What are the two approaches to AI modelling?
A: Rule-based and learning-based.
Q: What is a rule-based approach?
A: The developer defines the rules the machine follows.
Q: What is a learning-based approach?
A: The machine learns rules from data.
Q: What is machine learning?
A: Algorithms that learn patterns from data.
Q: What is supervised learning?
A: Learning from labelled data.
Q: What is unsupervised learning?
A: Finding patterns in unlabelled data.
Q: What is reinforcement learning?
A: Learning through rewards and penalties.
Q: What is deep learning?
A: Machine learning using multi-layer neural networks.
Q: What is a neural network?
A: A model inspired by the brain with layers of connected nodes.
Q: What is evaluation?
A: Testing the model against test data to measure performance.
Q: What is deployment?
A: Putting the model into real use.
Q: What are the three domains of AI?
A: Data, Computer Vision and Natural Language Processing.
Q: What is AI bias?
A: Unfair results caused by prejudiced data or design.
Q: What is AI access?
A: Making sure AI benefits all people, not only a few.
Q: What is data privacy?
A: Protecting personal data from misuse.
Q: Why is transparency important?
A: So people can understand how AI makes decisions.
Q: What is accountability in AI?
A: Being responsible for the outcomes of AI systems.
Q: What is AI ethics?
A: Moral principles guiding the design and use of AI.
Q: Name a risk of facial recognition.
A: Privacy violation and misidentification.
