# Natural Language Processing
minutes: 35
yt: class 10 ai and it natural language processing
@study
Natural Language Processing (NLP) enables computers to understand, interpret and generate human language. Applications include chatbots, voice assistants, translation, sentiment analysis and spam filters. Text processing steps: text normalisation (sentence segmentation, tokenisation, removing stopwords, converting to lowercase, stemming and lemmatisation). The bag-of-words model counts word occurrences in a corpus; TF-IDF weighs a word by how often it appears in a document and how rare it is across documents. Challenges include ambiguity, sarcasm and context.
## Deep dive: applications
NLP powers chatbots (script-bots and smart-bots), translation, voice assistants, sentiment analysis, summarisation and spam filtering.
## Deep dive: text normalisation
Steps: segment sentences, tokenise, remove stopwords, lowercase, then stem or lemmatise. Lemmatisation gives real words ("studies" → "study"), stemming may not ("studies" → "studi").
## Deep dive: bag of words and TF-IDF
Create a vocabulary from the normalised corpus and count words per document to form vectors. TF-IDF multiplies term frequency by inverse document frequency, so common words score low and distinctive words score high.
## Deep dive: challenges
Ambiguity, sarcasm, multiple meanings, context and code-mixed text like Hinglish make NLP hard.

Q: What is NLP?
A: The AI domain that lets machines understand and use human language.
Q: Give examples of NLP applications.
A: Chatbots, translation, voice assistants and spam filters.
Q: What is a chatbot?
A: A program that converses with users in natural language.
Q: What are the two types of chatbots?
A: Script-bot and smart-bot.
Q: What is a script-bot?
A: A chatbot that follows fixed scripts.
Q: What is a smart-bot?
A: A chatbot that learns and uses AI.
Q: What is sentiment analysis?
A: Finding whether text is positive, negative or neutral.
Q: What is automatic summarisation?
A: Producing a short version of a long text.
Q: What is a corpus?
A: A large collection of text.
Q: What is text normalisation?
A: Converting text to a simpler form for processing.
Q: What is sentence segmentation?
A: Dividing text into sentences.
Q: What is tokenisation?
A: Splitting sentences into tokens or words.
Q: What are stopwords?
A: Common words with little meaning like is, the and a.
Q: Why remove stopwords?
A: To reduce noise and focus on useful words.
Q: Why convert text to lowercase?
A: So that the same word in different cases is treated alike.
Q: What is stemming?
A: Removing word endings to get a root, which may not be a real word.
Q: What is lemmatisation?
A: Reducing a word to its meaningful dictionary base.
Q: How does lemmatisation differ from stemming?
A: It gives a valid word, whereas stemming may not.
Q: What is the bag-of-words model?
A: A model that counts word occurrences ignoring order.
Q: What is a document vector?
A: A list of word counts for a document.
Q: What is document frequency?
A: The number of documents containing a word.
Q: What is TF-IDF?
A: Term Frequency–Inverse Document Frequency, a measure of word importance.
Q: What is term frequency?
A: How often a word appears in a document.
Q: What is inverse document frequency?
A: A measure of how rare a word is across all documents.
Q: What does a high TF-IDF mean?
A: The word is important to that document.
Q: What happens to the value of stopwords in TF-IDF?
A: It is low, showing little importance.
Q: What is the formula for IDF?
A: IDF = total documents ÷ document frequency (often with log).
Q: What is ambiguity in language?
A: When a sentence has more than one meaning.
Q: What is a multiple meaning word?
A: A word with more than one meaning.
Q: What is perfect syntax, no meaning?
A: A sentence that is grammatically right but meaningless.
Q: What is Turing test?
A: A test of whether a machine can imitate human conversation.
Q: What is speech recognition?
A: Converting speech into text.
Q: What is machine translation?
A: Automatic translation between languages.
Q: Name a voice assistant.
A: Alexa, Siri or Google Assistant.
Q: What is a spam filter?
A: A tool that detects unwanted email.
Q: What is named entity recognition?
A: Identifying names of people, places and organisations.
Q: What is a challenge in NLP?
A: Sarcasm, ambiguity and context.
Q: What is text classification?
A: Assigning categories to text.
Q: What is a language model?
A: A model that predicts the next word.
Q: What is Hinglish?
A: A mix of Hindi and English, harder for NLP.
