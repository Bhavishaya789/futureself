# Manufacturing Industries
minutes: 35
yt: class 10 geography manufacturing industries
@study
Manufacturing is the production of goods in large quantities after processing raw materials. It boosts agriculture, creates jobs, reduces poverty and brings foreign exchange. Industries are classified by raw material (agro-based and mineral-based), role (basic and consumer), capital investment (small and large scale), ownership (public, private, joint, cooperative) and bulk and weight of raw material. Agro-based industries include cotton, jute, silk, woollen textiles, sugar and edible oil; mineral-based include iron and steel, cement, aluminium, fertilisers and petrochemicals. Iron and steel plants are at Jamshedpur (TISCO), Bhilai, Durgapur, Rourkela, Bokaro, Salem and Visakhapatnam. Cotton textiles are concentrated in Mumbai and Ahmedabad; jute in Hugli belt; sugar in Uttar Pradesh and Maharashtra. IT industry thrives in Bengaluru, Hyderabad and Noida. Industrial pollution of air, water, land and noise must be controlled.
## Deep dive: importance
Manufacturing modernises agriculture, creates secondary and tertiary jobs, earns foreign exchange and removes unemployment and poverty. Balanced regional development depends on it.
## Deep dive: classification
Raw material: agro-based, mineral-based. Role: basic, consumer. Size: small, large. Ownership: public, private, joint, cooperative.
## Deep dive: major industries
Cotton textiles: Mumbai, Ahmedabad. Jute: Hugli belt. Sugar: UP, Maharashtra. Iron and steel: TISCO, Bhilai, Durgapur, Rourkela, Bokaro. Aluminium: Odisha. Cement, fertilisers, IT: Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Noida.
## Deep dive: pollution and control
Industries cause air, water, thermal, noise and land pollution. Control by cleaner fuels, filters, treatment of effluents, noise barriers and rules like the Environment Protection Act.
