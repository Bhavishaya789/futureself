# Resources and Development
minutes: 35
yt: class 10 geography resources and development
@study
A resource is anything in the environment that can satisfy human needs, is technologically accessible, economically feasible and culturally acceptable. Resources are classified by origin (biotic and abiotic), exhaustibility (renewable and non-renewable), ownership (individual, community, national, international) and status of development (potential, developed, stock, reserves). Over-use created problems, so sustainable development and resource planning are essential; Agenda 21 was adopted at the Rio Earth Summit 1992. Land is a natural resource of utmost importance; land use categories are forests, land not available for cultivation, other uncultivated land, fallow land and net sown area. India has varied soils: alluvial, black, red and yellow, laterite, arid and forest soils. Soil erosion is checked by contour ploughing, terrace farming, strip cropping and shelter belts.
## Deep dive: classification of resources
By origin: biotic and abiotic. By exhaustibility: renewable (continuous or flow) and non-renewable (recyclable like metals, non-recyclable like fossil fuels). By ownership: individual, community, national, international. By development: potential, developed, stock and reserves.
## Deep dive: planning and sustainability
Resource planning identifies, surveys and evaluates resources, matches them to technology and development plans. Sustainable development requires equity and care for the environment (Agenda 21, Rio 1992).
## Deep dive: land
Land use: forests, land not available for cultivation (barren, non-agricultural use), other uncultivated land (pastures, groves, culturable waste), fallow lands and net sown area. Degradation arises from deforestation, mining, overgrazing, over-irrigation and industrial effluents.
## Deep dive: soils
Alluvial: Ganga-Brahmaputra plains; fertile; wheat, rice. Black: Deccan lava; cotton; moisture-retentive. Red and yellow: iron; low humus. Laterite: heavy rain, leaching; tea, coffee, cashew. Arid: Rajasthan; sandy, saline. Forest soils: hills; humus rich.
## Deep dive: conservation
Contour ploughing, terrace farming, strip cropping, shelter belts and afforestation prevent erosion.

Q: What is a resource?
A: Anything in the environment that satisfies human needs and is technologically accessible, economically feasible and culturally acceptable.
Q: How are resources classified by origin?
A: Biotic and abiotic.
Q: Give examples of biotic resources.
A: Human beings, forests, animals and fisheries.
Q: Give examples of abiotic resources.
A: Land, water, air and minerals.
Q: How are resources classified by exhaustibility?
A: Renewable and non-renewable.
Q: Give examples of renewable resources.
A: Solar energy, wind, water and forests.
Q: Give examples of non-renewable resources.
A: Minerals and fossil fuels.
Q: How are resources classified by ownership?
A: Individual, community, national and international.
Q: Give an example of an individual resource.
A: A house, plot or well owned by a person.
Q: Give an example of a community resource.
A: Village ponds, public parks and burial grounds.
Q: Give an example of a national resource.
A: Minerals, water, forests and land within a country's borders.
Q: Up to what distance from the coast is the Exclusive Economic Zone?
A: 200 nautical miles.
Q: What are potential resources?
A: Resources found in a region but not yet used.
Q: What are developed resources?
A: Resources surveyed and being used.
Q: What are stock resources?
A: Materials that can satisfy needs but lack the technology to use them.
Q: What are reserves?
A: Part of the stock that can be used with existing technology but is kept for the future.
Q: Why is resource planning necessary?
A: Resources are limited and unevenly distributed.
Q: What is sustainable development?
A: Development without damaging the environment and meeting needs without compromising future generations.
Q: What was the Rio Earth Summit?
A: A 1992 UN conference on environment and development in Rio de Janeiro.
Q: What is Agenda 21?
A: A declaration to combat environmental damage and poverty through global cooperation.
Q: Who wrote "Small is Beautiful"?
A: E. F. Schumacher.

A: Limits to Growth.
Q: What are the land use categories?
A: Forests, land not available for cultivation, other uncultivated land, fallow land and net sown area.
Q: What is net sown area?
A: The area sown with crops, counted once.
Q: What is gross cropped area?
A: Net sown area plus area sown more than once.
Q: What is current fallow?
A: Land left uncultivated for one or less than one agricultural year.
Q: What is the target forest area in the National Forest Policy 1952?
A: 33 per cent of the geographical area.
Q: What is a barren and waste land?
A: Rocky, arid and desert land not suitable for cultivation.
Q: What is a land degradation?
A: Reduction in the quality of land by human activities.
Q: Name the causes of land degradation.
A: Deforestation, overgrazing, mining, over-irrigation and industrial effluents.
Q: What is soil?
A: The most important renewable resource that supports plant growth.
Q: Which factors form soil?
A: Relief, parent rock, climate, vegetation, time and other life forms.
Q: Which soil is most widespread in India?
A: Alluvial soil.
Q: Where is alluvial soil found?
A: The northern plains and some coastal and delta regions.
Q: Which rivers deposited alluvial soil?
A: Indus, Ganga and Brahmaputra.
Q: What are bangar and khadar?
A: Older alluvium and newer alluvium respectively.
Q: Which crops grow in alluvial soil?
A: Wheat, rice, sugarcane and pulses.
Q: Which soil is called regur?
A: Black soil.
Q: Which crop is best suited to black soil?
A: Cotton.
Q: What is the black soil made of?
A: Volcanic rock (basalt); it is clayey and retains moisture.
Q: Which soil is formed by intense leaching?
A: Laterite soil.
Q: Which crops grow in laterite soil?
A: Tea, coffee and cashew nut.
Q: Where is red soil found?
A: Parts of the Deccan plateau, Odisha and Chhattisgarh.
Q: Why is red soil red?
A: Because of diffusion of iron in crystalline and metamorphic rocks.
Q: Which soil has kankar layers?
A: Arid soil.
Q: Where are forest soils found?
A: In hilly and mountainous areas.
Q: What is soil erosion?
A: Removal of soil cover by wind or water faster than it forms.
Q: What is gully erosion?
A: Running water cutting deep channels called gullies.
Q: What is sheet erosion?
A: Water flowing as a sheet removing top soil.
Q: What is contour ploughing?
A: Ploughing along contour lines to slow water.
Q: What is terrace farming?
A: Cutting steps on steep slopes to cultivate.
Q: What is strip cropping?
A: Planting crops in strips with grass bands between.
Q: What are shelter belts?
A: Rows of trees planted to check wind speed.
