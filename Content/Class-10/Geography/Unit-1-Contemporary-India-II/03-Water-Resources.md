# Water Resources
minutes: 35
yt: class 10 geography water resources
@study
About 96.5 per cent of the Earth's water is in oceans and only about 2.5 per cent is freshwater, of which most is locked as ice. India receives 4 per cent of the world's freshwater and has a high demand because of population and irrigation. Water scarcity arises from over-exploitation, unequal access, pollution and growing demand for irrigation and industry. Multipurpose river projects (Bhakra-Nangal, Hirakud, Sardar Sarovar, Tehri) provide irrigation, power, flood control and fisheries but face criticism on displacement, sedimentation and flooding; the Narmada Bachao Andolan protests against them. Rainwater harvesting has traditional forms: guls and kuls in the Western Himalayas, rooftop harvesting in Rajasthan, inundation channels in Bengal, khadins and johads in Rajasthan, bamboo drip irrigation in Meghalaya and tankas in Rajasthan. Tamil Nadu made rooftop harvesting mandatory.
## Deep dive: water scarcity
Water is a renewable resource but demand outpaces supply because of population, irrigation, industry, pollution and unequal access. India's per capita availability is falling.
## Deep dive: multipurpose projects
Dams on rivers like the Sutlej (Bhakra-Nangal), Mahanadi (Hirakud), Narmada (Sardar Sarovar) and Bhagirathi (Tehri) bring irrigation and power, but cause displacement, sedimentation, flood risks and conflicts. The Narmada Bachao Andolan opposes displacement.
## Deep dive: rainwater harvesting
Traditional systems: guls/kuls, rooftop tanks (tankas), khadins and johads, bamboo drip irrigation. Rooftop harvesting is compulsory in Tamil Nadu; Gendathur is a model.
