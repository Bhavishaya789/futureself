# Forest and Wildlife Resources
minutes: 35
yt: class 10 geography forest and wildlife resources
@study
India is rich in biodiversity, but forests and wildlife are under threat. The International Union for Conservation of Nature (IUCN) classes species as normal, endangered, vulnerable, rare, endemic and extinct. Causes of depletion include colonial exploitation, agricultural expansion, large development projects, mining, grazing and fuelwood collection, environmental destruction and unequal access to resources. Conservation measures include the Indian Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972, national parks, sanctuaries, biosphere reserves, Project Tiger (1973) and community efforts such as Chipko, Beej Bachao Andolan and Joint Forest Management. Forests are legally classified as reserved, protected and unclassed. Sacred groves also protect biodiversity.
## Deep dive: biodiversity and depletion
India has about 8 per cent of the world's species. Causes of depletion: colonial railways, shipbuilding and agriculture expansion; post-independence development projects, mining, grazing, fuelwood and large dams; poverty and unequal use of resources.
## Deep dive: classification of species
Normal, endangered, vulnerable, rare, endemic and extinct (IUCN Red List categories).
## Deep dive: conservation
Wildlife Protection Act 1972, Project Tiger 1973, national parks, sanctuaries and biosphere reserves. Community movements: Chipko, Beej Bachao Andolan and JFM (Odisha 1988); sacred groves.
## Deep dive: forests in India
Reserved (over half), protected (about one third), unclassed (remainder). Madhya Pradesh has the largest area of permanent forests.

Q: What does IUCN stand for?
A: International Union for Conservation of Nature.
Q: What are normal species?
A: Species whose population is normal for survival, like cattle, sal and pine.
Q: What are endangered species?
A: Species in danger of extinction, such as the black buck, crocodile and Indian wild ass.
Q: What are vulnerable species?
A: Species whose numbers are declining and may become endangered, such as the blue sheep and Asiatic elephant.
Q: What are rare species?
A: Species with small populations that may move into the vulnerable or endangered category.
Q: What are endemic species?
A: Species found only in particular areas, like the Andaman wild pig.
Q: What are extinct species?
A: Species no longer found, such as the Asiatic cheetah and pink-headed duck.
Q: Which animal was declared extinct in India in 1952?
A: The cheetah.
Q: What percentage of the world species does India have?
A: About 8 per cent of the world's species.
Q: What percentage of the world area does India have?
A: About 2.4 per cent.
Q: Why are forests important?
A: They provide oxygen, regulate climate, protect soil and support biodiversity.
Q: Which year was the Indian Wildlife Protection Act passed?
A: 1972.
Q: What did the Wildlife Act do?
A: Banned hunting, protected habitats and listed protected species.
Q: When was Project Tiger launched?
A: 1973.
Q: Why was Project Tiger launched?
A: Tiger numbers fell from about 40,000 at the beginning of the 20th century to 1,827 in 1973.
Q: Name a tiger reserve.
A: Corbett, Sunderbans, Bandhavgarh or Kaziranga.
Q: Which reserve is in Uttarakhand?
A: Corbett National Park.
Q: Which reserve is in Madhya Pradesh?
A: Bandhavgarh and Kanha.
Q: Where is the Sunderbans National Park?
A: West Bengal.
Q: Which park protects the one-horned rhino?
A: Kaziranga National Park in Assam.
Q: Which sanctuary protects the Asiatic lion?
A: The Gir forest in Gujarat.
Q: How are forests classified legally?
A: Reserved, protected and unclassed.
Q: What are reserved forests?
A: Forests permanently reserved for timber, with the most protection.
Q: What are protected forests?
A: Forests protected from further depletion.
Q: What are unclassed forests?
A: Other forests and wastelands owned by government or private individuals.
Q: Which state has the largest area under permanent forests?
A: Madhya Pradesh.
Q: Which states have large areas of reserved forests?
A: Jammu and Kashmir, Andhra Pradesh, Uttarakhand, Kerala, Tamil Nadu, West Bengal and Maharashtra.
Q: What is the Chipko Movement?
A: A movement in the Himalayas where villagers hugged trees to stop felling.
Q: Who is associated with Chipko?
A: Sunderlal Bahuguna.
Q: What is the Beej Bachao Andolan?
A: A movement in Tehri to save traditional seeds and promote diverse crops.
Q: What is Joint Forest Management?
A: A programme involving local communities in protecting forests.
Q: Where was JFM first launched?
A: Odisha in 1988.
Q: What are sacred groves?
A: Patches of forest protected by communities for religious reasons.
Q: What is a biosphere reserve?
A: A protected area conserving ecosystems and traditional ways of life.
Q: How many biosphere reserves does India have?
A: 18 (as per NCERT).
Q: What is a national park?
A: A protected area for wildlife and their habitat with no human activity.
Q: What is a sanctuary?
A: An area where animals are protected, with limited human activity allowed.
Q: What causes depletion of forests?
A: Agriculture expansion, large projects, mining, grazing and fuelwood.
Q: Which large project caused loss of forest in Madhya Pradesh?
A: Narmada Sagar and Sardar Sarovar projects.
Q: What is deforestation?
A: Clearing of forest cover.
Q: What is biodiversity?
A: Variety of living organisms in an area.
Q: What is flora?
A: The plants of a region.
Q: What is fauna?
A: The animals of a region.
Q: What are the benefits of Joint Forest Management?
A: Better protection and fair benefits to local communities.
Q: Why are mangroves important?
A: They protect the coast and support fisheries.
Q: What is the role of the Forest Rights Act?
A: To recognise the rights of tribal communities over forest land.
