@study
An ecosystem consists of biotic (producers, consumers, decomposers) and abiotic components. Food chains link organisms by who eats whom: grass -> grasshopper -> frog -> snake -> eagle. Energy flows in one direction and only about 10 per cent passes to the next trophic level (10 per cent law). Biomagnification increases the concentration of non-biodegradable chemicals such as DDT with each level, highest in the top carnivores. The ozone layer (O3) in the stratosphere absorbs harmful UV; chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs) deplete it, and the 1987 Montreal Protocol limited CFC use. Waste is biodegradable or non-biodegradable; Reduce, Reuse, Recycle; managing waste protects the environment.
## Deep dive: Ecosystem and food chain
Producers (green plants, algae) make food by photosynthesis; primary consumers (herbivores) eat plants; secondary consumers (carnivores) eat herbivores; tertiary consumers (top carnivores). Decomposers (bacteria, fungi) break down dead remains and return nutrients. Food chains usually have three or four trophic levels because energy loss at each step is large. A food web is a network of interconnected food chains. Artificial ecosystems include gardens, crop fields and aquarium; natural ecosystems include forest, pond and lake.
## Deep dive: Energy flow
Plants capture about 1 per cent of solar energy; at each trophic level only 10 per cent of energy is available to the next (Lindeman's 10 per cent law). So 1000 J in producers gives 100 J to herbivores, 10 J to carnivores and 1 J to top carnivores. Energy flows unidirectionally, whereas nutrients cycle. Pesticides like DDT accumulate in the food chain (biomagnification), appearing in the highest concentration in humans and other top carnivores.
## Deep dive: Ozone and human impact
Ozone is formed when UV splits O2 into free atoms, which join O2: O2 -> O + O; O + O2 -> O3. It shields life from UV rays. CFCs from refrigerators, aerosol sprays and fire extinguishers release chlorine radicals that destroy ozone, leading to a hole over the Antarctic in the 1980s. UNEP agreed in 1987 to freeze CFC production at 1986 levels (Montreal Protocol). UV exposure causes skin cancer, cataract and plant damage.
## Deep dive: Waste management
Biodegradable wastes (vegetable peels, paper, cotton, dung) are broken by bacteria and fungi; non-biodegradable (plastics, DDT, glass, metals) persist. Effects: pollution of soil and water, harm to animals and humans. Waste is managed by composting, vermicomposting, recycling, landfills and incineration. Principles: refuse, reduce, reuse, recycle and rethink. Ban on plastic bags, use of cloth bags and the Swachh Bharat campaign help.

## Moderate
P: What is the 10 per cent law? If producers have 5000 J of energy, find the energy at the tertiary consumer level.
A: Only 10 per cent of energy passes to the next level. Producers 5000 J -> herbivores 500 J -> carnivores 50 J -> tertiary 5 J.

## High
P: What is biomagnification? Why is it high in top carnivores?
A: Biomagnification is the increase in concentration of non-biodegradable chemicals like DDT at each higher trophic level, because they are not excreted but accumulate in tissues. A top carnivore feeds on many contaminated animals so it has the highest concentration.

## Advanced
P: Explain the depletion of the ozone layer and the steps taken to protect it.
A: UV splits O2 into atoms forming O3. CFCs release chlorine which breaks ozone molecules, forming a hole over Antarctica. UV causes skin cancer and cataract. The Montreal Protocol (1987) froze CFC production; alternatives like hydrofluorocarbons are used in refrigerators.
