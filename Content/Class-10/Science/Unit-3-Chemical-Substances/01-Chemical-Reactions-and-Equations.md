# Chemical Reactions and Equations

@study
A chemical reaction changes reactants into products with new properties. Signs are change in state, colour, evolution of gas, or temperature. A chemical equation must be balanced to obey the law of conservation of mass. Types: combination, decomposition (thermal, electrolytic, photolytic), displacement, double displacement (precipitation, neutralisation), and redox reactions. Oxidation is gain of oxygen or loss of hydrogen; reduction is loss of oxygen or gain of hydrogen. Corrosion and rancidity are everyday oxidation effects, prevented by painting, galvanising, or adding antioxidants and nitrogen flushing.
## Deep dive: Balancing equations
Count atoms of each element on both sides. Start with the compound with most atoms or the most complex formula, balance metals, then non-metals, then hydrogen and oxygen. Use only coefficients, never change subscripts. Add state symbols (s), (l), (g), (aq). Example: Fe + H2O -> Fe3O4 + H2 balances as 3Fe + 4H2O -> Fe3O4 + 4H2. The law of conservation of mass (Lavoisier) says mass is neither created nor destroyed in a chemical reaction.
## Deep dive: Types of reactions
Combination: CaO + H2O -> Ca(OH)2 (slaked lime, exothermic); 2Mg + O2 -> 2MgO. Decomposition: 2FeSO4 -> Fe2O3 + SO2 + SO3 (thermal); 2H2O -> 2H2 + O2 (electrolytic); 2AgCl -> 2Ag + Cl2 (photolytic, used in black and white photography). Displacement: Fe + CuSO4 -> FeSO4 + Cu, more reactive metal displaces less reactive. Double displacement: Na2SO4 + BaCl2 -> BaSO4 (white ppt) + 2NaCl. Neutralisation is acid + base -> salt + water.
## Deep dive: Oxidation and reduction
Oxidation is addition of oxygen or removal of hydrogen; reduction is removal of oxygen or addition of hydrogen. In CuO + H2 -> Cu + H2O, CuO is reduced and H2 is oxidised. Both occur together, so it is a redox reaction. The substance oxidised is the reducing agent; the substance reduced is the oxidising agent.
## Deep dive: Corrosion and rancidity
Corrosion is the slow eating away of metals by air, moisture or acids: iron rusts (Fe2O3.xH2O) and silver turns black (Ag2S), copper turns green (basic copper carbonate). Prevention: painting, oiling, galvanisation (zinc coating), alloying. Rancidity is the oxidation of fats and oils in food giving bad smell and taste; prevented by antioxidants, airtight containers, refrigeration, or flushing with nitrogen as in chips packets.

## Advanced
P: Explain corrosion and rancidity, their harmful effects and the methods of prevention.
A: Corrosion is the slow attack on metals by oxygen and moisture (rusting of iron, black silver, green copper); it damages structures costing billions. Rancidity is oxidation of fats giving bad smell and taste. Prevention: paint, oil, galvanising, alloying for corrosion; antioxidants, nitrogen flushing, airtight containers, cool storage for rancidity.
