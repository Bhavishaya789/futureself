# Metals and Non-metals
minutes: 40
yt: class 10 science metals and non metals
@study
Metals are lustrous, malleable, ductile, good conductors of heat and electricity, and form basic oxides by losing electrons to become cations. Non-metals are brittle, non-lustrous (except iodine, graphite), poor conductors (except graphite) and form acidic oxides. Amphoteric oxides such as Al2O3 and ZnO react with both acids and bases. The reactivity series is K, Na, Ca, Mg, Al, Zn, Fe, Pb, H, Cu, Hg, Ag, Au. Ionic compounds have high melting points, conduct in molten state and in solution. Extraction depends on reactivity: electrolytic reduction for top metals, carbon reduction for the middle, and heating for the least reactive. Corrosion is prevented by galvanisation and alloying.
## Deep dive: Physical and chemical properties
Metals: sonorous, hard (except Na, K soft), solid (except mercury liquid), high melting points (except Ga, Cs low). Gold and silver are the most malleable; copper and silver the best conductors; lead and mercury poor conductors. Chemically: metal + oxygen -> metal oxide (4Na + O2; 2Cu + O2 -> 2CuO black); Na and K are stored in kerosene; Mg burns brilliantly; Al2O3 and ZnO are amphoteric. Metal + water: Na and K react violently; Ca less; Mg with hot water/steam; Al, Fe, Zn with steam giving oxide and hydrogen. Metal + acid gives salt and hydrogen (not with nitric acid). Aqua regia (3 HCl : 1 HNO3) dissolves gold and platinum.
## Deep dive: Reactivity series and ionic compounds
More reactive metals displace less reactive from solutions: Zn + CuSO4 -> ZnSO4 + Cu. Ionic compounds like NaCl form by transfer of electrons: Na loses an electron to Cl making Na+ and Cl-. They are solids, hard and brittle, have high melting and boiling points due to strong electrostatic forces, are soluble in water and conduct electricity in molten and aqueous states but not in solid.
## Deep dive: Occurrence and extraction
Minerals contain metals and ores are minerals from which metals can be extracted profitably. Steps: concentration (enrichment) of ore by gravity separation, magnetic separation or froth flotation; extraction; refining. Metals at the top (K, Na, Ca, Mg, Al) by electrolysis of molten chlorides, e.g. NaCl -> Na + Cl2; Al2O3 is reduced electrolytically. Middle metals (Zn, Fe, Pb) are found as sulphides or carbonates; ores are roasted (sulphides heated in air to oxide) or calcined (carbonates heated in limited air) and then reduced by carbon: ZnO + C -> Zn + CO. Thermite reaction: Fe2O3 + 2Al -> 2Fe + Al2O3 used for welding rail tracks. Least reactive Cu, Hg: HgS heated gives Hg. Electrolytic refining of copper: impure copper is anode, pure copper cathode, acidified copper sulphate electrolyte, impurities settle as anode mud.
## Deep dive: Corrosion and alloys
Rusting of iron needs both air and water. Prevention: painting, oiling, galvanising (zinc), chrome plating, anodising (aluminium), alloying. Alloys are homogeneous mixtures of metals, or metal with non-metal: brass (Cu + Zn), bronze (Cu + Sn), solder (Pb + Sn), stainless steel (Fe, Ni, Cr), and amalgam has mercury. Pure gold is 24 carat; 22 carat gold is alloyed with copper or silver to make it hard. Iron is made hard by adding carbon.

## High
P: Explain the extraction of a metal of medium reactivity such as zinc from its ore.
A: Zinc sulphide is roasted: 2ZnS + 3O2 -> 2ZnO + 2SO2; zinc carbonate is calcined: ZnCO3 -> ZnO + CO2. ZnO is reduced with carbon: ZnO + C -> Zn + CO. Then refined electrolytically.

## Advanced
P: Describe the formation of NaCl and MgCl2 by electron transfer and list the properties of ionic compounds.
A: Na (2,8,1) loses 1 electron to become Na+ and Cl (2,8,7) gains it becoming Cl-; Mg (2,8,2) loses two electrons and two Cl atoms each gain one. Properties: solid, hard and brittle; high melting and boiling points; soluble in water; conduct in molten and aqueous states only.
