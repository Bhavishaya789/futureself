# Acids, Bases and Salts
minutes: 40
yt: class 10 science acids bases and salts
@study
Acids turn blue litmus red, taste sour and release H+ ions in water; bases turn red litmus blue, are bitter and slippery, and give OH- ions; those soluble in water are alkalis. The pH scale from 0 to 14 measures H+ concentration: acids below 7, neutral 7, bases above 7. Acid + metal gives salt + hydrogen; acid + base gives salt + water; acid + metal carbonate/hydrogencarbonate gives salt + CO2 + water. Common salts: NaCl, NaOH from chlor-alkali process, bleaching powder CaOCl2, baking soda NaHCO3, washing soda Na2CO3.10H2O, and Plaster of Paris CaSO4.1/2H2O.
## Deep dive: Properties and indicators
Natural indicators: litmus, turmeric, red cabbage, China rose. Synthetic: phenolphthalein (colourless in acid, pink in base), methyl orange (red in acid, yellow in base). Olfactory indicators like onion, vanilla and clove lose smell in a base. Acids need water to release H+ (H3O+); dry HCl gas does not change dry litmus. Diluting an acid is exothermic: always add acid to water slowly with stirring, never water to acid.
## Deep dive: Reactions of acids and bases
Acid + metal: Zn + 2HCl -> ZnCl2 + H2 (pop test). Base + metal: 2NaOH + Zn -> Na2ZnO2 + H2. Metal carbonate + acid: Na2CO3 + 2HCl -> 2NaCl + H2O + CO2, CO2 turns lime water milky (Ca(OH)2 + CO2 -> CaCO3 + H2O) and the milkiness disappears on excess CO2 forming calcium hydrogencarbonate. Neutralisation: NaOH + HCl -> NaCl + H2O. Metal oxides are basic; non-metal oxides are acidic.
## Deep dive: pH and its importance
pH is the negative logarithm of hydrogen ion concentration. Stomach acid pH about 1.5-3; lemon juice 2.2; tomato 4.2; milk 6.4; pure water 7; blood 7.4; baking soda solution 8.4; milk of magnesia 10; NaOH 14. Importance: tooth decay begins below pH 5.5, so use basic toothpaste; acid rain pH below 5.6 harms aquatic life; indigestion treated with antacids (Mg(OH)2); bee sting (methanoic acid) and nettle sting treated by baking soda or dock leaf; soil pH for crops.
## Deep dive: Important salts
Common salt NaCl from sea water; rock salt. Chlor-alkali: 2NaCl + 2H2O -> 2NaOH + Cl2 + H2. Bleaching powder CaOCl2 (Ca(OH)2 + Cl2) for bleaching and disinfecting water. Baking soda NaHCO3 (NaCl + H2O + CO2 + NH3 -> NH4Cl + NaHCO3) used in antacids and baking powder, 2NaHCO3 -> Na2CO3 + H2O + CO2 on heating. Washing soda Na2CO3.10H2O from recrystallising sodium carbonate, used in glass, soap, paper. Plaster of Paris CaSO4.1/2H2O made by heating gypsum CaSO4.2H2O at 373 K; it sets into gypsum on mixing with water, used for supporting fractured bones. Water of crystallisation: CuSO4.5H2O is blue, anhydrous white.

A: Salt, water and carbon dioxide are formed. Na2CO3 + 2HCl -> 2NaCl + H2O + CO2. CO2 turns lime water milky.

## High
P: Why is pH important in daily life? Give three examples.
A: Tooth decay starts when mouth pH falls below 5.5, so basic toothpaste neutralises acids; acid rain pH below 5.6 harms rivers and life; antacids neutralise stomach acid; bee sting is treated with baking soda; soil pH decides crops.

## Advanced
P: How is Plaster of Paris prepared? Why must it be stored in a moisture-proof container? Give uses.
A: POP is made by heating gypsum CaSO4.2H2O at 373 K: CaSO4.2H2O -> CaSO4.1/2H2O + 1.5 H2O. It absorbs water and sets to hard gypsum, so moisture would spoil it. Uses: plaster for fractures, toys, decoration, smoothing surfaces.
