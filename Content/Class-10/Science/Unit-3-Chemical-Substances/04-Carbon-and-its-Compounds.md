# Carbon and its Compounds
minutes: 40
yt: class 10 science carbon and its compounds
@study
Carbon forms covalent bonds by sharing electrons, due to tetravalency and catenation. It occurs as diamond, graphite and fullerene (C-60). Hydrocarbons are saturated (alkanes, CnH2n+2) or unsaturated (alkenes CnH2n, alkynes CnH2n-2). A functional group decides properties: alcohol -OH, aldehyde -CHO, ketone C=O, carboxylic acid -COOH, halo -Cl/-Br. Homologous series differ by CH2 (14 u). IUPAC naming uses prefix, word root and suffix. Ethanol and ethanoic acid are important; reactions include combustion, oxidation, addition (hydrogenation), substitution, esterification and saponification. Soaps are sodium or potassium salts of long-chain fatty acids and act by micelle formation; detergents work in hard water.
## Deep dive: Bonding and allotropes
Carbon (2,4) neither gains nor loses four electrons easily so it shares electrons forming covalent bonds with low melting and boiling points and poor conductivity. Tetravalency lets carbon bond with four atoms. Catenation is self-linking into chains, branches and rings. Diamond: each carbon bonded to four others in a rigid 3D lattice, the hardest natural substance, non-conductor. Graphite: each carbon bonded to three, hexagonal layers, slippery, conducts electricity, used as lubricant. Fullerene C-60 has 60 carbon atoms like a football. Isomers have the same molecular formula but different structures: butane and isobutane.
## Deep dive: Hydrocarbons and nomenclature
Alkanes (single bonds): methane CH4, ethane C2H6, propane C3H8, butane C4H10, pentane C5H12. Alkenes (double bond): ethene C2H4, propene C3H6. Alkynes (triple bond): ethyne C2H2. Count carbon atoms in the longest chain for the word root (meth, eth, prop, but, pent, hex), suffix -ane, -ene, -yne, and prefixes like chloro-, bromo-. Functional groups: alcohol -ol, aldehyde -al, ketone -one, carboxylic acid -oic acid. Homologous series: same functional group and similar chemical properties, with physical properties changing gradually.
## Deep dive: Chemical properties
Combustion: CH4 + 2O2 -> CO2 + 2H2O; saturated hydrocarbons burn with a clean blue flame, unsaturated with a yellow sooty flame. Oxidation: alcohols are oxidised to carboxylic acids with alkaline KMnO4 or acidified potassium dichromate. Addition: unsaturated hydrocarbon + H2 -> saturated in the presence of nickel (hydrogenation of vegetable oils to make ghee). Substitution: CH4 + Cl2 -> CH3Cl + HCl in sunlight. Ethanol: reacts with sodium giving H2; dehydrated with conc. H2SO4 at 443 K to ethene. Ethanoic acid: vinegar is 5-8% acetic acid; freezes at 290 K as glacial acetic acid; reacts with NaHCO3 giving CO2; esterification with ethanol (conc. H2SO4 catalyst) gives ethyl ethanoate with fruity smell; ester + NaOH gives alcohol + soap (saponification).
## Deep dive: Soaps and detergents
Soap is the sodium or potassium salt of a long-chain carboxylic acid. Its molecule has an ionic hydrophilic head and a long hydrophobic hydrocarbon tail. In water the tails dissolve in oil and the heads face water forming a micelle that traps dirt and gets rinsed. Soaps form scum with calcium and magnesium salts of hard water; detergents are ammonium or sulphonate salts that do not form scum and clean in hard water as well. Ethanol is consumed as alcoholic drinks but is toxic; methanol causes blindness; denatured alcohol is made unfit for drinking.

## High
P: Explain the cleansing action of soap with the help of micelle formation.
A: A soap molecule has a hydrophilic ionic head and a hydrophobic carbon tail. In water, tails attach to oil and dirt while heads stay in water, forming a micelle with oil inside. The micelle is suspended and washed away. In hard water Ca and Mg salts form scum.

## Advanced
P: Write the structures of the first three members of the alkane, alkene and alkyne series and explain homologous series.
A: Alkanes: CH4, C2H6, C3H8. Alkenes: C2H4, C3H6, C4H8. Alkynes: C2H2, C3H4, C4H6. A homologous series has the same functional group, same general formula, members differ by CH2 (14 u) and show gradation in physical properties but similar chemical properties.
