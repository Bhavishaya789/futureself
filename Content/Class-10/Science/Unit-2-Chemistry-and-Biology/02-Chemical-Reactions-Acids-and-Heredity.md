@study
**Balanced equation:** atoms of each element equal on both sides. Example: 2 Mg + O2 -> 2 MgO; Fe + CuSO4 -> FeSO4 + Cu (displacement). Types: combination, decomposition, displacement, double displacement, redox.
**Acids and bases:** acids turn blue litmus red; pH below 7 acidic, 7 neutral, above 7 basic. Neutralisation: acid + base -> salt + water; e.g. HCl + NaOH -> NaCl + H2O. Common salt, baking soda (NaHCO3) and washing soda (Na2CO3.10H2O).
**Heredity:** Mendel's monohybrid cross gives a 3:1 phenotypic ratio in the F2 generation; human sex is determined by XY (male) and XX (female); the sex of the child depends on the father's chromosome.
## Deep dive: chemical reactions and equations
**Equations.** A chemical equation uses formulae for reactants and products. It must be balanced so each element has the same number of atoms on both sides (law of conservation of mass). Balance by trial: balance elements that appear once first, hydrogen and oxygen last. State symbols: (s), (l), (g), (aq). Conditions such as heat or catalyst are written over the arrow. Example: 2 H2 + O2 -> 2 H2O; Fe + CuSO4 -> FeSO4 + Cu.
**Types.** Combination: A + B -> AB (CaO + H2O -> Ca(OH)2, exothermic). Decomposition: AB -> A + B, by heat (CaCO3 -> CaO + CO2), light (2 AgCl -> 2 Ag + Cl2) or electricity (electrolysis of water). Displacement: a more reactive metal displaces a less reactive one (Zn + CuSO4 -> ZnSO4 + Cu). Double displacement: ions exchange and often a precipitate forms (Na2SO4 + BaCl2 -> BaSO4 + 2 NaCl). Redox: oxidation is gain of oxygen or loss of electrons; reduction is loss of oxygen or gain of electrons; they occur together (CuO + H2 -> Cu + H2O).
**Everyday changes.** Corrosion is slow oxidation of metals (rusting of iron needs air and water; prevent by painting, galvanising). Rancidity is oxidation of fats and oils; prevent with antioxidants, airtight containers, nitrogen flushing. Exothermic reactions release heat (burning, respiration); endothermic reactions absorb heat (photosynthesis, thermal decomposition).
## Deep dive: acids, bases and salts
**Acids** taste sour, turn blue litmus red, give H+ ions in water (HCl, H2SO4, HNO3, CH3COOH). **Bases** are bitter, soapy, turn red litmus blue and give OH- in water (NaOH, KOH, Ca(OH)2); a water-soluble base is an alkali. Indicators: litmus, phenolphthalein (pink in base), methyl orange (red in acid, yellow in base), turmeric (red in base).
**Reactions.** Acid + metal -> salt + hydrogen (Zn + H2SO4 -> ZnSO4 + H2; the gas burns with a pop). Acid + metal carbonate -> salt + water + carbon dioxide (which turns lime water milky). Acid + base -> salt + water (neutralisation). Acid + metal oxide -> salt + water. Non-metal oxides are acidic; metal oxides are basic.
**pH scale.** pH = -log [H+]. 0 to 7 acidic, 7 neutral, 7 to 14 basic. Each unit is a tenfold change. Gastric juice about 1.5-3, lemon juice 2.2, milk 6.5, pure water 7, blood 7.4, baking soda solution 8.3, milk of magnesia 10, NaOH 14. Uses: antacids for acidity, tooth enamel decays below pH 5.5, soil pH, acid rain below 5.6.
**Salts.** Common salt NaCl (from brine; chlor-alkali process gives NaOH, Cl2 and H2). Bleaching powder CaOCl2 (Cl2 + Ca(OH)2). Baking soda NaHCO3 (NaCl + H2O + CO2 + NH3 -> NaHCO3 + NH4Cl); on heating gives Na2CO3 + H2O + CO2; used in antacids and baking powder. Washing soda Na2CO3.10H2O. Plaster of Paris CaSO4.1/2 H2O (gypsum heated at 373 K) sets as CaSO4.2H2O. Water of crystallisation is the fixed number of water molecules in one formula unit of a salt (CuSO4.5H2O is blue and turns white when heated).
## Deep dive: heredity
**Basics.** Heredity is the transmission of characters from parents to offspring; variation is the difference between individuals. Variations arise from DNA copying errors and sexual reproduction and are the raw material of evolution. Genes on chromosomes are the units of heredity. A gene has alleles (dominant and recessive).
**Mendel.** Worked on garden pea (Pisum sativum) with seven contrasting pairs of traits. Monohybrid cross: tall (TT) x dwarf (tt) gives all tall Tt in F1; selfing F1 gives F2 with phenotypic ratio 3 tall : 1 dwarf and genotypic ratio 1 TT : 2 Tt : 1 tt. Dihybrid cross (round yellow RRYY x wrinkled green rryy): F2 phenotypic ratio 9 : 3 : 3 : 1 shows independent assortment. Laws: dominance, segregation of alleles, independent assortment.
**Sex determination.** Humans have 23 pairs of chromosomes: 22 pairs of autosomes and one pair of sex chromosomes (XX female, XY male). The mother always gives X; the father gives X or Y, so the sex of the child depends on the father. The chance of a boy or a girl is 50% each.
**Evolution points.** Acquired traits are not inherited. Speciation arises from variation, natural selection and isolation. Homologous organs (forelimb of man, bat, whale) show common ancestry; analogous organs (wing of bird and insect) have the same function with different origin. Fossils and carbon dating give evidence of evolution.
