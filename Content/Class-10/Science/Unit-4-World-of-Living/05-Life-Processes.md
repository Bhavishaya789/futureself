# Life Processes
minutes: 45
yt: class 10 science life processes
@study
Life processes are the basic functions that maintain life: nutrition, respiration, transportation and excretion. Autotrophs make food by photosynthesis (6CO2 + 12H2O -> C6H12O6 + 6O2 + 6H2O) in chloroplasts using stomata for gas exchange. Heterotrophs take food in holozoic, saprophytic or parasitic ways. Human digestion: mouth (salivary amylase), oesophagus, stomach (HCl, pepsin, mucus), small intestine (bile, pancreatic juice, intestinal juice; villi absorb), large intestine absorbs water. Respiration is aerobic (glucose to CO2, water and 38 ATP) or anaerobic (ethanol or lactic acid). Humans have a double circulation by a four-chambered heart. Blood has plasma, RBC, WBC, platelets; plants transport water by xylem and food by phloem (translocation). Excretion in humans is by nephrons of kidneys forming urine; plants release waste in leaves, bark and gum.
## Deep dive: Nutrition
Autotrophic nutrition: chlorophyll absorbs light, light energy converts to chemical, water splits to release oxygen and CO2 reduced to carbohydrates. Stomata open and close by guard cells. Heterotrophs: Amoeba engulfs food with pseudopodia in a food vacuole; Paramecium uses cilia; saprophytes like fungi (bread mould) use extracellular digestion; parasites like Cuscuta. Human alimentary canal: saliva with salivary amylase digests starch to maltose; peristalsis moves food; stomach has gastric glands that secrete HCl (kills bacteria, activates pepsin), pepsin (proteins) and mucus (protects); the pyloric sphincter regulates exit; liver bile emulsifies fats (alkaline), pancreas gives trypsin (proteins), amylase (starch) and lipase (fats); intestinal juice completes digestion; villi increase surface area for absorption; large intestine absorbs water.
## Deep dive: Respiration
Glucose is broken in the cytoplasm to pyruvate (glycolysis). In the absence of oxygen yeast gives ethanol + CO2; muscle cells give lactic acid (cramps); in oxygen mitochondria complete oxidation giving CO2 + H2O + energy. ATP is the energy currency: ADP + Pi + energy -> ATP. Human respiratory path: nostrils, pharynx, larynx, trachea (cartilage rings), bronchi, bronchioles, alveoli (gas exchange), with diaphragm and rib muscles in breathing. Haemoglobin carries oxygen. Aquatic organisms breathe dissolved oxygen through gills; terrestrial have lungs; fish breathe faster as water has less oxygen.
## Deep dive: Transportation
Human heart has four chambers (two atria, two ventricles) preventing mixing of oxygenated and deoxygenated blood, which is needed because we are warm-blooded with high energy needs. Double circulation: pulmonary (heart to lungs) and systemic (heart to body). Arteries thick-walled carry blood from heart; veins thin with valves carry blood to heart. Capillaries are one-cell thick for exchange. Blood pressure: about 120/80 mm Hg. Lymph carries digested fats and drains tissue fluid. In plants, xylem (tracheids, vessels) carries water by root pressure and transpiration pull; phloem (sieve tubes, companion cells) translocates sugar using energy ATP.
## Deep dive: Excretion
Excretion is removal of metabolic wastes. In humans, a pair of kidneys filter blood; each has nephrons: Bowman's capsule with glomerulus filters, the tubule reabsorbs glucose, amino acids, salts and water, and urine collects in the ureter, bladder and out through the urethra. Artificial kidney is dialysis for failure. Plants excrete oxygen, store waste in old leaves, fall of leaves, bark, resins and gums.

## Moderate
P: Differentiate between aerobic and anaerobic respiration.
A: Aerobic uses oxygen, occurs in mitochondria, gives CO2, water and about 38 ATP. Anaerobic occurs in cytoplasm without oxygen giving ethanol + CO2 (yeast) or lactic acid (muscles) and little energy.

## High
P: Describe the double circulation of blood in humans and why it is necessary.
A: Blood passes twice through the heart in one cycle: right ventricle to lungs (pulmonary) and returns oxygenated to the left atrium, then left ventricle pumps to the body (systemic) and returns to the right atrium. It keeps oxygenated and deoxygenated blood separate, supplying high energy needs of warm-blooded animals.
R: 2 | Pulmonary
R: 2 | Systemic
R: 2 | Separation
R: 2 | Need
## Advanced
P: Explain the structure of a nephron and the formation of urine.
A: A nephron has Bowman's capsule with glomerulus, a long tubule and collecting duct. Blood is filtered in the glomerulus; the tubule reabsorbs glucose, amino acids, salts and water; the remaining nitrogenous waste (urea) is urine, which passes ureter to bladder to urethra. Water reabsorption depends on how much excess water there is and the amount of dissolved waste.

R: 2 | Filtration
R: 2 | Reabsorption
R: 2 | Urine route
