# Heredity

@study
Heredity is the transmission of characters from parents to offspring and variation is difference between individuals. Gregor Mendel experimented on the garden pea (Pisum sativum) and found the laws: a monohybrid cross of tall (TT) and dwarf (tt) gives all tall F1 (Tt) and 3 tall : 1 dwarf in F2, with genotype ratio 1:2:1. Dihybrid cross of round yellow and wrinkled green seeds gives 9:3:3:1 in F2, showing independent assortment. Traits are controlled by genes on chromosomes; dominant traits show in F1. In humans there are 23 pairs of chromosomes, 22 pairs autosomes and one pair sex chromosomes (XX female, XY male); the father decides the sex of the child. Variation arises by errors in DNA copying and sexual reproduction, and speciation results from natural selection and genetic drift; evolution is shown by fossils, homologous and analogous organs.
## Deep dive: Mendel's experiments
Mendel chose pea for contrasting characters such as tall/dwarf, round/wrinkled seeds, yellow/green seeds, violet/white flowers. He took pure lines, cross-pollinated them and counted the offspring. In a monohybrid cross (TT x tt): F1 all Tt tall; F1 self gives F2 tall:dwarf = 3:1 and genotype TT:Tt:tt = 1:2:1. This showed that traits are inherited independently, with each having two factors; dominant factors express in the presence of recessive ones, and the recessive reappear in F2. In a dihybrid cross (RRYY x rryy): F1 all RrYy round yellow; F2: 9 round yellow : 3 round green : 3 wrinkled yellow : 1 wrinkled green, showing that characters are inherited independently (law of independent assortment).
## Deep dive: Genes and sex determination
A gene is a segment of DNA that codes for a protein. Each cell has genes in pairs, one from each parent. Sexual reproduction: half of the chromosomes come from each parent, restoring the number in the zygote. Sex chromosomes: women have XX, men XY. All eggs carry X; sperm carry X or Y. If an X sperm fertilises the egg: XX girl; if a Y sperm: XY boy. So the probability of a boy or girl is 50 per cent and the father's chromosome decides the sex.
## Deep dive: Variation and inheritance of acquired traits
Variation helps survival (e.g. green beetles on green bushes are eaten by crows; a red beetle variant gets eaten more). Acquired traits (such as a weight loss or cutting tails of mice) are not inherited because they do not change the DNA in germ cells. Only inherited traits in germ cells pass to offspring.
## Deep dive: Evolution
Evolution is gradual change in organisms over generations. Homologous organs have the same structure and origin but different functions (forelimbs of human, bat, whale); analogous organs have different origin but same function (wings of birds and insects). Fossils are preserved remains (Archaeopteryx links reptiles and birds). Fossil dating uses relative position or radioactive carbon-14. Darwin proposed natural selection. Speciation arises by geographical isolation, genetic drift and natural selection. Acquired and inherited traits are different. Human evolution: Africa is the origin; Homo sapiens migrated across the earth.

## Moderate
P: State Mendel's findings in a monohybrid cross between tall and dwarf pea plants.
A: F1 all tall (Tt), showing tall is dominant. F2 gives 3 tall : 1 dwarf; genotype ratio 1 TT : 2 Tt : 1 tt. Recessive trait reappears in F2.
R: 2 | F1 result
R: 2 | F2 ratio
R: 2 | Genotype ratio
## High
P: Explain how sex of a child is determined in humans.
A: Females have XX, males XY. All eggs carry X; sperm carry either X or Y. X sperm + egg gives XX girl; Y sperm + egg gives XY boy; the father's chromosome decides the sex and the chance is 50:50.
R: 2 | Chromosomes
R: 2 | Gametes
R: 2 | Outcomes
R: 2 | Father decides
## Advanced
P: Distinguish between acquired and inherited traits, and explain how variation and natural selection lead to speciation.
A: Inherited traits are in germ-cell DNA and pass to offspring; acquired traits arise in body cells in a lifetime and are not inherited. Variations arise from DNA copying errors and sexual reproduction; those suited to the environment survive and reproduce (natural selection); geographical isolation and genetic drift accumulate differences until populations cannot interbreed, forming a new species.
R: 2 | Traits
R: 2 | Variation
R: 2 | Selection
R: 2 | Speciation
