# Control and Coordination
minutes: 40
yt: class 10 science control and coordination
@study
Animals respond to stimuli through the nervous system and hormones; plants use hormones and tropisms. A neuron has dendrites, cell body, axon and nerve endings, passing impulses via synapses. The reflex arc (receptor, sensory neuron, spinal cord, motor neuron, effector) gives rapid involuntary response. The brain has forebrain (cerebrum), midbrain and hindbrain (cerebellum, pons, medulla). The brain is protected by the cranium and cerebrospinal fluid. Plant hormones: auxin, gibberellin, cytokinin (growth promoters), abscisic acid (inhibitor), ethylene (ripening). Animal hormones: thyroxine, insulin, growth hormone, adrenaline and sex hormones, controlled by feedback.
## Deep dive: Nervous system
Receptors in sense organs (eye, ear, nose, tongue, skin) detect stimuli; the information travels from the dendrite tip to cell body, as an electrical impulse to the axon end where a chemical is released to cross the gap (synapse) to the next neuron. Muscle cells respond by contraction, as effectors; glands respond by secretion. Reflex action: touching a hot object; spinal cord handles the response without the brain thinking, so it is quick. Reflex arc: receptor -> sensory neuron -> relay neuron in spinal cord -> motor neuron -> muscle. Brain: forebrain (cerebrum) for thinking, memory and voluntary action; hypothalamus for hunger; midbrain; hindbrain: cerebellum for posture and balance, medulla for involuntary actions like blood pressure and salivation, pons.
## Deep dive: Plant movement and hormones
Plants move without muscles: sensitive plant Mimosa pudica droops on touch because of changes in turgor. Tropisms: phototropism (towards light, shoot positive), geotropism (root positive), hydrotropism, chemotropism (pollen tube growth). Auxin accumulates on the shady side of the shoot making it longer so the shoot bends towards light. Gibberellins help stem growth; cytokinins promote cell division in fruits and seeds; abscisic acid inhibits growth and causes wilting of leaves; ethylene ripens fruits.
## Deep dive: Hormones in animals
Endocrine glands release hormones directly into blood: pituitary (master gland, growth hormone), thyroid (thyroxine regulates metabolism; needs iodine; deficiency causes goitre), pancreas (insulin lowers blood sugar; deficiency causes diabetes), adrenal (adrenaline for fight or flight), testes (testosterone), ovaries (oestrogen). Feedback: if sugar rises, insulin is released; when sugar is normal, secretion stops. Iodised salt prevents goitre. Dwarfism arises from too little growth hormone and gigantism from too much.
## Deep dive: Comparing systems
Nervous coordination is rapid, short-lived, uses electrical impulses and chemicals, and acts through nerves on specific organs. Hormonal coordination is slow, long-lasting, carried in blood and reaches every cell. Both together make a flexible and efficient control system.

## Moderate
P: What is a reflex action? Trace the path of a reflex arc.
A: A reflex action is a rapid, involuntary response to a stimulus. Path: receptor -> sensory neuron -> spinal cord (relay neuron) -> motor neuron -> effector (muscle).

## High
P: How does auxin make a shoot bend towards light?
A: Light makes auxin move to the shady side of the shoot tip. Auxin stimulates cell elongation there so the shady side grows more and the shoot bends towards light (positive phototropism).

## Advanced
P: Compare nervous and hormonal control and explain feedback regulation of blood sugar.
A: Nervous: nerves, electrical impulses, fast, short-lived, specific. Hormonal: blood, chemical, slow, long-lasting, widespread. Blood sugar: rise stimulates pancreas to release insulin which lets cells and liver take up sugar; when level falls insulin secretion decreases.
