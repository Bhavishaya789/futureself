**Magnetic effects:** field around a current carrying wire; motor, generator, electromagnetic induction.
## Deep dive: electricity
**Charge and current.** Current I is the rate of flow of charge: I = Q / t. One ampere is one coulomb per second. The charge of one electron is 1.6 x 10^-19 C, so 1 C is about 6.25 x 10^18 electrons. Conventional current flows from the positive to the negative terminal, opposite to the electron flow.
**Potential difference.** V = W / Q: the work done to move one coulomb between two points. Unit: volt (1 V = 1 J/C). A voltmeter is connected in parallel across a component; an ammeter is connected in series.
**Ohm's law.** At constant temperature V is proportional to I, so V = I R. The graph of V against I is a straight line through the origin whose slope is R. Resistance depends on length, area and material: R = rho L / A. Doubling the length doubles R; doubling the area halves R. Resistivity rho depends only on the material and temperature (metals: low, alloys: higher, insulators: very high).
**Series and parallel.** Series: the same current passes through every resistor, R = R1 + R2 + ... and the voltages add. Parallel: every resistor has the same voltage, 1/R = 1/R1 + 1/R2 + ... and the currents add; the combined resistance is smaller than the smallest. Household appliances are in parallel so each gets 220 V and works independently.
**Heating effect.** H = I^2 R t = V I t = V^2 t / R (Joule's law). Used in heaters, geysers, irons and fuses. A fuse is a thin wire of low melting point in series with the appliance that melts when the current is too high.
**Electric power and energy.** P = V I = I^2 R = V^2 / R, in watts. Energy E = P t. The commercial unit is the kilowatt hour: 1 kWh = 3.6 x 10^6 J = 1 unit of electricity.
## Deep dive: light
**Reflection.** The angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection; the incident ray, reflected ray and normal lie in one plane. A plane mirror forms a virtual, erect image the same size as the object, as far behind the mirror as the object is in front.
**Spherical mirrors.** Focal length f = R / 2. Sign convention (Cartesian): distances measured from the pole; in the direction of the incident light is positive, against it is negative. For a concave mirror f is negative; for a convex mirror f is positive. Mirror formula: 1/v + 1/u = 1/f. Magnification m = h'/h = -v/u. A concave mirror gives a real inverted image when the object is beyond f; with the object between pole and f it gives a virtual, erect, enlarged image (shaving mirror, dentist's mirror). A convex mirror always gives a virtual, erect, diminished image (rear-view mirrors).
**Refraction.** Light bends when it passes from one medium to another because its speed changes. Snell's law: sin i / sin r = n21 = constant. Absolute refractive index n = c / v with c = 3 x 10^8 m/s. Going into a denser medium the ray bends towards the normal. Glass slab: the emergent ray is parallel to the incident ray but shifted sideways.
**Lenses.** Lens formula: 1/v - 1/u = 1/f. Magnification m = v/u. Power P = 1/f with f in metres; the unit is the dioptre. Convex lens: f positive, converging, P positive. Concave lens: f negative, diverging, P negative. Lenses placed in contact add their powers: P = P1 + P2.
**Human eye.** Myopia (short sight) is corrected by a concave lens; hypermetropia (long sight) by a convex lens. Presbyopia comes with age and needs bifocal lenses. Dispersion by a prism splits white light into VIBGYOR; a rainbow is dispersion plus internal reflection in raindrops; the sky is blue because shorter wavelengths scatter most.

Q: Formula for electric power?
A: P = VI.
Q: What is electric current?
A: The rate of flow of electric charge: I = Q / t. Its unit is the ampere.
Q: Define one ampere.
A: One ampere is a flow of one coulomb of charge per second.
Q: Define potential difference.
A: The work done to move a unit charge between two points: V = W / Q. Unit: volt.
Q: State Ohm's law.
A: At constant temperature, the current through a conductor is directly proportional to the potential difference across it: V = I R.
Q: What does the slope of a V-I graph show?
A: The resistance R of the conductor.
Q: On what factors does the resistance of a wire depend?
A: Its length, area of cross-section, the material, and the temperature.
Q: What is resistivity?
A: The resistance of a wire of unit length and unit area of cross-section; it depends only on the material and temperature.
Q: Why are alloys used in heating elements?
A: Alloys have high resistivity and do not oxidise easily at high temperature.
Q: Why is tungsten used for bulb filaments?
A: It has a very high melting point (about 3380 C).
Q: How should an ammeter be connected?
A: In series with the component, and it should have very low resistance.
Q: How should a voltmeter be connected?
A: In parallel across the component, and it should have very high resistance.
Q: What is the effect of doubling the length of a wire on its resistance?
A: It doubles.
Q: What is the effect of doubling the radius of a wire on its resistance?
A: It becomes one quarter, since the area becomes four times larger.
Q: Why are household appliances connected in parallel?
A: Each gets the same 220 V, can be switched independently, and the total current is shared.
Q: What is the use of a fuse?
A: It protects circuits: its thin wire melts and breaks the circuit when the current is too high.
Q: State Joule's law of heating.
A: H = I^2 R t: heat produced is proportional to the square of the current, the resistance and the time.
Q: What is the SI unit of electric power?
A: The watt (1 W = 1 J/s).
Q: What is 1 kilowatt hour?
A: The energy used by a 1 kW appliance in 1 hour: 3.6 x 10^6 J.
Q: Which effect of current is used in an electric heater?
A: The heating effect.
Q: Why does the filament of a bulb glow but the connecting wires do not?
A: The filament has much higher resistance, so more heat is produced in it for the same current.
Q: Why do parallel resistors have a smaller combined resistance?
A: More paths are available for the current, so the total opposition falls below the smallest single resistance.
Q: What is a short circuit?
A: A path of almost zero resistance across the supply, causing a very large current and overheating.
Q: Why is the earth wire used?
A: It carries leakage current to the ground and protects the user from shock.
Q: What colour is the live wire in India?
A: Red (neutral is black, earth is green).
Q: What is the direction of conventional current?
A: From the positive terminal to the negative terminal outside the cell.
Q: State the law of reflection.
A: The angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection, and the incident ray, reflected ray and normal lie in the same plane.
Q: What are the properties of an image in a plane mirror?
A: Virtual, erect, same size, laterally inverted, as far behind the mirror as the object is in front.
Q: What is the relation between focal length and radius of curvature?
A: f = R / 2.
Q: State the mirror formula.
A: 1/v + 1/u = 1/f.
Q: What does a negative magnification mean?
A: The image is real and inverted.
Q: What is the sign of f for a concave mirror?
A: Negative, by the Cartesian sign convention.
Q: What is the sign of f for a convex mirror?
A: Positive.
Q: Which mirror is used as a rear-view mirror and why?
A: A convex mirror: it gives an erect, diminished image and a wider field of view.
Q: Which mirror is used by dentists and in shaving?
A: A concave mirror: with the object within the focal length it gives an erect, enlarged, virtual image.
Q: What is refraction?
A: The bending of light when it passes obliquely from one transparent medium to another because its speed changes.
Q: State Snell's law.
A: The ratio sin i / sin r is a constant for a given pair of media and light colour.
Q: Define absolute refractive index.
A: n = speed of light in vacuum / speed of light in the medium = c / v.
Q: Which medium is optically denser: water (n = 1.33) or glass (n = 1.5)?
A: Glass, because it has the higher refractive index.
Q: In which direction does light bend entering a denser medium?
A: Towards the normal.
Q: What happens to a ray passing through a rectangular glass slab?
A: It emerges parallel to the incident ray but shifted sideways (lateral displacement).
Q: State the lens formula.
A: 1/v - 1/u = 1/f.
Q: What is the power of a lens?
A: P = 1/f with f in metres; the unit is the dioptre (D).
Q: What is the sign of power for a convex lens?
A: Positive. A concave lens has negative power.
Q: How is myopia corrected?
A: With a concave lens of suitable power.
Q: How is hypermetropia corrected?
A: With a convex lens.
Q: What causes the blue colour of the sky?
A: Scattering of light: the shorter blue wavelengths scatter most by air molecules.
Q: Why does the Sun appear red at sunrise and sunset?
A: Light travels through more atmosphere, so blue is scattered away and red reaches the eye.
Q: How does a rainbow form?
A: By dispersion, refraction and internal reflection of sunlight in water droplets.
Q: What is dispersion?
A: The splitting of white light into its seven colours when passing through a prism.
Q: Which colour deviates most in a prism?
A: Violet; red deviates least.
