# Magnetic Effects of Electric Current
minutes: 40
yt: class 10 science magnetic effects of electric current
@study
Oersted found that a current-carrying conductor deflects a compass needle. Magnetic field lines emerge from the north pole and enter the south pole, never intersect, and are closer where the field is stronger. A straight wire has concentric circular fields given by the right-hand thumb rule; a solenoid has a field like a bar magnet; the strength depends on current, number of turns and core (iron). A current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field feels force, with direction by Fleming's left-hand rule (force, field, current). An electric motor converts electrical energy to mechanical energy using a split ring commutator. Electromagnetic induction (Faraday) produces induced current when flux changes; Fleming's right-hand rule gives its direction. A generator converts mechanical energy to electrical energy; AC reverses every half rotation (50 Hz in India, so reverses every 1/100 s); DC flows one way. Domestic circuits use live, neutral and earth wires with fuses and earthing.
## Deep dive: Magnetic field and field lines
A compass needle is a small magnet. Field strength is greater where lines are close. Properties: lines are closed curves; direction north to south outside the magnet; tangent gives direction; never cross. Right-hand thumb rule: curl fingers; thumb along current gives field direction on the fingers (or: grasp the wire with the right thumb in direction of current, fingers show field). A circular loop produces a field at the centre perpendicular to the plane; for N turns the field is N times stronger. Solenoid: coil of many circular turns; one end acts as north pole, other south; inside the field is uniform. An electromagnet is a soft iron core in a solenoid; a permanent magnet uses steel.
## Deep dive: Force on a conductor and motor
A conductor perpendicular to the field experiences maximum force F; reverses if current or field is reversed. Fleming's left-hand rule: stretch the thumb, forefinger and middle finger mutually perpendicular: forefinger = field, middle = current, thumb = force. Motor: a rectangular coil between magnets with split-ring commutator that reverses current direction every half rotation, brushes and an armature; uses: fans, mixers, washing machines. The commercial motor has electromagnet, a large number of turns of soft iron core.
## Deep dive: Electromagnetic induction
Faraday: moving a magnet in a coil or changing the field induces current in the coil; galvanometer deflects. Induced current is greater with fast motion, more turns and stronger magnet. Fleming's right-hand rule: forefinger = field, thumb = motion, middle = induced current. AC generator: a rotating coil in a magnetic field with slip rings and brushes; produces alternating current; DC generator uses a split ring commutator. AC advantages: easy to transmit over long distances without much loss by transformers.
## Deep dive: Domestic electric circuits
Mains: live wire (red, 220 V), neutral (black), earth (green). Earth wire connects the metal body to the ground. A fuse of the right rating protects against overloading; MCB trips. Short-circuiting happens when live and neutral touch. Overloading when too many appliances are connected to one socket. Safety: earthing, fuse, insulated wires, not touching switches with wet hands.

## Moderate
P: State Fleming's left-hand rule and one use of it.
A: Stretch thumb, forefinger and middle finger perpendicular: forefinger = magnetic field, middle finger = current, thumb = force on the conductor. It is used for finding the direction of rotation in an electric motor.

## High
P: Explain the working of an electric motor with the role of the split ring commutator.
A: A rectangular coil is placed in a field; current flows, and forces act in opposite directions on the two arms making it rotate. After half a turn the split ring commutator reverses the current so that the force keeps the same direction of rotation.

## Advanced
P: Describe electromagnetic induction and an AC generator. How does it differ from a DC generator?
A: Changing flux through a coil induces an EMF. In an AC generator a coil rotates in the field; slip rings and brushes give an alternating current reversing every half rotation (50 Hz). In a DC generator split rings convert it to a unidirectional current.
