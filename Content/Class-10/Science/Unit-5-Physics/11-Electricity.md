@study
Electric current I = Q/t in ampere (A); potential difference V = W/Q in volt (V); resistance R = V/I in ohm. Ohm's law: V = IR at constant temperature. Resistance R = rho x l/A; resistivity depends on the material and temperature. In series: Rs = R1 + R2 + R3, same current; in parallel: 1/Rp = 1/R1 + 1/R2 + ..., same voltage. Heating effect H = I^2 R t (Joule's law). Electric power P = VI = I^2R = V^2/R in watt; energy in kWh (1 kWh = 3.6 x 10^6 J). Ammeter in series and voltmeter in parallel. Fuse protects circuits, and alloys like nichrome are used for heaters because of high resistivity and melting point.
## Deep dive: Current, potential difference and Ohm's law
Charge of one electron is 1.6 x 10^-19 C; 1 A is 1 C flowing per second, so number of electrons in 1 C is about 6.25 x 10^18. Conventional current flows from positive to negative while electrons move the other way. V = IR, giving a straight V-I graph; the slope is resistance. Ohmic conductors obey the law; filament lamps do not at high temperatures. Rheostat is a variable resistor; a key controls the circuit; the battery (cell combination) provides the potential difference.
## Deep dive: Resistance and combinations
R = rho l/A: resistance increases with length and decreases with area of cross-section. Resistivity of metals and alloys is low (silver 1.6 x 10^-8 ohm m, copper 1.62 x 10^-8) while insulators like rubber have 10^12-10^17. Alloys are used in heating devices since resistivity is high and they do not oxidise easily. Series: the same current flows, total resistance increases, voltage divides; the failure of one stops the whole circuit. Parallel: the potential difference is the same, total resistance is less than the smallest; if one fails the others work; household circuits are parallel.
## Deep dive: Heating effect and power
Heat produced H = I^2 R t = VIt = V^2 t/R. Applications: heaters, toasters, irons, electric bulb (tungsten with high melting point filled with inert gas nitrogen and argon), and fuse (low melting point wire of tin-lead alloy). Power P = VI. 1 watt = 1 volt x 1 ampere. Commercial unit: 1 kWh = 1 unit = 3.6 x 10^6 J. Energy = power x time. For example a 100 W bulb in 10 h uses 1 kWh.
## Deep dive: Circuits and safety
Household supply is 220 V, 50 Hz AC. Live wire (red), neutral wire (black), earth (green) in three-pin plug; earthing prevents shocks. Overloading and short-circuiting cause too much current; MCBs and fuses protect. Measurements: ammeter is connected in series with low resistance; voltmeter in parallel with high resistance. Internal resistance and EMF are beyond the Class 10 scope.

## Moderate
P: A current of 2 A flows through a 6 ohm resistor for 10 s. Find the potential difference and the heat produced.
A: V = IR = 2 x 6 = 12 V. H = I^2 R t = 4 x 6 x 10 = 240 J.

R: 2 | H = 240 J
## High
P: Three resistors of 2, 3 and 6 ohm are connected in parallel across 12 V. Find the total resistance and the total current.
A: 1/R = 1/2 + 1/3 + 1/6 = 1, so R = 1 ohm. I = V/R = 12 A.
R: 2 | Parallel formula
R: 2 | R = 1 ohm
R: 2 | I = 12 A
## Advanced
P: An electric heater of 2 kW is used for 5 hours daily for 30 days. Find the energy consumed and bill at Rs 6 per unit. Why is nichrome used for heating elements?
A: Energy = 2 x 5 x 30 = 300 kWh. Bill = 300 x 6 = Rs 1,800. Nichrome has high resistivity, a high melting point and does not oxidise at high temperature.
R: 2 | Energy 300 kWh
R: 2 | Cost Rs 1,800
