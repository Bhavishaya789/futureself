# The Human Eye and the Colourful World
minutes: 40
yt: class 10 science human eye and colourful world
@study
The human eye forms real, inverted images on the retina, with the cornea and lens refracting light; the iris controls the pupil size; ciliary muscles change the focal length of the lens (accommodation). The near point is 25 cm and the far point is infinity. Defects: myopia (far point nearer; corrected by concave lens), hypermetropia (near point farther; convex lens), presbyopia (old age; bifocal) and cataract (cloudy lens; surgery). Dispersion splits white light into VIBGYOR through a prism. Atmospheric refraction causes twinkling, advance sunrise and delayed sunset. Scattering explains blue sky, red sun at sunrise and sunset (Rayleigh scattering) and the Tyndall effect. A rainbow combines refraction, dispersion and total internal reflection in water drops.
## Deep dive: Structure and function
Light enters through the cornea, passes the aqueous humour, pupil and lens and forms an image on the retina with rods (dim light) and cones (colour). The optic nerve carries electrical signals to the brain. The iris controls pupil size. Ciliary muscles relax to flatten the lens for distant objects and contract to thicken it for near objects: this is accommodation. A normal eye sees objects between 25 cm (near point) and infinity (far point). Persistence of vision is about 1/16 s. Two eyes give a wider field of view and detect faint objects. Eye donation: cornea transplant helps blind people.
## Deep dive: Defects and correction
Myopia: distant objects blurred because the image forms in front of the retina due to too long an eyeball or too much curvature; correct with a concave lens. Hypermetropia: near objects blurred; image forms behind the retina; correct with a convex lens. Presbyopia: weakening of ciliary muscles in old age, requires bifocal lens (concave upper, convex lower). Cataract: cloudy lens, treated by surgery. Power of corrective lens P = 1/f, where f equals the far point distance (negative) for myopia.
## Deep dive: Dispersion and the atmosphere
Newton showed a glass prism splits white light into VIBGYOR (violet, indigo, blue, green, yellow, orange, red); red deviates least and violet most. A second inverted prism recombines the spectrum. Rainbow: sunlight enters water droplets, undergoes refraction and dispersion, internal reflection, and refraction again; seen with the Sun behind the observer. Twinkling of stars is due to atmospheric refraction; planets do not twinkle since they are extended sources. Advance sunrise and delayed sunset by about 2 minutes.
## Deep dive: Scattering of light
Tyndall effect is scattering of light by colloidal particles, as sunlight through a canopy of trees or a beam in a dusty room. Rayleigh scattering is inversely proportional to the fourth power of wavelength, so blue scatters more; the sky looks blue. At sunrise and sunset the light travels through a thicker atmosphere so most of the blue is scattered and red reaches us. The sky is dark to astronauts as there is no atmosphere. Danger signals use red since it is scattered least.

## Moderate
P: What is myopia? Explain how it is corrected.
A: Myopia is short-sightedness where distant objects look blurred because the image forms in front of the retina. It is corrected by a concave lens of suitable power which diverges rays so the image forms on the retina.

## High
P: Why is the sky blue and the sun red at sunrise?
A: Blue light has a shorter wavelength and is scattered more by atmospheric particles (Rayleigh), so we see a blue sky. At sunrise and sunset light travels a longer path, blue is scattered away and red reaches the eye.

## Advanced
P: A myopic person has a far point at 80 cm. Find the power of the lens needed.
A: The lens must form a virtual image of infinity-object at 80 cm: u = infinity, v = -80 cm, so f = -80 cm = -0.8 m and P = 1/f = -1.25 D (concave lens).
