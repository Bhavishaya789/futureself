# Outcomes of Democracy
minutes: 35
yt: class 10 political science outcomes of democracy
@study
How do we assess democracy's outcomes? Democracy is better than other forms of government because it promotes equality among citizens, enhances the dignity of the individual, improves the quality of decision making, provides a method to resolve conflicts, and allows room to correct mistakes. A democratic government is accountable, responsive and legitimate. Democracy produces a government that follows procedures and is accountable to the people, but it does not guarantee faster economic growth; it does reduce inequality in some respects, though economic inequality persists. Democracy accommodates social diversity and is legitimate because it is people's own. Challenges remain in poverty, inequality, and need for more participation.
## Deep dive: Accountable, responsive and legitimate government
Democracy is based on the idea of deliberation and negotiation, so decisions are slower but follow norms and procedures. Citizens have the right to know whether decisions followed procedure, and to see the working of the government (right to information, 2005). A democratic government is accountable to the citizens and responsive to the needs and expectations of citizens. Elections allow citizens to throw out leaders. Even if democracy does not always give the best decisions, it is a legitimate government: it may be slow and inefficient but it is people's own government, which is why there is a continuing support for it all over the world.
## Deep dive: Economic growth and inequality
Between 1950 and 2000, dictatorships had slightly higher economic growth rates (4.42 per cent) than democracies (3.95 per cent); but the difference between rich and poor democracies is large, as is the difference among dictatorships. So democracy is not guaranteed to deliver growth; the economic performance depends on population growth, global situation, cooperation from other countries and the economic priorities adopted. Democracies are not necessarily better at reducing economic disparities: in South Africa, the white minority holds most wealth even though democracy arrived in 1994; in India the top 20 per cent holds a larger share of the national income than the bottom 20 per cent, and poverty persists, with the poor contributing a large share of votes in India. Some democratic countries including Brazil show sharp inequalities.
## Deep dive: Social diversity and dignity
Democracies usually develop a procedure to conduct their competition, which reduces the possibility of tension becoming explosive or violent. Belgium and Sri Lanka examples show success where the majority listens to the minority; Yugoslavia, which broke up violently, shows the danger of a lack of accommodation. Democracy stands much superior to any other form of government in promoting dignity and freedom of the individual, as all citizens are equal. The achievement is the recognition of the principle of equality, though in practice caste and gender discrimination persist; Dalits and women have fought for a dignified place.
## Deep dive: Expectations from democracy
Democracy is a better form because it improves the quality of decisions through consultation and discussion, provides a method to deal with differences and conflicts, and enhances dignity. It allows room to correct mistakes because the rulers can be changed. Democracy is better judged by its outcomes in comparison with other systems, and by citizens' own expectations. The people want democracies to produce better outcomes, so complaints are a sign of the success of democracy: it has taught citizens to question and not be satisfied. Democracy is a continuing project.

## Moderate
P: Why is democracy considered a legitimate form of government?
A: Because it is people's own government, based on their consent and elections; even if it is slow and imperfect, people feel it is theirs and support it.

## High
P: Does democracy lead to economic development? Discuss with evidence.
A: Dictatorships had slightly higher growth (4.42 per cent) than democracies (3.95 per cent) in 1950-2000, but poor democracies and poor dictatorships differ greatly. Growth depends on population growth, global conditions, priorities, so democracy does not guarantee growth.

## Advanced
P: How far does democracy reduce inequality and promote dignity? Support your answer.
A: Democracy does not eliminate economic inequality: top 20 per cent income share is far higher than the bottom 20 per cent in India; South Africa has white minority wealth. But it recognises equality and dignity through equal vote, laws against discrimination and movements by dalits and women. Complaints show citizens have learned to question.
