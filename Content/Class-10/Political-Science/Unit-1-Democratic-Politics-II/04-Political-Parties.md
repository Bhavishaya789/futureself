# Political Parties
minutes: 35
yt: class 10 political science political parties
@study
A political party is a group of people who come together to contest elections and hold power in the government. Parties have three components: the leaders, the active members and the followers. Their functions are contesting elections, putting forward policies and programmes, making laws, forming and running governments, playing the role of opposition, shaping public opinion and providing access to government machinery. Party systems can be one-party, two-party or multi-party; India has a multi-party system with national and state parties recognised by the Election Commission. Challenges: lack of internal democracy, dynastic succession, money and muscle power, and meaningful choice. Remedies: anti-defection law (2003 amendment), affidavits, mandatory organisational elections and state funding of elections.
## Deep dive: Functions of parties
Parties contest elections, in most democracies election is fought mainly among candidates put up by political parties. They put forward policies and programmes, and voters choose from them. Parties play a decisive role in making laws; though legislators decide, they are bound by the party line. Parties form and run governments. Parties not in power play the role of opposition, criticising the government for failures and wrong policies. Parties shape public opinion by raising and highlighting issues, and have lakhs of members and activists. Parties provide people access to government machinery and welfare schemes.
## Deep dive: Necessity of parties
Without parties, every candidate in the elections would be independent, so no one would be responsible for how the country is run and no promises about major policies could be made. Parties are a political institution that gets people together to agree on some policies and programmes for the society, and is therefore necessary for representative democracy in large, complex societies.
## Deep dive: Party systems and national parties
Where one party is allowed to control and run the government, it is a one-party system, e.g. China. In some countries power usually changes between two main parties, a two-party system, e.g. the USA and the UK. If several parties compete for power and more than two have a reasonable chance, it is a multi-party system; when a number of parties form an alliance it is called a coalition (e.g. NDA, UPA). India has a multiparty system. A party that secures at least six per cent of the total votes in Lok Sabha or assembly elections in four states and wins at least four seats in Lok Sabha is recognised as a national party. A state party must secure at least six per cent of votes in the state assembly and win at least two seats. Examples of national parties include INC (1885), BJP (1980), BSP (1984), CPI(M) (1964), AAP, and others.
## Deep dive: Challenges and reforms
Challenges: lack of internal democracy (power concentrated in a few leaders, no organisational elections), dynastic succession, growing role of money and muscle power, and not offering a meaningful choice to voters as the parties are similar. Reforms: the Constitution was amended to prevent elected legislators from changing parties (anti-defection law, 91st Amendment 2003 to limit the size of ministries and tighten defection rules); the Supreme Court ordered all candidates to file affidavits on property and criminal cases; the Election Commission made it mandatory for parties to hold organisational elections and file income tax returns. Suggestions: law to regulate internal affairs, quota for women (one third), state funding of elections, and public pressure.

## Moderate
P: What are the three components of a political party?
A: The leaders, the active members and the followers. Leaders lead and decide, active members work in the party, and followers support it by voting.

## High
P: Explain any four functions of a political party.
A: Contest elections with candidates; put forward policies and programmes; make laws and form governments; play the role of opposition; shape public opinion; provide access to government schemes and machinery.

## Advanced
P: What are the challenges faced by political parties in India, and how can they be reformed?
A: Challenges: lack of internal democracy, dynastic succession, money and muscle power, no meaningful choice. Reforms: anti-defection law, affidavits of assets and cases, compulsory organisational elections and tax returns, quota for women, state funding of elections and public pressure through citizens.
