# Power-sharing
minutes: 35
yt: class 10 political science power sharing
@study
Power-sharing is the idea that power should be divided among different organs of government and social groups so that no one holds all of it. Belgium and Sri Lanka show two contrasting stories. Belgium accommodated the Dutch-speaking, French-speaking and German-speaking communities through a community government and equal representation, while Sri Lanka's majoritarian measures such as the 1956 Sinhala Only Act deepened Tamil alienation and led to civil war. Power-sharing is good because it reduces conflict between social groups (prudential reason) and because it is the very spirit of democracy (moral reason). It takes four forms: horizontal, vertical, between social groups, and between political parties and movements.
## Deep dive: Belgium
Belgium is a small European country with 59 per cent Dutch-speaking people in the Flemish region, 40 per cent French-speaking in Wallonia, and one per cent German-speaking. In the capital Brussels, 80 per cent speak French and 20 per cent Dutch. The Dutch-speaking community was economically poorer earlier, which caused tension. The Belgian leaders amended the constitution four times from 1970 to 1993: equal number of Dutch and French-speaking ministers in the central government, state governments not subordinate to the central government, Brussels with a separate government where both communities have equal representation, and a separate community government for each language community with power over cultural, educational and language issues.
## Deep dive: Sri Lanka
Sri Lanka became independent in 1948. The major community is Sinhala-speakers (74 per cent), Tamil-speakers are 18 per cent, including Sri Lankan Tamils (13 per cent) in the north and east and Indian Tamils (5 per cent) of the plantation areas; Muslims are about 7 per cent. In 1956 an Act recognised Sinhala as the only official language, disregarding Tamil. Preference was given to Sinhala applicants for jobs and Buddhism was given state protection. The Tamils felt discriminated against, the relations turned bitter, and a civil war broke out in the 1980s, with the LTTE demanding Tamil Eelam. The war ended in 2009.
## Deep dive: Forms of power-sharing
Horizontal power-sharing spreads power among legislature, executive and judiciary, so each checks the others (checks and balances). Vertical power-sharing divides power among governments at different levels: the union, state and local government, called federal division. Power can also be shared among social groups, e.g. community government in Belgium and reserved constituencies for SC and ST in India. Finally power is shared among political parties, pressure groups and movements which compete for power, as in coalition governments.
## Deep dive: Why power-sharing is desirable
The prudential reason: it helps reduce the possibility of conflict between social groups, and since social conflict leads to violence and political instability, power-sharing ensures stability of the political order. Imposing the will of the majority may look attractive in the short run but undermines the unity of the nation in the long run. The moral reason: power-sharing is the very spirit of democracy because people have a right to be consulted on how they are to be governed. A legitimate government is one where citizens acquire a stake in the system.

## Moderate
P: Distinguish between the prudential and moral reasons for power-sharing.
A: Prudential: power-sharing reduces conflict between social groups and so ensures political stability and unity. Moral: power-sharing is the spirit of democracy; people have a right to be consulted in how they are governed, giving the government legitimacy.

## High
P: Explain the steps taken by Belgium to accommodate regional and linguistic differences.
A: Belgium amended its constitution four times between 1970 and 1993. Dutch and French-speaking ministers are equal in the central government; state governments are not subordinate to the centre; Brussels has a separate government with equal representation; community governments exist for each language group with control over culture, education and language. This reduced the conflict.

## Advanced
P: Compare the approaches of Belgium and Sri Lanka to ethnic diversity and evaluate which is more democratic.
A: Belgium shared power with Dutch, French and German communities through community governments and equal representation, giving each a stake and keeping the country united. Sri Lanka imposed Sinhala as the only official language in 1956, favoured Sinhalas in jobs and gave state protection to Buddhism, which alienated Tamils, led to civil war from the 1980s and lasting damage. Belgium is more democratic as it respects minority rights and consultation; majoritarianism undermines national unity.
