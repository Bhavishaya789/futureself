# Gender, Religion and Caste
minutes: 35
yt: class 10 political science gender religion caste
@study
Social differences of gender, religion and caste affect Indian politics. Gender: the sexual division of labour keeps women in the private sphere; women's movements demanded the vote and equal pay; women's representation in Lok Sabha is below 15 per cent. Reserved seats for women exist only in local bodies (one third). Religion: communalism treats religion as the basis of the nation and politics; secular India has no state religion. Caste: caste inequalities persist but caste politics also helped dalits and OBCs gain voice; elections are not decided only by caste. Politics should not be divided on these lines, but a healthy democracy can use social differences to bring about equality.
## Deep dive: Gender and politics
Public/private division: women do most of the housework but it is unpaid and unrecognised. Women's movements in many countries demanded the right to vote and equality in education and careers. Sex ratio in India fell to 940 (1,000 men) in 2011 census; literacy of women is 54 per cent vs 76 per cent for men in 2011; the average woman earns less for equal work. Violence against women is widespread. In India, women are only 14.4 per cent of members in the Lok Sabha and less than 5 per cent in state assemblies. In Scandinavian countries like Sweden, Norway and Finland the participation of women in public life is very high. A bill for one third reservation in the Lok Sabha and assemblies was pending for years and was passed in 2023 as the Women's Reservation Act.
## Deep dive: Religion and politics
Gandhi said that religion can never be separated from politics, meaning ethical values drawn from religion should guide politics. Communalism means using religion in politics: one religion's followers constitute one community, interests are the same, and different religious communities cannot live as equal citizens. Forms include everyday beliefs and stereotypes, seeking majority dominance, political mobilisation on religious lines, and at the extreme violence, riots and massacre such as Partition in 1947. A secular state does not have an official religion, grants freedom to practise any faith, prohibits discrimination on religion, and permits the state to intervene to ensure equality within communities, e.g. banning untouchability.
## Deep dive: Caste and politics
Caste inequalities persist: earlier, castes were hierarchically ordered with occupations fixed, and the upper castes were ahead in literacy and income. Social and religious reformers like Jyotiba Phule, Gandhiji, B. R. Ambedkar and Periyar Ramaswami Naicker worked to establish a society free of caste. Economic development, urbanisation, literacy, occupational mobility and the weakening of landlords have reduced the hold of caste. Caste in politics: parties choose candidates and make appeals keeping caste in mind, and universal adult franchise gave a voice to dalits and backward classes. But no parliamentary constituency has a clear majority of one caste, and no party wins all the votes of a community, so caste does not decide everything. Caste politics can also lead to tension and violence and may divert attention from issues like development and poverty.
## Deep dive: Why differences matter
Social differences take different political forms. When overlapping social differences are cumulative, as with black and poor in the US, conflict is likelier; when cross-cutting, as in Northern Ireland where religion and class differ across groups, conflict is easier to handle. The politics of social divisions depends on how people perceive their identities, how political leaders raise the demands, and how the government responds. Democracy works best when demands are within the constitutional framework and do not threaten national unity.

P: What is communalism? Mention any two ways in which it appears.
A: Communalism is using religion as the basis of politics, believing that followers of one religion form one community with common interests. Forms: everyday stereotypes and majority dominance beliefs, political mobilisation along religious lines, violence and riots.

## High
P: Explain how caste influences politics in India. Is it the only factor?
A: Parties choose candidates by caste and appeal to caste sentiment; leaders mobilise caste groups and universal franchise gave voice to dalits and OBCs. But it is not the only factor: no constituency has a majority of one caste, voters consider performance, voters of a caste vote for different parties and many candidates lose despite caste appeal.

## Advanced
P: Discuss the status of women in India and what measures have been taken to improve their political participation.
A: Women face the sexual division of labour, lower literacy and wages, a falling sex ratio and violence. Their share in legislatures is low. One third seats are reserved in panchayats and municipalities since 1992 and the Women's Reservation Act 2023 reserves one third in Lok Sabha and assemblies. Further needs are enforcement of equal wages, education and safety.
