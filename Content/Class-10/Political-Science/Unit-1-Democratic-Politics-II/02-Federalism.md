# Federalism
minutes: 35
yt: class 10 political science federalism
@study
Federalism is a system of government in which power is divided between a central authority and various constituent units of the country. It has two or more levels of government, each with its own jurisdiction, whose existence and authority are guaranteed by the constitution, which cannot be unilaterally changed by one level. Courts act as umpires in disputes. India has a three-tier structure: Union, States and local governments. The Constitution has a Union List (97 subjects), State List (66) and Concurrent List (47). Federalism is practised by coming together (USA, Switzerland, Australia) or holding together (India, Spain, Belgium). Linguistic states, language policy, centre-state relations and decentralisation through the 73rd and 74th Amendments (1992) strengthen Indian federalism.
## Deep dive: Features of federalism
There are two or more levels of government. Different tiers govern the same citizens but have their own jurisdiction in specific matters of legislation, taxation and administration. The jurisdictions of the respective levels are specified in the constitution, so their existence and authority are constitutionally guaranteed. Fundamental provisions of the constitution cannot be unilaterally changed by one level of government; such changes require the consent of both. Courts have the power to interpret the constitution and the powers of different levels. Sources of revenue for each level are clearly specified. The dual objectives are to safeguard and promote unity of the country while accommodating regional diversity.
## Deep dive: Coming together and holding together
In coming together federations, independent states come together on their own to form a bigger unit to pool their sovereignty and retain identity, e.g. USA, Switzerland and Australia; all states usually have equal power. In holding together federations, a large country decides to divide its power between the national government and states, e.g. India, Spain and Belgium; the central government is usually more powerful and different constituent units may have unequal powers, as in India where some states have special status.
## Deep dive: Division of powers in India
The Union List includes subjects of national importance such as defence, foreign affairs, banking, communications and currency, on which only the Union can legislate. The State List includes police, trade, commerce, agriculture and irrigation, on which states legislate. The Concurrent List includes education, forests, trade unions, marriage, adoption and succession, where both can legislate, and if there is a conflict the Union law prevails. Residuary subjects, those not in any list such as computer software, are with the Union. Not all states have identical powers: Jammu and Kashmir had special status under Article 370 till 2019, and Union Territories such as Chandigarh are administered by the Centre.
## Deep dive: How federalism is practised
Linguistic states: from 1950 several old states were reorganised on linguistic lines, the State Reorganisation Commission (1956) producing 14 states and 6 Union Territories. Language policy: Hindi is the official language but spoken by only about 40 per cent; there are 22 scheduled languages and English is also used; no language was declared national. Centre-state relations: after 1990, regional parties and coalition governments strengthened federalism and Article 356 was used less after the S. R. Bommai case (1994). Decentralisation: the 73rd and 74th Amendments (1992) made local governments third tier; elections are regular, reservation of at least one third for women, and State Election Commissions conduct elections; Gram Sabha is the foundation.

## Advanced
P: Explain how decentralisation strengthened democracy in India after 1992.
A: 73rd and 74th Amendments made local governments a constitutional third tier; regular elections by State Election Commissions, reservation for SC, ST, OBC and one third for women, devolution of powers and resources, Gram Sabha participation, bringing decisions closer to people, though states often don't transfer enough funds.
