# Surface areas and volumes
minutes: 35
yt: class 10 surface areas and volumes
@study
Combine solids such as cone on cylinder, hemisphere on cylinder, and find the frustum of a cone. Curved surface area of cylinder 2 pi r h, cone pi r l, sphere 4 pi r^2, hemisphere 2 pi r^2. Volumes: cylinder pi r^2 h, cone one-third pi r^2 h, sphere (4/3) pi r^3, hemisphere (2/3) pi r^3.
## Deep dive: standard solids
Cylinder (radius r, height h): CSA 2 pi r h, TSA 2 pi r (r + h), volume pi r^2 h. Cone: slant height l = sqrt (r^2 + h^2), CSA pi r l, TSA pi r (l + r), volume (1/3) pi r^2 h. Sphere: surface 4 pi r^2, volume (4/3) pi r^3. Hemisphere: CSA 2 pi r^2, TSA 3 pi r^2, volume (2/3) pi r^3.
## Deep dive: combination of solids
For a toy made of a cone on a hemisphere, or a tent made of a cylinder and a cone, find the total surface area by adding the curved surfaces only for the joined parts (the circular faces that are joined are hidden). Total volume is the sum of volumes. For a capsule (cylinder with two hemispheres): TSA = 2 pi r h + 4 pi r^2 and volume = pi r^2 h + (4/3) pi r^3.
## Deep dive: conversion of solids and frustum
When a solid is melted and recast into another, the volume stays the same: a sphere of radius R recast into n small spheres of radius r gives n = R^3/r^3. For a frustum of a cone with radii r1 > r2 and height h: slant height l = sqrt (h^2 + (r1 - r2)^2); volume (1/3) pi h (r1^2 + r2^2 + r1 r2); CSA pi (r1 + r2) l; TSA = CSA + pi r1^2 + pi r2^2.
**Common mistakes:** using height instead of slant height in CSA of a cone, forgetting the joined faces are not exposed, and mixing units (cm to m means cubic factors).
