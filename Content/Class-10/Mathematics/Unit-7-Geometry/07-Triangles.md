Two figures with the same shape but not necessarily the same size are similar. Two triangles are similar if corresponding angles are equal and corresponding sides are in the same ratio. The Basic Proportionality Theorem (Thales) says that a line parallel to one side of a triangle divides the other two sides in the same ratio. The criteria AA, SSS and SAS prove similarity. The areas of similar triangles are in the ratio of the squares of corresponding sides. The Pythagoras theorem says that in a right triangle the square of the hypotenuse equals the sum of the squares of the other two sides.
## Deep dive: Basic Proportionality Theorem
If DE is parallel to BC in triangle ABC with D on AB and E on AC, then AD/DB = AE/EC. The converse is also true: if a line divides two sides in the same ratio, it is parallel to the third side. Use it to find unknown lengths: write the ratio of the segments on one side and equate it to the matching ratio on the other.
## Deep dive: Criteria for similarity
AA: two equal angles are enough, because the third is then equal. SSS: all three side ratios are equal. SAS: one angle equal and the including sides in the same ratio. Always match vertices in order, write the similarity as triangle ABC ~ triangle PQR, and then the corresponding sides are AB/PQ = BC/QR = CA/RP.
## Deep dive: Areas of similar triangles
If triangle ABC ~ triangle PQR then area(ABC)/area(PQR) = (AB/PQ)^2 = (BC/QR)^2 = (CA/RP)^2. The ratio of the areas is the square of the ratio of sides, and the ratio of perimeters, medians, altitudes and angle bisectors equals the ratio of sides. A scale factor k on lengths gives k squared on areas.
## Deep dive: Pythagoras theorem
In a right triangle with hypotenuse c and other sides a and b, a^2 + b^2 = c^2. The converse says that if the square of the longest side equals the sum of the squares of the other two, the angle opposite the longest side is a right angle. Common triples are 3-4-5, 5-12-13, 8-15-17 and 7-24-25. An altitude on the hypotenuse makes two triangles similar to the whole.

Q: State and prove the Basic Proportionality Theorem.
A: If DE || BC in triangle ABC, then AD/DB = AE/EC. Proof: join BE and CD. ar(ADE)/ar(BDE) = AD/DB (same height), ar(ADE)/ar(CDE) = AE/EC (same height), and ar(BDE) = ar(CDE) as they lie on the same base DE between parallels. Hence AD/DB = AE/EC.
Q: State and prove the converse of BPT.
A: If AD/DB = AE/EC, then DE || BC. Suppose not and draw DE' || BC meeting AC at E'. By BPT AD/DB = AE'/E'C, so AE/EC = AE'/E'C, which makes E and E' coincide, a contradiction. So DE || BC.
Q: How do you prove the AA criterion?
A: Take triangles ABC and DEF with angle A = angle D and angle B = angle E. Cut DP = AB and DQ = AC on DE and DF. Then triangle ABC is congruent to DPQ by SAS and PQ is parallel to EF, so DP/DE = DQ/DF by BPT, giving AB/DE = AC/DF. Equal angles then give the third ratio.
Q: Prove that the ratio of areas of two similar triangles is the square of the ratio of their sides.
A: Draw altitudes AM and PN in similar triangles ABC and PQR. ar(ABC)/ar(PQR) = (BC x AM)/(QR x PN). Triangles ABM and PQN are similar by AA, so AM/PN = AB/PQ = BC/QR. Substituting gives (BC/QR)^2.
Q: Prove the Pythagoras theorem using similar triangles.
A: In right triangle ABC with right angle at B, draw BD perpendicular to AC. Triangles ADB, BDC and ABC are similar. From ADB ~ ABC, AB^2 = AD x AC. From BDC ~ ABC, BC^2 = DC x AC. Adding gives AB^2 + BC^2 = AC(AD + DC) = AC^2.
Q: Prove the converse of the Pythagoras theorem.
A: Given AC^2 = AB^2 + BC^2, construct a right triangle PQR with PQ = AB and QR = BC and right angle at Q. Then PR^2 = AB^2 + BC^2 = AC^2, so PR = AC. Triangles ABC and PQR are congruent by SSS, so angle B = 90 degrees.
Q: What is the difference between similar and congruent triangles?
A: Congruent triangles have equal corresponding sides and angles. Similar triangles have equal angles and proportional sides. Every congruent pair is similar but not the reverse.
Q: What are the common mistakes in similarity problems?
A: Matching vertices wrongly, using the ratio of sides as ratio of areas, forgetting to square or take the square root, and applying Pythagoras to a triangle that is not right-angled.
Q: How do you decide which side is the hypotenuse?
A: It is the side opposite the right angle and is always the longest. In the converse test, square the longest side and compare it with the sum of squares of the other two.
Q: If two triangles have equal angles, are their sides equal?
A: No. Equal angles only prove similarity (AA). Sides are proportional, and they are equal only when the scale factor is 1.
Q: What is the ratio of medians of similar triangles?
A: It equals the ratio of the corresponding sides, as do altitudes, angle bisectors and perimeters.
Q: What is the midpoint theorem and how does it follow from BPT?
A: The line joining the midpoints of two sides is parallel to the third side and half of it. With AD/DB = AE/EC = 1 the converse of BPT gives DE || BC and similar triangles give DE = BC/2.

## Moderate
P: In triangle ABC, DE is parallel to BC with D on AB and E on AC. If AD = 4 cm, DB = 6 cm and AE = 6 cm, find EC.
A: AD/DB = AE/EC, so 4/6 = 6/EC and EC = 6 x 6 / 4 = 9 cm.

## High
P: Two similar triangles have areas 81 cm^2 and 144 cm^2. If a side of the smaller triangle is 6 cm, find the corresponding side of the larger one.
A: Area ratio = 81/144 = (6/x)^2. So 6/x = 9/12 = 3/4 and x = 8 cm.

## Advanced
P: Prove that if a line is drawn parallel to one side of a triangle to intersect the other two sides in distinct points, the other two sides are divided in the same ratio.
A: In triangle ABC, DE || BC. Join BE and CD. Triangles ADE and BDE share the same height from E, so ar(ADE)/ar(BDE) = AD/DB. Triangles ADE and CDE share the height from D, so ar(ADE)/ar(CDE) = AE/EC. Triangles BDE and CDE are on the same base DE and between the same parallels, so ar(BDE) = ar(CDE). Hence AD/DB = AE/EC.
R: 2 | Construction: join BE and CD
R: 2 | Area ratios with common heights
R: 2 | Equal areas between parallels
R: 2 | Conclusion
