# Glimpses of India
minutes: 30
yt: class 10 english glimpses of india
@study
This chapter has three parts. "A Baker from Goa" by Lucio Rodrigues describes the Goan baker, or pader, whose bread, bol and other items remain an essential part of Goan life even after the Portuguese left; the baker comes with a bamboo staff to announce his arrival, wears a kabai, and bakes bread in a furnace. "Coorg" describes the beautiful hill district of Karnataka, its coffee plantations, brave Kodava people who are said to be descended from Arabs or Kurds, and the Kaveri river. "Tea from Assam" follows two friends, Rajvir and Pranjol, on a train to Assam; Pranjol's father is a tea planter, and the boys see vast tea gardens and learn the legend that tea was discovered by a Chinese emperor and its Indian origin in Assam.
## Deep dive: A Baker from Goa
The pader and his family are a living link to Goa's Portuguese past. Bread is eaten daily and bol is a gift for marriages. The baker visits twice a day, collects payment at month end and is a part of every household.
## Deep dive: Coorg
Coorg is a hill district known for its coffee, spices, rivers and the brave Kodavas. It has given India top army officers. Tourists enjoy trekking, rafting and misty hills.
## Deep dive: Tea from Assam
Rajvir and Pranjol take a train to Assam. Mr Barua's estate shows large tea gardens; tea is plucked by workers. The legend says tea was discovered when leaves fell into hot water of a Chinese emperor.
## Deep dive: unity
All three parts show diversity of India: food, hills and plantations.
