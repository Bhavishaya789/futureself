# Nelson Mandela: Long Walk to Freedom
minutes: 30
yt: class 10 english nelson mandela: long walk to freedom
@study
This extract from Nelson Mandela's autobiography describes his inauguration as the first democratic, non-racial President of South Africa in May 1994, held in the amphitheatre formed by the Union Buildings in Pretoria. Thousands of dignitaries from across the world attended. Mandela reflects on the long struggle against apartheid, the unity of the new nation and the gratitude owed to freedom fighters. He says that the sun must never set on so glorious a human achievement. He recalls the loyalty of the former military generals who saluted him, the singing of two anthems and his learning that courage is not the absence of fear but the triumph over it. He ends with the thought that freedom means liberating both the oppressed and the oppressor, and he describes the four ideals of courage, love of freedom, the role of heroes and the cost of apartheid.
## Deep dive: the occasion
The inauguration at Pretoria on 10 May 1994 ended centuries of white-minority rule. Over a hundred international leaders attended; the military saluted the new president.
## Deep dive: reflections on courage
Mandela says he learned that bravery is not lack of fear. The brave man is not he who does not feel afraid, but he who conquers that fear.
## Deep dive: freedom for all
Freedom means breaking the chains of both oppressed and oppressor. Both are robbed of humanity. The new nation must work for the dignity of all.
## Deep dive: values
Gratitude to those who sacrificed, unity of the nation, courage in difficulty and responsibility to build a just society.
