# Determiners
minutes: 30
yt: class 10 english determiners
@study
Determiners are words placed before nouns to show which one or how many. Types: articles (a, an, the), demonstratives (this, that, these, those), possessives (my, your, his, her, its, our, their), quantifiers (some, any, much, many, few, a few, little, a little, all, enough), distributives (each, every, either, neither) and numerals (one, two, first, second). A or an is used with singular countable nouns for something mentioned for the first time; the is used for something specific or unique. Some is used in positive sentences and any in negatives and questions. Few and little have a negative sense; a few and a little have a positive sense; many is used with countable and much with uncountable nouns.
## Deep dive: articles
A/an: first mention or any one; the: specific or unique. Use "an" before vowel sounds (an hour, an MLA), "a" before consonant sounds (a university, a one-rupee note).
## Deep dive: quantifiers
Many/few with countable; much/little with uncountable. A few/a little = some; few/little = hardly any. Some for positives; any for negatives and questions.
## Deep dive: distributives
Each, every, either, neither take singular verbs.
