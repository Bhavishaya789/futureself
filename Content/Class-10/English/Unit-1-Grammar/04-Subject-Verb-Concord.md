# Subject-Verb Concord
minutes: 30
yt: class 10 english subject-verb concord
@study
Subject-verb concord (agreement) means the verb must agree with its subject in number and person. A singular subject takes a singular verb and a plural subject takes a plural verb. Subjects joined by "and" take a plural verb, unless they form one idea (bread and butter is). With "or", "nor", "either...or" and "neither...nor", the verb agrees with the nearer subject. Each, every, either, neither, everyone and anybody take singular verbs. Collective nouns take a singular verb when seen as one unit and plural when members are seen individually. "A number of" takes a plural verb while "the number of" takes a singular. Nouns plural in form but singular in meaning (news, mathematics) take a singular verb; scissors, trousers and police take plural verbs.
## Deep dive: basic rules
Singular subject → singular verb, plural subject → plural verb. Subjects with "and" take plural verbs except when they form one idea.
## Deep dive: or / nor / as well as
With or, nor, either...or, neither...nor, the verb agrees with the nearer subject. With as well as, along with, with, the verb agrees with the first subject.
## Deep dive: special nouns
Each, every, everyone, neither, either, one of: singular. A number of: plural; the number of: singular. News, mathematics: singular; scissors, trousers, police: plural.
