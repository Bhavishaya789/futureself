# Social Change and Social Order in Rural and Urban Society
minutes: 40
yt: class 11 sociology social change rural urban agrarian structure urbanisation migration
@study
Social change is the alteration in social structure, institutions and relationships over time. Types: evolutionary and revolutionary; short-term and long-term. Causes: ecological, economic (Marx), political, cultural (Weber's Protestant ethic), technology and ideas. Social order is maintained by social control. Rural society: in India about 65 percent of the population lives in villages (2011 Census). Agrarian structure: land ownership, tenancy and labour. Rural classes: landlords, tenants, small farmers and landless labourers. Caste and land are linked. Land reforms: abolition of zamindari (intermediaries), tenancy reforms, land ceilings and consolidation. The Green Revolution (1960s) used high-yielding seeds, irrigation and fertilisers and raised food production but increased regional and class inequality. Other changes: mechanisation, commercialisation, migration, indebtedness and agrarian distress including farmers' suicides. Bonded labour (abolished by law in 1976), and migrant labour. Rural social change includes panchayati raj institutions (73rd Amendment, 1992). Globalisation affects agriculture through trade and contract farming. Urban society: urbanisation is the growth of towns and cities. In 2011, about 31 percent of Indians lived in urban areas. A town: population at least 5,000, density 400 per sq km and at least 75 percent of male working population in non-agricultural work (Census definition). Features of urban life: heterogeneity, anonymity, formal relations and division of labour. Urban problems: slums, housing shortage, pollution, traffic, crime and unemployment. Louis Wirth described urbanism as a way of life. Migration: push factors (poverty, unemployment) and pull factors (jobs, education). Industrialisation spurred urban growth in Mumbai, Kolkata, Delhi and Chennai. Cities in colonial India: Bombay, Calcutta and Madras as port cities. The idea of class in urban society and the role of the middle class. Urban planning and Smart Cities. Rural-urban differences: occupation, density, kinship ties and mobility. Rural-urban continuum. Social movements and change: tribal, women's and Dalit movements. Sociologists use ideas of modernisation. Factory work and trade unions in cities. Mahatma Gandhi emphasised village republics.
## Deep dive: Social change
Change is continuous; some is gradual and some sudden. Many factors—economy, technology, ideas and politics—cause change. Social order and change coexist.
## Deep dive: Rural change
Land reforms, the Green Revolution and panchayati raj reshaped villages. Inequality and distress remain. Migration links villages to cities.
## Deep dive: Urban life
Cities are large, dense and diverse. Anonymity and division of labour mark urban life. Slums and pollution are challenges.
