# Culture and Socialisation
minutes: 40
yt: class 11 sociology culture socialisation norms values agencies cooley mead
@study
Culture is the whole way of life of a society: its knowledge, beliefs, art, morals, laws, customs and habits acquired by members as part of society (E B Tylor). Dimensions: the cognitive (knowledge and ideas), the normative (rules and values) and the material (tools, clothing, buildings). Material culture consists of physical objects; non-material culture consists of ideas, beliefs and values. Cultural lag (William Ogburn) refers to the delay of non-material culture in adjusting to changes in material culture. Cultural diversity: subcultures (youth culture, regional culture) and countercultures. Ethnocentrism is judging other cultures by the standards of one's own and considering them inferior; cultural relativism is understanding a culture in its own terms. Cosmopolitanism welcomes cultural difference. Cultural change occurs through innovation, diffusion and contact. Sanskritisation is a process by which lower castes imitate the practices of higher castes (M N Srinivas); Westernisation is the adoption of Western ways. Socialisation is the process by which individuals learn the culture, norms and values of society and develop a sense of self. Primary socialisation occurs in childhood within the family; secondary socialisation occurs later in school, peers and the workplace. Anticipatory socialisation prepares for future roles; resocialisation involves new norms (as in prison or army). Agents of socialisation: family, peer group, school, mass media, religion and workplace. Gender socialisation teaches gender roles. Theories of self: Charles Cooley's looking-glass self (we see ourselves as others see us), George Herbert Mead's stages of the self (preparatory, play, game stage; the I and the me; the generalised other) and Freud's id, ego and superego. Feral children such as Kamala and Amala show the importance of socialisation. Socialisation and individuality: both nature and nurture shape the person. Conformity and deviance. Sociologists view socialisation as a lifelong process. Mass media like television and the internet influence values. Culture and identity: language, symbols and rituals. Sociology studies how culture is transmitted across generations. Popular culture and consumer culture are modern forms. Cultural globalisation spreads global brands and ideas.
## Deep dive: Culture
Culture includes knowledge, norms and material objects. Cultural lag appears when ideas lag behind technology. Sanskritisation and Westernisation are Indian processes of change.
## Deep dive: Socialisation
Socialisation turns the child into a social being. The family is the main agent in childhood; peers, school and media follow.
## Deep dive: Self
Cooley and Mead explain how the self develops through interaction with others. The generalised other represents society's expectations.
