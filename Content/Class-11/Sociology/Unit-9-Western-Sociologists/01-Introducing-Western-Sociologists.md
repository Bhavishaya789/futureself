# Introducing Western Sociologists
minutes: 40
yt: class 11 sociology western sociologists comte durkheim marx weber
@study
Early sociology developed in Europe in the nineteenth century amid industrialisation and revolution. Auguste Comte (1798–1857) coined sociology and proposed positivism; he described the law of three stages of human thought (theological, metaphysical and positive) and divided sociology into social statics and social dynamics. Herbert Spencer applied evolution to society (survival of the fittest). Emile Durkheim (1858–1917) established sociology as an academic discipline in France; he defined social facts as ways of acting, thinking and feeling that are external to the individual and exercise coercion. His works include The Division of Labour in Society (1893), Rules of Sociological Method (1895), Suicide (1897) and The Elementary Forms of Religious Life (1912). He distinguished mechanical solidarity (traditional societies, similarity) and organic solidarity (modern societies, interdependence). Types of suicide: egoistic, altruistic, anomic and fatalistic. Anomie is a state of normlessness. He stressed the collective conscience and the sacred–profane distinction in religion. Karl Marx (1818–1883) saw history as class struggle; with Friedrich Engels, he wrote The Communist Manifesto (1848); Das Kapital (1867) analysed capitalism. Historical materialism says that the economic base (forces and relations of production) shapes the superstructure (law, politics, religion). Modes of production: primitive communism, ancient (slave), feudal, capitalist and socialist. In capitalism, the bourgeoisie own capital and the proletariat sell labour; surplus value is extracted; alienation separates workers from their product, process, fellow workers and self. Class consciousness leads to revolution. Max Weber (1864–1920) emphasised interpretive understanding (verstehen) and social action. His types of social action: instrumentally rational, value rational, affective and traditional. The ideal type is an analytical construct. Bureaucracy is rational organisation with hierarchy, rules and expertise. Authority types: traditional, legal-rational and charismatic. In The Protestant Ethic and the Spirit of Capitalism (1905), he linked Calvinist values to the rise of capitalism. Class, status and party are dimensions of stratification. Comparison: Durkheim stressed social solidarity and order; Marx stressed conflict and change; Weber stressed meaning and rationalisation. Disenchantment of the world and the iron cage of rationality are Weberian ideas. Georg Simmel and Talcott Parsons are other thinkers. Positivism versus interpretivism.
## Deep dive: Comte and Spencer
Comte named sociology and proposed the law of three stages. Spencer applied evolution to society.
## Deep dive: Durkheim
Durkheim treated social facts as things. He explained suicide, division of labour and religion through social causes.
## Deep dive: Marx and Weber
Marx saw class conflict and alienation under capitalism. Weber stressed meaning, authority and rationalisation.
