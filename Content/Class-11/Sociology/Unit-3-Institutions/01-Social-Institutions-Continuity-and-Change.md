# Social Institutions: Continuity and Change
minutes: 40
yt: class 11 sociology social institutions family marriage kinship religion economy politics education
@study
Social institutions are established and organised sets of norms and roles that meet basic social needs. Main institutions: family, marriage, kinship, economy, polity, education and religion. Family is a group of persons related by blood, marriage or adoption who live together and cooperate economically. Types by structure: nuclear (parents and children) and joint or extended (several generations). Types by authority: patriarchal (male authority) and matriarchal (female authority; as among the Khasis of Meghalaya). Types by residence: patrilocal (living with the husband's family), matrilocal (with the wife's) and neolocal. Types by descent: patrilineal and matrilineal. Changes in the family: industrialisation, urbanisation, education and women's employment lead to nuclear families, smaller size, and changing roles. Marriage is a socially approved union between two persons. Forms: monogamy (one spouse), polygamy (polygyny: many wives; polyandry: many husbands, as among the Todas). Endogamy is marriage within a group (caste) and exogamy is marriage outside a group (gotra). Hypergamy and hypogamy refer to marriage into a higher or lower group. Practices: dowry, child marriage, bride price. Kinship is the web of relationships through blood (consanguineal) and marriage (affinal). Descent groups and kin terms. Economy: types of economy: hunting-gathering, agricultural, industrial; capitalism, socialism and mixed economy. Karl Marx described modes of production. The jajmani system is a traditional exchange of goods and services between castes in villages. Globalisation and liberalisation have changed work. Gender division of labour. Politics: power is the ability to carry out one's will despite resistance (Weber); authority is legitimate power. Weber's types of authority: traditional, legal-rational and charismatic. State is a political institution with territory, government and sovereignty. Democracy, monarchy and dictatorship. Education: formal and informal; socialisation, selection and social mobility; the role of the school in transmitting culture. Inequalities in education by caste, class and gender; the Right to Education Act, 2009 provides free and compulsory education for ages 6 to 14. Religion: Durkheim defined religion as a unified system of beliefs and practices relative to sacred things; the sacred and the profane. Functions: social solidarity and meaning. Secularisation reduces the influence of religion. Communalism is use of religious identity for political aims. Religion in India is diverse. Continuity and change run together in institutions.
## Deep dive: Family and marriage
Family forms vary by structure, authority, residence and descent. Marriage rules include endogamy and exogamy. Industrialisation and education have changed family patterns.
## Deep dive: Economy and politics
Economy organises production and distribution. Politics concerns power and authority. Weber described three types of authority.
## Deep dive: Education and religion
Education socialises and sorts. Religion creates solidarity and meaning. Secularisation and communalism are modern issues.
