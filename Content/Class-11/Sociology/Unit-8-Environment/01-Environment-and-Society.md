# Environment and Society
minutes: 40
yt: class 11 sociology environment ecology chipko silent valley sardar sarovar
@study
Environment includes the physical and living surroundings of humans. Ecology is the study of relationships between organisms and their environment. Sociologists see environmental problems as social: they arise from social organisation, power and inequality. Social ecology studies how social structures shape environment use. Ecology and society: pre-modern societies lived close to nature; industrialisation intensified exploitation. Environmental problems: resource depletion (water, forests, minerals), pollution (air, water, soil, noise), climate change, loss of biodiversity, waste and disasters. Ecological degradation is unequal: the poor are often most affected. Resources are used unequally; environmental conflicts arise over access to land, forest and water. Examples: the Chipko movement (1973) in Uttarakhand, where villagers, many of them women, hugged trees to protect forests from commercial felling; leaders included Sunderlal Bahuguna and Chandi Prasad Bhatt. The Silent Valley movement in Kerala (1970s–80s) saved a rainforest from a hydroelectric project. The Narmada Bachao Andolan (Medha Patkar) opposed the Sardar Sarovar dam over displacement. The Bhopal gas tragedy (1984) was an industrial disaster at the Union Carbide plant that killed thousands. Disasters can be natural or human-made; the 2004 tsunami and the 2001 Bhuj earthquake are examples. Sociologists see disasters as social because vulnerability depends on poverty and policy. Environmental law and policy: the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986, the Forest Rights Act, 2006, and the Water Act, 1974. Globalisation and consumption raise pressure. Sustainable development meets present needs without harming future generations (Brundtland Report, 1987). Ideas of environmental justice and the rights of the poor to natural resources. The Rio Earth Summit (1992), the Kyoto Protocol (1997) and the Paris Agreement (2015) address climate change. Water scarcity and privatisation of water. Role of communities in managing resources: community forest management. Environmental movements link livelihood with ecology. Ramachandra Guha described the environmentalism of the poor. Urban environmental issues: waste, slums and air quality. Individuals and governments share responsibility. Awareness and education help protect the environment.
## Deep dive: Environment as social
Environmental problems arise from how societies organise production and consumption. Power and inequality decide who gains and who bears the cost.
## Deep dive: Movements
Chipko, Silent Valley and Narmada are major movements. They link livelihood, ecology and rights.
## Deep dive: Disasters and law
Disasters have social causes of vulnerability. Laws and international agreements seek protection and sustainable development.
