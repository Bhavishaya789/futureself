# Understanding Society
minutes: 40
yt: class 11 sociology understanding society groups stratification status role
@study
Society is a system of social relationships; a web of interactions among individuals and groups. Sociology treats the individual and society as interlinked. Individuals are born into groups: a social group is a collection of people who interact regularly and share a sense of belonging. Types: primary groups (face-to-face, close and personal; family, friends; Charles Cooley) and secondary groups (large, impersonal, goal-oriented; school, office, political party); in-groups (we) and out-groups (they); reference groups (those whose standards we use to judge ourselves); peer groups. Quasi-groups are aggregates without structure, such as a crowd or a queue. Social stratification is the hierarchy of groups in society by power, prestige and wealth, as in caste, class, estate and slavery. Status is a socially defined position; ascribed status is assigned at birth (caste, sex, race) and achieved status is earned (doctor, teacher). A person holds many statuses (status set). Role is the behaviour expected of the person who holds a status; role conflict arises when roles of different statuses clash and role strain arises within one role. Social norms are rules of behaviour: folkways (customs), mores (strong moral norms) and laws. Values are shared ideas of what is good. Sanctions reward conformity (positive) or punish deviance (negative); formal and informal sanctions. Social control maintains order through agencies such as family, religion and law. Culture and social structure: structure refers to patterned relationships among the parts of society. Social interaction involves cooperation, competition and conflict. Cooperation is working together for a common goal; competition is striving for the same limited goal in a rule-bound way; conflict is a struggle to win or dominate and may involve violence; accommodation is a temporary adjustment; assimilation is merging of cultures. Social inequality takes forms of class, caste, gender and race. Karl Marx saw classes as groups defined by ownership of means of production (bourgeoisie and proletariat); Weber added status and party to class. Society and the individual: Durkheim stressed that social facts are external to and coercive of individuals. Social order and change. Types of society: hunting and gathering, pastoral, agrarian, industrial and post-industrial. The social contract, marketplace and community are older ideas. Groups can be formal (organised rules) or informal. Group size, membership and boundaries define groups.
## Deep dive: Groups
Primary groups are small and intimate; secondary groups are large and goal-oriented. Reference groups shape aspirations. In-group and out-group distinctions create boundaries.
## Deep dive: Status and role
Status is a position and role is the expected behaviour. Ascribed status is given; achieved status is earned. Role conflict and role strain create stress.
## Deep dive: Interaction and inequality
Cooperation, competition and conflict are social processes. Stratification ranks groups. Marx and Weber explain class differently.
