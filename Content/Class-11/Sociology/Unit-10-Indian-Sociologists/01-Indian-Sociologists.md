# Indian Sociologists

@study
Indian sociology developed as scholars studied caste, tribe, village and family in the context of colonial rule and nationalism. G S Ghurye (1893–1983), regarded as the founder of institutionalised sociology in India, headed the Department of Sociology at Bombay University. He wrote Caste and Race in India (1932) and studied caste, tribes (calling them backward Hindus), religion and kinship. His six features of caste: segmental division of society, hierarchy, restrictions on feeding and social intercourse, civil and religious disabilities and privileges, lack of unrestricted choice of occupation, and restrictions on marriage. He took an Indological approach using texts, and supported integration of tribes. D P Mukerji (1894–1961) of Lucknow University advocated Marxist ideas applied to Indian traditions; he believed that Indian sociologists should know Indian traditions, yet be open to change, and he stressed the role of tradition, class conflict and the importance of the interplay of tradition, change and conflict. A R Desai (1915–1994) used a Marxist approach; he wrote Social Background of Indian Nationalism (1948); he argued that nationalism arose from material changes under colonial rule and criticised the modern state as a welfare state which worked for the rich. Radhakamal Mukerjee (1889–1968) was a sociologist-economist at Lucknow who studied social ecology, values and civilisation. M N Srinivas (1916–1999) did village studies, wrote The Remembered Village (1976) on Rampura, and coined Sanskritisation, dominant caste and Westernisation; he used fieldwork and the structural-functional approach; he established the Delhi School of Sociology and wrote on religion and society among the Coorgs (1952). A K Saran, a Lucknow sociologist, criticised Western approaches. Louis Dumont studied hierarchy in Homo Hierarchicus. Iravati Karve studied kinship. Comparisons: Ghurye (Indological), Mukerji (dialectical), Desai (Marxist), Srinivas (fieldwork). Indian sociologists tried to understand society through its own context. They contributed to understanding caste, tribe, village and nationalism. Later scholars: Andre Beteille studied Sripuram and caste, class and power; Yogendra Singh on modernisation of Indian tradition. Colonial influence on Indian sociology.
## Deep dive: Ghurye and Srinivas
Ghurye used texts to study caste and tribes. Srinivas pioneered fieldwork and village studies. Both shaped Indian sociology.
## Deep dive: D P Mukerji and A R Desai
Mukerji combined Marxism with Indian tradition. Desai analysed nationalism and the state in Marxist terms.
## Deep dive: Other scholars
Karve studied kinship; Dumont, caste hierarchy; Beteille, caste and class. Indian sociology grew through debate with Western ideas.
