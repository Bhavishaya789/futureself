# Social Structure, Stratification and Social Processes
minutes: 40
yt: class 11 sociology social structure stratification caste class gender social control
@study
Social structure is the pattern of relationships among individuals, groups and institutions in society. Social stratification is the ranking of groups in a hierarchy of power, prestige and wealth. It is persistent over generations, universal but variable, social (not biological), and supported by ideas. Forms: slavery, estate, caste and class. Theories: functionalist (Davis and Moore: stratification is necessary to fill important roles), Marxist (class conflict) and Weberian (class, status and party). Caste: a hereditary, endogamous, occupation-based, ranked system in India; features include hierarchy, restrictions on food and social contact, purity and pollution, and rules of marriage; the four varnas are Brahmins, Kshatriyas, Vaishyas and Shudras, with Dalits (outcastes or Scheduled Castes) outside. Colonial rule changed caste through censuses. Caste today: sanskritisation, dominant caste, caste associations, politics and reservations. Untouchability was abolished by Article 17 of the Constitution. Class: groups based on economic position (Marx: owners and workers). Tribe: community linked to territory, with a common culture, often called Adivasi; tribal issues include land alienation and displacement. Gender: socially constructed roles of men and women, distinct from sex; patriarchy is male dominance; women's movement for equality; sex ratio and gender gaps. Social inequality in India includes disability and discrimination. Affirmative action: reservations for SC, ST and OBC (Mandal Commission, 1990 implementation) and for women in local bodies. Prejudice is a pre-formed negative attitude and discrimination is unfair action; stereotype is a fixed image. Social exclusion denies groups full participation. Social mobility: vertical (up or down) and horizontal; open and closed systems; intergenerational and intragenerational. Social control: formal (law, police, courts) and informal (family, religion, gossip) means. Deviance is behaviour violating norms; Durkheim and Merton (strain theory) explained it. Crime is deviance punished by law. Conformity versus deviance. Social order is maintained by control. Social processes: cooperation, competition, conflict. Karl Marx said conflict drives change. Social inequality is also found in race and religion. The Indian state works through laws such as the Protection of Civil Rights Act, 1955 and the SC and ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act, 1989. Social movements for equality include those of B R Ambedkar, Jyotiba Phule and Periyar.
## Deep dive: Stratification
Societies rank groups by power, prestige and wealth. Closed systems like caste limit mobility; open systems like class allow it. Theories explain it as necessary or as exploitative.
## Deep dive: Caste, tribe and gender
Caste involves hierarchy, purity and endogamy. Tribes have faced displacement. Gender is socially constructed and patriarchy shapes inequality.
## Deep dive: Control and deviance
Social control maintains order. Deviance breaks norms. Merton's strain theory links deviance to a gap between goals and means.
