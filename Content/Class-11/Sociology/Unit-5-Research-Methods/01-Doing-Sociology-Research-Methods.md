# Doing Sociology: Research Methods
minutes: 40
yt: class 11 sociology research methods survey observation interview objectivity
@study
Sociological research uses systematic methods to study society. Research can be quantitative (numbers, statistics) or qualitative (meanings, descriptions). Challenges: subjectivity, objectivity and reflexivity. Objectivity means studying without personal bias; Max Weber said sociologists should be value-neutral but aware of values; Durkheim argued for studying social facts like things. Subjectivity involves personal views and beliefs. Reflexivity means reflecting on one's own role in research. Insider and outsider perspectives; participants may behave differently when observed. Methods: survey, interview, observation, case study and use of secondary sources. Survey: collects data from a large number of people, often by a questionnaire, from a sample drawn from the population. Sampling: random, stratified and quota sampling; a representative sample reflects the population. Interview: a face-to-face conversation; structured (fixed questions), unstructured and semi-structured; advantages are depth and flexibility, disadvantages include time and interviewer bias. Observation: participant observation (researcher joins the group; used by anthropologists and in village studies) and non-participant observation. Advantage: rich, in-depth information; disadvantage: time-consuming and subjective. Questionnaire: written set of questions, open or closed. Case study: intensive study of a single case. Use of secondary sources: census, official records, newspapers and documents. Steps of research: choosing a problem, framing hypotheses, selecting a method, collecting data, analysis and reporting. Triangulation combines methods. A pilot study tests the method before the main study. Ethics: informed consent, confidentiality and avoiding harm. Questions of Indian sociology: village studies by M N Srinivas (Rampura) used participant observation. Census of India is a large secondary source. Benefits and limits of each method: surveys are broad but shallow; observation is deep but limited. Choice of method depends on the research question. Sociological research can lead to policy, but needs care to avoid stereotypes. Reliability and validity apply. Measurement and variables: a variable is a characteristic that varies; a hypothesis is a testable statement; a universe is the whole population. Fieldwork is research in natural settings.
## Deep dive: Objectivity
Sociologists strive to be objective but know values affect research. Reflexivity helps them see their own influence. Weber advised value neutrality.
## Deep dive: Methods
Surveys give breadth; observation and interviews give depth. Sampling makes surveys feasible. Triangulation increases confidence.
## Deep dive: Ethics
Researchers must protect participants through consent and confidentiality. Fieldwork requires sensitivity.
