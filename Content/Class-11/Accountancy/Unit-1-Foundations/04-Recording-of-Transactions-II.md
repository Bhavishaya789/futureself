# Recording of Transactions II
minutes: 45
yt: class 11 accountancy cash book subsidiary books petty cash
@study
To save time, a business uses special journals (subsidiary books): purchases book (credit purchases of goods), sales book (credit sales), purchases return book, sales return book, bills receivable and payable books, cash book and journal proper. The cash book is both a journal and a ledger. Types: single column, double column (cash and discount / cash and bank), triple column (cash, bank, discount). Contra entries affect both cash and bank. Petty cash book records small expenses under the imprest system; the analytical petty cash book has columns for each expense. Accounts of purchases and sales book are posted periodically to Purchases A/c and Sales A/c; totals are credited/debited.
## Deep dive: Subsidiary books
Purchases book records credit purchases of goods only (not assets): posted to Purchases A/c Dr on total and to each supplier Cr. Sales book similarly. Returns books record returns; debit note to purchases return; credit note to sales return. GST columns show input and output taxes. Journal proper handles opening entries, closing entries, adjustment entries, rectifications and credit purchase or sale of assets.
## Deep dive: Cash book
A single column cash book has one amount column on each side: receipts on debit, payments on credit; balance is always debit or nil. In a double column cash book with discount, the discount allowed column on debit and discount received on credit are totals posted separately. A triple column cash book has cash, bank and discount columns. Contra entry: cash deposited in bank (Bank Dr, Cash Cr) shown on both sides with C in LF. Bank column can show a credit balance as an overdraft.
## Deep dive: Petty cash book
The chief cashier gives an advance (imprest) to the petty cashier who spends on small items such as postage, stationery, carriage and refreshments. The petty cashier records expenses in an analytical way and at the end of the period submits the totals; the head cashier reimburses the amount spent to restore the imprest. Advantage: saves time, controls petty expenses.
## Deep dive: Advantages and posting
Special journals divide work among staff, allow quick posting of totals and reduce errors. Totals of sales book posted to Sales A/c credit; each customer debited individually. The cash book is balanced like an account.

## Advanced
P: Explain the imprest system of petty cash and its advantages.
A: A fixed amount is given to the petty cashier at the start of a period; he spends on small items; at the end the head cashier reimburses the amount spent so the imprest is restored. Advantages: control on small expenses, saving of time of the chief cashier, less risk, analysis of expenses.
