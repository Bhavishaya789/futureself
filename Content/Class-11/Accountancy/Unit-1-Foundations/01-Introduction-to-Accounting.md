# Introduction to Accounting
minutes: 35
yt: class 11 accountancy introduction to accounting
@study
Accounting is the process of identifying, measuring, recording, classifying, summarising, analysing and communicating financial information. Book-keeping is the recording part; accounting includes summarising and interpretation. The users of accounting information are internal (owners, managers, employees) and external (investors, creditors, banks, government, customers, researchers). Basic terms: assets, liabilities, capital, drawings, revenue, expenses, income, gain, profit, loss, debtors, creditors, goods, purchases, sales, stock, discount and bad debts. The accounting equation is Assets = Liabilities + Capital. Branches: financial, cost and management accounting. Limitations include historical cost basis and the exclusion of non-monetary information.
## Deep dive: Meaning and objectives
Accounting maintains systematic records, ascertains profit or loss, ascertains financial position, provides information to users and helps in assessing tax liability. Book-keeping is the art of recording transactions in the books; accounting is the process of summarising and interpreting. Accountancy is the body of principles and rules. The accounting cycle is the sequence: transactions, journal, ledger, trial balance, adjustments, final accounts.
## Deep dive: Basic terms
Asset: a resource owned with future economic benefit (fixed, current, tangible, intangible). Liability: obligation to be paid (long-term, current, contingent). Capital: amount invested by the owner. Drawings: money or goods taken by the owner for personal use, reducing capital. Revenue is the amount earned from business activity; expenditure is amount spent; capital expenditure benefits many years while revenue expenditure benefits the current period. Debtor owes money to the business; creditor is owed money. Discount: trade discount is on list price and not recorded; cash discount is for prompt payment and recorded.
## Deep dive: Accounting equation
Assets = Liabilities + Capital (A = L + C). Capital = Assets - Liabilities. Each transaction has two aspects (dual aspect) and the equation stays balanced. Example: purchase of goods for cash increases stock and decreases cash; no change in total. Starting business with Rs 1,00,000 gives assets Rs 1,00,000 and capital Rs 1,00,000. Borrowing Rs 20,000 raises assets and liabilities to Rs 1,20,000 each side. Profit increases capital; drawings decrease it.
## Deep dive: Users and branches
Internal users: owners, managers, employees. External users: investors, creditors, lenders, government, customers, researchers. Financial accounting records and reports financial position. Cost accounting finds the cost of products. Management accounting helps in decision making. Accounting information qualities: reliability, relevance, understandability, comparability. Advantages: systematic records, tax assessment, legal evidence, comparison; limitations: historical cost, personal judgement, ignores price level changes.
