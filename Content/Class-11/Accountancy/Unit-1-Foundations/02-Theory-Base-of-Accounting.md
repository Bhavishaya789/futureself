# Theory Base of Accounting
minutes: 40
yt: class 11 accountancy theory base of accounting concepts
@study
Accounting is based on generally accepted accounting principles (GAAP): concepts, principles and conventions. The basic accounting concepts are business entity, money measurement, going concern, accounting period, cost, dual aspect, revenue recognition, matching, full disclosure, consistency, conservatism, materiality, and objectivity. Accounting standards (AS in India, Ind AS converged with IFRS) are issued by ICAI. Systems of accounting are cash basis and accrual basis; the books can be kept on the double entry system, which uses debit and credit. Accounting basis, bases of measurement and the accounting equation under Indian GAAP apply to all entities.
## Deep dive: Accounting concepts
Business entity: the business is separate from the owner, so drawings are not expenses. Money measurement: only transactions in money are recorded. Going concern: the business will continue indefinitely, so assets are valued at cost, not sale value. Accounting period: usually 12 months (1 April to 31 March). Cost: assets are recorded at purchase cost. Dual aspect: each transaction has two aspects. Revenue recognition: revenue is recorded when earned. Matching: expenses are matched with revenue of the same period. Full disclosure: all material facts are reported. Consistency: same methods used year to year. Conservatism: anticipate no profits but provide for all possible losses. Materiality: only significant items need to be reported separately. Objectivity: records are based on verifiable evidence.
## Deep dive: Accounting standards and IFRS
Accounting standards are written policy documents issued by ICAI (the Accounting Standards Board) to harmonise practices. Indian Accounting Standards (Ind AS) converge with IFRS (International Financial Reporting Standards) of the IASB; they are applicable to companies as notified under the Companies Act. IFRS provide global comparability.
## Deep dive: Bases of accounting
Cash basis: transactions are recorded only when cash is received or paid. Accrual basis: revenue is recorded when earned and expenses when incurred whether or not cash is exchanged; required by the Companies Act. Accrual basis follows matching. Double entry: every transaction affects at least two accounts, with equal debit and credit.
## Deep dive: Rules of debit and credit
Accounts classify into personal (natural, artificial, representative), real (tangible, intangible) and nominal accounts. Personal: debit the receiver, credit the giver. Real: debit what comes in, credit what goes out. Nominal: debit expenses and losses, credit incomes and gains. Modern approach: assets and expenses increase by debit; liabilities, capital and income increase by credit.

## Moderate

A: The business is assumed to continue for the foreseeable future, so fixed assets are recorded at cost, not at sale value, and depreciation is charged.

A: Cash basis records only cash receipts and payments; accrual basis records revenue when earned and expenses when incurred irrespective of cash. Accrual follows the matching concept and is compulsory for companies.

## Advanced
P: Explain conservatism, consistency and materiality with examples.
A: Conservatism: provide for probable losses and ignore unrealised gains (stock at cost or market, whichever is lower). Consistency: use the same method (like straight line depreciation) every year. Materiality: only significant items are shown separately (a small stationery is expensed at once).
