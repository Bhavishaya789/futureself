# Financial Statements II
minutes: 45
yt: class 11 accountancy balance sheet financial statements
@study
The balance sheet is a statement of assets and liabilities (including capital) at a given date. Assets = Liabilities + Capital. Assets are classified as fixed (tangible, intangible), investments, current assets (cash, bank, debtors, stock, bills receivable, prepaid expenses), and fictitious assets. Liabilities are long-term (loans, debentures) and current (creditors, bills payable, outstanding expenses, bank overdraft). Assets can be arranged in the order of liquidity or permanence. A balance sheet in vertical format shows capital and liabilities first in order of permanence. Capital = opening capital + net profit - drawings (+ additional capital). The balance sheet is prepared after the trading and profit and loss accounts. Adjustments affect both final accounts and the balance sheet.
## Deep dive: Classification
Assets: current assets (cash, bank, stock, debtors, bills receivable, prepaid expenses, accrued income, short-term investments); non-current assets (fixed assets, long-term investments). Fixed assets: tangible (land, building, machinery, furniture) and intangible (goodwill, patents, trademarks). Liabilities: current (creditors, bills payable, outstanding expenses, bank overdraft, income received in advance) and long-term (loans, debentures). Capital is the owner's equity.
## Deep dive: Marshalling
Order of liquidity: assets arranged from the most liquid (cash) to the least liquid (land); liabilities from the most urgent. Order of permanence: assets arranged from fixed to current (land first, cash last); liabilities from capital to current liabilities. Banks follow liquidity; companies follow permanence under the Companies Act (Schedule III).
## Deep dive: Preparing the balance sheet
Steps: calculate capital (opening + profit - drawings + additional capital). Show closing stock, debtors less bad debts and provision, outstanding expenses (liability) and prepaid (asset), fixed assets less depreciation, bills and investments. Check that assets equal liabilities plus capital. The balance sheet is a statement, not an account; it does not use Dr and Cr, and it is prepared as on a date.
## Deep dive: Significance and limits
The balance sheet shows financial position, solvency, liquidity, and capital structure; it helps compare with previous years. Limitations: valued at historical cost, ignores non-monetary assets (employee skill), window dressing may distort, and it only shows position on a given date.
