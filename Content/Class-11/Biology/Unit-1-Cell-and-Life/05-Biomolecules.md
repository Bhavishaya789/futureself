# Biomolecules
minutes: 45
yt: class 11 biology biomolecules enzymes proteins carbohydrates lipids nucleic acids
@study
Biomolecules include carbohydrates, proteins, lipids, nucleic acids, vitamins and enzymes. Amino acids have an amino group, carboxyl group and R group; peptide bond links them. Protein structure: primary, secondary (alpha helix, beta sheet), tertiary, quaternary (haemoglobin). Monosaccharides (glucose), disaccharides (sucrose, maltose, lactose), polysaccharides (starch, glycogen, cellulose, chitin) linked by glycosidic bonds. Lipids: fats with glycerol and fatty acids; phospholipids. Nucleic acids: nucleotide = sugar + base + phosphate; DNA and RNA. Enzymes lower activation energy; Michaelis-Menten constant Km; factors: temperature, pH, substrate concentration; inhibitors competitive and non-competitive.
## Deep dive: Carbohydrates, lipids, amino acids
Glucose is an aldohexose (C6H12O6); fructose a ketohexose. Sucrose = glucose + fructose; maltose = glucose + glucose; lactose = glucose + galactose. Starch has amylose and amylopectin; glycogen is highly branched; cellulose is beta-1,4 linked. Fatty acids may be saturated or unsaturated; triglycerides contain glycerol and 3 fatty acids; phospholipids contain phosphate and are amphipathic (lecithin). Essential amino acids cannot be made by the body; zwitterion has both charges at isoelectric pH.
## Deep dive: Proteins and nucleic acids
Primary structure is the linear sequence of amino acids; secondary has alpha helix stabilised by hydrogen bonds; tertiary is 3D folding; quaternary has multiple chains (haemoglobin: 2 alpha + 2 beta). Collagen is the most abundant protein in animals; RuBisCO is the most abundant protein in the biosphere. Nucleotides: adenine, guanine (purines), cytosine, thymine, uracil (pyrimidines); nucleoside = sugar + base. DNA is a double helix with A=T and G≡C (Chargaff).
## Deep dive: Enzymes
Enzymes are mostly proteins (ribozymes are RNA). Active site binds substrate (lock and key; induced fit). Classes: oxidoreductases, transferases, hydrolases, lyases, isomerases, ligases. Cofactors: prosthetic group, coenzyme (NAD, NADP from vitamins), metal ions. Rate rises with temperature up to the optimum then falls by denaturation. Optimum pH differs. Km is the substrate concentration at half Vmax. Competitive inhibitors resemble the substrate (e.g., malonate on succinate dehydrogenase). Feedback inhibition regulates pathways.
