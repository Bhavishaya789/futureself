# Cell Cycle and Cell Division
minutes: 45
yt: class 11 biology cell cycle mitosis meiosis
@study
The cell cycle has interphase (G1, S, G2) and M phase. Mitosis (equational division) has prophase, metaphase, anaphase, telophase and cytokinesis; it produces two identical diploid daughter cells. Meiosis (reductional) has two divisions: meiosis I (prophase I with leptotene, zygotene, pachytene, diplotene, diakinesis) and meiosis II; it produces four haploid cells. Crossing over occurs at pachytene with recombination nodules; chiasmata are seen at diplotene. G0 is the quiescent stage. Significance: growth, repair, gamete formation and genetic variation.
## Deep dive: Cell cycle phases
Interphase is the longest. G1: cell grows and prepares; S: DNA replicates (amount doubles from 2C to 4C, chromosome number unchanged); G2: protein synthesis for mitosis. Human cell cycle about 24 hours with M phase about 1 hour. Cells exiting the cycle enter G0 (heart cells). Checkpoints (G1/S, G2/M, spindle) are regulated by cyclins and CDKs.
## Deep dive: Mitosis
Prophase: chromatin condenses, centrioles move to poles, nucleolus and nuclear envelope disappear. Metaphase: chromosomes align at the equatorial plate, spindle fibres attach at kinetochores. Anaphase: centromeres split and chromatids move to opposite poles. Telophase: chromosomes decondense, nuclear envelope reforms. Cytokinesis: furrow in animals, cell plate in plants. Karyokinesis is nuclear division.
## Deep dive: Meiosis
Meiosis I prophase I: leptotene (condensation), zygotene (synapsis, synaptonemal complex, bivalents), pachytene (crossing over), diplotene (chiasmata, dictyotene in oocytes), diakinesis (terminalisation). Metaphase I bivalents on plate; anaphase I homologous chromosomes separate (no centromere division); telophase I gives dyad. Meiosis II resembles mitosis. Result: four haploid cells. Significance: constancy of chromosome number, variation through crossing over and independent assortment.
