@study
Plant classification uses artificial, natural and phylogenetic systems. Plants include Algae (thallophytes: green, brown, red), Bryophytes (liverworts and mosses; amphibians of the plant kingdom), Pteridophytes (first vascular plants), Gymnosperms (naked seeds) and Angiosperms (flowering plants, monocots and dicots). Life cycles show alternation of generations: haplontic (algae), diplontic (gymnosperms, angiosperms), haplo-diplontic (bryophytes, pteridophytes). Algae: Chlorophyceae (chlorophyll a, b), Phaeophyceae (fucoxanthin, laminarin), Rhodophyceae (phycoerythrin, floridean starch). Bryophyte sporophyte depends on the gametophyte. Pteridophytes have well-developed vascular tissues. Gymnosperms bear naked ovules; angiosperms show double fertilisation and a triploid endosperm.
## Deep dive: Algae
Chlorophyceae (green algae): pigments chlorophyll a and b, cellulose wall, food reserve starch; Chlamydomonas, Volvox, Spirogyra, Chara. Phaeophyceae (brown algae): fucoxanthin, food reserve laminarin and mannitol; Ectocarpus, Dictyota, Laminaria, Sargassum, Fucus. Rhodophyceae (red algae): phycoerythrin, floridean starch; Polysiphonia, Porphyra, Gracilaria, Gelidium. Algae are used for food (Porphyra, Laminaria), agar (Gelidium, Gracilaria), carrageen, hydrocolloids (algin) and as primary producers.
## Deep dive: Bryophytes and Pteridophytes
Bryophytes: liverworts (Marchantia) and mosses (Funaria, Sphagnum); thalloid or leafy; rhizoids; antheridia and archegonia; sporophyte (foot, seta, capsule) is attached to the gametophyte. Sphagnum gives peat; mosses prevent soil erosion and initiate ecological succession. Pteridophytes: Psilopsida, Lycopsida (Selaginella, Lycopodium), Sphenopsida (Equisetum), Pteropsida (Dryopteris, Pteris, Adiantum); dominant sporophyte, independent gametophyte (prothallus); heterospory in Selaginella is a step towards the seed habit.
## Deep dive: Gymnosperms and angiosperms
Gymnosperms: Cycas, Pinus, Cedrus, Ginkgo, Gnetum; tap roots; coralloid roots (Cycas) with cyanobacteria; male cones with microsporophylls and female cones with megasporophylls; no fruit. Angiosperms: flowering plants with seed in fruit; two classes: Dicotyledons and Monocotyledons. Male gametophyte is pollen grain; female gametophyte is embryo sac (7-celled, 8-nucleate). Double fertilisation: one male gamete fuses with the egg (zygote, 2n), the other with two polar nuclei (primary endosperm nucleus, 3n).
## Deep dive: Alternation of generations
Haplontic: dominant gametophyte, zygote only diploid (Volvox, Spirogyra). Diplontic: dominant sporophyte, gametophyte reduced (gymnosperms, angiosperms, Fucus). Haplo-diplontic: both multicellular (bryophytes, pteridophytes, Ectocarpus, Polysiphonia, kelps). In bryophytes the gametophyte dominates; in pteridophytes the sporophyte dominates.

## High
P: Why are bryophytes called amphibians of the plant kingdom?
A: They live in soil but depend on water for sexual reproduction since the male gamete swims to the egg.

## Advanced
P: Explain double fertilisation and the three types of life cycles in plants.
A: Double fertilisation: one male gamete + egg = zygote; other + 2 polar nuclei = triploid endosperm. Haplontic: gametophyte dominant; diplontic: sporophyte dominant; haplo-diplontic: both multicellular.
