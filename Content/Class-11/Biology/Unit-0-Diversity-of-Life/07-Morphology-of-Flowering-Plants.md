# Morphology of Flowering Plants

@study
Morphology studies external form. Root: tap root and fibrous root; regions: root cap, meristematic, elongation, maturation zones; modifications (carrot, radish, prop, stilt, pneumatophores). Stem: nodes, internodes, buds; modifications (tuber, rhizome, tendrils, thorns, runners). Leaf: lamina, petiole, leaf base; venation (reticulate, parallel); phyllotaxy (alternate, opposite, whorled); simple and compound leaves. Inflorescence: racemose and cymose. Flower: calyx, corolla, androecium, gynoecium; aestivation; placentation; ovary position (hypogynous, perigynous, epigynous). Fruit develops from the ovary; seed has seed coat, embryo; dicot and monocot seeds. Families: Fabaceae, Solanaceae, Liliaceae.
## Deep dive: Root, stem and leaf
Tap root (dicots), fibrous (monocots), adventitious (grass, Monstera, banyan). Root hairs absorb water. Modified roots: storage (carrot, turnip, sweet potato), prop roots (banyan), stilt roots (maize, sugarcane), pneumatophores (Rhizophora). Stem modifications: underground (potato tuber, ginger rhizome, colocasia corm), tendrils (cucumber, pumpkin), thorns (Citrus, Bougainvillea), flattened (Opuntia), runners (grass). Leaf: venation reticulate in dicots, parallel in monocots; modifications: tendrils (pea), spines (cacti), pitcher (Nepenthes).
## Deep dive: Inflorescence and flower
Racemose: main axis grows continuously; cymose: main axis ends in a flower. Flower is bisexual or unisexual; actinomorphic (radial) or zygomorphic (bilateral). Calyx has sepals, corolla petals; aestivation: valvate, twisted, imbricate, vexillary. Androecium: stamens (anther and filament); gynoecium: carpels (stigma, style, ovary); apocarpous or syncarpous. Placentation: marginal, axile, parietal, free central, basal. Ovary position: superior (hypogynous), half-inferior (perigynous), inferior (epigynous).
## Deep dive: Fruit, seed and families
Fruit is the mature ovary; parthenocarpic fruit (banana) develops without fertilisation. Pericarp has epicarp, mesocarp, endocarp. Dicot seed (bean, gram): seed coat (testa and tegmen), two cotyledons, plumule, radicle; monocot (maize): single scutellum, endosperm, coleoptile, coleorhiza. Fabaceae: papilionaceous corolla, diadelphous stamens, legume. Solanaceae: bicarpellary superior ovary, berry or capsule. Liliaceae: perigonium, trimerous flowers, 6 stamens, capsule.
## Deep dive: Floral formula
Floral formula uses K for calyx, C for corolla, A for androecium, G for gynoecium; the underline of G shows a superior ovary; % zygomorphic; ⊕ actinomorphic. Fabaceae: % K(5) C1+2+(2) A(9)+1 G1. Solanaceae: ⊕ K(5) C(5) A5 G(2). Liliaceae: ⊕ P(3+3) A3+3 G(3).

## Moderate
P: Distinguish racemose from cymose inflorescence.
A: Racemose has indefinite axis growth with acropetal flowers; cymose has the axis ending in a flower with basipetal opening.
