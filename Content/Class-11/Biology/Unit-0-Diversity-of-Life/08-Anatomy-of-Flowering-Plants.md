# Anatomy of Flowering Plants

@study
Plant tissues: meristematic (apical, intercalary, lateral) and permanent (simple: parenchyma, collenchyma, sclerenchyma; complex: xylem, phloem). Tissue systems: epidermal, ground and vascular. Dicot root has radial vascular bundles with exarch xylem, 2-4 xylem bundles (di- to tetrarch). Monocot root has polyarch. Dicot stem: vascular bundles in a ring, conjoint, collateral, open. Monocot stem: scattered, closed. Dorsiventral and isobilateral leaves. Secondary growth by vascular cambium and cork cambium: annual rings, heartwood, sapwood, bark, lenticels.
## Deep dive: Tissues
Meristematic: apical (root and shoot tip), intercalary (grasses), lateral (cambium). Parenchyma: living, thin walled, photosynthesis and storage. Collenchyma: pectin and cellulose thickening at corners, support in young stems. Sclerenchyma: dead, lignified (fibres, sclereids). Xylem: tracheids, vessels, xylem fibres, xylem parenchyma; conducts water. Phloem: sieve tube elements, companion cells, phloem parenchyma, phloem fibres; conducts food. Protoxylem is towards the centre in stems (endarch) and towards the periphery in roots (exarch).
## Deep dive: Tissue systems
Epidermal: epidermis, cuticle, stomata (guard cells and subsidiary cells), root hairs, trichomes. Ground: all tissues except epidermis and vascular bundles (cortex, pericycle, pith). Vascular: xylem and phloem; conjoint (same radius), radial (different radii); open (cambium present, dicots), closed (monocots). Guard cells of dumb-bell shape in grasses and kidney shape in dicots.
## Deep dive: Primary structure
Dicot root: epidermis with root hairs, cortex, endodermis with Casparian strips, pericycle, vascular bundles 2-4, conjunctive tissue, small pith. Monocot root: more than 6 xylem bundles, large pith. Dicot stem: epidermis, hypodermis (collenchyma), cortex, endodermis, pericycle, bundles in ring, pith. Monocot stem: sclerenchymatous hypodermis, scattered bundles with sclerenchymatous sheaths, no differentiated ground tissue. Dorsiventral leaf: palisade and spongy mesophyll; isobilateral leaf (monocot): bulliform cells.
## Deep dive: Secondary growth
Vascular cambium forms secondary xylem inward and secondary phloem outward. Cork cambium (phellogen) forms cork (phellem) outward and secondary cortex (phelloderm) inward; periderm = phellogen + phellem + phelloderm. Spring wood (light, wide vessels) and autumn wood (dark, narrow) form an annual ring; dendrochronology counts rings. Heartwood is dark with tannins and resins; sapwood conducts water. Lenticels allow gas exchange. Monocots lack secondary growth.

## Moderate
P: Differentiate xylem and phloem.
A: Xylem conducts water and minerals upward and has tracheids and vessels; phloem conducts food and has sieve tubes and companion cells.
R: 3 | Xylem
R: 3 | Phloem
## High
P: Distinguish dicot root and monocot root anatomy.
A: Dicot root has 2-4 xylem bundles and a small pith; monocot root has more than six bundles and a large pith.
