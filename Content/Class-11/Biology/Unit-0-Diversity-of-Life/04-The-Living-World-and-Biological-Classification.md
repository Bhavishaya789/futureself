# The Living World and Biological Classification
minutes: 45
yt: class 11 biology living world biological classification five kingdoms
@study
Living organisms show growth, reproduction, metabolism, cellular organisation, consciousness (response to environment) and self-regulation. Taxonomy identifies, names and classifies organisms. Taxonomic hierarchy: Species, Genus, Family, Order, Class, Phylum/Division, Kingdom. Binomial nomenclature (Linnaeus) gives each organism a generic and a specific name (Homo sapiens). Whittaker proposed five kingdoms in 1969: Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae, Animalia. Monera are prokaryotes (bacteria, archaebacteria, cyanobacteria, mycoplasma). Protista include chrysophytes, dinoflagellates, euglenoids, slime moulds, protozoans. Fungi have chitin cell walls and are heterotrophic. Viruses, viroids and prions are non-cellular. Lichens are symbiotic associations of algae and fungi.
## Deep dive: What is living
Growth is an increase in mass and size; non-living objects grow by accumulation from outside, living ones from inside. Reproduction is not a defining characteristic (mules are sterile). Metabolism is the sum of all chemical reactions and is the defining feature of life. Consciousness: all organisms respond to stimuli. Cellular organisation is a characteristic. Biodiversity: about 1.7 to 1.8 million species described. Nomenclature rules: biological names are in Latin, written in italics, genus first with capital letter, species second in lower case (Mangifera indica). Author citation follows.
## Deep dive: Taxonomic categories
Species is the lowest category (a group of organisms that can interbreed). Genus (Panthera) includes related species; Family (Felidae); Order (Carnivora); Class (Mammalia); Phylum (Chordata); Kingdom (Animalia). Man: Homo sapiens, Hominidae, Primata, Mammalia, Chordata, Animalia. Mango: Mangifera indica, Anacardiaceae, Sapindales, Dicotyledonae, Angiospermae, Plantae. Taxonomic aids: herbarium (preserved plants), botanical gardens, museums, zoological parks, keys.
## Deep dive: Five kingdom classification
Monera: bacteria are the most abundant; shapes: coccus, bacillus, vibrio, spirillum; some are autotrophs; archaebacteria live in harsh habitats (methanogens, halophiles, thermoacidophiles); cyanobacteria (Nostoc, Anabaena) fix nitrogen; mycoplasma lack cell wall. Protista: unicellular eukaryotes; chrysophytes (diatoms), dinoflagellates (red tide), euglenoids (Euglena), slime moulds, protozoans (Amoeba, Paramecium, Plasmodium). Fungi: Phycomycetes (Rhizopus), Ascomycetes (Penicillium, yeast), Basidiomycetes (mushrooms), Deuteromycetes. Plantae: eukaryotic, chlorophyll, cellulose cell wall; Animalia: heterotrophic, no cell wall.
## Deep dive: Viruses, viroids, lichens
Viruses were studied by D. J. Ivanowsky (1892) in tobacco mosaic and M. W. Beijerinck called the fluid contagium vivum fluidum; W. M. Stanley crystallised them. Viruses have nucleic acid and protein coat (capsid); they are obligate parasites. Viroids (T. O. Diener) are free RNA without a protein coat. Prions are infectious proteins causing BSE (mad cow disease). Lichens are symbiotic associations of algae (phycobiont) and fungi (mycobiont) and are pollution indicators.

## High
P: Compare viruses, viroids and prions.
A: Viruses have nucleic acid and a protein coat and are obligate parasites; viroids are free RNA without a coat (potato spindle tuber disease); prions are infectious proteins (mad cow disease).

## Advanced
P: Describe Whittaker's five kingdom classification and its criteria.
A: Based on cell structure, body organisation, mode of nutrition, reproduction and phylogeny. Monera (prokaryotes), Protista (unicellular eukaryotes), Fungi (heterotrophic, chitin), Plantae (autotrophic), Animalia (heterotrophic, no cell wall).
