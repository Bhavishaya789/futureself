@study
Endocrine glands: hypothalamus, pituitary, pineal, thyroid, parathyroid, thymus, adrenal, pancreas, gonads. Hormones are non-nutrient chemicals acting as intercellular messengers. Hypothalamic releasing and inhibiting hormones. Pituitary: adenohypophysis (GH, PRL, TSH, ACTH, LH, FSH, MSH) and neurohypophysis (oxytocin, vasopressin). Thyroid: T3, T4, calcitonin; parathyroid: PTH. Adrenal medulla: adrenaline and noradrenaline; cortex: glucocorticoids (cortisol), mineralocorticoids (aldosterone), sex steroids. Pancreas: insulin, glucagon. Testis: testosterone; ovary: oestrogen, progesterone. Heart: ANF; kidney: erythropoietin. Mechanism: membrane receptors (cAMP) and intracellular receptors. Disorders: dwarfism, gigantism, acromegaly, goitre, cretinism, Graves, Addison, Cushing, diabetes mellitus.
## Deep dive: Pituitary and hypothalamus
Hypothalamus produces GnRH, TRH, somatostatin. GH deficiency in childhood causes pituitary dwarfism; excess causes gigantism (children) and acromegaly (adults). Prolactin regulates milk production. TSH stimulates thyroid; ACTH stimulates adrenal cortex; LH and FSH regulate gonads. MSH acts on melanocytes. Oxytocin causes contraction of uterus at childbirth and milk ejection; vasopressin (ADH) promotes water reabsorption in the kidney.
## Deep dive: Thyroid, parathyroid, adrenal, pancreas
Thyroid hormones regulate BMR; iodine deficiency causes simple goitre; hypothyroidism in infants gives cretinism; hyperthyroidism gives exophthalmic goitre (Graves disease). Calcitonin lowers blood calcium. PTH raises blood Ca2+ (hypercalcemic), acts on bones and kidneys; vitamin D is also involved. Adrenaline is the emergency hormone (fight or flight). Cortisol: gluconeogenesis, anti-inflammatory. Aldosterone: Na+ reabsorption. Addison disease: hypocortisolism; Cushing: excess cortisol. Insulin (beta cells) reduces blood glucose; glucagon (alpha cells) raises; diabetes mellitus causes glycosuria and ketonuria.
## Deep dive: Gonads, others and mechanism
Testosterone: male secondary sexual characters and spermatogenesis. Oestrogen: development of female characters; progesterone: maintains pregnancy. Pineal: melatonin (circadian rhythm). Thymus: thymosin (T-cell differentiation). GI hormones: gastrin, secretin, CCK, GIP. Hormone action: water soluble (peptide) via membrane receptors, second messengers cAMP, IP3, Ca2+; steroids via intracellular receptors regulating gene expression. Feedback regulation: negative feedback.

## High
P: Distinguish between the actions of insulin and glucagon.
A: Insulin lowers glucose by promoting glycogenesis and uptake; glucagon raises glucose via glycogenolysis and gluconeogenesis.
R: 3 | Insulin
R: 3 | Glucagon
