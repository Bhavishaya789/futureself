# Photosynthesis in Higher Plants
minutes: 45
yt: class 11 biology photosynthesis in higher plants light reaction calvin cycle C4
@study
Photosynthesis converts light energy into chemical energy: 6CO2 + 12H2O -> C6H12O6 + 6H2O + 6O2. Early experiments: Priestley, Ingenhousz, Sachs, Engelmann, Van Niel, Hill, Calvin. Chloroplast pigments: chlorophyll a (bright blue-green), chlorophyll b, xanthophylls, carotenoids. Photosystem I (P700) and II (P680). Light reaction: photolysis of water, Z scheme, photophosphorylation (non-cyclic and cyclic), chemiosmotic hypothesis. Dark reaction (Calvin cycle): carboxylation by RuBisCO, reduction, regeneration of RuBP; C3 plants give 3-PGA. C4 pathway (Hatch and Slack): PEP carboxylase, Kranz anatomy, OAA. Photorespiration. Limiting factors (Blackman).
## Deep dive: Light reaction
Photosystems: PS II has P680 reaction centre and splits water to give O2, protons and electrons. PS I has P700. Electrons from PS II pass via pheophytin, plastoquinone, cytochrome b6f, plastocyanin to PS I, then ferredoxin and NADP reductase to NADPH. Non-cyclic photophosphorylation produces ATP and NADPH; cyclic involves only PS I and gives ATP only. Chemiosmosis: protons accumulate in the lumen, ATP synthase CF0-CF1 makes ATP in stroma.
## Deep dive: Calvin cycle and C4
Calvin cycle in stroma: carboxylation (CO2 + RuBP -> 2 PGA by RuBisCO), reduction (using 2 ATP and 2 NADPH per CO2 to give G3P), regeneration (1 ATP). For one glucose, 6 turns need 18 ATP and 12 NADPH. C4 plants (maize, sugarcane): mesophyll PEP carboxylase fixes CO2 into OAA, converted to malate or aspartate, transported to bundle sheath where decarboxylation releases CO2 for the Calvin cycle; no photorespiration; optimum temperature 30-45 C.
## Deep dive: Photorespiration and factors
RuBisCO has oxygenase activity at high O2; the process occurs in chloroplast, peroxisome, mitochondria and wastes energy. Blackman law of limiting factors: light, CO2 concentration (0.03-0.04% atmospheric), temperature, water. C3 plants respond to rising CO2 up to 450 ppm, C4 saturate about 360 ppm. Red and blue wavelengths are most effective (action spectrum, Engelmann).
