# The Browning Version
minutes: 40
yt: class 11 english hornbill the browning version terence rattigan summary
@study
The Browning Version is an extract from a play by Terence Rattigan. Mr Andrew Crocker-Harris, a Classics teacher, is retiring from a public school because of ill health. He is a strict, unpopular and lonely teacher, nicknamed the Crock, whose wife Millie is unkind and unfaithful. The young pupil Taplow comes to his room for extra work. Taplow gives him a parting gift: a second-hand copy of Browning's translation of the Agamemnon of Aeschylus, inscribed with a line from the play, God from afar looks graciously upon a gentle master. The kindness moves Crocker-Harris to tears, showing that he has feelings after all. Millie mocks him, saying Taplow wanted to get good marks. The scene reveals the contrast between the gentle pupil and the cold teacher, the hidden humanity in the harsh and the cruelty of the unthinking. Themes: appreciation, loneliness, emotional repression and kindness.
## Deep dive: Characters
Andrew Crocker-Harris is a middle-aged Classics master, once an excellent scholar, now disliked by students for his rigid discipline. He hides his emotions. Taplow is a 15-year-old sensitive student who likes the teacher more than others do. Frobisher is a young science master who is cheerful and speaks frankly. Millie is Crocker-Harris's wife, who mocks him and has an affair. The headmaster is mentioned. John Taplow's act of kindness.
## Deep dive: Story and symbols
The gift of a book symbolises respect and humanity. The Agamemnon is a play by Aeschylus, and the translation is by Robert Browning. The inscription shows the pupil's understanding. The tears shows the teacher's hidden emotional life. The contrast between Taplow's sincerity and Millie's cynicism. Humour and irony: Taplow tries to appear indifferent. The dramatic form uses stage directions and dialogue.
## Deep dive: Themes
Kindness changes people; the gap between how others see us and who we are; the need for appreciation; loneliness in old age; the cruelty of mockery; the education system. Language: dialogue, stage directions, irony and understatement.
