Discovering Tut: the Saga Continues by A. R. Williams is about Tutankhamun, the boy pharaoh of Egypt (c. 1333 to 1323 BCE), who became king at about nine and died at about eighteen or nineteen. Howard Carter found his tomb in 1922 in the Valley of the Kings, with golden treasure. The mummy was examined by X-rays in 1968 and scanned by CT in 2005 under Zahi Hawass. Scientists debated the cause of death: the 1968 X-ray suggested a blow to the head and a bone fragment in the skull, indicating murder; the CT scan showed no sign of a blow, but a broken leg that may have become infected, suggesting an accident. Theories of the curse of the pharaohs. Mummification and Egyptian beliefs in afterlife; the discovery lets historians learn about royal life. The text also traces how modern science (forensic examination, 3-D reconstruction) is solving ancient mysteries.
## Deep dive: The discovery
Howard Carter, a British archaeologist, spent years searching and discovered the tomb with steps in November 1922, funded by Lord Carnarvon. The tomb was found almost intact and contained thousands of objects, including the gold mask. Tutankhamun became pharaoh after the religious upheaval of his father Akhenaten; he restored the old gods.
## Deep dive: Investigations of death
1968: Liverpool University team under R. G. Harrison X-rayed the mummy and found a thickening at the skull. 2005: Egyptian team led by Zahi Hawass used CT scanners and found no blow, but evidence of a broken leg possibly infected shortly before death. Modern DNA tests later suggested malaria and a bone disease. The mystery remains. Theories include murder by court officials such as Ay or Horemheb.
## Deep dive: Themes and style
Themes: the persistence of history, scientific curiosity, how technology revises old ideas, the allure of mystery. The article is factual and journalistic, with names, dates and quotations; headings direct the reader. Words: pharaoh, tomb, mummy, artefact, forensic.
