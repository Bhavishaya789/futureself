# The Three Orders
minutes: 40
yt: class 11 history three orders feudalism medieval europe
@study
Between the ninth and sixteenth centuries Western Europe was organised in what the French called three orders: those who fought (the nobility), those who prayed (the clergy) and those who worked (the peasants). This was the feudal society. After the fall of Rome, Europe was fragmented; Charlemagne (crowned emperor by the Pope in 800 CE) united much of it. Feudalism developed in France and then England after the Norman conquest of 1066 under William, Duke of Normandy. Lords granted land (fiefs) to vassals in return for military service, and vassals swore homage. The manor was the lord's estate, with a manor house, a demesne and peasant holdings. Serfs were bound to the land and owed labour services (corvee) and dues, and paid the tithe to the Church. The Church was powerful: monasteries such as Benedictine (founded by St Benedict, 529) and Cistercian houses, and the Pope in Rome. In the eleventh century, population growth, new technology like the heavy plough and the three-field system, and trade led to the growth of towns. The fourteenth century saw the Black Death (1347 to 1350), the Hundred Years' War and the Jacquerie peasant revolt in France (1358) and the English Peasants' Revolt (1381), which weakened serfdom. Strong monarchies emerged in France, England and Spain.
## Deep dive: Feudalism
Feudalism was a system of landholding and obligations between lords and vassals. The king gave land to great lords, who gave it to knights. Knights fought in return. Castles were the centres of power. The code of chivalry guided knights.
## Deep dive: Manor and peasants
Peasants held land from the lord and worked on his demesne. Serfs could not leave the manor. The three-field system raised yields. Water mills and the heavy plough helped. Peasants paid taxes in labour, kind and money.
## Deep dive: Church and towns
The Church owned land and collected the tithe. Monasteries were centres of learning. Towns grew with trade and craft guilds; the saying was that town air makes you free. Cathedrals such as Chartres show Gothic architecture.
