# Changing Cultural Traditions
minutes: 40
yt: class 11 history changing cultural traditions renaissance humanism copernican
@study
Between the fourteenth and seventeenth centuries Europe saw the Renaissance, a rebirth of interest in classical Greek and Roman learning, beginning in the Italian city-states such as Florence, Venice, Genoa, Milan and Rome. Wealthy merchants and patrons like the Medici family supported artists and scholars. Humanism, taught by thinkers such as Petrarch (the father of humanism), emphasised human potential and the study of the classics. Artists like Leonardo da Vinci (Mona Lisa, The Last Supper), Michelangelo (Sistine Chapel, David) and Raphael produced masterpieces, and Brunelleschi designed the dome of Florence cathedral. Literary figures included Dante and Machiavelli. The printing press of Johann Gutenberg (about 1450) spread ideas quickly. The Reformation began in 1517 when Martin Luther posted his 95 theses in Wittenberg against indulgences; John Calvin and Zwingli also led reforms, and Henry VIII founded the Church of England. The Catholic Counter-Reformation included the Society of Jesus (Jesuits) founded by Ignatius Loyola. The Scientific Revolution followed: Copernicus (1543) proposed a sun-centred universe, Galileo used the telescope and was condemned by the Church in 1633, Kepler described elliptical orbits, Vesalius studied anatomy, and Newton explained gravitation in 1687. Exploration also expanded horizons, with Columbus in 1492 and Vasco da Gama reaching India in 1498.
## Deep dive: Renaissance
Italian cities were rich from trade and had links with the Byzantine and Islamic worlds. Humanists studied classical texts. Painters used perspective and realism. Architecture revived classical forms. Women such as Isabella d'Este patronised art.
## Deep dive: Reformation
Luther challenged indulgences and the authority of the Pope. The Bible was translated into German. Protestant churches arose in Germany, Switzerland and England. Religious wars followed. The Catholic Church responded with reforms.
## Deep dive: Scientific revolution
Observation and experiment replaced reliance on ancient authority. Copernicus, Galileo, Kepler and Newton changed astronomy and physics. Printing and universities spread new knowledge. These changes shaped the modern outlook.
