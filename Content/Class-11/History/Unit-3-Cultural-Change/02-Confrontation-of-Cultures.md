# Confrontation of Cultures
minutes: 40
yt: class 11 history confrontation of cultures aztecs mayas incas columbus
@study
In the late fifteenth century European voyages brought contact between Europe and the Americas, with devastating results for American peoples. Christopher Columbus, sailing for Spain, reached the Caribbean in 1492, thinking he had reached the Indies; the people he met were called Indians. Portugal, under Prince Henry the Navigator, explored the African coast, and Vasco da Gama reached Calicut in 1498. Amerigo Vespucci showed that the land was a new continent, which was named America. Three great civilisations then existed. The Aztecs of Mexico, with the capital Tenochtitlan on Lake Texcoco, were conquered by Hernan Cortes in 1521. The Mayas of Central America built cities and temples and developed a calendar and writing. The Incas of Peru, with the capital Cuzco and a vast road network, were conquered by Francisco Pizarro in 1532. The Spanish and Portuguese came for gold, God and glory. Diseases such as smallpox killed millions of Native Americans. Spanish rule created the encomienda system of forced labour; African slaves were brought to work on plantations and mines, beginning the Atlantic slave trade. The Treaty of Tordesillas in 1494 divided the new lands between Spain and Portugal. Silver from Potosi flowed to Europe. Later the English settled in North America at Jamestown in 1607. The Columbian Exchange moved crops like potato, maize and tomato to Europe, and horses, sugar and diseases to the Americas.
## Deep dive: Voyages
Advances in ships and navigation, the compass, and the desire for trade with Asia, wealth and spread of Christianity drove exploration. Spain and Portugal led. Portugal found the sea route to India. Spain funded Columbus.
## Deep dive: American civilisations
The Aztecs, Mayas and Incas had advanced agriculture, cities and states. Aztecs practised human sacrifice and built causeways in Tenochtitlan. Mayas had a calendar and writing. Incas used quipu cords and terraced farming.
## Deep dive: Consequences
Conquest, disease and forced labour destroyed native societies. The slave trade grew. Europe gained silver, gold and new foods. The world economy changed. Debates in Spain, such as by Las Casas, questioned the treatment of the Indians.
