# Displacing Indigenous Peoples
minutes: 40
yt: class 11 history displacing indigenous peoples native americans australia aborigines
@study
From the sixteenth century European settlers in North America and Australia displaced the native peoples. In North America, the Native Americans (called Red Indians by Europeans) lived in societies such as the Iroquois, Cherokee, Sioux and Apache. The British settled from Jamestown in 1607 and the Pilgrim Fathers reached New England in 1620 on the Mayflower. The Thirteen Colonies became the United States after the Declaration of Independence (1776). The nation expanded westwards; the Louisiana Purchase of 1803 doubled its size. Policies like the Indian Removal Act (1830) forced tribes onto reservations, and the Trail of Tears (1838) saw Cherokee marched west. The Homestead Act (1862) gave land to settlers, railways crossed the plains, buffalo were hunted nearly to extinction, and battles like Wounded Knee (1890) ended resistance. In Canada Europeans displaced native peoples too. In Australia, the Aboriginal people had lived for about 60,000 years before Captain James Cook claimed the east coast for Britain in 1770. The first British convict settlement was set up at Sydney in 1788. Settlers took land for sheep, farming and gold after 1851. Aboriginal people died through disease and violence, and children were removed from families in the policy of the Stolen Generations. Land rights were recognised only later, as in the Mabo decision of 1992. Europeans justified displacement through ideas of civilisation and progress, while indigenous peoples had different ideas of land as shared and sacred.
## Deep dive: North America
Native societies were hunters and farmers with rich cultures and a deep link to land. European settlement brought conflict, disease and treaties often broken. The government pushed tribes west to reservations. Cattle ranching and mining spread.
## Deep dive: Australia
Aboriginal people had complex clan systems and Dreaming stories. British settlers occupied land treating it as empty, a doctrine called terra nullius. Frontier violence and disease reduced the population. Protest and court cases later won rights.
## Deep dive: Perspectives
Settlers saw frontier as opportunity; indigenous peoples saw loss. Museums and history writing were shaped by settlers. Modern debates include apologies, land return and recognition.
