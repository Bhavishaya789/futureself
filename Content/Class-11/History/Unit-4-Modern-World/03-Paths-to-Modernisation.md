# Paths to Modernisation
minutes: 40
yt: class 11 history paths to modernisation china japan meiji restoration opium war
@study
In the nineteenth and twentieth centuries Japan and China followed different paths to modernity. Japan was ruled by the Tokugawa shoguns from 1603, who followed a policy of isolation (sakoku), with the emperor in Kyoto. In 1853 the American Commodore Matthew Perry arrived with warships and the Treaty of Kanagawa in 1854 opened Japanese ports. The shogunate collapsed and in 1868 the Meiji Restoration restored power to the emperor Meiji, who moved the capital to Tokyo. Japan's slogan was Fukoku Kyohei, rich country, strong army. The Meiji government abolished the feudal domains, introduced conscription, a new education system, railways, factories and a constitution in 1889. Japan became the first Asian industrial power, defeated China in 1894 to 1895 and Russia in 1904 to 1905, and annexed Korea in 1910. Thinkers like Fukuzawa Yukichi urged learning from the West. In China, the Qing dynasty faced the Opium Wars: the First (1839 to 1842) ended with the Treaty of Nanjing, ceding Hong Kong; the Second (1856 to 1860) followed. The Taiping Rebellion (1850 to 1864) led by Hong Xiuquan killed millions. Reformers like Kang Youwei urged change in the Hundred Days Reform (1898), and the Boxer Uprising (1900) opposed foreigners. Sun Yat-sen led the Revolution of 1911, ending the empire; his Three Principles were nationalism, democracy and livelihood. The May Fourth Movement of 1919 stressed new culture. The Communist Party was founded in 1921, and under Mao Zedong won the civil war, founding the People's Republic of China in 1949, followed by the Great Leap Forward (1958) and Cultural Revolution (1966). Deng Xiaoping launched reforms from 1978.
## Deep dive: Japan
Isolation under the shoguns ended with Perry. The Meiji leaders modernised the army, schools, economy and state. Japan became an imperial power. Zaibatsu firms such as Mitsubishi led industry. Militarism grew in the 1930s.
## Deep dive: China
The Qing faced foreign powers and unequal treaties. Opium trade drained silver and led to war. Reform failed under the Qing. The 1911 Revolution ended the dynasty. The Nationalists (Kuomintang) and Communists later fought for power.
## Deep dive: Comparison
Japan modernised from above under a strong state and avoided colonisation. China was weakened by foreign pressure and internal rebellion. Both searched for national strength, but with different results.
