# The Industrial Revolution
minutes: 40
yt: class 11 history industrial revolution britain steam engine factories
@study
The Industrial Revolution began in Britain from about 1760 and later spread to Europe, the United States and Japan. It changed production from handwork to machines and from homes to factories. Britain had advantages: coal and iron deposits, colonial markets and raw materials, capital, an agricultural revolution with enclosures, a growing population and good rivers and canals. Key inventions in textiles were John Kay's flying shuttle (1733), James Hargreaves' spinning jenny (1764), Richard Arkwright's water frame (1769), Samuel Crompton's mule and Edmund Cartwright's power loom (1785). Thomas Newcomen built an early steam engine; James Watt improved it in 1769, and Matthew Boulton helped to produce it. Abraham Darby used coke for iron smelting. George Stephenson's locomotive Rocket (1829) and the Liverpool and Manchester Railway (1830) began the railway age. Roads and canals improved transport. Factory towns like Manchester and Birmingham grew rapidly. Workers, including women and children, worked long hours in poor conditions; Factory Acts such as that of 1833 limited child labour. Humphry Davy invented the safety lamp for miners. Luddites broke machines between 1811 and 1817. The Chartist movement demanded political rights. Social thinkers such as Robert Owen and Karl Marx criticised industrial capitalism. Industrialisation also fuelled imperialism and, in India, harmed handloom industries.
## Deep dive: Causes
Britain had capital from trade, coal and iron, new agricultural methods and colonies for markets. Inventors responded to demand for cloth. Cotton from India and America was the key raw material. Government policy favoured trade.
## Deep dive: Technology
Textile machines, the steam engine, iron and coal and railways formed the base. Factories used power machines and division of labour. Steamships followed. These raised output and cut costs.
## Deep dive: Social effects
Urbanisation brought slums, disease and pollution. Child labour was widespread. Trade unions, reform laws and later welfare followed. A new working class and industrial middle class emerged. Wealth grew, but unequally.
