# From the Beginning of Time

@study
Human history begins in Africa, where the earliest hominids appeared about 5.6 million years ago. The main stages are the Australopithecines (about 5.6 to 2.5 million years ago), Homo habilis (about 2.5 to 2 million years ago), Homo erectus (about 1.8 million years ago) and Homo sapiens (modern humans, about 195,000 years ago). Charles Darwin's On the Origin of Species (1859) and The Descent of Man (1871) gave the theory of evolution by natural selection. Early humans were hunter-gatherers who made stone tools, learned to use fire, developed speech and later made cave paintings, as at Altamira in Spain and Lascaux and Chauvet in France. The Stone Age is divided into the Palaeolithic, Mesolithic and Neolithic periods. Around 10,000 to 12,000 years ago the last Ice Age ended, climates warmed, and people began to domesticate plants and animals, which led to agriculture, settled life and the first villages. Sources include fossils, tools, paintings and the study of living hunter-gatherer societies such as the Hadza.
## Deep dive: Evolution of humans
Primates appeared about 36 million years ago, hominoids about 24 million and hominids about 5.6 million. Hominids walk on two legs, have larger brains and make tools. Australopithecus fossils such as Lucy were found in Ethiopia. Homo erectus used fire and spread out of Africa. Homo sapiens developed larger brains and complex language; the Neanderthals lived in Europe.
## Deep dive: Ways of life
The Palaeolithic way of life was hunting and gathering in small mobile groups. Tools were made of stone, bone and wood. Fire was used for cooking, warmth and protection. Art, rituals and burials show symbolic thought. The Neolithic brought farming, pottery and permanent villages.
## Deep dive: Sources and debates
Archaeologists use stone tools, bones, hearths and cave art. Scientists use radiocarbon and potassium-argon dating. Debates include the origin of language, the cause of Neanderthal extinction and the role of gender in hunting and gathering.
