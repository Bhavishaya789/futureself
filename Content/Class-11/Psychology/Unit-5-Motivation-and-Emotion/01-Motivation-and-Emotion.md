# Motivation and Emotion
minutes: 40
yt: class 11 psychology motivation emotion maslow hierarchy james lange emotional intelligence
@study
Motivation is the force that energises and directs behaviour toward a goal. It involves needs (a lack or deficit), drives (internal states that push to satisfy needs) and incentives (external objects or goals that attract). Types of motives: biological motives (hunger, thirst, sleep, sex, avoidance of pain) and psychosocial motives (achievement, affiliation, power, curiosity). Theories: instinct theory (behaviour driven by innate tendencies), drive-reduction theory (Clark Hull; behaviour reduces tension), arousal theory (optimal level of arousal; Yerkes-Dodson law) and incentive theory. Maslow's hierarchy of needs places needs in a pyramid: physiological, safety, love and belongingness, esteem and self-actualisation; lower needs must be satisfied before higher ones; the first four are deficiency needs and self-actualisation is a growth need. Hunger: controlled by the hypothalamus (lateral hypothalamus starts eating; ventromedial hypothalamus stops it), blood glucose level and stomach contractions. Thirst is regulated by water balance. Achievement motivation (David McClelland): need for achievement (n-Ach) is the desire to excel; people with high n-Ach set moderately difficult goals, seek feedback and take personal responsibility. Affiliation and power are other needs. Intrinsic motivation (internal satisfaction) versus extrinsic motivation (rewards). Conflicts of motives: approach-approach, avoidance-avoidance and approach-avoidance. Emotion is a complex state with physiological arousal, subjective feeling and expressive behaviour. Components: cognitive, physiological and behavioural. Theories: James-Lange (bodily arousal comes first and the emotion follows), Cannon-Bard (arousal and emotion occur simultaneously through the thalamus), Schachter-Singer two-factor theory (arousal plus cognitive label) and Lazarus's cognitive appraisal theory. The limbic system, especially the amygdala, is central to emotions such as fear. Basic emotions (Paul Ekman): happiness, sadness, fear, anger, surprise and disgust, recognised universally through facial expressions. Cultural display rules govern how emotions are expressed. Emotional intelligence (Salovey and Mayer; popularised by Daniel Goleman) is the ability to perceive, understand and regulate emotions in oneself and others; it includes self-awareness, self-regulation, motivation, empathy and social skills. Managing negative emotions: relaxation, cognitive reappraisal, positive thinking, social support, exercise and yoga. Stress, anxiety and anger affect health. Positive emotions help well-being. Emotions and motivation are linked. Lie detectors (polygraph) measure physiological arousal.
## Deep dive: Motivation
Motives arise from needs and are directed by incentives. Biological motives support survival; psychosocial motives are learned. Maslow's hierarchy arranges them by priority.
## Deep dive: Emotion
Emotions combine arousal, feeling and expression. The theories differ on the order of arousal and emotion. Culture shapes display rules.
## Deep dive: Emotional intelligence
Emotional intelligence helps in relationships and success. Skills can be developed. Managing negative emotions protects health.
