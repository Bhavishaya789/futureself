# The Bases of Human Behaviour

@study
Human behaviour has biological and cultural bases. Evolution: Darwin's theory of natural selection, survival of the fittest, and adaptation. Genes are units of heredity found on chromosomes; humans have 23 pairs of chromosomes (46) in each cell; DNA carries genetic code. Genotype is the genetic make-up and phenotype is the observable characteristics; both heredity and environment interact. The nervous system has the Central Nervous System (CNS: brain and spinal cord) and the Peripheral Nervous System (somatic and autonomic; the autonomic includes sympathetic, which arouses the body, and parasympathetic, which calms it). The neuron is the basic unit of the nervous system with dendrites (receive), cell body or soma, axon (transmits) and axon terminals; myelin sheath speeds conduction; the gap between neurons is the synapse, crossed by neurotransmitters such as acetylcholine, dopamine, serotonin and GABA. The brain: hindbrain (medulla, pons, cerebellum), midbrain, and forebrain (thalamus, hypothalamus, limbic system, cerebrum). The cerebrum has two hemispheres joined by the corpus callosum and four lobes: frontal (thinking, planning, movement), parietal (touch), temporal (hearing, memory) and occipital (vision). The left hemisphere is associated with language, logic and analysis; the right with spatial, music and intuition. Broca's area (speech production) and Wernicke's area (comprehension) are in the left hemisphere. The cerebellum controls balance and coordination; the medulla controls heartbeat and breathing; the hypothalamus regulates hunger, thirst, temperature and emotion; the thalamus relays sensory signals. The endocrine system has glands secreting hormones into blood: pituitary (the master gland), thyroid, adrenal (adrenaline), pancreas (insulin) and gonads. Methods of studying the brain: EEG, CT, MRI, fMRI and PET scans. Culture is the human-made part of the environment. Enculturation is the process of learning one's culture; socialisation is learning social rules; acculturation is change due to contact with another culture. Culture shapes behaviour and values; biology and culture interact. Behaviour is also influenced by the social environment and family. Human beings have adapted using tools and language. The brain shows plasticity. Reflex action is an automatic response controlled by the spinal cord. The reticular formation controls alertness.
## Deep dive: Biological bases
Behaviour depends on genes, neurons, brain structure and hormones. Neurons communicate by electrical and chemical signals. The brain has specialised areas.
## Deep dive: Brain structure
The hindbrain handles basic life functions; the forebrain handles thinking and emotion. The two hemispheres specialise but cooperate. Damage to areas leads to specific loss of function.
## Deep dive: Culture and behaviour
Culture is learned. Enculturation passes culture across generations. Acculturation changes cultures in contact. Biology provides capacity and culture gives direction.
