# Sensory, Attentional and Perceptual Processes
minutes: 40
yt: class 11 psychology sensation attention perception gestalt depth illusion
@study
Sensation is the process by which sense organs receive stimuli from the environment and convert them into neural impulses; perception is the process of organising and interpreting sensations to give them meaning. Sense organs: eye (vision), ear (audition), nose (smell), tongue (taste), skin (touch, pressure, temperature, pain), and the kinesthetic and vestibular senses (movement and balance). In the eye, light passes through the cornea, pupil and lens and forms an image on the retina, where rods (about 120 million; dim light, no colour) and cones (about 6 million; bright light, colour, detail) convert it to neural signals that go through the optic nerve to the occipital lobe. Colour vision theories: trichromatic (Young–Helmholtz) and opponent process (Hering). In the ear, sound waves vibrate the eardrum, the ossicles (hammer, anvil, stirrup) and the cochlea, and are carried by the auditory nerve. The absolute threshold is the minimum stimulus intensity detected 50 percent of the time; the difference threshold or just noticeable difference (JND) is the minimum difference detected; Weber's law states that the JND is a constant proportion of the original stimulus. Sensory adaptation is the decreasing sensitivity to unchanging stimuli. Attention is the focusing of mental resources on selected stimuli. Types: selective attention (cocktail party effect), sustained attention (vigilance) and divided attention. Factors: external (size, intensity, contrast, movement, novelty) and internal (interest, motivation, mental set). Perceptual organisation: Gestalt laws such as proximity, similarity, closure, continuity and figure-ground (Rubin's vase). Perception of depth uses binocular cues (retinal disparity, convergence) and monocular cues (relative size, interposition, linear perspective, texture gradient, light and shadow, motion parallax). Perceptual constancies: size, shape and brightness constancy. Perception of movement includes the phi phenomenon (apparent motion), as used in films. Illusions are misperceptions, such as the Muller-Lyer illusion, Ponzo illusion and Muller-Lyer; hallucinations are perceptions without stimuli. Perceptual set, motivation, culture and learning influence perception: people from different cultures perceive illusions differently (Segall et al). Extra-sensory perception (ESP) lacks scientific proof. Subliminal perception occurs below the threshold of awareness. Attention deficit and distraction affect learning. Signal detection theory explains detection of weak signals. Selective attention explains how we filter noise.
## Deep dive: Sensory processes
Sense organs are transducers. Rods work in dim light; cones in bright light and colour. Thresholds measure sensitivity. Adaptation reduces response to constant stimuli.
## Deep dive: Attention
Attention is selective and limited. External and internal factors direct it. Practice reduces effort in divided attention.
## Deep dive: Perception
Gestalt laws explain grouping. Depth cues come from both eyes and one eye. Culture and experience shape perception. Illusions show that perception is constructed.
