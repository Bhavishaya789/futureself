# Human Development

@study
Development is the pattern of progressive, orderly and predictable changes that begin at conception and continue throughout life. Growth refers to physical increase in size, while maturation is the unfolding of genetically programmed changes. Principles: development is continuous, follows a sequence, proceeds from general to specific, is influenced by both nature and nurture, and shows individual differences. Prenatal development has three stages: the germinal stage (conception to two weeks), the embryonic stage (two weeks to eight weeks, when organs form) and the fetal stage (from eight weeks to birth). Teratogens such as alcohol, tobacco and certain drugs harm the foetus. Infancy: reflexes (sucking, grasping), motor development and attachment. John Bowlby described attachment; Harlow's monkey experiments showed the importance of contact comfort. Piaget's theory of cognitive development: schemas, assimilation and accommodation, and four stages: sensorimotor (0 to 2 years; object permanence), preoperational (2 to 7 years; egocentrism, symbolic thought, centration), concrete operational (7 to 11 years; conservation, logical thinking about concrete objects) and formal operational (11 years and above; abstract and hypothetical thinking). Vygotsky's sociocultural theory stresses the role of social interaction, language and the zone of proximal development (ZPD) with scaffolding. Erikson's psychosocial theory has eight stages: trust vs mistrust (infancy), autonomy vs shame and doubt, initiative vs guilt, industry vs inferiority, identity vs role confusion (adolescence), intimacy vs isolation, generativity vs stagnation, and integrity vs despair (old age). Kohlberg's moral development: preconventional (punishment and reward), conventional (conformity and law and order) and postconventional (social contract and universal principles); the Heinz dilemma was used. Adolescence (about 12 to 19 years) is a period of rapid physical change, puberty, identity formation, and emotional storm and stress (G Stanley Hall). Challenges for adolescents: peer pressure, identity crisis, risky behaviour and substance use. Adulthood and old age: career, relationships, parenting and ageing. Havighurst's developmental tasks. Parenting styles: authoritative, authoritarian and permissive (Baumrind). Factors influencing development: heredity, environment, nutrition, family and culture. Nature-nurture debate and the critical period: Lorenz's imprinting in geese. Early childhood care and education matters. Socialisation in the family and school helps development.
## Deep dive: Principles and stages
Development is lifelong and multidirectional. Prenatal, infancy, childhood, adolescence, adulthood and old age are the main periods. Each has tasks and challenges.
## Deep dive: Theories
Piaget describes cognitive stages; Erikson psychosocial crises; Kohlberg moral reasoning; Vygotsky the social context of learning. Each adds a different view.
## Deep dive: Adolescence
It is a bridge between childhood and adulthood. Identity and independence are central. Support from family and school helps adolescents handle stress.
