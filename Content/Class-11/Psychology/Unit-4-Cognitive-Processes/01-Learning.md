# Learning
minutes: 40
yt: class 11 psychology learning classical conditioning operant observational insight
@study
Learning is a relatively permanent change in behaviour or behavioural potential produced by experience. It is not due to maturation, fatigue or drugs. Paradigms: classical conditioning, operant conditioning, observational learning, cognitive learning and verbal learning. Classical conditioning (Ivan Pavlov): a neutral stimulus paired with an unconditioned stimulus (UCS, which naturally produces a response) becomes a conditioned stimulus (CS) that produces a conditioned response (CR). In Pavlov's dog experiment, food (UCS) caused salivation (UCR); a bell (neutral) paired with food became a CS producing salivation (CR). Concepts: acquisition (learning the CS-UCS link), extinction (CR weakens when CS is presented without UCS), spontaneous recovery (reappearance after rest), generalisation (response to similar stimuli) and discrimination (response only to the CS). Determinants: time relationship (forward conditioning with the CS just before the UCS), intensity of stimuli and the type of UCS. Watson and Rayner's Little Albert experiment showed conditioned fear. Classical conditioning explains emotional learning and phobias. Operant (instrumental) conditioning (B F Skinner, building on Thorndike's law of effect): behaviour is shaped by its consequences. Reinforcement increases behaviour: positive reinforcement adds a pleasant stimulus; negative reinforcement removes an aversive stimulus. Punishment decreases behaviour. Primary reinforcers satisfy biological needs (food); secondary reinforcers gain value through association (money, praise). Schedules of reinforcement: continuous and partial: fixed ratio (reward after a fixed number of responses), variable ratio (unpredictable number, gives the highest and most persistent responding as in gambling), fixed interval (after a fixed time) and variable interval. Shaping rewards successive approximations. Skinner box was used. Observational learning (Albert Bandura): learning by watching others (modelling); the Bobo doll experiment showed children imitate aggression. Stages: attention, retention, reproduction and motivation. Cognitive learning: insight learning (Kohler's chimpanzee Sultan) is sudden understanding of a solution; latent learning (Tolman's rat maze) is learning without reinforcement shown later. Verbal learning: paired associates, serial learning and free recall. Skill learning: acquired through practice; stages are cognitive, associative and autonomous. Factors influencing learning: motivation, readiness, practice, nature of material, meaning and learning style. Transfer of learning: positive, negative and zero. Learning disabilities include dyslexia, dysgraphia and dyscalculia. Learning curves show progress and plateau.
## Deep dive: Classical conditioning
Learning by association. Pavlov's experiments showed salivation to a bell. Extinction and spontaneous recovery show how conditioned responses fade and return.
## Deep dive: Operant conditioning
Behaviour is controlled by its consequences. Reinforcement strengthens; punishment weakens. Variable ratio schedules produce persistent responding.
## Deep dive: Observational and cognitive learning
People learn by watching models. Insight and latent learning show that mental processes matter. Practice, motivation and meaningful material help learning.
