# Thinking

yt: class 11 psychology thinking problem solving reasoning creativity concepts language
@study
Thinking is a mental process in which we manipulate information to form concepts, solve problems, reason and make decisions. It is a higher mental process that involves representations such as images, symbols and concepts. Concepts are mental categories of objects, events or ideas that share features; prototypes are the best examples of a concept (a sparrow for a bird). Language is a system of symbols and rules used for communication; Benjamin Whorf and Edward Sapir proposed the linguistic relativity hypothesis that language influences thought. Problem solving is thinking directed toward a goal. Steps: identifying the problem, representing it, selecting a strategy, carrying out and evaluating. Strategies: trial and error, algorithms (step-by-step rules that guarantee a solution), heuristics (mental shortcuts such as means-end analysis and working backward) and insight (sudden realisation). Obstacles to problem solving: mental set (tendency to use previously successful strategies), functional fixedness (seeing objects only for their usual function, as shown by Duncker's candle problem), lack of motivation, and confirmation bias. Reasoning is drawing conclusions from facts: deductive reasoning goes from general to specific (all humans are mortal), and inductive reasoning goes from specific to general. Decision making is choosing among alternatives; it can be influenced by heuristics and biases such as availability and anchoring (Kahneman and Tversky). Creative thinking produces original and useful ideas. J P Guilford distinguished convergent thinking (one correct answer) from divergent thinking (many possible answers), which is the basis of creativity. Features of divergent thinking: fluency, flexibility, originality and elaboration. Graham Wallas described four stages of creativity: preparation, incubation, illumination and verification. Characteristics of creative persons: openness, curiosity, independence and risk-taking. Barriers: fear of failure, conformity and habit. Strategies to foster creativity: brainstorming, mind mapping and lateral thinking (Edward de Bono). Critical thinking involves evaluating evidence and arguments. Thought processes can be conscious or unconscious. Development of thinking: Piaget's stages. Language and thought: Vygotsky said that thought and language develop together. Culture influences thinking. Training in thinking skills improves learning.
## Deep dive: Concepts and language
Thinking uses concepts and language. Prototype theory explains categorisation. Language shapes how we describe the world.
## Deep dive: Problem solving
Algorithms are reliable but slow; heuristics are quick but fallible. Insight shows sudden restructuring. Mental set and functional fixedness block solutions.
## Deep dive: Creativity
Creativity needs divergent thinking and the four stages. Brainstorming and open attitudes foster it. Critical thinking balances creativity.
