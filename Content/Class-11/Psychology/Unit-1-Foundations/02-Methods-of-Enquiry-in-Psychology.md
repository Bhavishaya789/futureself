# Methods of Enquiry in Psychology
minutes: 40
yt: class 11 psychology methods of enquiry experiment observation survey case study correlation
@study
Psychology uses scientific methods to gather reliable information. Goals of psychological enquiry: description, prediction, explanation, control and application. Steps in scientific research: conceptualising a problem, collecting data, drawing conclusions, revising research conclusions and reporting. A hypothesis is a testable statement about the relationship between variables. A variable is any characteristic that can change. The independent variable (IV) is manipulated by the researcher; the dependent variable (DV) is the response measured; extraneous variables are other variables that may affect the DV and must be controlled. In an experiment, the experimental group receives the treatment and the control group does not. Experiments allow cause-and-effect conclusions; they can be laboratory experiments (high control), field experiments and quasi-experiments. Observation is the systematic and careful watching of behaviour, either naturalistic (in natural settings) or controlled (in laboratory); it can be participant or non-participant. Survey research collects information from a large sample by questionnaires or interviews. Psychological tests measure ability, personality and attitude and must be reliable (consistent) and valid (measure what they claim); standardised tests have norms. Case study is an in-depth study of a single individual or group (as used by Freud). Interviews may be structured or unstructured. Correlational research examines the relationship between variables; the correlation coefficient ranges from -1 to +1: positive means both increase together, negative means one increases as the other decreases, and zero means no relationship; correlation does not prove causation. A population is the entire group of interest and a sample is the subset studied; sampling should be random to avoid bias. Data analysis may be quantitative (numbers, statistics) or qualitative (themes, descriptions). Limitations: psychological data are variable; the observer may affect the participants; participants may give socially desirable answers; self-reports may be biased. Ethical guidelines: informed consent, voluntary participation, confidentiality, no harm and debriefing, and protection of animals; the Indian Council of Medical Research and the American Psychological Association give such guidelines. Basic and applied research, longitudinal and cross-sectional studies, and cross-cultural research are other terms. Psychology tries to be objective and to avoid bias from the researcher's expectations.
## Deep dive: Variables and experiment
Experiments manipulate the IV and measure the DV while controlling others. Random assignment forms equivalent groups. Experiments show cause and effect.
## Deep dive: Other methods
Observation records natural behaviour; surveys reach many people; tests measure traits; case studies give depth. Each has strengths and weaknesses.
## Deep dive: Correlation and ethics
Correlation shows association but not cause. Researchers must respect participants. Consent and confidentiality are basic.
