# India: Natural Vegetation and Soils
minutes: 40
yt: class 11 geography india natural vegetation forest types soils
@study
Natural vegetation refers to plant cover that has grown naturally without human help. India has a great variety of vegetation because of its relief, soil, climate and rainfall. The forest area is about 21 percent of the country, according to the India State of Forest Report. The forests are classified as tropical evergreen (rainfall over 200 cm; Western Ghats, Assam, Andaman; mahogany, ebony, rosewood), tropical deciduous (monsoon forests; 70 to 200 cm; teak, sal, sandalwood, shisham), tropical thorn (below 70 cm; babool, acacia, cactus; Rajasthan, Gujarat), montane (altitude-dependent; temperate forests with oak, chestnut and deodar; sub-alpine with fir, juniper; alpine meadows) and mangrove or littoral forests (Sundarbans: sundari tree). Wildlife includes the Indian elephant, one-horned rhinoceros, Asiatic lion (Gir), tiger, snow leopard and others. Biosphere reserves include Nilgiri, Sundarbans, Gulf of Mannar and Nanda Devi. Soil is the thin layer of loose material on the earth, formed by weathering, and its factors are parent rock, relief, climate, vegetation and time. Soils of India as classified by ICAR: alluvial (the most widespread, about 40 percent; Indo-Gangetic; fertile; khadar and bhangar), black or regur (Deccan lava; cotton; retains moisture; self-ploughing), red and yellow (iron oxide; crystalline rocks), laterite (high rainfall; leached; tea, coffee, cashew), arid (desert), saline, peaty and marshy, and forest soils. Soil erosion by water and wind is controlled by contour ploughing, terracing, shelter belts and afforestation. Conservation includes strip cropping and check dams.
## Deep dive: Vegetation types
Tropical evergreen: dense, tall, multi-layered, no dry season. Deciduous: moist (Sal, teak) and dry (above 70 cm). Thorn: scrub, long roots, small leaves. Montane: change with altitude as per temperature. Mangrove: tidal creeks, breathing roots. Wildlife sanctuaries, national parks and reserve forests protect them.
## Deep dive: Soils
Alluvial: potash rich, phosphorus-poor; wheat, rice, sugarcane. Black: clayey, lime and iron rich; cotton, called the black cotton soil. Red: iron oxide, low in nitrogen. Laterite: leaching in heavy rain, acidic. Arid: sandy, saline, low humus. Peaty: Kerala (Kari). Forest soils: hills.
## Deep dive: Erosion and conservation
Gully erosion creates ravines (Chambal), sheet erosion removes topsoil. Remedies: contour bunding, terracing, strip cropping, shelter belts, check dams, afforestation and regulating grazing. Soil Health Card scheme.
