# Natural Hazards and Disasters
minutes: 40
yt: class 11 geography natural hazards disasters earthquake flood cyclone drought landslide management
@study
A hazard is a natural or human-induced event with the potential to cause harm; a disaster is a sudden, calamitous event that causes great loss of life and property. Disasters are natural (earthquakes, floods, cyclones, tsunamis, droughts, landslides, avalanches, volcanoes) or human-made (industrial accidents, fires, nuclear accidents and the Bhopal gas tragedy). Earthquakes are measured on the Richter scale; India is divided into seismic zones II to V, with Zone V the most severe. Tsunamis follow undersea quakes; the 2004 Indian Ocean tsunami affected the Andaman and Nicobar, Tamil Nadu and Andhra Pradesh. Cyclones form over warm seas; the east coast is more affected, in Odisha and Andhra Pradesh, especially in October-November. Floods occur in the Ganga-Brahmaputra basins due to heavy rain, deforestation and siltation. Droughts are meteorological, hydrological, agricultural and ecological, and the arid and semi-arid areas of India are drought-prone. Landslides occur in the Himalaya, Western Ghats and Nilgiri, triggered by heavy rain, earthquakes and road building. Disaster management has three phases: pre-disaster (prevention, preparedness, mitigation), during disaster (response, rescue) and post-disaster (recovery, rehabilitation). In India, the Disaster Management Act 2005 created the NDMA, SDMAs and DDMAs, and the NDRF. The Yokohama Strategy (1994) and the Sendai Framework (2015-2030) are global frameworks. Community-based disaster management and early warning systems reduce vulnerability.
## Deep dive: Types of disasters
Earthquake: Latur 1993, Bhuj 2001, Kashmir 2005. Cyclone: Odisha super cyclone 1999, Phailin 2013, Amphan 2020. Flood: Kerala 2018, Bihar 2008 (Kosi). Drought: Rajasthan, Marathwada. Landslide: Kedarnath 2013. Tsunami: 2004. Avalanche: Siachen. Heat wave and cold wave. Industrial: Bhopal 1984, Chernobyl 1986.
## Deep dive: Vulnerability and risk
Vulnerability depends on poverty, population density, poor buildings and lack of awareness. Risk = hazard x vulnerability / capacity. Mapping includes hazard zonation, seismic microzonation and flood maps.
## Deep dive: Management
Preparedness: early warning, drills, building codes, mock exercises. Response: search and rescue, relief camps, medical aid. Recovery: rebuilding, livelihood, insurance. NDMA headquartered in Delhi, headed by the Prime Minister. Mitigation measures: embankments, afforestation, earthquake-resistant buildings, cyclone shelters, rainwater harvesting.
