# India: Drainage System
minutes: 40

@study
The drainage system of India is divided by the Indian watershed into the Arabian Sea drainage (about 23 percent) and the Bay of Bengal drainage (77 percent). By origin, rivers are Himalayan (perennial; fed by snow and rain; long courses with gorges, meanders and deltas) and Peninsular (seasonal; shorter, mostly fed by rain). Drainage patterns include dendritic, trellis, rectangular and radial. The Himalayan rivers are the Indus, Ganga and Brahmaputra systems. The Indus rises near Mansarovar lake in Tibet, flows through Ladakh, enters Pakistan and falls in the Arabian Sea; its tributaries include the Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, Beas and Sutlej. The Ganga rises as the Bhagirathi from Gangotri glacier, meets the Alaknanda at Devprayag, and flows through Haridwar, Prayagraj, Patna and Farakka; tributaries include the Yamuna, Ghaghara, Gandak, Kosi, Son and Chambal. In Bangladesh it joins the Brahmaputra and the combined stream (Meghna) enters the Bay of Bengal, forming the Sundarbans delta. The Brahmaputra rises in Tibet as the Tsangpo and enters India as the Dihang, where it is wide and braided. Peninsular rivers: east flowing (Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri) form deltas; west flowing (Narmada, Tapi) fall in estuaries. Godavari is the longest peninsular river (Dakshin Ganga). Lakes include Wular, Dal, Chilika and Sambhar. River uses: irrigation, hydropower, navigation. River pollution is addressed by the Ganga Action Plan and Namami Gange. Interlinking of rivers is a proposal.
## Deep dive: Himalayan rivers
Indus: 2880 km; Indus Waters Treaty 1960 gave Ravi, Beas and Sutlej to India. Ganga: 2525 km; Yamuna joins at Prayagraj; Kosi called the Sorrow of Bihar. Brahmaputra: 2900 km; flows through Arunachal Pradesh and Assam; floods frequent; Majuli is the largest river island.
## Deep dive: Peninsular rivers
Godavari 1465 km, Krishna 1400 km, Kaveri 800 km, Mahanadi 851 km; Narmada flows in a rift valley and forms Dhuandhar falls; Tapi also rift valley. Peninsular rivers have fixed courses, are seasonal and have no deep meanders. Western Ghats form the watershed.
## Deep dive: Lakes and use
Lakes: freshwater (Dal, Wular), saline (Sambhar, Chilika is a lagoon). Uses: irrigation, power, navigation, fisheries and tourism. Problems: pollution, siltation, floods. Remedy: Ganga Action Plan (1985), Namami Gange (2014), river linking.
