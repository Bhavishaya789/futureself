# World Climate and Climate Change

@study
Climate is the average weather over about 30 years. Climatic classification by Koppen (1918, modified 1931) uses letter codes: A for tropical humid, B for dry, C for warm temperate, D for cold snow forest, E for polar and H for highland. Koppen's subtypes use second and third letters: f (no dry season), m (monsoon), w (dry winter), s (dry summer), W (desert), S (steppe), T (tundra), F (ice cap). Thornthwaite proposed a classification based on precipitation effectiveness and potential evapotranspiration. The climatic groups include Af (tropical wet), Am (monsoon), Aw (savanna), BWh (hot desert), BSh (steppe), Cs (Mediterranean), Cfa (humid subtropical), Cfb (marine west coast), Dfc (taiga), ET (tundra) and EF (ice cap). Climate change has occurred through geological time: ice ages and interglacials, driven by astronomical (Milankovitch cycles), volcanic and solar variations. Present global warming is due to the enhanced greenhouse effect from burning fossil fuels, deforestation and agriculture. Greenhouse gases include carbon dioxide, methane, nitrous oxide, CFCs and water vapour. Effects: rise in temperature of about 1.1 degrees Celsius since the pre-industrial era, melting of glaciers, sea-level rise and extreme weather. International efforts include the Kyoto Protocol (1997), the Paris Agreement (2015), and the IPCC.
## Deep dive: Koppen classification
A: all months above 18 C. B: evaporation exceeds rainfall. C: coldest month between minus 3 and 18 C. D: coldest below minus 3 C and warmest above 10 C. E: warmest month below 10 C. H: highlands. Examples: Af in the Amazon and Congo basins; Aw in African and Indian savannas; Cs in Mediterranean lands; Dfc in Siberia; ET in Arctic tundra.
## Deep dive: Past change
Evidence: tree rings, ice cores, pollen, lake sediments and historical records. Glacial periods and interglacials. Causes: Milankovitch cycles (eccentricity, tilt, precession), solar output, volcanism, plate tectonics.
## Deep dive: Global warming
Greenhouse effect is natural; enhancement is due to human activity. Carbon dioxide levels have risen from 280 to above 415 ppm. Mitigation: renewable energy, afforestation, efficiency and international accords like Paris Agreement with a 1.5 to 2 C target. Carbon credits.
