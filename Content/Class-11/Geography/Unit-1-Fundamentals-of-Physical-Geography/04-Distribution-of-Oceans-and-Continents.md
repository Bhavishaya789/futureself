# Distribution of Oceans and Continents
minutes: 40
yt: class 11 geography continental drift plate tectonics sea floor spreading
@study
The distribution of oceans and continents is explained by the theory of continental drift, sea floor spreading and plate tectonics. Alfred Wegener proposed continental drift in 1912: all continents were once joined as a supercontinent called Pangaea, surrounded by the Panthalassa ocean, which broke up into Laurasia and Gondwanaland and then drifted. His evidence included the matching coastlines of Africa and South America, similar rock formations, fossils such as Mesosaurus and Glossopteris, placer deposits and glaciation. Wegener suggested pole-fleeing and tidal forces as the cause, which were rejected. Harry Hess proposed sea floor spreading in the 1960s, based on mid-ocean ridges, magnetic anomalies and the age of the sea floor. Plate tectonics, proposed by McKenzie, Parker and Morgan, divided the lithosphere into seven major plates and several minor ones. The movement is driven by convection currents in the mantle. Plates move at divergent, convergent and transform boundaries. The Indian plate moved north, collided with the Eurasian plate and formed the Himalayas.
## Deep dive: Continental drift
Pangaea, Panthalassa, Laurasia, Gondwanaland. Evidences: jigsaw fit, rocks of similar age, fossils, tillite deposits and placer deposits (gold in Ghana). Gondwana rocks in India, Africa, South America, Australia and Antarctica. Forces: pole-fleeing and tidal force were criticised.
## Deep dive: Sea floor spreading
Mid-ocean ridges are underwater mountains with a rift; lava rises and spreads. The rocks near the ridge are young and those far away are older. Magnetic reversals recorded in rocks are symmetrical on both sides of the ridge. The ocean floor is no older than about 200 million years.
## Deep dive: Plate tectonics
Major plates: Pacific, North American, South American, Eurasian, African, Indo-Australian and Antarctic. Boundaries: divergent (plates move apart, new crust forms), convergent (plates collide, subduction or mountain building) and transform (plates slide past). The Indian plate has moved about 15 cm per year at times, and now about 5 cm.
