# Geography as a Discipline

@study
Geography is the study of the earth as the home of humankind. The word comes from the Greek geo (earth) and graphos (description). Eratosthenes first used the term and calculated the circumference of the earth. Geography studies the spatial distribution of phenomena, the relationships between humans and the environment, and the changes over time. It is an integrating discipline that draws from natural and social sciences. Its two main branches are physical geography and human geography. Physical geography includes geomorphology, climatology, hydrology and soil geography; human geography includes social, economic, population and settlement geography. Approaches include systematic and regional approaches, and the discipline uses maps, GIS and remote sensing. The dualisms of physical versus human, systematic versus regional and ideographic versus nomothetic have shaped the subject.
## Deep dive: Scope and branches
Physical geography studies the natural environment: the lithosphere, atmosphere, hydrosphere and biosphere. Human geography studies people, cultures, economies and settlements. Biogeography links life to environment. Systematic geography studies a phenomenon across the world; regional geography studies all phenomena in one region.
## Deep dive: Approaches and thinkers
Eratosthenes coined the word geography. Humboldt and Ritter founded modern geography in Germany. Ratzel proposed environmental determinism; Vidal de la Blache proposed possibilism; Griffith Taylor proposed neo-determinism or stop-and-go determinism. Geography is both a science and an art of description.
## Deep dive: Tools and importance
Maps, globes, GIS, GPS and remote sensing are major tools. Geography helps in planning, resource management, disaster preparedness and understanding the environment. Career fields include cartography, planning, meteorology and environmental management.
