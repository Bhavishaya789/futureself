# Solar Radiation, Heat Balance and Temperature
minutes: 40
yt: class 11 geography insolation heat budget temperature inversion
@study
The earth receives energy from the sun as short-wave radiation called insolation. The amount of insolation received at the top of the atmosphere is about 1.94 calories per square cm per minute, called the solar constant. Insolation varies with the angle of the sun's rays, the length of the day, the transparency of the atmosphere and the land's configuration. The earth is at perihelion (nearest, about 147 million km) on 3 January and at aphelion (farthest, 152 million km) on 4 July. Of 100 units of insolation, about 35 are reflected back (the albedo), 14 are absorbed by the atmosphere and 51 reach the earth's surface. The earth radiates 51 units as terrestrial radiation, and the heat budget is balanced. Heating takes place through conduction, convection, advection and radiation. The temperature decreases with height at the normal lapse rate of 6.5 C per km. Temperature inversion occurs when the temperature rises with height, such as on long winter nights with clear skies and calm air. Isotherms are lines joining places of equal temperature. The range of temperature, latitudes, altitude, ocean currents and distance from the sea control temperature.
## Deep dive: Insolation
Solar constant 1.94 cal per cm2 per min. Latitude and the angle of the sun's rays decide the heat. Cloud cover, aerosols and land-sea contrast alter it. The albedo of the earth is 35 percent. The heat budget: 100 units in, 35 reflected, 14 absorbed in the air, 51 absorbed at the surface; then 51 radiated. Atmosphere radiates 48 in total and 34 comes from the surface; this leaves the earth neither warming nor cooling.
## Deep dive: Heating and cooling
Conduction in contact; convection vertical; advection horizontal (the most important in mid-latitudes). Sunshine heats the land faster than the sea. Terrestrial radiation heats the atmosphere from below, which is why the temperature falls with height. The greenhouse effect traps outgoing radiation.
## Deep dive: Temperature distribution
January and July isotherms bend over oceans and continents. Equator has high temperature and low range; continental interiors have high range. Cold and warm currents change coastal temperature. Temperature inversion produces fog and smog and is common in valleys.
