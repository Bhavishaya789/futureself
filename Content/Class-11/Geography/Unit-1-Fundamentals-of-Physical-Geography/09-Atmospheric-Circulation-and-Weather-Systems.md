# Atmospheric Circulation and Weather Systems
minutes: 40
yt: class 11 geography atmospheric circulation pressure belts winds monsoon cyclones
@study
Atmospheric pressure is the weight of the column of air and is measured by a barometer in millibars. Pressure decreases with height. The pressure belts on the earth are the equatorial low, the subtropical highs, the subpolar lows and the polar highs. Winds blow from high to low pressure and are deflected by the Coriolis force (right in the northern hemisphere and left in the southern). The planetary winds are the trade winds (easterlies), the westerlies and the polar easterlies. Seasonal winds include monsoons; local winds include land and sea breezes, mountain and valley breezes, loo, foehn and chinook. General circulation has three cells: Hadley, Ferrel and Polar. Jet streams are narrow bands of fast winds in the upper troposphere. El Nino is the warming of the Pacific off Peru, which weakens the Southwest monsoon; La Nina is the opposite. Air masses (continental, maritime) meet at fronts. Cyclones are low-pressure systems; tropical cyclones form over warm seas and have eyes. Thunderstorms and tornadoes are localised systems.
## Deep dive: Pressure and winds
Pressure gradient force, Coriolis force and friction determine wind direction. Geostrophic wind blows parallel to isobars. Trade winds blow from subtropical highs to the equator; westerlies from subtropical highs to subpolar lows; polar easterlies from polar highs. The Inter-Tropical Convergence Zone shifts with the sun.
## Deep dive: Cells and jet streams
Hadley cell: 0 to 30 degrees; Ferrel cell: 30 to 60; Polar cell: 60 to 90. Subtropical jet stream and polar front jet stream. Jet streams affect the monsoon and western disturbances. Walker circulation links to El Nino and the Southern Oscillation.
## Deep dive: Weather systems
Air masses: cP, cT, mP, mT. Fronts: cold front, warm front, occluded front. Temperate cyclones form at the polar front and travel west to east. Tropical cyclones have winds over 120 km per hour, a calm eye and torrential rain: hurricanes in the Atlantic, typhoons in the China Sea, cyclones in the Indian Ocean, willy-willies in Australia. Thunderstorms arise from convection, and tornadoes are narrow, violent funnels.
