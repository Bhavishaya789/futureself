# Composition and Structure of the Atmosphere
minutes: 40
yt: class 11 geography composition and structure of the atmosphere layers
@study
The atmosphere is the envelope of gases around the earth held by gravity. By volume, dry air has about 78 percent nitrogen, 21 percent oxygen, 0.93 percent argon, 0.04 percent carbon dioxide and traces of neon, helium, ozone and hydrogen. Water vapour varies from 0 to 4 percent and dust particles act as hygroscopic nuclei. The atmosphere has five layers: the troposphere (average 13 km, up to 18 km at the equator and 8 km at the poles; temperature falls at 6.5 degrees Celsius per km, the layer of weather), the stratosphere (to about 50 km; contains the ozone layer, with temperature rising upward), the mesosphere (to about 80 km; coldest, up to minus 100 degrees Celsius), the thermosphere (80 to 400 km; ionosphere reflects radio waves; temperature rises up to 1000 degrees Celsius) and the exosphere (the uppermost layer merging into space). The tropopause, stratopause and mesopause are the boundaries between layers.
## Deep dive: Composition
Gases: nitrogen 78.08, oxygen 20.95, argon 0.93, carbon dioxide 0.036. Oxygen supports respiration; nitrogen dilutes oxygen and is used in fixation; carbon dioxide absorbs heat and causes the greenhouse effect; ozone absorbs ultraviolet radiation. Water vapour is the source of clouds and rain. Dust gives rise to scattering, red sunsets and condensation.
## Deep dive: Layers
Troposphere: contains 90 percent of air, weather occurs, temperature falls with height. Stratosphere: ozone, stable, jet aeroplanes. Mesosphere: meteors burn. Thermosphere: aurora, satellites, ionosphere. Exosphere: lightest gases escape.
## Deep dive: Importance
The atmosphere provides air for breathing, shields from ultraviolet rays, regulates temperature and supports the water cycle. Ozone depletion, caused by CFCs, makes the ozone hole over the Antarctic. The Montreal Protocol of 1987 phased out CFCs.
