# Oceans and Ocean Water Movements
minutes: 40
yt: class 11 geography oceans relief waves tides currents
@study
The oceans cover about 71 percent of the earth. The ocean floor has four major divisions: the continental shelf, the continental slope, the deep sea plain and the oceanic deeps or trenches. Minor relief features include mid-oceanic ridges, seamounts, guyots, canyons and atolls. The Mariana Trench in the Pacific is the deepest, about 11,000 metres. The temperature of ocean water decreases with depth and with latitude, and the thermocline is the zone of rapid temperature change. Salinity is the amount of salt in grams per thousand grams of water; average ocean salinity is 35 parts per thousand. It is very high in landlocked water bodies such as the Dead Sea (about 340 ppt) and Lake Van, and in the subtropical oceans where evaporation is high. Movements of ocean water are of three types: waves, tides and currents. Waves are caused by the wind; their parts are the crest, trough, wave height, wavelength, period and frequency. Tides are the rise and fall of sea level caused by the moon and sun's gravitation. Spring tides occur at new and full moon; neap tides occur at the first and third quarter. The tidal range is highest in the Bay of Fundy. Ocean currents are large streams of water flowing in a definite direction; they may be warm or cold. Major currents include the Gulf Stream, North Atlantic Drift, Kuroshio, Labrador, Canary, Humboldt (Peru), Benguela, California and Oyashio.
## Deep dive: Relief and temperature
Continental shelf: gentle slope up to 200 m depth, rich in fisheries and petroleum. Continental slope: steep. Deep sea plain: flat, 3000 to 6000 m. Trenches: deepest. Temperature: surface warm at the equator, falls to near 0 at the poles. Salinity: higher where evaporation is high and inflow is low (Red Sea, subtropics), lower near river mouths and poles.
## Deep dive: Waves and tides
Wave height depends on wind speed. Tsunamis are seismic sea waves. Tides: diurnal, semi-diurnal and mixed. Spring tide: sun, moon and earth in line. Neap tide: at right angles. Tides help navigation and tidal power.
## Deep dive: Currents
Currents are driven by wind, temperature and salinity differences, and the Coriolis force deflects them. Gyres rotate clockwise in the northern hemisphere and anticlockwise in the southern. Warm currents: Gulf Stream, Kuroshio, Brazil, Agulhas. Cold currents: Labrador, Canary, Humboldt, Benguela, California. Effects: temperature, fog, fisheries (Grand Banks where warm and cold meet).
