# Interior of the Earth
minutes: 40
yt: class 11 geography interior of the earth seismic waves crust mantle core
@study
The interior of the earth cannot be seen directly; it is studied through direct sources such as mining, drilling and volcanic eruptions, and indirect sources such as temperature, pressure and density with depth, meteorites, gravity, magnetic fields and, most importantly, seismic waves. Earthquakes produce body waves (P and S waves) and surface waves (L waves). P waves are primary or compressional waves that travel through solids, liquids and gases; S waves are secondary or shear waves that travel only through solids. The shadow zones of P and S waves show that the outer core is liquid. The earth has three layers: the crust (thin, up to about 35 km in the continents and 5 km in oceans), the mantle (up to 2900 km) and the core (from 2900 km to 6371 km, with a liquid outer core and a solid inner core). The Mohorovicic discontinuity separates the crust from the mantle, the Gutenberg discontinuity separates the mantle from the core and the Lehmann discontinuity separates the outer and inner core. Volcanoes arise from magma and are classified as shield, composite, caldera, flood basalt and mid-ocean ridge types.
## Deep dive: Sources and waves
Direct: surface rocks, mines, deep drilling (Kola, Russia), volcanoes. Indirect: temperature rises with depth, pressure and density increase, meteorites and gravity anomalies. Seismic waves: P waves are faster and longitudinal; S waves are slower and transverse; L waves travel along the surface and cause the most damage. The earthquake focus is where energy is released; the epicentre is the point above it.
## Deep dive: Layers
Crust: silica and alumina (sial) in continents; silica and magnesium (sima) in oceans; average density about 3 g per cubic cm. Mantle: upper and lower, containing the asthenosphere, rich in iron and magnesium silicates; density 3.4 to 5.6. Core: nife (nickel and iron), density 9.9 to 13; temperature up to about 6000 degrees Celsius.
## Deep dive: Earthquakes and volcanoes
Earthquake causes: faulting, volcanic activity, plate movement, human activity. Measured by the Richter scale (magnitude) and the Mercalli scale (intensity). Volcanoes: active, dormant and extinct; shield volcanoes are gentle; composite volcanoes are explosive; Krakatoa is an example of a caldera.
