# Fundamentals of Anatomy and Physiology
minutes: 40
yt: class 11 physical education anatomy physiology skeletal muscular circulatory respiratory exercise effects
@study
Anatomy is the study of the structure of the body and physiology is the study of its functions. The skeletal system has 206 bones in an adult: 80 in the axial skeleton (skull, vertebral column, ribs, sternum) and 126 in the appendicular skeleton (limbs and girdles). Bones are long (femur), short (carpals), flat (skull, ribs) and irregular (vertebrae). Functions of the skeleton: support, protection, movement, storage of minerals and production of blood cells in the bone marrow. A joint is where two bones meet; types are fixed (skull), slightly movable (vertebrae) and freely movable or synovial joints such as ball and socket (shoulder, hip), hinge (elbow, knee), pivot (neck), gliding (wrist), saddle (thumb) and condyloid. Muscles are of three types: skeletal (voluntary, striated), smooth (involuntary, in organs) and cardiac (involuntary, in heart). The human body has more than 600 muscles. Skeletal muscle fibres are slow twitch (red, endurance) and fast twitch (white, power). Muscle contraction can be isotonic or isometric. The circulatory system consists of the heart, blood and blood vessels. The heart has four chambers: right and left atria and right and left ventricles. Arteries carry blood away from the heart, veins carry blood towards it and capillaries exchange materials. Blood contains plasma, red blood cells (carry oxygen with haemoglobin), white blood cells (defence) and platelets (clotting). Normal resting heart rate is about 72 beats per minute; trained athletes have lower rates. Stroke volume is the blood pumped per beat and cardiac output equals stroke volume times heart rate. The respiratory system includes the nose, pharynx, larynx, trachea, bronchi and lungs; gas exchange occurs in the alveoli. Tidal volume is about 500 ml. Vital capacity is the maximum air exhaled after maximum inhalation. Exercise effects: it strengthens muscles and bones, increases stroke volume and cardiac output, lowers resting heart rate, improves lung capacity, helps digestion and improves mood. Oxygen debt arises during heavy exercise when oxygen supply is less than demand.
## Deep dive: Skeletal system
Bones give shape and protection. Red bone marrow makes blood cells. Joints allow different movement. Cartilage covers joint surfaces and ligaments join bone to bone while tendons join muscle to bone.
## Deep dive: Muscular and circulatory
Skeletal muscles move bones. Slow-twitch fibres suit long-distance events; fast-twitch suit sprints. The heart pumps blood in a double circulation: pulmonary and systemic. Blood carries oxygen, nutrients and wastes.
## Deep dive: Respiratory system and exercise
Inhaled air reaches alveoli where oxygen diffuses into blood. Regular exercise improves vital capacity and the efficiency of the heart. Athletes show bradycardia (low resting heart rate).
