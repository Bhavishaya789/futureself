# Training and Doping
minutes: 40
yt: class 11 physical education sports training strength endurance speed doping wada nada
@study
Sports training is a systematic process of preparing an athlete to improve performance through exercise. Principles: overload, specificity, progression, reversibility, individual differences and variety. Warm-up prepares the body and reduces injury; cool-down helps recovery. Strength is the ability to overcome resistance; types are maximum strength, explosive strength (power) and strength endurance. Methods include isometric (no change in length), isotonic (concentric and eccentric) and isokinetic (constant speed). Endurance is the ability to resist fatigue; aerobic endurance uses oxygen while anaerobic endurance works without enough oxygen. Methods: continuous running, interval training and fartlek (speed play, developed in Sweden). Speed is the ability to perform movement in the shortest time: reaction speed, movement speed and acceleration speed. Flexibility is the range of movement; it can be static or dynamic, active or passive, improved by stretching. Coordinative abilities include balance, rhythm, orientation, differentiation and reaction. Training load has intensity and volume. Periodisation divides training into preparatory, competition and transition periods. Doping is the use of banned substances or methods to improve performance unfairly. The World Anti-Doping Agency (WADA) was formed in 1999 and has its headquarters at Montreal; the National Anti-Doping Agency (NADA) was set up in India in 2009. WADA publishes the Prohibited List annually. Classes of prohibited substances include anabolic steroids, stimulants, narcotics, diuretics and masking agents, peptide hormones and growth factors, and beta-2 agonists; prohibited methods include blood doping and gene doping. Reasons athletes use drugs: pressure to win, fame and money. Side effects: steroids cause liver damage, heart disease, hormonal imbalance and aggression; stimulants cause high blood pressure and addiction; diuretics cause dehydration; blood doping causes clotting and stroke. Consequences include disqualification, loss of medals and bans. Prevention: education, testing and strict penalties. The athlete is responsible for substances found in the body (strict liability). Dope tests use urine and blood samples taken in two bottles A and B.
## Deep dive: Training
Overload means training harder than normal to adapt. Specificity means training the energy system and muscles used in the sport. Progression raises the load gradually. Reversibility means fitness is lost when training stops.
## Deep dive: Training methods
Isometric exercises hold a position. Interval training alternates work and rest. Fartlek varies speed over distance. Stretching develops flexibility. Circuit training mixes exercises at stations.
## Deep dive: Doping
Doping is unethical and unsafe. WADA and NADA test athletes. Penalties can end careers. Athletes should know the list and take only permitted medicines.
