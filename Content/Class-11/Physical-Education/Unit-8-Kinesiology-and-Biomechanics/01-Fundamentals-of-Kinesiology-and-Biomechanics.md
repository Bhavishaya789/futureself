# Fundamentals of Kinesiology and Biomechanics
minutes: 40
yt: class 11 physical education kinesiology biomechanics levers newton laws planes axes friction
@study
Kinesiology is the study of human movement, and biomechanics is the application of mechanical principles to the study of living bodies. Importance: improves technique, prevents injury, improves equipment design and helps coaches analyse performance. Movements about joints: flexion (decreasing the angle), extension (increasing the angle), abduction (moving away from the midline), adduction (moving towards the midline), rotation, circumduction, pronation and supination. Planes of the body: sagittal (divides into left and right), frontal or coronal (front and back) and transverse or horizontal (upper and lower). Axes are the lines about which movement occurs: frontal (horizontal) axis, sagittal axis and vertical (longitudinal) axis. Flexion and extension occur in the sagittal plane about the frontal axis. Levers: a lever is a rigid bar that turns about a fulcrum with effort and load. First class: fulcrum between effort and load (seesaw, neck extension). Second class: load between fulcrum and effort (wheelbarrow, calf raise standing on toes). Third class: effort between fulcrum and load (biceps curl); this is the most common in the body. Newton's laws: first (inertia): an object remains at rest or in uniform motion unless acted on by an external force; second: force = mass x acceleration; third: for every action there is an equal and opposite reaction. Applications: starting a race, swimming push-off, jump landing. Friction is the force opposing relative motion; types are static, sliding, rolling and fluid. Friction helps in running, gripping and braking but wastes energy, and is reduced by lubrication and streamlined shapes. Equilibrium is a state of balance: stable (large base, low centre of gravity), unstable and neutral. Centre of gravity is the point where the weight of the body acts. Lower centre of gravity and wider base give more stability. Projectile motion depends on speed, angle (about 45 degrees for maximum range) and height of release. Force is a push or pull; Torque is turning effect.
## Deep dive: Movements and planes
Joint actions are described using flexion, extension, abduction, adduction and rotation. Each happens in a plane about an axis. Examples: a cartwheel moves in the frontal plane; a somersault in the sagittal plane.
## Deep dive: Levers
Lever systems give mechanical advantage. In first class levers the fulcrum is in the middle. In second class the load is in the middle and gives a large force. In third class the effort is in the middle giving speed and range.
## Deep dive: Newton's laws and friction
Inertia explains why a ball stays at rest. F = ma explains why a lighter ball goes farther for the same force. Action-reaction explains push-off. Friction depends on surfaces and force pressing them.
