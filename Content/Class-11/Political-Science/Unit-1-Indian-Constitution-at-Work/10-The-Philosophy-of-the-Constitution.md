# The Philosophy of the Constitution
minutes: 40
yt: class 11 political science philosophy of the constitution liberty equality secularism
@study
The philosophy of the Constitution means the vision and values behind it. The framers wanted to end colonial and social oppression and create a democratic society. The Preamble, drawn from the Objectives Resolution of Jawaharlal Nehru, expresses this vision: justice (social, economic and political), liberty of thought, expression, belief, faith and worship, equality of status and of opportunity, and fraternity assuring the dignity of the individual and the unity and integrity of the nation. Key features of the philosophy: commitment to individual freedom and rights; social justice and the protection of the weaker sections through reservations and special provisions (Articles 15, 16, 17); respect for diversity and group rights, including minority educational rights (Articles 29 and 30); the Indian model of secularism in which the state keeps principled distance from all religions and intervenes to reform them, as with abolition of untouchability, rather than strict separation; universal franchise, giving every adult one vote regardless of caste, gender or wealth; federalism with a strong Centre and asymmetric provisions; a national identity based on unity in diversity; and procedural achievements such as the commitment to parliamentary democracy and constitutional government. Ambedkar insisted that political democracy must lead to social and economic democracy and warned about contradictions: one person one vote but one person one value was denied. Gandhi's influence is seen in the Directive Principles (village panchayats, prohibition, cottage industries). Nehru's influence is seen in the welfare state and secularism. Criticisms: it is unwieldy and too long; unrepresentative, because the Assembly was not elected by universal suffrage; and alien, borrowed from the West. Replies: the Constitution adapted ideas to the Indian context; it was framed after wide debate; borrowing is not a defect; and continuity with national movement ideas such as the Nehru Report (1928) and the Karachi Resolution (1931) shows it grew from the freedom struggle. The Constitution is a means for self-government and a social revolution. Ideas of dignity, equality and non-discrimination guide it. Constitutional morality means loyalty to constitutional values. The philosophy is also expressed in the Fundamental Duties and the Directive Principles.
## Deep dive: Core values
Liberty, equality, justice and fraternity are central. The state protects rights but also promotes social change. Secular state means neither a state religion nor hostility to religion.
## Deep dive: Balance
The Constitution balances individual rights with community values and group rights. It balances a strong Centre with state autonomy and political rights with social justice.
## Deep dive: Criticisms and replies
Critics say it is a borrowed document. Defenders say it was rooted in the national movement. It has been creatively used by citizens and courts.
