# Local Governments
minutes: 40

@study
Local self-government is the government of villages and towns by locally elected bodies. It brings democracy closer to people, promotes participation and ensures local problems are solved locally. Gandhi stressed gram swaraj. After Independence, the Balwant Rai Mehta Committee (1957) recommended a three-tier Panchayati Raj; the first Panchayati Raj was launched at Nagaur in Rajasthan on 2 October 1959. The Ashok Mehta Committee (1977) recommended a two-tier system. The 73rd Amendment (1992, in force from 24 April 1993; National Panchayati Raj Day) added Part IX of the Constitution (Articles 243 to 243O) and the Eleventh Schedule with 29 subjects. The 74th Amendment (1992, in force from 1 June 1993) added Part IX-A (Articles 243P to 243ZG) and the Twelfth Schedule with 18 subjects for urban local bodies. Structure of Panchayati Raj: Gram Panchayat at the village level, Panchayat Samiti at the block level and Zila Parishad at the district level. The Gram Sabha consists of all adult voters of a village and is the foundation. Members are directly elected for a five-year term; if a body is dissolved, elections must be held within six months. One-third of seats (and offices) are reserved for women, and seats are reserved for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes in proportion to their population; many states have raised women reservation to 50 percent. A State Election Commission conducts elections and a State Finance Commission reviews finances every five years. Urban local bodies are Nagar Panchayat (transitional areas), Municipal Council (smaller urban areas) and Municipal Corporation (large cities). Ward committees and the Mayor or Chairperson lead them. The Panchayats (Extension to Scheduled Areas) Act, PESA, was passed in 1996 for tribal areas. Challenges: inadequate funds, dependence on state governments, bureaucratic control, limited powers (the 3 Fs: functions, funds, functionaries) and proxy rule by relatives of elected women (sarpanch-pati). Benefits: participation, accountability, development and empowerment of women and weaker sections. Social audit and the MGNREGA scheme (2005) are linked to Gram Sabhas. Panchayat committee work: planning for economic development and social justice.
## Deep dive: Why local governments
They bring government closer, solve local problems and train leaders. They enable participation by weaker sections. Local needs differ and are best known locally.
## Deep dive: 73rd and 74th Amendments
They made local bodies constitutional institutions with regular elections, reserved seats, finance commissions and defined powers. State laws determine details.
## Deep dive: Working and challenges
Funds and staff remain with states. Gram Sabha meetings may be poorly attended. Women reservation improved representation but needs real empowerment.
