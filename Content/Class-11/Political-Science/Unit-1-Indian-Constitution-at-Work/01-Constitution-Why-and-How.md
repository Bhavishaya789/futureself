# Constitution: Why and How?
minutes: 40
yt: class 11 political science constitution why and how constituent assembly
@study
A constitution is a set of fundamental rules that decide how a country is governed. Its functions are to create trust and coordination among people, to specify how the government will be constituted and who has power to take decisions, to limit the power of government over citizens and to enable society to fulfil its aspirations. The Indian Constitution was made by the Constituent Assembly, formed in 1946 under the Cabinet Mission Plan. Its first meeting was held on 9 December 1946 with Sachchidananda Sinha as the temporary chairman, and Dr Rajendra Prasad was elected permanent president on 11 December 1946. The Assembly originally had 389 members, reduced to 299 after Partition. The Drafting Committee was set up on 29 August 1947 with Dr B R Ambedkar as chairman, and B N Rau was the constitutional adviser. The Objectives Resolution, moved by Jawaharlal Nehru on 13 December 1946 and adopted on 22 January 1947, laid down the philosophy. The Assembly took 2 years, 11 months and 18 days, held 11 sessions, and adopted the Constitution on 26 November 1949; it came into force on 26 January 1950. 26 January was chosen because the Purna Swaraj resolution was adopted at Lahore in 1929 and Independence Day was celebrated on that date in 1930. Constitution Day is celebrated on 26 November. The original Constitution had 395 articles, 22 parts and 8 schedules. Sources: the Government of India Act 1935 for the federal scheme and administration; Britain for parliamentary government; the USA for fundamental rights and judicial review; Ireland for Directive Principles; Canada for a strong Centre; Australia for the concurrent list; Germany (Weimar) for emergency provisions; the USSR for fundamental duties; and South Africa for the amendment procedure. The Preamble describes India as sovereign, socialist, secular, democratic and republic, with justice, liberty, equality and fraternity; the words socialist and secular were added by the 42nd Amendment in 1976. The Constitution is flexible and rigid, a living document. Ambedkar warned that constitutional morality must be cultivated.
## Deep dive: Making of the constitution
The Constituent Assembly was not elected by universal franchise but represented all major groups. It debated in open sessions and drew on national movement ideals. Committees such as the Union Powers Committee and Fundamental Rights Committee prepared drafts.
## Deep dive: Features and philosophy
The Constitution protects rights, limits government and provides for democracy. It balances liberty and social justice. The Preamble expresses its philosophy; the Constitution is the longest written constitution of any country.
## Deep dive: Procedures and balance
It balances a strong Centre and states. It allows amendment but protects the basic structure. It accommodates diversity through special provisions.
