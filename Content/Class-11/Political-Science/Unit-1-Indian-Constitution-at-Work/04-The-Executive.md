# The Executive

@study
The executive implements laws and runs the government. It has political executives (President, Prime Minister, ministers) and a permanent executive (civil servants). India has a parliamentary system in which the real executive power rests with the Prime Minister and Council of Ministers, while the President is the nominal head of the state. The President (Article 52) is elected by an Electoral College of the elected members of both Houses of Parliament and the elected members of the state legislative assemblies (Article 54) by proportional representation using the single transferable vote. Qualifications: citizen of India, at least 35 years old and qualified to be elected to the Lok Sabha. The term is five years and the President can be removed by impeachment (Article 61) for violating the Constitution. The Vice President (Article 63) is elected by members of both Houses and is the ex-officio chairman of the Rajya Sabha. Powers of the President: executive (appoints PM, ministers, governors, judges), legislative (summons and prorogues Parliament, dissolves the Lok Sabha, issues ordinances under Article 123), financial (money bills need recommendation), judicial (pardoning power under Article 72) and emergency powers (Articles 352, 356, 360). Veto powers: absolute, suspensive and pocket. By the 42nd and 44th Amendments the President must act on the advice of the Council of Ministers but may return it once for reconsideration. The Prime Minister (Article 74 and 75) is the leader of the majority in the Lok Sabha; the Council of Ministers has Cabinet ministers, ministers of state and deputy ministers and is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha (Article 75(3)). The 91st Amendment (2003) limits the size of the council to 15 percent of the Lok Sabha membership. The Cabinet is the core decision-making body. The Governor (Article 153) is appointed by the President and acts on the advice of the state council of ministers. The Chief Minister heads the state council. The permanent executive consists of civil servants recruited by the Union Public Service Commission (UPSC) through all India services (IAS, IPS, IFS). Neutrality, anonymity and permanence are features of the civil service. Collective responsibility means that the council sinks or swims together; individual responsibility applies to each minister. The first President was Dr Rajendra Prasad. The Prime Minister Office (PMO) assists the PM.
## Deep dive: President and Vice President
The President is the constitutional head. Election is indirect. Powers are mostly exercised on the advice of ministers. Situations such as a hung Parliament give the President real discretion.
## Deep dive: Prime Minister and ministers
The PM chooses ministers and allocates portfolios. Cabinet takes policy decisions. The council is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha and can be removed by a no-confidence vote.
## Deep dive: Governor and bureaucracy
The Governor is the nominal head of a state with some discretion. Civil servants advise ministers and implement policy. UPSC ensures merit-based recruitment.
