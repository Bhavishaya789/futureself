# The Legislature

@study
The legislature makes laws, controls the executive and approves the budget. India has a bicameral Parliament at the Union level, consisting of the President, the Lok Sabha (House of the People) and the Rajya Sabha (Council of States) (Article 79). The Lok Sabha has up to 552 members under the Constitution and currently 543 elected; its term is five years, and it can be dissolved earlier. The Speaker presides. The Rajya Sabha has a maximum of 250 members, of whom 238 are elected by the state legislatures and 12 are nominated by the President for expertise in literature, science, art and social service; it is a permanent house, with one-third of members retiring every two years, and each member has a six-year term. The Vice President is its chairman. Functions of Parliament: legislative, executive control, financial, representative, deliberative, judicial (impeachment) and constituent (amendment under Article 368). Bills are ordinary, money, financial or constitutional amendment bills. A Money Bill (Article 110) can be introduced only in the Lok Sabha on the recommendation of the President; the Rajya Sabha can only recommend changes within 14 days. A bill passes through three readings in each House, and then gets the President's assent. In a deadlock over an ordinary bill the President summons a joint sitting (Article 108), presided by the Speaker. Parliament controls the executive through question hour, zero hour, adjournment motions, calling attention motions, no-confidence motions and committees. Parliamentary committees include the Public Accounts Committee (PAC), Estimates Committee and Committee on Public Undertakings. The Budget is presented annually by the Finance Minister. The Anti-Defection law (52nd Amendment, 1985; Tenth Schedule) disqualifies members who defect from their party; the 91st Amendment removed the exemption for splits. The Lok Sabha Speaker decides disqualification. The quorum is one-tenth of the total members. Privileges protect freedom of speech inside the House. State legislatures have a Vidhan Sabha and in some states a Vidhan Parishad. Parliament amends the Constitution by special majority. The first Lok Sabha was constituted in 1952, and the first sitting of the Rajya Sabha was on 13 May 1952.
## Deep dive: Houses
Lok Sabha represents the people; Rajya Sabha represents the states. Lok Sabha has more power over money and the council of ministers. Rajya Sabha has special powers over states (Article 249).
## Deep dive: Law making
A bill is introduced, debated, voted, passed by both Houses and signed by the President. Money bills are special. Joint sittings resolve deadlocks except for money and constitutional amendment bills.
## Deep dive: Control of executive
Parliament holds the executive accountable by questions, debates and committees. No-confidence vote can topple the government. Committees scrutinise spending and policies.
