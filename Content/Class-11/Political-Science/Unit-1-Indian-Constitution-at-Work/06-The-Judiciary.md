# The Judiciary

@study
The judiciary settles disputes, interprets the Constitution and protects rights. India has an integrated and independent judiciary with the Supreme Court at the top, High Courts in the states and subordinate courts below. Independence is secured by the method of appointment, security of tenure, fixed salaries charged to the Consolidated Fund, and the power to punish for contempt. Judges are appointed by the President in consultation with the Chief Justice of India; in practice the collegium system of senior judges recommends appointments (evolved through the Three Judges cases). A judge of the Supreme Court retires at 65 and a High Court judge at 62; judges can be removed only by impeachment on proven misbehaviour or incapacity by a special majority in Parliament (Article 124(4)). The Supreme Court was inaugurated on 28 January 1950; it has the Chief Justice and a maximum of 33 other judges (34 in total). Jurisdiction: original (disputes between the Union and states, and enforcement of fundamental rights under Article 32), appellate (civil, criminal and constitutional cases from High Courts), advisory (Article 143, opinion for the President) and writ jurisdiction. The Supreme Court is a court of record and the final interpreter of the Constitution, and its law binds all courts (Article 141). Judicial review is the power to examine the constitutionality of laws and executive actions and strike down those against the Constitution. The Kesavananda Bharati case (1973) held that Parliament cannot alter the basic structure. High Courts (Article 214) have original, appellate and supervisory jurisdiction, with 25 High Courts today. The first High Courts were set up in 1862 at Calcutta, Bombay and Madras. Subordinate courts include district courts, sessions courts and family courts; Lok Adalats and Nyaya Panchayats give speedy justice. Public Interest Litigation (PIL) began in the late 1970s with Justices P N Bhagwati and V R Krishna Iyer, allowing anyone to approach the court on behalf of the poor or for public causes. Judicial activism refers to an active role of courts in protecting rights; critics warn of judicial overreach. Concerns include pendency of cases and delays. The idea of separation of powers keeps the judiciary free from executive control. The Constitution also provides for a Supreme Court Collegium, National Judicial Appointments Commission (struck down in 2015 as unconstitutional).
## Deep dive: Independence
Independent judiciary is essential for rule of law. Fixed tenure and salary protect judges. Parliament cannot discuss the conduct of a judge except during removal proceedings.
## Deep dive: Powers
The Supreme Court protects fundamental rights through writs and settles federal disputes. Judicial review checks laws and actions. Advisory opinion helps the President on legal questions.
## Deep dive: Access to justice
PIL, Lok Adalats and legal aid help the poor. Courts have extended rights on environment and gender. Delays and pendency remain challenges.
