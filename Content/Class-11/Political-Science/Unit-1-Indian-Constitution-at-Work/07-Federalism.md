# Federalism
minutes: 40
yt: class 11 political science federalism union list state list concurrent list centre state relations
@study
Federalism is a system of government in which power is divided between a central authority and constituent units, each with its own sphere. The Indian Constitution describes India as a Union of States (Article 1). It sets up a federation with a strong Centre, sometimes called quasi-federal. Features: a written constitution, two levels of government, division of powers, an independent judiciary and rigid amendment on federal matters. The Seventh Schedule has three lists: the Union List (defence, foreign affairs, currency, railways; originally 97 subjects, now 98), the State List (police, public order, agriculture, local government; now 59 subjects) and the Concurrent List (education, forests, marriage, criminal law; now 52 subjects). Residuary powers lie with the Union. In a conflict on the concurrent list, the Union law prevails. Unitary features: a strong Centre, single citizenship, an integrated judiciary, All India Services, Governors appointed by the Centre, emergency powers (Article 356, President's Rule) and the power of Parliament to reorganise states. Centre-state relations: legislative (Articles 245 to 255), administrative (Articles 256 to 263) and financial (Articles 268 to 293). The Finance Commission (Article 280) is set up every five years to recommend sharing of taxes. The Inter-State Council (Article 263) promotes coordination. Planning Commission was replaced by NITI Aayog in 2015. The Goods and Services Tax (101st Amendment, 2016) is governed by the GST Council. The Sarkaria Commission (1983) and the Punchhi Commission (2007) studied centre-state relations. States Reorganisation: linguistic reorganisation followed the Fazl Ali Commission (1953) and the States Reorganisation Act 1956; Andhra Pradesh was the first linguistic state in 1953; Telangana became the 29th state in 2014. Special provisions for some states under Article 371 and the Fifth and Sixth Schedules for tribal areas; Article 370 was abrogated in 2019 and Jammu and Kashmir was reorganised as Union Territories. Language policy: Hindi in Devanagari is the official language (Article 343) with English; the Eighth Schedule recognises 22 languages. Demands for autonomy and disputes over river waters (Cauvery) show tension. Cooperative federalism emphasises partnership.
## Deep dive: Division of powers
The three lists divide subjects. States are supreme in the State List. Parliament may legislate on a State List subject in the national interest if the Rajya Sabha passes a resolution (Article 249).
## Deep dive: Strong Centre
Emergency, financial dependence and Governor powers give the Centre strength. Asymmetric provisions accommodate diversity. Tensions arise over President's Rule and grants.
## Deep dive: Linguistic states and diversity
Reorganisation on linguistic lines strengthened unity. Language and regional movements show diversity. Federalism accommodates multiple identities within one nation.
