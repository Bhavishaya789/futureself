# Political Theory: An Introduction
minutes: 40

@study
Politics is concerned with the collective decisions that affect society: how power is gained, used and shared, and how a community lives together. Aristotle called man a political animal. Political theory systematically thinks about the ideas and principles that shape constitutions, governments and social life, such as freedom, equality, justice, rights, citizenship, nationalism and secularism. The word theory comes from the Greek theoria, meaning to see; political theory asks what should be, as well as what is. Political theory is distinct from political science, which empirically studies institutions. It deals with the meaning of concepts, their justification and the critique of existing arrangements. Political theorists examine how political ideas apply in real life: for example, what freedom means when people are poor. Ancient thinkers include Plato (The Republic), Aristotle (Politics), and Kautilya (Arthashastra); modern thinkers include Hobbes, Locke, Rousseau, Mill and Marx. Political theory is not only academic: it grew from practical problems in the Indian national movement, the French Revolution and other struggles. Gandhi, Ambedkar, Tagore and Nehru produced political thought. Why study political theory? It helps citizens to develop reasoning, to question ideas, to participate in debates, to resolve conflicts and to understand the world. Political concepts such as liberty and equality are contested: their meaning differs by context. The study of political theory helps us to see that we are all part of political life, to value freedom of thought, and to defend good arguments. Theory should be balanced with reality; abstract ideas should be tested against experience. Key ideas: sovereignty, state, government, law, power and authority. Political theory also includes debates between schools such as liberalism, socialism, conservatism and feminism. Political theorists discuss both ends (values) and means (institutions). Techniques include logical reasoning, clarity of language and examples. A democratic society needs citizens who can argue reasonably and with tolerance. Political theory promotes critical thinking, including about gender, caste and class. Empirical study looks at what is; normative study at what ought to be.
## Deep dive: What is politics
Politics deals with power and public life, not only elections. It is present in families, schools and nations when decisions affect others. Gandhi saw politics as service.
## Deep dive: Political theory
It clarifies concepts and defends values. It questions assumptions and connects ideas to real problems. It uses reasoning and evidence.
## Deep dive: Why study it
Studying theory builds citizenship, tolerance and critical thinking. It prepares one to take part in democracy and to resolve disputes through argument.
