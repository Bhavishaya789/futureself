# Secularism
minutes: 40
yt: class 11 political science secularism indian secularism western separation principled distance
@study
Secularism is the doctrine that separates the state from religion and treats all religions equally. A secular state has no official religion and does not discriminate on the basis of religion. Secularism opposes inter-religious domination (one religious group dominating another), and intra-religious domination (domination within a religion, such as caste or gender hierarchy, for example restrictions on Dalits and women entering temples). Secularism seeks freedom of belief and equal citizenship. Western (strict) secularism, as in the USA (First Amendment) or France (laicite), involves mutual exclusion of state and religion; state does not interfere in religion and religion does not interfere in state. The Indian model is different: the state keeps principled distance from all religions and may intervene to protect equality, to remove untouchability, to reform religious practice (the Hindu Code Bills, temple entry laws) and to support minority educational institutions. India is not anti-religious. Constitutional provisions: the Preamble (the word secular added by the 42nd Amendment, 1976); Article 14 and 15 (equality and non-discrimination); Article 16 (equal opportunity); Articles 25 to 28 (freedom of religion: freedom of conscience and the right to profess, practise and propagate religion, subject to public order; no religious instruction in state-funded institutions; no tax for promotion of any religion); Articles 29 and 30 (minority rights). In S R Bommai v Union of India (1994) the Supreme Court held that secularism is a basic feature. Cases: Shayara Bano (2017) declared instant triple talaq unconstitutional; the Sabarimala judgment (2018) allowed women of all ages to enter the temple. Secularism in Turkey, where Mustafa Kemal Ataturk imposed state-led secularism, is a contrast. Criticisms in India: that secularism is anti-religious, Western, promotes minorityism or vote bank politics, interferes in religion, and that it is impossible. Replies: it is not anti-religious; it is a Western-influenced concept but with Indian roots such as the Ashoka and Akbar traditions of tolerance (Ashoka's edicts, Akbar's sulh-i-kul); intervention is for equality; and minority protection is not appeasement. India's tradition of sarva dharma sama bhava (equal respect for all religions) supports pluralism. Communalism is the opposite, using religion for political ends. Secularism is a protection for both religion and the individual. Secular education and religious tolerance are important. Peace and harmony depend on respect for all faiths. Fundamental duty includes promoting harmony.
## Deep dive: Meaning
Secularism means equal respect to all religions and freedom of belief. It rejects religious domination and discrimination. A secular state is neutral in the treatment of religions.
## Deep dive: Indian secularism
It allows state support for religious freedom and intervention for equality. It recognises community rights. The Constitution protects both individual and group religious freedom.
## Deep dive: Criticisms
Critics say it is Western or minority-favouring. Defenders say it is rooted in India's tradition of tolerance and necessary for a plural society.
