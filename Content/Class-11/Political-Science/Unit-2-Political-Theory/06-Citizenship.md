# Citizenship
minutes: 40
yt: class 11 political science citizenship full membership rights duties citizenship act 1955
@study
Citizenship is full and equal membership of a political community, with rights and duties. Citizens have equal rights under law and take part in public life. T H Marshall described three kinds of citizenship rights: civil, political and social. Citizenship brings political rights (vote, hold office), civil rights (liberty, equality before law) and social rights (education, health, welfare). It also carries duties such as obeying laws, paying taxes and respecting others' rights. Citizenship and equality: equal rights for all citizens regardless of caste, religion and gender. In India, citizenship is provided in Part II of the Constitution (Articles 5 to 11) and regulated by the Citizenship Act, 1955. There is single citizenship for the whole country (unlike some federations). Citizenship by birth (those born in India before 1 July 1987, with changes later), by descent (born abroad to Indian parents), by registration (persons of Indian origin or married to Indian citizens), by naturalisation (foreigners after a long period of residence) and by incorporation of territory (e.g., Goa, Pondicherry, Sikkim). Citizenship may be lost by renunciation, termination (acquiring another nationality) or deprivation. India does not allow dual citizenship; it provides Overseas Citizenship of India (OCI) status. Citizenship (Amendment) Act, 2019 gave a path to citizenship to certain religious minority refugees from Afghanistan, Bangladesh and Pakistan who came before 2015; it was debated for exclusion on the basis of religion. National Register of Citizens (NRC) was prepared in Assam. Citizens and non-citizens: foreigners enjoy some rights but cannot vote or hold public office. Refugees are people forced to flee from persecution; stateless people lack any citizenship and have difficulties accessing rights. Equal citizenship demands inclusion of minorities and women; the Shah Bano case and women's rights; migrants are a challenge; Domicile and local residence rules differ from citizenship. Global citizenship stresses concern for humanity and universal human rights; international agreements and migration challenge borders. Citizenship involves participation in democracy, volunteering and community life. Fundamental duties include harmony and protection of public property. Nation-state and citizenship: states define membership. Debates: Should citizenship be based on birth, descent or residence? How to treat migrants and refugees?
## Deep dive: Citizenship rights
Citizenship gives civil, political and social rights. Equal citizenship means equal status and opportunity. Duties include obeying law and paying taxes.
## Deep dive: Indian citizenship
Single citizenship with acquisition by birth, descent, registration, naturalisation and incorporation of territory. Loss may occur by renunciation, termination or deprivation. OCI is not dual citizenship.
## Deep dive: Issues
Migrants, refugees and stateless people challenge citizenship. The CAA and NRC are debated. Global citizenship broadens concern.
