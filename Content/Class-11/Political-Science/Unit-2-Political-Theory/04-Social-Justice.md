# Social Justice
minutes: 40
yt: class 11 political science social justice rawls veil of ignorance distributive
@study
Justice is giving each person what is due. Plato linked justice to each part of society doing its duty. Justice is the central concept of social and political life. The principles of justice: equal treatment for equals (people should not be discriminated on irrelevant grounds like caste, religion or gender), proportionate justice (rewards should be in proportion to effort and merit) and recognition of special needs (those with special needs or disadvantages deserve special treatment, as with persons with disabilities). Just distribution: the state should ensure a basic minimum standard of living and equal opportunities. Distributive justice concerns the fair distribution of goods, burdens and opportunities; retributive justice concerns punishment proportionate to crime; procedural justice concerns fair procedures. John Rawls in A Theory of Justice (1971) argued for justice as fairness through a thought experiment: people choose principles of justice from the original position behind a veil of ignorance, not knowing their own class, caste, gender, talents or position. Not knowing, they would choose rules that are fair to all, protecting the worst-off. Rawls's two principles: equal basic liberties for all, and social and economic inequalities arranged to benefit the least advantaged (difference principle) with fair equality of opportunity. Utilitarianism (Bentham and Mill) says justice is the greatest good of the greatest number, but it may ignore minority interests. Free market vs. state: libertarians such as Nozick favour a minimal state and market rewards; egalitarians favour state intervention for a fair share. Social justice means fair and just relations between individuals and society, including the distribution of wealth, opportunities and privileges. In India, social justice is a Constitutional goal: the Preamble promises social, economic and political justice; Directive Principles (Articles 38, 39, 46) and reservations for SC, ST and OBC. The Mandal Commission (1979) led to 27 percent OBC reservation in central jobs in 1990; in the Indra Sawhney case (1992) the Supreme Court upheld it with a ceiling of 50 percent and the creamy layer exclusion. The Minimum Wages Act and MGNREGA are measures of social justice. Poverty, hunger and discrimination are challenges. Justice also means gender justice and environmental justice. Caste inequality is addressed by Article 17 and the Protection of Civil Rights Act and SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act 1989. Fair play and impartiality are basic to justice.
## Deep dive: Principles of justice
Equal treatment, proportionality and special needs are the three principles. They sometimes conflict. Balancing them requires reasoned debate.
## Deep dive: Rawls's theory
The veil of ignorance ensures impartiality. People would not accept rules that harm the weakest, since they might be in that position. This supports a basic minimum for all.
## Deep dive: Social justice in India
The Constitution seeks to remove inequality. Reservations and welfare laws are tools. Courts balance merit and equity.
