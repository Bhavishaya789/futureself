# Freedom
minutes: 40
yt: class 11 political science freedom liberty negative positive harm principle
@study
Freedom (liberty) is the absence of constraints and the ability to make choices and develop one's potential. Ideal of freedom was central in the national movement: Gandhi's Hind Swaraj (1909) distinguished swaraj as self-rule and self-mastery, and Tilak said that swaraj is my birthright. Freedom has two aspects: the absence of external constraints and the presence of conditions that enable development. Constraints may come from external force, from laws, from social structures such as caste, from poverty and from internalised ideas. Isaiah Berlin in Two Concepts of Liberty (1958) separated negative liberty (freedom from interference, the area in which a person is left alone) and positive liberty (freedom to develop and act, which needs conditions such as education and resources). Negative liberty asks how much area is free of interference; positive liberty asks whether people can be masters of their own lives. Some constraints are necessary: law restricts some freedoms to protect others. John Stuart Mill in On Liberty (1859) proposed the harm principle: the only reason for interfering with an individual is to prevent harm to others. He distinguished self-regarding actions (affecting only oneself, where there should be no interference) from other-regarding actions (affecting others, which can be regulated). Mill defended freedom of thought and expression because truth emerges from debate. Freedom of expression has limits (reasonable restrictions on speech under Article 19(2)): incitement to violence, defamation, public order. Freedom and the state: the state protects freedom but may also threaten it. Freedom and social groups: customs and caste can restrict freedom; women have faced restrictions. Freedom from fear was emphasised by Aung San Suu Kyi. Freedom of belief; the right to dissent; the importance of pluralism. Freedom of the press is part of freedom of expression. Positive freedom is advanced by policies of education and health. The Constitution protects freedom through Article 19 (speech, assembly, association, movement, residence, profession), Article 21 (life and liberty) and Article 25. Debates include banning books, censorship, hate speech and the right to protest. Freedom has costs and responsibilities. Reasonable limits are placed by the state, but must be justified. Freedom and equality are linked.
## Deep dive: Meaning of freedom
Freedom means freedom from constraint and freedom to act. Constraints may be external or social. A free society allows diverse ways of life.
## Deep dive: Negative and positive liberty
Negative liberty protects the private space. Positive liberty empowers people. Both are needed: a poor person may be legally free but unable to use freedom.
## Deep dive: Harm principle and expression
Mill allowed limits only to prevent harm to others. Free expression helps truth and democracy. Debates continue about hate speech and censorship.
