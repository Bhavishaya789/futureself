# Equality
minutes: 40
yt: class 11 political science equality opportunity outcome feminism marxism liberalism
@study
Equality means that all human beings have equal worth and should have equal opportunities to develop. It does not mean sameness; it means treating people as equals and removing unjust barriers. Importance: equality is a powerful moral and political ideal in the struggles against slavery, apartheid, caste and gender discrimination, as in the French Revolution slogan of liberty, equality, fraternity. Dimensions: political equality (equal citizenship, one person one vote, right to contest elections, freedom of expression), social equality (equal opportunities and facilities, access to healthcare, education and basic needs, abolition of caste and untouchability) and economic equality (reduction of gaps in wealth and income, class inequality). Natural inequalities arise from differences in talent and ability; social inequalities arise from differences in opportunity and status created by society, such as caste, class and gender. Formal equality means equal rights before law (Article 14); substantive equality needs special measures to remove disadvantages. Equality of opportunity gives everyone a fair start; equality of outcome aims at equal results; these can conflict. Affirmative action or positive discrimination includes reservations for SC, ST and OBC in India (Articles 15(4), 16(4)) to correct historical injustice. The Indian Constitution guarantees equality before the law (Article 14), prohibits discrimination (Article 15), ensures equal opportunity in public employment (Article 16), abolishes untouchability (Article 17) and titles (Article 18); the Directive Principles require equal pay for equal work (Article 39). Perspectives: Liberals support equality of opportunity and a minimum standard of living; Marxists see class inequality arising from private ownership of the means of production and advocate economic equality (Marx and Engels); Feminists point to gender inequality and patriarchy (the private-public divide); Egalitarians such as Rousseau noted that inequality arises from society. Rawls argued that inequalities are acceptable only if they benefit the least advantaged. Ambedkar fought caste inequality. Universal adult franchise gave political equality in 1950. Issues: wealth concentration, wage gaps between men and women, access to education, and the debate over reservation. Equality and liberty complement each other. Social movements (Dalit, women, tribal) demand equality. Equality means equal respect for the dignity of all.
## Deep dive: What equality means
Equality is about equal moral worth. It does not remove all differences. Unequal treatment may be justified only where it corrects disadvantage.
## Deep dive: Dimensions and types
Political, social and economic equality interact. Formal equality is not enough where starting points differ. Affirmative action corrects past discrimination.
## Deep dive: Perspectives
Liberals, Marxists and feminists approach equality differently. Rawls links equality to fairness. Debates concern how far the state should intervene.
