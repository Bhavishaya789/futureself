# Nationalism
minutes: 40
yt: class 11 political science nationalism nation self-determination civic ethnic
@study
A nation is a large group of people who believe in a common identity, though it is not based on any single factor; Benedict Anderson called nations imagined communities. Nations are formed by shared beliefs, history, territory, shared political ideals, common political identity and common culture. Ernest Renan said a nation is a daily plebiscite, a will to live together. Nationalism is a sentiment of loyalty and love for the nation; it has inspired people to fight colonial rule and to unify. Nationalism and self-determination: nations claim the right to determine their political destiny and to form their own state (a nation-state). Woodrow Wilson's Fourteen Points in 1918 supported self-determination. After the First World War, Austro-Hungarian and Ottoman empires broke up; after the Second World War, Asia and Africa decolonised, and later the Soviet Union broke up in 1991. Forms: civic nationalism is based on shared political values and citizenship (as in India and USA), while ethnic nationalism is based on common descent, language or religion. Pros: unity, freedom and development; cons: chauvinism, wars (two World Wars), exclusion of minorities and separatist conflicts. Examples: Kurds, Palestinians, Basques and Quebec seek self-determination; demands of separate states in India show claims of minorities. Solutions: democratic rights, federalism, autonomy and cultural rights so that different groups feel at home within a state. The right to secession is debated; many states resist it as a threat to integrity. Tagore criticised narrow nationalism and warned against it (Nationalism, 1917), urging humanism and universal values. Gandhi's nationalism was inclusive and based on non-violence. Nehru stressed unity in diversity. The Indian national movement united people of different religions, castes and regions against colonialism. Minority rights, linguistic diversity and special provisions maintain unity. Nationalism requires accommodating pluralism rather than uniformity. National symbols such as the flag and anthem build identity. International institutions and globalisation are challenging national borders. Humanity can be a wider identity. Nationalism must respect human rights and democracy.
## Deep dive: What is a nation
Nations are imagined communities held by belief. They share identity but not necessarily a single language, religion or race. The will to live together is essential.
## Deep dive: Self-determination
Nations claim the right to rule themselves. This has led to nation-states and to secession demands. States try to balance unity and rights.
## Deep dive: Pluralism
Inclusive nationalism accommodates diversity. Tagore warned about aggression and hate. Rights, federalism and tolerance strengthen unity.
