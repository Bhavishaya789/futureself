# Rights
minutes: 40
yt: class 11 political science rights natural rights human rights udhr
@study
Rights are claims of individuals that society and the state recognise as necessary for a good and dignified life. Rights are essential to live with dignity; they make it possible to develop one's talents. Origin: natural rights theory (Locke) held that people have rights to life, liberty and property by nature, independent of the state; today most thinkers say that rights are claims recognised by society. Charters of rights: Magna Carta (1215), the English Bill of Rights (1689), the American Declaration of Independence (1776) and Bill of Rights (1791), the French Declaration of the Rights of Man and the Citizen (1789) and the Universal Declaration of Human Rights (UDHR), adopted by the UN General Assembly on 10 December 1948 (Human Rights Day), with Eleanor Roosevelt chairing the drafting Commission, which has 30 articles. The two International Covenants (1966) on civil and political rights and on economic, social and cultural rights followed. Types of rights: political rights (vote, contest elections, form parties), civil rights (life, liberty, speech, religion, equality before law), and economic and social rights (work, education, health, social security). Legal rights are enforced by law; moral rights are based on ethics. Positive rights require the state to act (education, health), while negative rights require non-interference (liberty). Rights and the state: the state is the protector of rights but can also violate them; rights limit state power; courts protect rights. Rights and duties: every right implies a duty to respect others' rights. Rights may be restricted for public order, security and morals, but restrictions must be reasonable. New claims include the right to privacy (Puttaswamy, 2017), the right to a healthy environment, the right to information, rights of children and rights of indigenous peoples. Human rights are universal and indivisible. Violations such as custodial violence, discrimination, forced labour and trafficking are challenges. Institutions: NHRC (1993), state human rights commissions, Amnesty International and Human Rights Watch. International Human Rights Day is celebrated annually. Rights are linked to equality and freedom; rights make freedom effective. India's Constitution guarantees fundamental rights and directive principles. Contests over rights: should economic rights be as important as civil? Rights of groups such as women, Dalits and minorities are important. Gender equality is a human right.
## Deep dive: Nature of rights
Rights are claims that are justified and recognised. They protect dignity and enable development. Natural rights theory is replaced by the idea of human rights.
## Deep dive: Kinds of rights
Political, civil and economic rights complement each other. Positive and negative rights differ in what they demand from the state. Legal rights give enforcement.
## Deep dive: Limits and new claims
Rights come with responsibilities and reasonable restrictions. New rights like privacy and environment arise from changing circumstances. Institutions protect rights.
