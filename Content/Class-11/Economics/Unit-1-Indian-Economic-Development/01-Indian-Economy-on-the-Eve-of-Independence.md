# Indian Economy on the Eve of Independence
minutes: 40
yt: class 11 economics indian economy on the eve of independence colonial rule
@study
Under British rule (1757 to 1947) India was a raw material supplier and a market for British goods. Economic policies of the colonial government: drain of wealth, de-industrialisation (decline of handicrafts), commercialisation of agriculture, zamindari system, land revenue. Agricultural stagnation: low productivity (about 0.4 to 0.5 percent growth), little irrigation. Industrial sector: decline of handicrafts, limited modern industry (TISCO 1907, cotton and jute mills), capital goods industry absent. Foreign trade: net exporter of primary goods and importer of finished goods; Suez Canal (1869); drain of wealth. Demographic: birth rate 48 per thousand, death rate 40, infant mortality 218 per thousand, life expectancy 32 years, literacy 16 percent, female literacy 7 percent. Occupational structure: 70-75 percent in agriculture. Infrastructure: railways (1850), ports, post and telegraph. Per capita income stagnant. Dadabhai Naoroji (Poverty and Un-British Rule in India).
## Deep dive: Colonial policies
Drain of wealth: surplus of exports over imports used to pay Home charges, administrative costs of the British, wars. Land revenue systems: Permanent Settlement (1793) of Lord Cornwallis, Ryotwari, Mahalwari. Commercialisation: indigo, cotton, jute instead of foodgrains. Decline of handicrafts: heavy duties on Indian goods, cheap British machine-made goods, tariffs; unemployment of artisans. Zamindars collected rent and did not invest in agriculture. Famines.
## Deep dive: Sectors and trade
Agriculture: low productivity, no irrigation or fertilisers, partition effect on jute and cotton. Industry: TISCO set up 1907 at Jamshedpur; cotton textile mills in Bombay and Ahmedabad; jute in Bengal; no capital goods industry; public sector small. Trade: India exported raw cotton, jute, sugar, indigo, jute; imported finished goods; Britain held monopoly control; Suez Canal reduced cost of trade; no tariffs against British imports.
## Deep dive: Demography, occupation and infrastructure
Birth rate 48 and death rate 40 per thousand; IMR 218; life expectancy 44 in 1951 (about 32 earlier); literacy 16 percent. Occupational structure: agriculture 72 percent, manufacturing 10, services 18. Regional disparity: Madras, Bombay and Bengal presidencies. Positive aspects: railways, ports, communication, introduction of modern administrative system, though railways served British interests. Poverty was widespread with no estimates of national income before Naoroji, V. K. R. V. Rao.
