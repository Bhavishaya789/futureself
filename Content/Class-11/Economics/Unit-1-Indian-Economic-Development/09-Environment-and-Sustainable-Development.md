# Environment and Sustainable Development
minutes: 35
yt: class 11 economics environment and sustainable development
@study
Environment is the total planetary inheritance and the totality of resources. Functions: supplies resources, assimilates waste, sustains life, provides aesthetic services. Carrying capacity and absorptive capacity. Environmental issues: global warming, ozone depletion, biodiversity loss, land degradation, air and water pollution, waste. Causes in India: poverty, population, industrialisation, urbanisation, deforestation. Sustainable development (Brundtland Commission 1987): meeting present needs without compromising future generations. Strategies: use of non-conventional energy (solar, wind, hydro), LPG and gobar gas in rural areas, CNG in urban transport, mass transit, wind power, small hydro, traditional knowledge and practices, biocomposting, biopest control, organic farming. Sustainable development goals (17 SDGs, 2015). Ecological economics. Polluter pays, CPCB, Environment Protection Act 1986.
## Deep dive: Environment and economy
Environment performs four vital functions: supplies resources (renewable and non-renewable), assimilates waste, sustains life by genetic and biodiversity, provides aesthetic services. Absorptive capacity: ability to absorb degradation. Carrying capacity: resource extraction should not exceed regeneration. Environmental crisis: when waste exceeds absorptive capacity and resource extraction exceeds regeneration. Opportunity cost of negative environmental impact.
## Deep dive: Issues in India
Land degradation, biodiversity loss, global warming and climate change (greenhouse gases), ozone depletion (CFCs, Montreal Protocol), air pollution (Delhi, vehicles), water pollution (Ganga), urban waste, loss of forest cover; India's forest cover about 24 percent. Population pressure and poverty degrade land. Industrialisation and agriculture (intensive use of chemicals). Economic cost: health costs, loss of productivity. Environmental laws and institutions: Central Pollution Control Board (1974), Air Act 1981, Water Act 1974.
## Deep dive: Sustainable development
Brundtland: meeting needs of the present without compromising the future. Herman Daly: harvesting rates no greater than regeneration, waste emission within assimilative capacity. Strategies: renewable energy, LPG and gobar gas in rural areas, CNG in cities, mass transport, wind power, solar photovoltaic, small hydro plants, traditional knowledge (Ayurveda, Yoga), biocomposting, biopest control, organic farming, waste recycling. Costs: change of mindset. SDGs: 17 goals including clean energy and climate action. Status: Rio Earth Summit, Paris Agreement.
