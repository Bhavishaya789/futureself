# Human Capital Formation in India
minutes: 35
yt: class 11 economics human capital formation education health
@study
Human capital is the stock of skills, knowledge, health and productive capacity embodied in people. Physical capital versus human capital. Human capital formation sources: expenditure on education, expenditure on health, on-the-job training, migration, information. Human capital and human development: human capital treats human beings as a means, human development treats them as ends. Education: literacy, enrolment, adult literacy; Sarva Shiksha Abhiyan, RTE Act 2009 (6-14 years), mid-day meal, National Education Policy 2020, higher education, vocational education. Education expenditure about 4.6 percent of GDP (target 6 percent). Problems: low female literacy, rural-urban gaps, brain drain, high dropout rates, quality, low public spending, unemployment of educated. Health: preventive and curative; NHM; Ayushman Bharat. Role of human capital in growth.
## Deep dive: Concept and sources
Human capital improves labour productivity. Sources: education (increases efficiency and knowledge), health (improves ability to work: preventive medicine, curative medicine, social medicine, provision of clean drinking water and sanitation), on-the-job training (formal, informal), migration (for jobs with higher wages), information about labour market and education. Example: the difference between a skilled worker and an unskilled worker.
## Deep dive: Education in India
Education expenditure by government and households; literacy rate 2011: 74 percent (male 82, female 65). Schemes: SSA, RTE, NPEGEL, Beti Bachao Beti Padhao, National Skill Development Mission. NEP 2020 recommends 5+3+3+4 structure and multi-disciplinary higher education; target gross enrolment ratio of 50 percent by 2035. Higher education: UGC, AICTE, central and state universities. Economic benefits: higher earnings, productivity, innovation. Problems: dropouts, gender gap, low quality, brain drain, spending below 6 percent of GDP.
## Deep dive: Health and appraisal
Health indicators: life expectancy, IMR, MMR, nutrition. National Health Mission; Ayushman Bharat; immunisation. Public health spending about 2 percent of GDP. Private health care expansion. Appraisal: human capital formation enhances growth, but inequality in access, regional disparities and low public spending remain. Comparison with Kerala. Brain drain/brain gain. Role of NGOs and private sector.
