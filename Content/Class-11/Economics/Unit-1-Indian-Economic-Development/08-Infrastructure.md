# Infrastructure
minutes: 35
yt: class 11 economics infrastructure energy health transport
@study
Infrastructure: facilities supporting economic activity and social life. Economic infrastructure: energy, transport, communication, banking. Social infrastructure: education, health, housing. Importance: raises productivity, contributes to growth, improves quality of life, attracts investment. Energy: commercial (coal, petroleum, electricity) and non-commercial (firewood, dung, agricultural waste); conventional (thermal, hydel, nuclear) and non-conventional (solar, wind, tidal, biogas); installed capacity; transmission and distribution losses; Rural energy: firewood dependence, LPG. Health: preventive and curative; hospitals (public and private); life expectancy; infant mortality; India's health expenditure low; traditional systems (AYUSH); problems. Challenges: low public spending, rural-urban gaps, power shortage, inefficiency. Public-private partnership (PPP). Infrastructure in Delhi, Kerala.
## Deep dive: Energy
Commercial energy: coal (about 55 percent of commercial use), oil, gas, hydropower, nuclear. Electricity generation: thermal (about 60 percent), hydel, nuclear (about 2 percent), renewable. Per capita consumption low. T&D losses 20-25 percent. Non-conventional energy: solar (National Solar Mission), wind (Tamil Nadu, Gujarat), biogas. Problems: shortage of power, financial losses of SEBs, delay, pollution, price subsidies. Reforms: Electricity Act 2003, private participation, Ujjwala for LPG.
## Deep dive: Transport and communication
Roads: National Highways (2 percent of roads but carry large traffic), PMGSY, railways (largest network), airports, ports. Communication: telecom, internet, post. Digital India. Infrastructure bottlenecks. Quality of life: housing, drinking water, sanitation (Swachh Bharat). Role of infrastructure in attracting FDI and enhancing exports.
## Deep dive: Health infrastructure
Health indicators: life expectancy 69 years, IMR about 30, MMR. Health care: primary health centres, community centres, district hospitals, medical colleges. Public spending about 1.3 to 2 percent of GDP. Private sector dominates (about 70 percent of out-patient). Problems: shortage of doctors and beds, rural-urban gap, high out-of-pocket expenditure, malnutrition, communicable diseases. Initiatives: NHM, Ayushman Bharat, AYUSH (Ayurveda, Yoga, Unani, Siddha, Homeopathy), telemedicine.

Aadharbhoot sanrachna: urja, parivahan, swasthya. Gair-paramparik urja: saur, pavan. Vidyut vitran haani.
