# Liberalisation, Privatisation and Globalisation: An Appraisal
minutes: 45
yt: class 11 economics liberalisation privatisation globalisation LPG reforms 1991
@study
Background of 1991 reforms: balance of payments crisis (foreign exchange reserves covering 2 weeks of imports), inflation, fiscal deficit, poor public sector performance, Gulf war. New Economic Policy: stabilisation (short-term) and structural reforms (long-term). Liberalisation: abolition of industrial licensing (except hazardous), deregulation of financial sector (RBI role from regulator to facilitator, bank branch freedom, interest rates), tax reforms (reduction in tax rates, VAT/GST later), foreign exchange reforms (devaluation of rupee by 18-19 percent in 1991, market determined exchange rate), trade and investment policy reforms (removal of quantitative restrictions, lower tariffs, FDI up to 51 percent initially). Privatisation: disinvestment and reduction of the public sector (navratna, maharatna). Globalisation: outsourcing, WTO (1995), BPO. Appraisal: GDP growth about 6-7 percent, service sector growth, poor agriculture growth, employment and inequality concerns.
## Deep dive: Background and reforms
1991 crisis: forex reserves fell to cover about 2 weeks of imports; borrowing from IMF and World Bank with conditionality. LPG: stabilisation measures to maintain sufficient forex and control inflation; structural measures to improve efficiency by making Indian industry competitive. Industrial sector reforms: delicensing, reduction of public sector reservation (from 17 to 3 industries, later 2: atomic energy and railways), MRTP restrictions removed, freedom to import capital goods.
## Deep dive: Components
Financial sector: private banks (HDFC, ICICI), FII allowed to invest, SEBI established (1992), RBI as facilitator. Tax: reduced income and corporate tax rates, simplification, GST (2017). Foreign exchange: rupee devalued, later market determined. Trade: removal of quantitative restrictions on imports, tariff reduction, no licensing for exports, export duties removed. Privatisation: disinvestment (sale of shares of PSUs), strategic sale. Globalisation: integration with the world economy, outsourcing (BPO), WTO membership since 1995.
## Deep dive: Appraisal
Positives: GDP growth rose to about 7 percent, service sector emerged (contributing over 50 percent of GDP), forex reserves increased, FDI and FII inflows, exports rose, IT growth. Negatives: poor agricultural growth, industrial growth slowed, disinvestment criticism, jobless growth, inequality, reduction of agri subsidies, fiscal consolidation reducing social spending, lack of public investment, competition for small units. The reforms did not benefit all equally.
