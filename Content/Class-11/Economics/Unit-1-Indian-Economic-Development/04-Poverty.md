# Poverty
minutes: 40
yt: class 11 economics poverty line headcount ratio poverty alleviation MGNREGA
@study
Poverty is the inability to meet basic needs. Absolute and relative poverty. Poverty line: minimum expenditure for basic needs; in India based on calorie norm (2400 kcal rural, 2100 kcal urban per person per day, earlier); Tendulkar committee (2009-10 Rs 27 per day rural, Rs 33 urban in 2011-12 estimates), Rangarajan committee. Head Count Ratio: percentage of poor. Poverty gap and poverty severity. Causes: colonial legacy, low growth, population pressure, unemployment, unequal land distribution, caste and gender, illiteracy. Poverty estimates: 1993-94 about 36 percent; 2011-12 about 22 percent (Tendulkar), 21.9 percent. Multidimensional Poverty Index (UNDP and OPHI) health, education, living standard. Anti-poverty programmes: MGNREGA (100 days wage employment), PMGSY, SGSY/NRLM, PMRY, SJSRY, PMAY, ICDS, PDS, National Food Security Act 2013, Mid-day meal, Jan Dhan. Two-pronged approach: growth and targeted programmes.
## Deep dive: Measurement and causes
Poverty line is estimated on consumption expenditure; poverty is concentrated among scheduled castes, scheduled tribes, agricultural labourers, casual workers and in states such as Bihar, Odisha, Uttar Pradesh. Causes: low per capita income, population growth, unequal distribution of land and assets, unemployment and underemployment, social factors (customs, caste), colonial exploitation, lack of social security. Dimensions: income, health, education, vulnerability, social exclusion.
## Deep dive: Poverty alleviation programmes
Growth-oriented approach (Green Revolution, industrialisation). Targeted: MGNREGA (2005) guarantees 100 days of wage employment to rural households; PMGSY for rural roads; NRLM for self-help groups; Swarnajayanti Gram Swarozgar Yojana; PMAY for housing; PDS provides subsidised foodgrains; National Food Security Act 2013 covers about two-thirds of the population; Antyodaya Anna Yojana for the poorest. Evaluation: leakages, corruption, targeting errors, lack of awareness.
## Deep dive: Multidimensional poverty and appraisal
MPI uses 10 indicators: nutrition, child mortality, years of schooling, school attendance, cooking fuel, sanitation, drinking water, electricity, housing, assets. India has reduced multidimensional poverty over the last decade. Challenges: regional inequality, urban poverty, vulnerability, quality of life, child malnutrition. Programmes should combine growth with human development (health, education), empower women and raise agricultural productivity.
