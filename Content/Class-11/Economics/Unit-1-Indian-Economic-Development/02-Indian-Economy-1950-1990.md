# Indian Economy 1950-1990
minutes: 45
yt: class 11 economics indian economy 1950 1990 five year plans green revolution
@study
Goals of planning: growth, modernisation, self-reliance, equity. Economic systems: capitalist, socialist, mixed. India adopted a mixed economy; Planning Commission (1950), Five Year Plans (First 1951-56 Harrod-Domar model, agriculture; Second 1956-61 Mahalanobis model, heavy industry). Agriculture: land reforms (abolition of intermediaries, tenancy regulation, ceiling on land holdings, consolidation), Green Revolution (HYV seeds, fertilisers, irrigation; wheat and rice; 1960s), subsidies and MSP. Industry: Industrial Policy Resolution 1956 (Schedule A, B, C), licensing, public sector, small scale industries reservation, MRTP Act 1969, FERA 1973. Trade policy: import substitution, protection (tariffs, quotas). Achievements: GDP growth about 3.5 percent (Hindu rate of growth), diversified industry, self-sufficiency in foodgrains. Limitations: inefficient public sector, licence raj, low growth, poverty. Economic reforms 1991 followed.
## Deep dive: Planning and objectives
Planning Commission, headed by the Prime Minister, replaced by NITI Aayog in 2015. Plans: sector-wise priorities. Goals: growth in GDP, modernisation (technology, social outlook), self-reliance (reducing imports), equity (reducing inequality). Planning is a process of setting goals and allocating resources. Five Year Plans of 1951-90: first (agriculture, irrigation), second (heavy industry).
## Deep dive: Agriculture and Green Revolution
Land reforms: abolition of zamindari (largely successful in removing intermediaries), tenancy reform, ceiling (limit on holding), consolidation. Green Revolution: HYV seeds, chemical fertilisers, irrigation, pesticides; wheat in Punjab, Haryana, west UP; rice in Andhra and Tamil Nadu; foodgrain output from 51 million tonnes (1950-51) to over 170 million tonnes. Marketable surplus rose. Risks: regional disparity, small farmers left out, risk of pest attacks. Subsidies: fertiliser, power; debate on subsidies and MSP.
## Deep dive: Industry, trade and evaluation
IPR 1956: Schedule A (17 industries reserved for state), B (progressively state-owned), C (private). Industrial licensing under the Industries (Development and Regulation) Act 1951. Small scale industries for employment. Import substitution: protect domestic industries from foreign competition. Public sector: heavy industries, infrastructure. Criticism: delays, inefficiency, no competition. Achievements: diversified industrial base, self-reliant, growth about 4 percent. Failure: poverty, inefficiency, balance of payments crisis in 1991.
