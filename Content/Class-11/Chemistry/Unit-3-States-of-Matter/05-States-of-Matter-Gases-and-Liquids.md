@study
Boyle: P V = constant (T, n fixed); Charles: V/T = constant; Gay-Lussac: P/T = constant; Avogadro: V proportional to n. Combined: PV = nRT with R = 0.0821 L atm/(mol K) = 8.314 J/(mol K). Dalton: P_total = sum of partial pressures; Graham: r1/r2 = sqrt(M2/M1). Real gases follow (P + a n^2/V^2)(V - n b) = nRT.
## Deep dive: gas laws
Boyle law: at constant temperature P1 V1 = P2 V2. Charles law: at constant pressure V1/T1 = V2/T2 (T in kelvin). Gay-Lussac law: at constant volume P1/T1 = P2/T2. Avogadro law: equal volumes of gases at the same T and P contain equal numbers of molecules; molar volume at STP (0 C, 1 atm) is 22.4 L and at 1 bar 22.7 L. Combined gas law P1 V1/T1 = P2 V2/T2 and the ideal gas equation PV = nRT = (m/M) RT, giving density d = PM/(RT). R = 0.0821 L atm K^-1 mol^-1 = 8.314 J K^-1 mol^-1 = 0.0831 L bar K^-1 mol^-1.
## Deep dive: mixtures, diffusion and kinetic energy
Dalton law of partial pressures: P_total = P1 + P2 + ...; the partial pressure P_i = x_i P_total where x_i is the mole fraction. Graham law of diffusion: r1/r2 = sqrt (M2/M1) = sqrt (d2/d1) (at equal pressure). Kinetic molecular theory: pressure arises from molecular collisions; average kinetic energy is (3/2) k T, so it depends only on temperature. Speeds: u_rms = sqrt (3RT/M), u_avg = sqrt (8RT/(pi M)), u_mp = sqrt (2RT/M), with u_mp < u_avg < u_rms.
## Deep dive: real gases and liquids
Real gases deviate from ideality at high pressure and low temperature. The compressibility factor Z = PV/(nRT) is 1 for an ideal gas, below 1 where attractions dominate and above 1 where repulsion dominates. van der Waals equation (P + a n^2/V^2)(V - n b) = nRT, where a measures attraction and b is the excluded volume. Boyle temperature T_B = a/(R b). Critical constants: T_c = 8a/(27 R b), P_c = a/(27 b^2), V_c = 3 b. Liquids: vapour pressure rises with temperature (boiling when it equals external pressure); surface tension and viscosity decrease with temperature; intermolecular forces include London dispersion, dipole-dipole and hydrogen bonding.

Q: State Boyle's law.
A: At constant temperature the pressure of a fixed mass of gas is inversely proportional to its volume.
Q: State Charles's law.
A: At constant pressure the volume of a fixed mass of gas is directly proportional to its absolute temperature.
Q: State Gay-Lussac's law.
A: At constant volume the pressure of a fixed mass of gas is directly proportional to its absolute temperature.
Q: State Avogadro's law.
A: Equal volumes of gases at the same temperature and pressure contain equal numbers of molecules.
Q: What is the molar volume of a gas at STP?
A: 22.4 L.
Q: What is the value of R in L atm?
A: 0.0821 L atm K^-1 mol^-1.
Q: What is the value of R in SI units?
A: 8.314 J K^-1 mol^-1.
Q: State Dalton's law of partial pressures.
A: The total pressure of a gas mixture is the sum of the partial pressures of its components.
Q: State Graham's law of diffusion.
A: The rate of diffusion of a gas is inversely proportional to the square root of its molar mass.
Q: What is the compressibility factor?
A: Z = PV/(nRT).
Q: What is the value of Z for an ideal gas?
A: 1.
Q: What does 'a' in van der Waals equation represent?
A: The strength of intermolecular attraction.
Q: What does 'b' in van der Waals equation represent?
A: The excluded volume per mole of the molecules.
Q: What is the Boyle temperature?
A: The temperature at which a real gas behaves ideally over a wide pressure range.
Q: What is the critical temperature?
A: The temperature above which a gas cannot be liquefied by pressure alone.
Q: When do real gases deviate most from ideal behaviour?
A: At high pressure and low temperature.
Q: What is vapour pressure?
A: The pressure exerted by the vapour in equilibrium with its liquid.
Q: When does a liquid boil?
A: When its vapour pressure equals the external pressure.
Q: What is surface tension?
A: The force per unit length along the liquid surface.
Q: What is viscosity?
A: The resistance of a liquid to flow.
Q: What is a hydrogen bond?
A: An attraction between H bonded to N, O or F and a lone pair on another electronegative atom.
Q: What are London dispersion forces?
A: Weak attractions due to temporary dipoles in all molecules.
Q: Which speed is greatest, rms, average or most probable?
A: The rms speed.
Q: What happens to viscosity as temperature rises?
A: It decreases for liquids.
