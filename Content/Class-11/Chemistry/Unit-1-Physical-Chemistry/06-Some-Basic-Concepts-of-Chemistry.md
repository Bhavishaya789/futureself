# Some Basic Concepts of Chemistry

@study
Matter: elements, compounds, mixtures. Laws of chemical combination: conservation of mass (Lavoisier), definite proportions (Proust), multiple proportions (Dalton), Gay-Lussac law of gaseous volumes, Avogadro law. Dalton atomic theory. Atomic mass unit = 1/12 of C-12 mass = 1.66 x 10^-24 g. Mole = 6.022 x 10^23 entities; molar mass in g/mol; molar volume 22.4 L at STP (22.7 L at 1 bar, 273 K). Percentage composition, empirical and molecular formula (n = molecular mass / empirical mass). Stoichiometry, limiting reagent. Concentration: mass %, mole fraction, molarity (mol/L), molality (mol/kg), normality. Significant figures, scientific notation, SI units, dimensional analysis. Precision and accuracy.
## Deep dive: Laws and the mole
Lavoisier: mass of reactants equals mass of products. Proust: a compound always has the same elements in a fixed ratio by mass. Dalton: if two elements form more than one compound, masses of one combining with a fixed mass of the other are in simple whole-number ratio (CO and CO2). Gay-Lussac: gases react in simple volume ratios. Avogadro: equal volumes of gases at the same T and P contain equal numbers of molecules. Mole: 1 mol contains 6.022 x 10^23 particles; number of moles = mass / molar mass = particles / NA = volume at STP / 22.4 L. Molar mass of H2O = 18 g/mol, CO2 = 44 g/mol.
## Deep dive: Formulae and stoichiometry
Empirical formula: simplest whole-number ratio; molecular formula = n x empirical formula. Steps: percentage to mass, divide by atomic mass, divide by smallest, convert to whole numbers. Stoichiometric coefficients give mole ratios, not mass. Limiting reagent: the reactant that is completely consumed and fixes the amount of product; the other is in excess. Example: 2H2 + O2 -> 2H2O. Percent yield = actual / theoretical x 100. Combustion analysis for C and H.
## Deep dive: Concentration terms
Mass % = mass of solute / mass of solution x 100. Mole fraction x_A = n_A / (n_A + n_B); sum of mole fractions is 1. Molarity M = moles of solute / volume of solution in L, changes with temperature. Molality m = moles of solute / kg of solvent, independent of temperature. Dilution: M1V1 = M2V2. Relation: M = (density x 1000 x mass % ) / (100 x molar mass). Significant figures: rules for zeros; calculations. Dimensional analysis uses unit factors.
