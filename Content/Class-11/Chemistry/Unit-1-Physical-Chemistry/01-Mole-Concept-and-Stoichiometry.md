# Some basic concepts: mole and stoichiometry

@study
**Mole:** the amount of substance containing 6.022 x 10^23 particles (Avogadro number N_A). Molar mass in g/mol equals the relative atomic/molecular mass: water (18 g/mol), 18 g = 1 mol.

## Concentration terms
- **Molarity** (M) = moles of solute / litres of solution. 5.85 g NaCl (58.5 g/mol) in 500 mL: moles = 0.1; M = 0.1 / 0.5 = **0.2 M**.
- Molality = moles / kg of solvent; mole fraction x_A = n_A / (n_A + n_B); mass percent.

## Percentage composition and empirical formula
H2SO4: M = 98.08 g/mol; H 2.06%, S 32.69%, O 65.26%.
Empirical formula example: C 40%, H 6.7%, O 53.3%. Moles: C 40/12 = 3.33, H 6.7/1 = 6.7, O 53.3/16 = 3.33. Ratio 1 : 2 : 1, so empirical formula **CH2O**.

## Limiting reagent
In 2 H2 + O2 -> 2 H2O, with 4 g H2 (2 mol) and 16 g O2 (0.5 mol), O2 needs 1 mol H2 so O2 is limiting; it forms 1 mol (18 g) of water.

## Gas laws
At STP (0 C, 1 atm) 1 mol of ideal gas occupies about 22.4 L. PV = nRT with R = 0.0821 L atm/(mol K): 2 mol at 300 K and 1 atm occupies 49.3 L.

## Advanced
P: 4 g of hydrogen reacts with 16 g of oxygen. Find the limiting reagent and the mass of water formed.
A: H2 = 2 mol, O2 = 0.5 mol. 2 H2 + O2 -> 2 H2O needs 1 mol H2 for 0.5 mol O2, so O2 is limiting and 1 mol water (18 g) forms.
R: 2 | Limiting reagent O2
R: 2 | 18 g
