# Chemical Bonding and Molecular Structure
minutes: 45

@study
Kossel-Lewis approach: octet rule, Lewis dot structures, formal charge. Ionic bond: lattice enthalpy, Born-Haber cycle. Covalent bond: bond parameters (length, angle, enthalpy, order), resonance, polarity, dipole moment (mu = q x d), Fajans rules. VSEPR theory predicts shapes: linear (BeCl2), trigonal planar (BF3), tetrahedral (CH4), trigonal bipyramidal (PCl5), octahedral (SF6), bent (H2O), pyramidal (NH3), see-saw (SF4), T-shaped (ClF3), square planar (XeF4). Valence bond theory, hybridisation sp, sp2, sp3, sp3d, sp3d2; sigma and pi bonds. Molecular orbital theory: bonding and antibonding MOs, bond order = (Nb - Na)/2; O2 is paramagnetic; N2 bond order 3. Hydrogen bonding (intramolecular, intermolecular).
## Deep dive: Ionic and covalent bonding
Octet rule exceptions: incomplete octet (BeCl2, BF3), expanded octet (PCl5, SF6), odd electron (NO). Formal charge = V - L - B/2. Lattice enthalpy increases with higher charge and smaller size; NaCl 788 kJ/mol. Born-Haber cycle: sublimation, ionisation, dissociation, electron gain, lattice. Resonance: CO3^2- with bond order 1.33; benzene. Fajans: small cation, large anion, high charge gives more covalent character (LiCl vs NaCl). Dipole moment: NH3 > NF3; CO2 zero; CH4 zero; H2O 1.85 D.
## Deep dive: VSEPR and hybridisation
Lone pair-lone pair repulsion > lone pair-bond pair > bond pair-bond pair. Bond angles: CH4 109.5, NH3 107, H2O 104.5. sp: linear (BeCl2, C2H2); sp2: trigonal planar 120 (BF3, C2H4); sp3: tetrahedral (CH4); sp3d: PCl5; sp3d2: SF6. A sigma bond is formed by head-on overlap, pi by sideways overlap; single bond one sigma, double one sigma + one pi, triple one sigma + two pi.
## Deep dive: MO theory and hydrogen bond
LCAO: bonding MO lower energy; antibonding higher. Order for O2, F2: sigma1s < sigma*1s < sigma2s < sigma*2s < sigma2pz < pi2px = pi2py < pi*2px = pi*2py < sigma*2pz; for B2, C2, N2: pi before sigma2pz. Bond order: H2 1, He2 0, N2 3, O2 2, O2^+ 2.5, O2^- 1.5. Hydrogen bond: H bonded to F, O, N; explains the high boiling point of water and the lower density of ice; intramolecular in o-nitrophenol (lower boiling point than p-isomer).
