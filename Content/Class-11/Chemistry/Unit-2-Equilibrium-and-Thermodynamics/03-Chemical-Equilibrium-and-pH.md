@study
At equilibrium the forward and reverse rates are equal; concentrations stay constant. For a A + b B <=> c C + d D, **K_c = [C]^c [D]^d / ([A]^a [B]^b)**. K depends only on temperature.
**Le Chatelier's principle:** a system at equilibrium shifts to oppose any change in concentration, pressure or temperature. A catalyst does not shift equilibrium; it only speeds approach.

### Example
N2O4 <=> 2 NO2, start 1 mol/L N2O4, 20% dissociates: [N2O4] = 0.8, [NO2] = 0.4, K_c = 0.4^2 / 0.8 = **0.2**.

## pH
pH = -log [H+]; pH + pOH = 14 at 25 C. 0.01 M HCl has pH **2**. For a weak acid, [H+] = sqrt(K_a C): 0.1 M acetic acid (K_a = 1.8 x 10^-5): [H+] = 1.34 x 10^-3, pH = **2.87**.
**Buffer** (Henderson-Hasselbalch): pH = pKa + log([salt]/[acid]).
