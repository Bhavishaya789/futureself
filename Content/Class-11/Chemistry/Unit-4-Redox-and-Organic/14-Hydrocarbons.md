@study
Alkanes (CnH2n+2): preparation (Wurtz reaction, Kolbe electrolysis, hydrogenation, decarboxylation of sodium salts with soda lime), properties (substitution by halogenation - free radical mechanism: initiation, propagation, termination; combustion; isomerisation; aromatisation; pyrolysis), conformations of ethane (staggered more stable than eclipsed; torsional strain). Alkenes (CnH2n): preparation (dehydrohalogenation, dehydration of alcohols, partial hydrogenation of alkynes), properties (electrophilic addition, Markovnikov rule, anti-Markovnikov (Kharasch, peroxide effect), ozonolysis, oxidation with KMnO4 (Baeyer test), polymerisation), geometrical isomerism (cis-trans). Alkynes (CnH2n-2): preparation (CaC2 + H2O, dehydrohalogenation), acidic nature of terminal alkynes, addition reactions, hydration to ketone/aldehyde, polymerisation to benzene. Aromatic hydrocarbons: benzene, Kekule structure, resonance, Huckel rule (4n+2) pi electrons, electrophilic substitution (nitration, halogenation, sulphonation, Friedel-Crafts), directing groups (o,p-activating: -OH, -NH2, -CH3; m-directing: -NO2, -COOH, -CN), carcinogenicity.
## Deep dive: Alkanes
Wurtz: 2R-X + 2Na -> R-R + 2NaX (dry ether). Kolbe: 2RCOONa + 2H2O -> R-R + 2CO2 + H2 + 2NaOH. Decarboxylation: RCOONa + NaOH -> RH + Na2CO3 (CaO). Halogenation reactivity: F2 > Cl2 > Br2 > I2; hydrogen order 3 > 2 > 1. Combustion: CnH2n+2 + (3n+1)/2 O2 -> n CO2 + (n+1) H2O. Conformations: staggered (60 degrees dihedral, minimum energy), eclipsed (0, maximum, energy difference 12.5 kJ/mol).
## Deep dive: Alkenes and alkynes
Markovnikov: the negative part adds to the carbon with fewer hydrogens. Peroxide effect: HBr adds anti-Markovnikov. Ozonolysis gives carbonyl compounds (propene gives CH3CHO + HCHO). Cold dilute KMnO4 gives diols (Baeyer), hot gives acids/ketones. Geometrical isomers cis and trans (but-2-ene). Alkynes: acetylene has sp carbons, 180 degrees, bond length 120 pm; terminal H acidic (sodium, ammoniacal AgNO3, Cu2Cl2 give precipitates); hydration with HgSO4/H2SO4 gives acetaldehyde from ethyne, acetone from propyne; trimerisation to benzene via red-hot iron tube at 873 K.
## Deep dive: Aromatic hydrocarbons
Benzene C6H6: planar, all C-C bonds 139 pm, sp2, resonance energy 150 kJ/mol; aromatic if cyclic, planar, conjugated with 4n+2 pi electrons (benzene 6, naphthalene 10, cyclopentadienyl anion 6). Electrophilic substitution mechanism: electrophile generation, sigma complex (arenium ion), loss of H+. Nitration: HNO3 + H2SO4 gives NO2+. Friedel-Crafts alkylation and acylation with AlCl3. Activating groups direct o/p; deactivating (except halogens) direct meta. Addition to benzene under drastic conditions: hydrogenation (cyclohexane), chlorination (BHC).
