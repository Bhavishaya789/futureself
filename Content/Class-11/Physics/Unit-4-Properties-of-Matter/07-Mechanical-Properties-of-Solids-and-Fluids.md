@study
Stress = force/area, strain = change/original, Young modulus Y = stress/strain. Fluid pressure P = P0 + rho g h; Pascal law transmits pressure equally; Archimedes: buoyant force = weight of fluid displaced. Continuity A1 v1 = A2 v2; Bernoulli P + (1/2) rho v^2 + rho g h = constant. Surface tension S = F/l, capillary rise h = 2 S cos theta/(rho g r), terminal velocity v = 2 r^2 (rho - sigma) g/(9 eta).
## Deep dive: elasticity
When a deforming force acts on a body, internal restoring forces appear. Stress = restoring force per unit area (N/m^2 = Pa). Strain = fractional change: longitudinal (change in length/length), volumetric (change in volume/volume) or shear (angle of shear). Hooke law: within the elastic limit stress is proportional to strain. Young modulus Y = (F/A)/(DL/L), bulk modulus B = -P/(DV/V) with compressibility 1/B, shear modulus G = shear stress/shear strain. Poisson ratio = lateral strain/longitudinal strain, with a value between -1 and 0.5. Elastic energy per unit volume = (1/2) x stress x strain. Work done in stretching a wire = (1/2) F x DL = (1/2) Y A DL^2/L. Steel is more elastic than rubber because it has a higher modulus.
## Deep dive: fluid statics and dynamics
Pressure P = F/A; in a fluid at rest P = P0 + rho g h and acts equally in all directions at a given depth. Pascal law: pressure applied to an enclosed fluid is transmitted undiminished (hydraulic lift F2/F1 = A2/A1). Archimedes principle: the upthrust equals the weight of fluid displaced; a body floats when its density is less than the fluid (fraction submerged = rho_body/rho_fluid). Steady (streamline) flow of an incompressible fluid satisfies the equation of continuity A1 v1 = A2 v2. Bernoulli theorem: P + (1/2) rho v^2 + rho g h = constant along a streamline; speed of efflux from a hole at depth h is v = sqrt (2 g h) (Torricelli). Applications: venturimeter, aerofoil lift, atomiser, Magnus effect.
## Deep dive: viscosity and surface tension
Viscous force F = eta A (dv/dx); the SI unit of viscosity eta is Pa s (poise in CGS: 1 Pa s = 10 poise). Stokes law: F = 6 pi eta r v. Terminal velocity of a sphere falling in a fluid v_t = 2 r^2 (rho - sigma) g/(9 eta). Reynolds number Re = rho v d/eta decides laminar (< 1000) or turbulent (> 2000) flow. Surface tension S = F/l = energy per unit area; excess pressure inside a drop is 2S/R, inside a soap bubble 4S/R. Capillary rise h = 2 S cos theta/(r rho g). Angle of contact is acute for liquids that wet glass (water), obtuse for non-wetting liquids (mercury). Surface energy of a drop = S x area; work to form a bubble of radius R is 8 pi R^2 S.

Q: What is stress?
A: The restoring force per unit area.
Q: What is strain?
A: The fractional change in the dimension of the body.
Q: State Hooke's law.
A: Within the elastic limit, stress is proportional to strain.
Q: What is Young's modulus?
A: The ratio of longitudinal stress to longitudinal strain.
Q: What is the bulk modulus?
A: The ratio of volume stress (pressure) to volume strain.
Q: What is the shear modulus?
A: The ratio of shear stress to shear strain.
Q: What is Poisson's ratio?
A: The ratio of lateral strain to longitudinal strain.
Q: What is the SI unit of pressure?
A: Pascal (N/m^2).
Q: State Pascal's law.
A: Pressure applied to an enclosed fluid is transmitted undiminished in every direction.
Q: State Archimedes' principle.
A: A body in a fluid experiences an upthrust equal to the weight of fluid it displaces.
Q: What is the equation of continuity?
A: A1 v1 = A2 v2.
Q: State Bernoulli's theorem.
A: For streamline flow of an ideal fluid, P + (1/2) rho v^2 + rho g h is constant.
Q: What is viscosity?
A: The internal friction of a fluid that opposes relative motion of its layers.
Q: What is Stokes' law?
A: The viscous drag on a sphere is 6 pi eta r v.
Q: What is terminal velocity?
A: The constant velocity reached when drag and buoyancy balance weight.
Q: What is surface tension?
A: The force per unit length acting along the surface of a liquid.
Q: Why do liquid drops become spherical?
A: Surface tension minimises the surface area.
Q: What is the angle of contact?
A: The angle between the liquid surface and the solid surface at the point of contact.
Q: Why is steel more elastic than rubber?
A: Steel has a larger Young's modulus.
Q: What is capillarity?
A: The rise or fall of a liquid in a narrow tube.
Q: What is the unit of viscosity?
A: Pa s (or poise in CGS).
Q: What is the Reynolds number?
A: rho v d / eta, which decides if the flow is laminar or turbulent.
Q: Why does an aerofoil get lift?
A: Faster air above lowers the pressure there (Bernoulli).
Q: Which has greater excess pressure, a drop or a soap bubble of the same radius?
A: A soap bubble, 4S/R versus 2S/R.
