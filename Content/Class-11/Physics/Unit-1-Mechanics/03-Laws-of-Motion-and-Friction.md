## Newton's laws
1. **First law (inertia):** a body stays at rest or in uniform motion unless a net external force acts.
2. **Second law:** F = m a (net force equals rate of change of momentum, F = dp/dt).
3. **Third law:** to every action there is an equal and opposite reaction (acting on different bodies).

## Free-body diagrams
Draw only forces on one body: weight mg, normal N, tension T, friction f, applied force. Resolve along and perpendicular to motion, then apply F = ma.

## Friction
Static friction f_s <= mu_s N (self-adjusting); kinetic friction f_k = mu_k N. On a rough incline of angle t: a = g (sin t - mu cos t); a block just starts to slide when tan t = mu_s.

## Atwood machine
Masses m1 < m2 over a light pulley: a = (m2 - m1) g / (m1 + m2), T = 2 m1 m2 g / (m1 + m2).
For 3 kg and 5 kg (g = 10): a = 2 x 10 / 8 = **2.5 m/s^2**, T = 2 x 15 x 10 / 8 = **37.5 N**.

### Incline example
Block on a 30 degree incline with mu = 0.2: a = 10 (0.5 - 0.2 x 0.866) = **3.27 m/s^2** down the slope.

## Circular motion and banking
Needed centripetal force F = m v^2 / r. On a level road, friction supplies it: v_max = sqrt(mu g r).

Q: State Newton's second law.
A: The net force on a body equals its mass times acceleration, F = ma, or the rate of change of momentum.
Q: Why do action and reaction not cancel?
A: They act on two different bodies.
Q: Acceleration in an Atwood machine with 3 kg and 5 kg?
A: a = (5 - 3) x 10 / 8 = 2.5 m/s^2.
Q: Maximum speed on a level curve of radius r with friction mu?
A: v = sqrt(mu g r).
