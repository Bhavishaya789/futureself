@study
Simple harmonic motion has acceleration a = -omega^2 x, period T = 2 pi/omega, displacement x = A sin (omega t + phi), speed v = omega sqrt(A^2 - x^2). For a spring T = 2 pi sqrt(m/k); for a simple pendulum T = 2 pi sqrt(L/g). A wave has speed v = f lambda; on a string v = sqrt(T/mu); in a gas v = sqrt(gamma P/rho); stationary waves, beats and the Doppler effect follow.
## Deep dive: simple harmonic motion
In SHM the restoring force is proportional to displacement and directed towards the mean position: F = -k x, so a = -omega^2 x with omega^2 = k/m. Displacement x = A sin (omega t + phi); velocity v = A omega cos (omega t + phi) = omega sqrt (A^2 - x^2), maximum A omega at the mean position; maximum acceleration A omega^2 at the extremes. Time period T = 2 pi/omega, frequency f = 1/T, and omega = 2 pi f. Energy: kinetic (1/2) m omega^2 (A^2 - x^2), potential (1/2) m omega^2 x^2, total (1/2) m omega^2 A^2 = (1/2) k A^2, constant. Kinetic and potential energies each oscillate at twice the frequency of the motion; their time-average is half the total.
## Deep dive: spring, pendulum and combinations
A mass m on a spring: T = 2 pi sqrt (m/k). Springs in series: 1/k = 1/k1 + 1/k2; in parallel: k = k1 + k2. Simple pendulum: T = 2 pi sqrt (L/g), independent of mass and amplitude (small angles); the seconds pendulum (T = 2 s) has length about 1 m. In an accelerating lift g becomes g +- a; on the Moon g is one-sixth so T is larger by sqrt 6. Physical pendulum: T = 2 pi sqrt (I/(m g d)). Damped oscillations lose amplitude exponentially; forced oscillations show resonance when the driving frequency equals the natural frequency.
## Deep dive: wave motion
A progressive wave y = A sin (k x - omega t) has wave number k = 2 pi/lambda, speed v = omega/k = f lambda. Transverse waves (particles move perpendicular to propagation) travel in solids on strings; longitudinal waves (compressions and rarefactions) such as sound need a medium. Speed on a stretched string v = sqrt (T/mu); in a gas v = sqrt (gamma P/rho) = sqrt (gamma R T/M), increasing about 0.6 m/s per degree Celsius near 20 C. Stationary waves arise from superposition of two opposite waves: nodes (zero amplitude) and antinodes (maximum), adjacent nodes lambda/2 apart. A string fixed at both ends: f_n = n v/(2L); an open pipe: f_n = n v/(2L); a closed pipe: f_n = (2n - 1) v/(4L) (odd harmonics only). Beats: two close frequencies f1 and f2 give beat frequency |f1 - f2|. Doppler effect: f' = f (v +- v_o)/(v -+ v_s).

A: 2 pi sqrt (m/k).
Q: What is the period of a simple pendulum?
A: 2 pi sqrt (L/g).
Q: Does the period of a pendulum depend on mass?
A: No.
Q: What is a seconds pendulum?
A: A pendulum with a period of 2 s.
Q: What is resonance?
A: Large amplitude oscillation when the driving frequency equals the natural frequency.
Q: What is damping?
A: Loss of amplitude due to dissipative forces.
Q: What is a transverse wave?
A: A wave where particles vibrate perpendicular to the direction of propagation.
Q: What is a longitudinal wave?
A: A wave where particles vibrate parallel to the direction of propagation.
Q: What is wavelength?
A: The distance between two consecutive points in the same phase.
Q: What is the relation between v, f and lambda?
A: v = f lambda.
Q: What is a stationary wave?
A: A wave formed by two identical waves travelling in opposite directions; it has nodes and antinodes.
Q: What is the distance between adjacent nodes?
A: lambda/2.
Q: What is the beat frequency?
A: The difference of the two frequencies.
Q: What is the Doppler effect?
A: The change of observed frequency due to relative motion of source and observer.
Q: Which harmonics does a closed pipe have?
A: Only odd harmonics.
Q: What is the speed of sound in a gas?
A: sqrt (gamma P/rho).
Q: What is an overtone?
A: A frequency higher than the fundamental.
Q: What is the principle of superposition?
A: The resultant displacement is the sum of the individual displacements.
