# Work, Energy and Power
minutes: 45
yt: class 11 physics work energy power conservation collisions
@study
Work is done when a force produces displacement: W = F d cos theta = F.d (a scalar). Its unit is the joule (1 J = 1 N m). Work is positive when the force has a component along the displacement, negative when opposite (friction) and zero when perpendicular or when there is no displacement (carrying a load horizontally). Work done by a variable force is the area under the force-displacement graph, W = integral F dx. Energy is the capacity to do work; unit joule. Kinetic energy K = (1/2) m v^2 = p^2/(2m). Work-energy theorem: net work done equals the change in kinetic energy, W = K_f - K_i. Potential energy is the energy due to position or configuration: gravitational PE = m g h; spring PE = (1/2) k x^2. A force is conservative if the work done is independent of the path or zero in a closed path (gravity, spring); friction is non-conservative. Conservation of mechanical energy: for conservative forces K + U = constant. Hooke's law F = -k x, spring constant k in N/m. Power is the rate of doing work: P = W/t = F.v; unit watt (1 W = 1 J/s); 1 horsepower = 746 W; 1 kilowatt hour = 3.6 x 10^6 J. Efficiency = useful output/input x 100. Collisions: in an elastic collision both momentum and kinetic energy are conserved; in an inelastic collision only momentum is conserved; in a perfectly inelastic collision the bodies stick together. For an elastic head-on collision of masses m1 and m2: v1' = ((m1 - m2) v1 + 2 m2 v2)/(m1 + m2) and v2' = ((m2 - m1) v2 + 2 m1 v1)/(m1 + m2). For equal masses the velocities exchange. Coefficient of restitution e = relative speed of separation divided by relative speed of approach; e = 1 for elastic and 0 for perfectly inelastic. Rain, bouncing ball, pendulum and roller coaster illustrate energy changes. Mass-energy relation E = m c^2 by Einstein. Forms of energy include mechanical, heat, light, sound, chemical, nuclear and electrical. The loss of kinetic energy in perfectly inelastic collision is (1/2) mu v_rel^2 with reduced mass mu. A body dropped from height h has speed sqrt(2gh) at the ground.
## Deep dive: Work and energy
Work depends on force, displacement and the angle between them. Kinetic energy depends on speed squared. Potential energy depends on position. The work-energy theorem applies to all forces.
## Deep dive: Conservation
Mechanical energy is conserved if only conservative forces act. A swinging pendulum converts potential to kinetic energy and back. With friction, mechanical energy falls and becomes heat.
## Deep dive: Power and collisions
Power tells how fast work is done. Elastic collisions conserve both momentum and kinetic energy. Inelastic collisions lose kinetic energy but momentum is conserved. e measures elasticity.
