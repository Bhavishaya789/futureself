# Work, energy and power
minutes: 20
yt: work energy theorem class 11
@study
**Work** W = F d cos t (scalar). Positive if force has a component along the displacement.
**Kinetic energy** K = (1/2) m v^2. **Work-energy theorem:** net work = change in kinetic energy.
**Potential energy:** gravitational U = m g h; spring U = (1/2) k x^2. Conservative forces conserve mechanical energy K + U.
**Power** P = W / t = F v. 1 horsepower is about 746 W.

### Examples
- A 2 kg body at rest pushed by 10 N over 5 m (no friction): W = **50 J**, so v = sqrt(2 x 50 / 2) = **7.07 m/s**.
- Spring k = 200 N/m compressed 0.1 m stores (1/2)(200)(0.01) = **1 J**.
- Free fall from 80 m (g = 10): speed at the ground from energy: v = sqrt(2 g h) = 40 m/s.

## Collisions
Momentum is conserved in every collision. **Elastic:** kinetic energy also conserved. **Perfectly inelastic:** bodies stick together; maximum KE loss. Coefficient of restitution e = (speed of separation) / (speed of approach).

## Moderate
P: A 2 kg body at rest is pushed by a 10 N force over 5 m without friction. Find its final speed.
A: W = 50 J = (1/2)(2)v^2, so v = 7.07 m/s.

## High
P: A 1 kg ball moving at 4 m/s hits a stationary 1 kg ball elastically head-on. Find the final velocities.
A: For equal masses in a head-on elastic collision velocities are exchanged: first ball 0, second ball 4 m/s.
R: 2 | Uses conservation laws or the exchange result
R: 1 | 0 and 4 m/s
## Advanced
P: A 2 kg block moving at 3 m/s sticks to a 1 kg block at rest. Find the common velocity and the energy lost.
A: v = 2 x 3 / 3 = 2 m/s. KE before = 9 J, after = (1/2)(3)(4) = 6 J, loss = 3 J.
R: 2 | v = 2 m/s
R: 2 | KE before and after
R: 1 | 3 J lost
