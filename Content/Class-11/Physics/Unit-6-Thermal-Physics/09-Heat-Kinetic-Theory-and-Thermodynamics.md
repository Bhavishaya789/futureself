@study
Heat Q = m c DT; PV = nRT; the kinetic energy per molecule is (3/2) k T and v_rms = sqrt(3RT/M). First law: Q = DU + W. Efficiency of a Carnot engine = 1 - T2/T1. Heat flow by conduction H = k A DT/L; Stefan law E = sigma T^4; Wien law lambda_m T = b.
## Deep dive: temperature, heat and calorimetry
Temperature measures the average kinetic energy of molecules; heat is energy in transit due to a temperature difference. Scales: C/5 = (F - 32)/9 = (K - 273)/5. Heat required Q = m c DT; for a phase change Q = m L (latent heat of fusion for ice 336 kJ/kg, of vaporisation of water 2260 kJ/kg). Principle of calorimetry: heat lost = heat gained. Specific heat of water is 4200 J/(kg K). Thermal expansion: DL = alpha L DT, DA = 2 alpha A DT, DV = 3 alpha V DT. Water shows anomalous expansion between 0 and 4 C, with maximum density at 4 C.
## Deep dive: kinetic theory of gases
Ideal gas equation PV = nRT = N k T with R = 8.31 J/(mol K) and k = 1.38 x 10^-23 J/K. Pressure P = (1/3) rho v_rms^2. Average kinetic energy per molecule (3/2) k T; v_rms = sqrt (3RT/M), v_avg = sqrt (8RT/(pi M)), v_mp = sqrt (2RT/M), with v_rms : v_avg : v_mp = sqrt 3 : sqrt (8/pi) : sqrt 2. Degrees of freedom f: energy per mole (f/2) R T; monatomic f = 3, diatomic f = 5 at room temperature. C_v = (f/2) R, C_p = C_v + R (Mayer), gamma = C_p/C_v = 1 + 2/f: 5/3 for monatomic, 7/5 for diatomic. Mean free path lambda = 1/(sqrt 2 pi d^2 n).
## Deep dive: thermodynamics and transfer
First law: Q = DU + W where W = integral P dV. Isothermal: DU = 0, W = nRT ln (V2/V1), PV = constant. Adiabatic: Q = 0, P V^gamma = constant, W = (P1 V1 - P2 V2)/(gamma - 1). Isobaric: W = P DV. Isochoric: W = 0. Heat engine efficiency = W/Q1 = 1 - Q2/Q1; a Carnot engine between T1 and T2 has efficiency 1 - T2/T1 (maximum). Refrigerator coefficient of performance = T2/(T1 - T2). Second law: heat cannot flow by itself from cold to hot (Clausius); no engine converts all heat to work (Kelvin-Planck). Conduction H = k A (T1 - T2)/L; convection; radiation: a black body emits E = sigma T^4 per unit area (Stefan), with sigma = 5.67 x 10^-8 W/(m^2 K^4); Wien displacement lambda_m T = 2.9 x 10^-3 m K; Newton law of cooling: rate of cooling proportional to temperature difference.

Q: What is temperature?
A: A measure of the average kinetic energy of the molecules.
Q: What is heat?
A: Energy transferred due to a temperature difference.
Q: What is the specific heat capacity?
A: The heat needed to raise the temperature of 1 kg of a substance by 1 K.
Q: What is latent heat?
A: The heat absorbed or released during a change of state at constant temperature.
Q: State the principle of calorimetry.
A: Heat lost by hot bodies equals heat gained by cold bodies.
Q: What is the ideal gas equation?
A: PV = nRT.
Q: What is the average kinetic energy of a molecule?
A: (3/2) k T.

A: 3.

A: 5.

A: C_p - C_v = R.
Q: What is the value of gamma for a monatomic gas?
A: 5/3.
Q: What is the value of gamma for a diatomic gas?
A: 7/5.
Q: State the first law of thermodynamics.
A: Q = DU + W: heat supplied equals the change in internal energy plus the work done.
Q: What is an isothermal process?
A: A process at constant temperature.
Q: What is an adiabatic process?
A: A process with no heat exchange.
Q: State the second law of thermodynamics.
A: Heat cannot spontaneously flow from a colder to a hotter body; no engine is 100% efficient.
Q: What is the efficiency of a Carnot engine?
A: 1 - T2/T1.
Q: What is Stefan's law?
A: The energy radiated per unit area per second by a black body is sigma T^4.
Q: What is Wien's displacement law?
A: lambda_m T = constant (2.9 x 10^-3 m K).
Q: What is Newton's law of cooling?
A: The rate of cooling is proportional to the temperature difference.
Q: What is a black body?
A: A body that absorbs all radiation falling on it.
Q: Why is water used as a coolant?
A: It has a high specific heat capacity.
Q: What is the anomalous expansion of water?
A: Between 0 and 4 C its volume decreases on heating.
Q: What is the mean free path?
A: The average distance a molecule travels between collisions.
