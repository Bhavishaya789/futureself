# Trigonometric Functions

@study
An angle is measured in degrees or radians. 180 degrees = pi radians, so 1 radian is about 57.3 degrees; radian measure = degree measure x pi/180. For a circle of radius r, the arc length is s = r times theta (theta in radians). The trigonometric functions are sin, cos, tan, cosec, sec and cot. On the unit circle a point has coordinates (cos x, sin x). Signs by quadrant follow the rule All, Sin, Tan, Cos (ASTC): all positive in the first, sin in the second, tan in the third and cos in the fourth. Fundamental identities: sin^2 x + cos^2 x = 1, 1 + tan^2 x = sec^2 x, 1 + cot^2 x = cosec^2 x. Values: sin 0 = 0, sin 30 = 1/2, sin 45 = 1/sqrt 2, sin 60 = sqrt3/2, sin 90 = 1; cos is the reverse. tan 45 = 1. Domain and range: sin and cos have domain R and range [-1, 1]; tan has period pi and range R. Periods: sin, cos, cosec, sec have period 2 pi; tan and cot have pi. sin(-x) = -sin x (odd), cos(-x) = cos x (even). Compound angles: sin(A + B) = sin A cos B + cos A sin B; cos(A + B) = cos A cos B - sin A sin B; tan(A + B) = (tan A + tan B)/(1 - tan A tan B). Double angle: sin 2A = 2 sin A cos A; cos 2A = cos^2 A - sin^2 A = 2cos^2 A - 1 = 1 - 2 sin^2 A; tan 2A = 2 tan A/(1 - tan^2 A). Triple angle: sin 3A = 3 sin A - 4 sin^3 A; cos 3A = 4 cos^3 A - 3 cos A. Product to sum: 2 sin A cos B = sin(A+B) + sin(A-B). Sum to product: sin C + sin D = 2 sin((C+D)/2) cos((C-D)/2). Equations: sin x = sin y gives x = n pi + (-1)^n y; cos x = cos y gives x = 2n pi plus or minus y; tan x = tan y gives x = n pi + y.
## Deep dive: Angles and circle
Convert degrees to radians by multiplying by pi/180. Arc length s = r theta and sector area is half r^2 theta. A full turn is 2 pi radians. Angles in standard position are measured from the positive x-axis.
## Deep dive: Functions and identities
Use ASTC for signs. Reduce angles with pi plus or minus x. sin(90 - x) = cos x and cos(90 - x) = sin x. Identities simplify and prove expressions.
## Deep dive: Compound angles and equations
Formulas for A plus or minus B lead to double and triple angle formulas. Product and sum formulas help in transformations. General solutions use the integer n.
