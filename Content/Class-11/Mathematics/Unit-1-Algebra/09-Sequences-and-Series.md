@study
A sequence is a list of numbers in a definite order; a series is the sum of the terms. In an Arithmetic Progression (AP) the difference d between consecutive terms is constant: a, a + d, a + 2d, ... The nth term is a_n = a + (n - 1)d and the sum of n terms is S_n = n/2 [2a + (n - 1)d] = n/2 (a + l), where l is the last term. Three numbers in AP can be taken as a - d, a, a + d. The arithmetic mean of a and b is (a + b)/2, and n arithmetic means between a and b are inserted using d = (b - a)/(n + 1). In a Geometric Progression (GP) the ratio r is constant: a, ar, ar^2, ... The nth term is a_n = a r^(n-1) and the sum is S_n = a (r^n - 1)/(r - 1) for r not 1 (or a(1 - r^n)/(1 - r)); for r = 1, S_n = na. The sum to infinity exists when |r| < 1 and equals a/(1 - r). The geometric mean of a and b is sqrt(ab), and n geometric means between a and b use r = (b/a)^(1/(n+1)). Relation between means: AM >= GM, with equality when the numbers are equal, and AM times HM = GM squared for two numbers. Special sums: sum of first n natural numbers = n(n + 1)/2, sum of squares = n(n + 1)(2n + 1)/6 and sum of cubes = [n(n + 1)/2]^2. Arithmetico-geometric series have terms (a + (k-1)d) r^(k-1). Applications include compound interest, population growth and recurring deposits. If a, b, c are in AP then 2b = a + c; if in GP then b^2 = ac.
## Deep dive: AP
Common difference d = a_(n+1) - a_n. Middle term gives the average. Sum can also be written using the first and last terms. If the terms of an AP are all multiplied or increased by a constant it stays an AP.
## Deep dive: GP
Common ratio r = a_(n+1)/a_n. The sum to infinity applies only for |r| < 1. A recurring decimal like 0.333... is the sum of a GP. Compound interest amounts form a GP.
## Deep dive: Means and special sums
AM, GM and HM relate by AM >= GM >= HM for positive numbers. The sums of n, n squared and n cubed are standard. They are used to sum series by writing the nth term.
