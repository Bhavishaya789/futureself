# Statistics: measures of dispersion

@study
Range, mean deviation, variance and standard deviation measure how spread out data are. Variance = (1/n) sum (x - mean)^2, standard deviation is its square root, and coefficient of variation = (s / mean) x 100.
## Deep dive: measures of dispersion
Range = largest - smallest. Mean deviation about the mean = sum |x - mean| / n; about the median = sum |x - M| / n. Variance sigma^2 = sum (x - mean)^2 / n = (sum x^2)/n - mean^2. Standard deviation sigma = sqrt (variance). For a frequency table: sigma^2 = sum f (x - mean)^2 / N, N = sum f. For continuous data use class marks as x. Coefficient of variation CV = (sigma / mean) x 100 compares the variability of two series: the series with smaller CV is more consistent.
## Deep dive: effect of change of origin and scale
If every value is increased by a constant, the mean increases by it but the variance and SD stay the same. If every value is multiplied by k, the mean and SD are multiplied by |k| and the variance by k^2. For a combined group of n1 and n2 items with means m1, m2, the combined mean is (n1 m1 + n2 m2)/(n1 + n2). The shortcut method: with a assumed mean, sigma^2 = (sum f d^2)/N - (sum f d / N)^2 where d = x - a.
**Common mistakes:** dividing by n - 1 (not used in this syllabus), forgetting the square root, and comparing variability using SD instead of CV when the means differ.
