Probability of an event E is P(E) = favourable outcomes / total outcomes for equally likely outcomes. 0 <= P(E) <= 1, P(not E) = 1 - P(E), P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A and B). Mutually exclusive events cannot occur together; exhaustive events cover the whole sample space.
## Deep dive: sample space and events
An experiment has outcomes forming the sample space S. An event is a subset of S. A simple event has one outcome; a compound event has more. The complement of E is E-bar (not E); A or B is the union, A and B is the intersection. Events A and B are mutually exclusive if A and B cannot both happen (A intersect B is empty), and exhaustive if their union is S. Typical sample spaces: a coin (2 outcomes), a die (6), two coins (4), two dice (36), three coins (8), a pack of 52 cards (4 suits x 13 ranks).
## Deep dive: rules of probability
For equally likely outcomes P(E) = n(E)/n(S). Axioms: P(E) >= 0, P(S) = 1, and for mutually exclusive events P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B). Addition law: P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A and B). Complement: P(A-bar) = 1 - P(A). P(A but not B) = P(A) - P(A and B). P(neither A nor B) = 1 - P(A or B). Odds in favour of E = P(E) : P(E-bar).
## Deep dive: counting methods in probability
Use the multiplication principle and combinations: choosing r objects from n gives C(n, r) = n!/(r!(n - r)!). Drawing 2 cards from 52: total C(52, 2) = 1326. Two dice: the sum 7 occurs in 6 of 36 ways. P(at least one) = 1 - P(none). Dice sums: 2 and 12 occur once each; 7 occurs 6 times, the most likely sum.
**Common mistakes:** treating outcomes as equally likely when they are not (the sum of two dice), double-counting overlap in P(A or B), and forgetting to use combinations for selections.
