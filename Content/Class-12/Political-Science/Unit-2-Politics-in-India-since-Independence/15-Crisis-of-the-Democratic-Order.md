# Crisis of the Democratic Order
minutes: 40
yt: class 12 political science crisis of democratic order emergency 1975 JP movement allahabad verdict shah commission janata party
@study
The early 1970s saw growing discontent over inflation, unemployment and corruption. The Navnirman movement in Gujarat (1974) and the Bihar movement led by Jayaprakash Narayan (JP) in 1974 called for Total Revolution; the railway strike of 1974 under George Fernandes paralysed services. Tension grew between the executive and judiciary: in Kesavananda Bharati (1973) the Supreme Court held that Parliament cannot change the basic structure of the Constitution, and the appointment of Chief Justice A. N. Ray superseded three senior judges. On 12 June 1975 the Allahabad High Court (Justice Jagmohanlal Sinha) found Indira Gandhi guilty of electoral malpractice in the case brought by Raj Narain, and set aside her election. The Supreme Court granted a conditional stay. On 25 June 1975 JP called for mass protest, and that night Emergency was declared under Article 352 on the ground of internal disturbance, and remained until 21 March 1977. Fundamental rights were suspended; press censorship was imposed; preventive detention under MISA jailed thousands including opposition leaders; some organisations like the RSS and Jamaat-e-Islami were banned. The 42nd Amendment of 1976 curtailed judicial review and extended the Lok Sabha term to six years. The Supreme Court in ADM Jabalpur (1976) held that fundamental rights could be suspended in the Emergency. Sanjay Gandhi’s extra-constitutional role, forced sterilisation and slum demolitions drew criticism. Elections in March 1977 saw Congress lose, and the Janata Party formed the first non-Congress government at the Centre with Morarji Desai as Prime Minister. The Shah Commission investigated Emergency excesses. The 44th Amendment (1978) made it harder to impose an Emergency, limiting grounds to armed rebellion. The Janata government fell in 1979, and Congress returned in 1980.
## Deep dive: Movements and tension
Gujarat and Bihar movements, JP’s Total Revolution, the railway strike and clashes with the judiciary built the crisis.
## Deep dive: The Emergency
Declared on 25 June 1975 under Article 352; rights, press and opposition were suppressed until March 1977.
## Deep dive: Aftermath
The 1977 elections brought the Janata government; the 44th Amendment restricted future Emergencies.
