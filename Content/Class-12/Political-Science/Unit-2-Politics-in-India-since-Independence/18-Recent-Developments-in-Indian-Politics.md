# Recent Developments in Indian Politics
minutes: 40

@study
The 1990s marked a new phase of Indian politics. The Congress system gave way to the era of coalitions: from 1989 no single party won a majority for several elections, and regional parties became important. Three factors shaped politics. Mandal: the 1990 implementation of the Mandal Commission by the V. P. Singh National Front government, giving 27 per cent reservation to OBCs in central jobs, brought backward castes into politics, and the Supreme Court upheld it in the Indra Sawhney case of 1992 with a creamy layer rule. Mandir (Ayodhya): the Ram Janmabhoomi movement led to the demolition of the Babri Masjid on 6 December 1992 and communal riots; the Supreme Court gave its verdict on the dispute in November 2019. Market: the economic reforms of 1991 under P. V. Narasimha Rao and Manmohan Singh began liberalisation. Rajiv Gandhi was assassinated in May 1991. Coalition governments followed: the National Front (1989), the United Front (1996), the BJP-led National Democratic Alliance under Atal Bihari Vajpayee (1998-2004), and the Congress-led United Progressive Alliance under Manmohan Singh (2004-2014). In 2014 the BJP under Narendra Modi won a single-party majority of 282 seats, the first in 30 years, and in 2019 it won 303 seats. Features of the new era include OBC and Dalit parties, regional parties, alliance politics, stability through the 91st Amendment of 2003 limiting the size of the council of ministers to 15 per cent, and the rise of identity politics. Other trends include the Election Commission’s role strengthened under T. N. Seshan, higher voter turnout, and debates on caste and secularism.
## Deep dive: Three Ms
Mandal, Mandir and Market shaped the 1990s.
## Deep dive: Coalitions
NDA and UPA coalitions governed from 1998 until 2014.
## Deep dive: New majority
The BJP won a single-party majority in 2014 (282) and 2019 (303).
