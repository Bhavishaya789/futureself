# India’s External Relations
minutes: 40
yt: class 12 political science india external relations non alignment china war 1962 pakistan wars 1965 1971 nuclear policy
@study
India’s foreign policy aims to protect territorial integrity, sovereignty and economic development, and flows from the freedom movement, the Constitution (Article 51) and the Cold War context. Jawaharlal Nehru shaped early policy: non-alignment, peace and cooperation with all, anti-colonialism and the five principles of Panchsheel, signed with China in 1954. India supported decolonisation, hosted the Asian Relations Conference of 1947, supported Indonesia’s independence, took part in the Bandung Conference of 1955, and opposed apartheid. Relations with China began warmly: India recognised the People’s Republic in 1949-50, and Panchsheel was signed in 1954. Tibet became a point of tension, and after the 1959 revolt the Dalai Lama sought asylum in India. The border dispute led to the Sino-Indian war of October-November 1962, which was a setback for India; the defence budget rose and Nehru’s image suffered. With Pakistan, Kashmir led to the 1947-48 war. The 1965 war ended in the Tashkent Agreement of 1966 between Lal Bahadur Shastri and Ayub Khan, after which Shastri died at Tashkent. The 1971 crisis in East Pakistan created ten million refugees; the Indo-Soviet Treaty of Peace, Friendship and Cooperation of August 1971 was signed, and war in December 1971 led to Bangladesh and the Shimla Agreement of 1972. India tested a nuclear device in 1974 (Pokhran-I) and conducted five tests in 1998 (Pokhran-II). The Kargil conflict took place in 1999. India refused to sign the NPT and CTBT. After 1991, with the end of the Soviet Union, India adopted the Look East policy and improved relations with the US, signing a civil nuclear agreement in 2008. India’s Goa liberation took place in 1961.
## Deep dive: Early policy
Non-alignment, Panchsheel and anti-colonialism shaped Nehru’s foreign policy.
## Deep dive: Wars
1947-48 and 1965 and 1971 with Pakistan and 1962 with China shaped security policy.
## Deep dive: Nuclear and post-1991 policy
Tests in 1974 and 1998, no first use, Look East and engagement with the US.
