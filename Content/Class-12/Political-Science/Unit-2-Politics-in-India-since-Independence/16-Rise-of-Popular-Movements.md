# Rise of Popular Movements
minutes: 40
yt: class 12 political science rise of popular movements chipko dalit panthers bharatiya kisan union narmada bachao andolan RTI SEWA
@study
Popular movements are collective actions by people outside the party system to press for demands. After the Emergency and the decline of the Congress system, many non-party movements arose, because parties did not address all issues. The Chipko movement began in 1973 in Uttarakhand (then Uttar Pradesh), when villagers, many of them women led by Gaura Devi, hugged trees to stop commercial felling; Sunderlal Bahuguna and Chandi Prasad Bhatt were its leaders; it stressed ecology and livelihood and is called a movement for protecting the forest and women’s participation. The Dalit Panthers was formed in 1972 in Maharashtra by Namdeo Dhasal and J. V. Pawar to fight caste atrocities and untouchability, inspired by Ambedkar and the Black Panthers. The Bharatiya Kisan Union (BKU) in western Uttar Pradesh under Mahendra Singh Tikait demanded better prices and electricity rates in the 1980s, with a large Delhi rally in 1988. The anti-arrack movement in Andhra Pradesh was led by rural women against alcohol. Self-Employed Women’s Association (SEWA), founded by Ela Bhatt in 1972, organised poor women workers. The Narmada Bachao Andolan (NBA) from 1985, led by Medha Patkar and Baba Amte, opposed the Sardar Sarovar dam on displacement and environment grounds. The Right to Information movement started in Rajasthan with the Mazdoor Kisan Shakti Sangathan (MKSS) in the 1990s and led to the RTI Act of 2005. The Mandal Commission (1980) recommended 27 per cent reservation for OBCs, which the V. P. Singh government implemented in 1990, leading to protests. The National Fishworkers’ Forum protested against trawlers. These movements show that popular movements are a part of democratic politics, using petitions, rallies and satyagraha, and they raised the demand of the marginalised, but some were limited by lack of resources.
## Deep dive: Environment and livelihood
Chipko 1973 and Narmada Bachao Andolan 1985 linked ecology to people’s livelihoods.
## Deep dive: Social movements
Dalit Panthers, BKU, SEWA and anti-arrack movement represented marginalised groups.
## Deep dive: Rights movements
The RTI movement led to the 2005 Act and the Mandal Commission opened OBC reservation.
