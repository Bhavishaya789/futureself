# Politics of Planned Development
minutes: 40
yt: class 12 political science planned development planning commission five year plans green revolution white revolution land reforms mixed economy
@study
India chose planned development with a mixed economy: a strong public sector alongside private enterprise, to remove poverty and build industry after independence. The Bombay Plan of 1944 by industrialists favoured state involvement. The Planning Commission was established in 1950 with the Prime Minister as chairman, and the National Development Council in 1952 for state participation. The First Five Year Plan (1951-56) stressed agriculture, irrigation and dams like Bhakra Nangal and followed the Harrod-Domar model, with K. N. Raj as an architect. The Second Plan (1956-61), under P. C. Mahalanobis, stressed heavy industry and the public sector, setting up steel plants at Bhilai, Rourkela and Durgapur, and was backed by the Industrial Policy Resolution of 1956. The Third Plan (1961-66) was hit by wars with China (1962) and Pakistan (1965) and drought, leading to plan holidays from 1966 to 1969. Land reforms abolished zamindari, introduced tenancy reform, ceilings and consolidation, but with uneven success. The food crisis led to the Green Revolution in the 1960s with high-yield variety seeds, fertiliser and irrigation, supported by M. S. Swaminathan and Norman Borlaug, benefiting Punjab, Haryana and western Uttar Pradesh but increasing regional and class inequalities. The White Revolution (Operation Flood) by Verghese Kurien, with Amul, made India a milk-surplus nation. Critics said the model gave too much power to the bureaucracy and slowed growth. The economic crisis of 1991 led to liberalisation. The Planning Commission was replaced by NITI Aayog on 1 January 2015. Kerala showed a model of human development.
## Deep dive: Planning machinery
The Planning Commission (1950) and NDC (1952) guided Five Year Plans in a mixed economy.
## Deep dive: Plans
The First Plan stressed agriculture; the Second Plan, Mahalanobis, stressed heavy industry and the public sector.
## Deep dive: Rural change
Land reforms, the Green Revolution and Operation Flood changed agriculture with uneven benefits.
