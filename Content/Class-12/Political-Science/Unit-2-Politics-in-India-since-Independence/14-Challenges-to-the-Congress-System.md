# Challenges to the Congress System
minutes: 40
yt: class 12 political science challenges to congress system 1967 elections syndicate congress split bank nationalisation garibi hatao
@study
After Nehru’s death in May 1964 Lal Bahadur Shastri became Prime Minister; he promoted the slogan Jai Jawan Jai Kisan and led during the 1965 war, and died at Tashkent in January 1966. The Congress Syndicate, a group of regional leaders including K. Kamaraj, S. Nijalingappa and Atulya Ghosh, then chose Indira Gandhi as Prime Minister in January 1966, expecting she would be dependent on them. She faced drought, devaluation of the rupee in 1966 under US pressure and high prices. In the 1967 elections the Congress won a reduced majority in the Lok Sabha and lost power in several states: opposition parties formed coalitions called Samyukta Vidhayak Dal (SVD) governments, and the DMK under C. N. Annadurai won in Madras (Tamil Nadu). The era saw defections, the phrase ‘Aaya Ram Gaya Ram’ coming from Haryana, and political instability. Naxalbari uprising of 1967 began the Naxalite movement. The split came in 1969: after Zakir Husain died, the Syndicate named Sanjiva Reddy for President, Indira Gandhi supported V. V. Giri, who won, and the Congress split into Congress (O) and Congress (R). Indira Gandhi nationalised 14 major banks in July 1969, abolished the privy purses of former princes through the 26th Amendment in 1971, and took a left-leaning course. In 1971 she called early Lok Sabha elections, with the slogan Garibi Hatao against the Grand Alliance of opposition parties slogan Indira Hatao; Congress (R) won 352 seats. After the Bangladesh war of 1971 her position became dominant. The anti-defection law came much later in 1985. The new Congress was more personalised and centralised, with a direct appeal to voters.
## Deep dive: 1967 elections
Congress lost many states; SVD coalitions and defections began a new phase.
## Deep dive: 1969 split
Syndicate versus Indira Gandhi led to Congress (O) and Congress (R) after the presidential contest.
## Deep dive: 1971 mandate
Bank nationalisation, privy purse abolition and Garibi Hatao won Congress (R) 352 seats.
