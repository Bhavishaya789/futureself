# Alternative Centres of Power
minutes: 40

@study
After the Cold War several regions emerged as alternative centres of power to the United States. The European Union grew out of the Second World War destruction. The European Coal and Steel Community of 1951 (from the Schuman Plan of 1950) led to the European Economic Community under the Treaty of Rome in 1957, and the Maastricht Treaty of 1992 created the European Union (in force 1993). It has a common currency, the euro, introduced in 2002 for notes and coins, a European Parliament, a Council and a Commission, a flag, anthem and passports, and the Schengen area for open borders. The EU has the world’s large economy, but some members, like the UK, did not adopt the euro, and the UK voted to leave in 2016 (Brexit). ASEAN, the Association of Southeast Asian Nations, was founded in Bangkok in 1967 by Indonesia, Malaysia, the Philippines, Singapore and Thailand; later Brunei, Vietnam, Laos, Myanmar and Cambodia joined, making ten members. The ASEAN Way means informal, non-confrontational and cooperative behaviour. ASEAN Vision 2020 (1997) led to the ASEAN Community with three pillars: the ASEAN Security Community, the ASEAN Economic Community and the ASEAN Socio-Cultural Community. The ASEAN Regional Forum (1994) handles security dialogue. Its secretariat is in Jakarta. China has been a rising economic power since Deng Xiaoping’s reforms of 1978 under the open door policy, creating Special Economic Zones and agricultural decollectivisation; it joined the WTO in 2001. China followed One Country, Two Systems for Hong Kong (1997) and Macau (1999). India and China fought a war in 1962, and relations were normalised after Rajiv Gandhi’s 1988 visit.
## Deep dive: European Union
From ECSC 1951 and Treaty of Rome 1957 to Maastricht 1992, with a common currency and shared institutions.
## Deep dive: ASEAN
Formed in 1967 with five members; the ASEAN Way and three community pillars guide it.
## Deep dive: China
Deng Xiaoping’s 1978 reforms and SEZs made China a global economic power.
