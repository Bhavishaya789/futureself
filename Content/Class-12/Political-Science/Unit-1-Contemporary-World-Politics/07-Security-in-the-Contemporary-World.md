# Security in the Contemporary World
minutes: 40
yt: class 12 political science security traditional non traditional human security arms control NPT terrorism nuclear
@study
Security means freedom from threats. Traditional security concerns military threats to a state’s sovereignty, territorial integrity and independence, mostly from other states. Its responses are building military strength, alliances, deterrence, balance of power, disarmament, arms control and confidence building measures. Disarmament means giving up certain weapons, as in the Biological Weapons Convention (1972) and Chemical Weapons Convention (1992); arms control regulates their acquisition, as in the Partial Test Ban Treaty (1963), the Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty (1968), SALT and the Anti-Ballistic Missile Treaty (1972), START and the Comprehensive Test Ban Treaty (1996). Confidence building measures include exchanging information and prior notice of exercises. Non-traditional security covers threats to human beings and not just states: environmental degradation, global poverty, health epidemics such as HIV/AIDS, bird flu, SARS and Ebola, terrorism, human rights violations and migration. Human security puts the individual at the centre and includes freedom from fear and freedom from want. Cooperative security stresses cooperation through international institutions. India’s security strategy has four components: strengthening military capability, strengthening international norms and institutions, meeting internal security challenges, and developing the economy. India conducted nuclear tests in 1974 (Pokhran-I) and in May 1998 (Pokhran-II) and follows a no first use doctrine, with a minimum credible deterrent. India has not signed the NPT and CTBT, calling them discriminatory. Terrorism, as on 9/11 and in India on 26/11 (Mumbai 2008), is a major non-traditional threat. Refugees and internally displaced persons are another concern.
## Deep dive: Traditional security
Military threats between states answered by deterrence, alliances, arms control and disarmament.
## Deep dive: Non-traditional security
Poverty, epidemics, terrorism, environment and migration threaten people; human security is the individual’s freedom from fear and want.
## Deep dive: India’s approach
Military strength, norms, internal security and development; nuclear tests 1974 and 1998; no first use.
