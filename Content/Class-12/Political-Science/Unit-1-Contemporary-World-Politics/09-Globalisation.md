# Globalisation
minutes: 40
yt: class 12 political science globalisation economic cultural political dimensions IMF WTO impact india anti globalisation
@study
Globalisation is the widening and deepening of flows of ideas, capital, commodities and people across the world. It is not new: trade along the Silk Route and colonial trade linked regions long ago. Its present phase is driven by technology (telecommunications, the internet, container shipping) and by policies of liberalisation. Its dimensions are economic (trade, finance, outsourcing), political (a changing role of the state) and cultural (spread of ideas, food, films and brands). Causes include the transport and communication revolution, trade liberalisation, and the role of the IMF, World Bank and WTO. The WTO (1995) replaced GATT (1947). The impact is mixed. Economic gains include higher trade, growth, cheaper goods, jobs in services such as call centres, and spread of technology; losses include unequal gains, job insecurity, farm distress and pressure on welfare and small producers. Politically, the state has retreated from welfare, but state capacity has not vanished, and some states have become stronger with technology. Culturally, globalisation spreads global culture and McDonaldisation, but it can also produce cultural homogenisation and, in reaction, cultural hybridisation, such as Indian food and films abroad. Critics include the Left, who see exploitation, and the Right, who see threats to culture. The World Social Forum, first held at Porto Alegre in Brazil in 2001, brought together anti-globalisation movements. India began economic reforms in 1991 under Narasimha Rao and Manmohan Singh, with liberalisation, privatisation and globalisation, and has grown in IT and services. Globalisation also raises migration, brain drain, labour mobility and issues of global trade rules.
## Deep dive: Dimensions
Economic flows, political changes in the state and cultural exchange define globalisation.
## Deep dive: Impact
Growth, jobs and technology on one side; inequality, insecurity and cultural homogenisation on the other.
## Deep dive: Resistance and India
The World Social Forum opposes it; India liberalised its economy in 1991.
