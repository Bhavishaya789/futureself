@study
South Asia includes Afghanistan, Bangladesh, Bhutan, India, Maldives, Nepal, Pakistan and Sri Lanka. The region has diverse political systems. Pakistan has alternated between civilian and military rule: Ayub Khan took power in 1958, Yahya Khan in 1969, Zia-ul-Haq in 1977 and Pervez Musharraf in 1999. Bangladesh emerged in 1971 after the language and autonomy movement of East Pakistan led by Sheikh Mujibur Rahman and the war in which India helped. Nepal was a monarchy that became a federal democratic republic; the monarchy was abolished in 2008 and the new constitution adopted in 2015. Bhutan moved to constitutional monarchy, with its constitution in 2008. Sri Lanka had a civil war between the government and the Tamil group LTTE that ended in 2009; the Indo-Sri Lanka Accord of 1987 brought an Indian Peace Keeping Force. The Maldives became a republic in 1968. India is the largest democracy. Conflicts include India and Pakistan over Kashmir, wars in 1947-48, 1965, 1971 and 1999 (Kargil), the Tashkent Agreement of 1966 and the Shimla Agreement of 1972. The Indus Waters Treaty of 1960 shared the Indus rivers; the Ganga Water Treaty with Bangladesh was signed in 1996; the Indo-Nepal Treaty of Peace and Friendship dates from 1950; India’s treaty with Bhutan was revised in 2007. SAARC was founded at Dhaka in December 1985 with seven members, and Afghanistan joined in 2007; its headquarters is in Kathmandu. SAFTA was signed in 2004 and came into force in 2006. India’s role in the region is that of the largest economy, which creates both cooperation and mistrust. External powers such as the US and China have also increased involvement.
## Deep dive: Political systems
Pakistan swung between military and civilian rule; Nepal, Bhutan and Bangladesh changed governance.
## Deep dive: Conflicts and treaties
Kashmir, Kargil, Indus Waters, Ganga Waters and the 1950 Nepal treaty shape relations.
## Deep dive: SAARC
Founded 1985 at Dhaka; SAFTA 2004; cooperation limited by political tension.
