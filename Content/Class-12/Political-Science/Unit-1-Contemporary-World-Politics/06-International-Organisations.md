# International Organisations
minutes: 40
yt: class 12 political science international organisations united nations security council reform IMF world bank WTO
@study
International organisations are needed to resolve conflicts, promote cooperation and manage global issues. The League of Nations was formed in 1920 but failed to prevent the Second World War. The United Nations was established on 24 October 1945, with its Charter signed at San Francisco in June 1945, and its headquarters is in New York. It has six principal organs: the General Assembly (all members, one country one vote), the Security Council, the Secretariat headed by the Secretary General, the Economic and Social Council, the International Court of Justice at The Hague and the Trusteeship Council (suspended since 1994). The Security Council has 15 members: five permanent (the US, UK, France, Russia and China) with veto power, and ten non-permanent members elected for two-year terms. Resolutions on substantive matters need nine votes and no veto. The ICJ has 15 judges elected for nine-year terms. The Secretary General is appointed for five years. UN specialised agencies include WHO, UNESCO, FAO, ILO and UNICEF; the IMF and World Bank (from Bretton Woods in 1944) handle finance, and the WTO (1995, successor to GATT of 1947) governs trade. The Universal Declaration of Human Rights was adopted on 10 December 1948. UN peacekeeping missions have been important. Reform is demanded: the Security Council has not been expanded since 1965, when non-permanent seats rose from six to ten, and the composition reflects 1945. India, Brazil, Germany and Japan (the G4) seek permanent seats, and India supports a more representative and democratic UN. India has been a major troop contributor to peacekeeping and a founding member of the UN. Reform of the UN includes structural, procedural and financial reforms.
## Deep dive: United Nations
Six organs including the General Assembly, Security Council and ICJ; formed 1945.
## Deep dive: Security Council
Five permanent members with veto and ten elected for two years; reform demanded by India and the G4.
## Deep dive: Economic bodies
The IMF, World Bank and WTO regulate finance and trade.
