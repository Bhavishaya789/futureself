# The End of Bipolarity
minutes: 40
yt: class 12 political science end of bipolarity soviet disintegration gorbachev glasnost perestroika shock therapy CIS
@study
The Soviet Union emerged from the Russian Revolution of 1917 and formally came into being in 1922 as a federation of 15 republics. It was a one-party, state-controlled socialist system and a superpower with a huge military and nuclear arsenal. By the 1980s the system was weakened by stagnation under Brezhnev, bureaucracy, lack of freedom, a failing economy, the Afghanistan war after 1979 and the burden of the arms race. Mikhail Gorbachev became leader in 1985 and introduced reforms: perestroika (restructuring) and glasnost (openness). Reform raised demands for freedom in Eastern Europe, where the Berlin Wall fell in November 1989 and Germany reunified in October 1990. The Chernobyl disaster of 1986 exposed the system’s weaknesses. In August 1991 hardliners attempted a coup against Gorbachev and failed. The Baltic states Estonia, Latvia and Lithuania declared independence and Boris Yeltsin led Russia. In December 1991 Russia, Ukraine and Belarus formed the Commonwealth of Independent States (CIS), and the USSR ceased to exist on 25 December 1991, when Gorbachev resigned. The Warsaw Pact dissolved in 1991. Russia became the successor state, with the Soviet seat in the UN Security Council and the nuclear arsenal. Russia then adopted shock therapy under IMF and World Bank advice, a rapid shift to capitalism, which led to collapse of industries, inflation, unemployment and inequality, and a decline in the economy. The consequences were the end of the Cold War, the US becoming the sole superpower, the end of the socialist ideological bloc, rise of new states, and conflicts in Chechnya, Tajikistan, and the Balkans. Czechoslovakia split peacefully in 1993. India’s relations with Russia continued to be strong in defence, space and energy.
## Deep dive: Soviet system
A one party socialist system with 15 republics that weakened through stagnation and war.
## Deep dive: Gorbachev reforms
Perestroika and glasnost opened the system and led to demands for freedom.
## Deep dive: Aftermath
The USSR dissolved in 1991; Russia adopted shock therapy; the US became the sole superpower.
