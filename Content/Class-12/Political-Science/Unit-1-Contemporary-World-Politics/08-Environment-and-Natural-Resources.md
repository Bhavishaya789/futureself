# Environment and Natural Resources

@study
Environmental concerns became a global political issue because of climate change, ozone depletion, loss of biodiversity, depletion of fresh water and degradation of land. The 1972 Stockholm Conference on the Human Environment began global debate. The 1987 Brundtland Report, Our Common Future, introduced sustainable development: meeting present needs without compromising future generations. The 1992 United Nations Conference on Environment and Development at Rio de Janeiro (the Earth Summit) produced the Rio Declaration, Agenda 21, the Convention on Biological Diversity and the UN Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC). Its principle of common but differentiated responsibilities says developed countries, which caused most emissions, must bear more of the burden. The Montreal Protocol (1987) controlled ozone-depleting substances. The Kyoto Protocol (1997, in force 2005) set binding emission cuts for developed countries, while India and China had no binding targets. The Paris Agreement (2015) aims to limit warming to well below 2 degrees C and pursue 1.5 degrees C, using nationally determined contributions. Global commons are areas outside any state’s sovereignty: the atmosphere, Antarctica, the ocean floor and outer space. The Antarctic Treaty of 1959 and the Madrid Protocol of 1991 protect Antarctica from mining. Resource politics includes oil and water; West Asia has half of oil reserves, and water conflicts arise over shared rivers. Indigenous peoples have special claims over resources and lifestyles. India supports common but differentiated responsibility, has a national climate plan, and argues that per capita emissions matter. Environmental movements in India include Chipko and the Narmada movement. The Global Environment Facility and Agenda 21 helped finance efforts.
## Deep dive: Global agreements
Stockholm 1972, Brundtland 1987, Rio 1992, Kyoto 1997 and Paris 2015 shaped environmental politics.
## Deep dive: Common but differentiated responsibility
Developed countries caused more emissions and must do more, a principle India defends.
## Deep dive: Global commons
Antarctica, atmosphere, oceans and outer space belong to no one and need common protection.
