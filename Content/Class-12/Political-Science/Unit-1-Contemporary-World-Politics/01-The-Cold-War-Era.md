# The Cold War Era

@study
The Cold War was the rivalry between the United States and the Soviet Union and their allies after the Second World War, fought through ideology, alliances, arms races and proxy wars rather than direct war between the superpowers. It followed the atomic bombing of Hiroshima on 6 August 1945 and Nagasaki on 9 August 1945. The world was bipolar: the capitalist, democratic Western bloc led by the US and the communist Eastern bloc led by the USSR. The Western bloc built NATO (1949), SEATO (1954) and CENTO (1955); the Soviet bloc formed the Warsaw Pact (1955) and Comecon (1949). The US launched the Truman Doctrine and Marshall Plan in 1947. The Berlin blockade of 1948-49 and the Berlin Wall of 1961 symbolised the division of Germany. Wars in Korea (1950-53) and Vietnam showed the Cold War turned hot in the Third World. The Cuban Missile Crisis of October 1962, when the USSR placed missiles in Cuba under Fidel Castro and John F. Kennedy responded with a naval quarantine, was the closest the world came to nuclear war and ended with Khrushchev’s withdrawal of missiles. Arms control followed: the Partial Test Ban Treaty (1963), the Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty (1968), SALT I (1972) and the ABM Treaty (1972). Third World nations formed the Non-Aligned Movement: Nehru, Tito and Nasser led it with Sukarno and Nkrumah, and the first summit was at Belgrade in 1961. Non-alignment did not mean neutrality or isolation but independent judgement on each issue. Developing countries demanded a New International Economic Order, supported by UNCTAD’s 1972 report on trade. India followed non-alignment as an independent foreign policy.
## Deep dive: Superpower rivalry
The US and USSR formed NATO and the Warsaw Pact and fought proxy wars without direct war.
## Deep dive: Cuban Missile Crisis
October 1962 was the peak of nuclear danger and led to arms control talks.
## Deep dive: Non-alignment
NAM chose independent policy, not neutrality; its first summit was in Belgrade in 1961.
