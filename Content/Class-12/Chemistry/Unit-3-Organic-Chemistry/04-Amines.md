Amines are derivatives of ammonia in which one, two or three hydrogens are replaced by alkyl or aryl groups, giving primary (1 degree), secondary (2 degree) and tertiary (3 degree) amines. The nitrogen is sp3 hybridised with a lone pair; the C-N-C angle in trimethylamine is about 108 degrees. Nomenclature: alkanamine (CH3NH2 methanamine), N-methylmethanamine, aniline (benzenamine). Preparation: reduction of nitro compounds (Sn/HCl, Fe/HCl, or H2/Pd) gives primary amines (iron with HCl is preferred industrially since FeCl2 formed is hydrolysed, so only a small amount of HCl is needed); ammonolysis of alkyl halides gives a mixture of 1, 2, 3 amines and quaternary salt; reduction of nitriles (LiAlH4 or H2/Ni) and amides (LiAlH4); Gabriel phthalimide synthesis (phthalimide with ethanolic KOH forms potassium phthalimide, which reacts with alkyl halide and is hydrolysed with alkali to give pure primary aliphatic amines; aromatic primary amines cannot be made because aryl halides do not undergo nucleophilic substitution); Hofmann bromamide degradation (amide with Br2 and NaOH gives a primary amine with one carbon atom less). Physical properties: lower amines are gases with fishy odour; primary and secondary amines form hydrogen bonds so have higher boiling points than hydrocarbons but lower than alcohols; tertiary amines cannot form intermolecular hydrogen bonds; boiling point order 1 > 2 > 3; lower amines are soluble in water; aniline is a colourless liquid turning brown on exposure to air. Basicity: amines are basic because of the lone pair on nitrogen; in aqueous solution the order for methyl amines is (CH3)2NH > CH3NH2 > (CH3)3N > NH3 due to the combined inductive, solvation and steric effects; for ethyl amines (C2H5)2NH > (C2H5)3N > C2H5NH2 > NH3; aniline is a weaker base than aliphatic amines because the lone pair is delocalised into the benzene ring by resonance; electron-donating groups on the ring increase basicity and electron-withdrawing groups decrease it. Chemical reactions: alkylation; acylation (with acid chlorides, anhydrides in presence of a base like pyridine to give amides; used to protect the amino group); carbylamine reaction (isocyanide test): aliphatic and aromatic primary amines with chloroform and alcoholic KOH give foul-smelling isocyanides; reaction with nitrous acid: aliphatic primary amines give alcohols with N2 evolution, while aromatic primary amines give diazonium salts at 273-278 K (diazotisation). Hinsberg test: benzenesulphonyl chloride reacts with primary amines to give a product soluble in alkali, with secondary amines to give an insoluble product, and tertiary amines do not react. Aniline: electrophilic substitution is strongly activated and ortho-para directing: bromine water gives 2,4,6-tribromoaniline immediately; nitration directly gives a mixture including meta product because anilinium ion formed in acid is meta directing, so protection by acetylation is used; sulphonation gives sulphanilic acid (zwitterion); Friedel-Crafts reaction fails with aniline as it forms a salt with AlCl3. Diazonium salts (ArN2+X-): benzene diazonium chloride is stable only at 273-278 K; the stability of aromatic diazonium salts arises from resonance; reactions with loss of nitrogen: Sandmeyer reaction (CuCl/HCl, CuBr/HBr, CuCN/KCN), Gattermann reaction (Cu/HX), replacement by iodide (KI), by fluoride (HBF4 then heat: Balz-Schiemann), by H (hypophosphorous acid H3PO2, or ethanol), by OH (warming with water; phenol) and reactions retaining nitrogen: coupling reactions to form azo dyes (with phenol in mild alkaline medium gives p-hydroxyazobenzene, orange dye; with aniline in mild acidic medium gives p-aminoazobenzene, yellow dye). Importance: diazonium salts are important for synthesising aryl fluorides, iodides, cyanides and azo dyes; amines in drugs, dyes and nylon intermediates (hexamethylenediamine).
## Deep dive: Basicity
Lone pair on nitrogen makes amines basic. Aliphatic amines are stronger than ammonia; aniline is weaker due to resonance.
## Deep dive: Preparation and tests
Gabriel gives pure primary amines. Hofmann degradation shortens the chain by one carbon. Carbylamine and Hinsberg tests identify classes.
## Deep dive: Diazonium salts
Diazonium salts give a range of aryl compounds by Sandmeyer and related reactions, and azo dyes by coupling.

P: Distinguish primary, secondary and tertiary amines by the Hinsberg test.
