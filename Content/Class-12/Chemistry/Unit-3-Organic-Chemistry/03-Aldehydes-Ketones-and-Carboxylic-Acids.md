# Aldehydes, Ketones and Carboxylic Acids
minutes: 45

@study
Aldehydes (RCHO) and ketones (RCOR') contain the carbonyl group C=O; carboxylic acids contain -COOH. The carbonyl carbon is sp2 hybridised and trigonal planar; the C=O bond is polar and carbon is electrophilic. IUPAC: aldehyde suffix -al, ketone -one, acid -oic acid; common names formaldehyde (methanal), acetaldehyde (ethanal), acetone (propanone), formic (methanoic) and acetic (ethanoic) acid. Preparation of aldehydes and ketones: oxidation of primary and secondary alcohols; dehydrogenation of alcohols with Cu at 573 K; ozonolysis of alkenes; hydration of alkynes (ethyne gives ethanal; other alkynes give ketones); from acyl chlorides by Rosenmund reduction (H2, Pd-BaSO4, gives aldehydes), from nitriles by Stephen reaction (SnCl2/HCl then hydrolysis gives aldehydes) and with DIBAL-H; ketones from acyl chlorides with dialkylcadmium and by Friedel-Crafts acylation; aromatic aldehydes from toluene by oxidation with CrO3 in acetic anhydride (Etard reaction with CrO2Cl2) and by Gattermann-Koch reaction (benzene, CO, HCl, anhydrous AlCl3/CuCl). Properties: boiling points are higher than hydrocarbons and ethers but lower than alcohols (no hydrogen bonding between molecules); lower members are soluble in water. Nucleophilic addition: the mechanism where Nu attacks the carbonyl carbon; aldehydes are more reactive than ketones due to less steric hindrance and greater electrophilicity. Reactions: addition of HCN forms cyanohydrins; NaHSO3 forms bisulphite addition product; alcohols form hemiacetals and acetals (dry HCl); ammonia derivatives (NH2-Z) such as hydroxylamine, hydrazine, 2,4-DNP, semicarbazide give oximes, hydrazones, 2,4-DNP derivatives (orange/yellow precipitate used to detect the carbonyl) and semicarbazones. Reduction to alcohols (NaBH4, LiAlH4), to alkanes by Clemmensen reduction (Zn-Hg/conc. HCl) or Wolff-Kishner reduction (N2H4 then KOH/ethylene glycol). Oxidation: aldehydes are easily oxidised by mild oxidants: Tollens' test (ammoniacal silver nitrate; silver mirror), Fehling's test (alkaline copper(II) tartrate gives reddish brown Cu2O; aromatic aldehydes do not respond) and Benedict's test; ketones are not oxidised easily. Haloform reaction: methyl ketones (and ethanol, ethanal) with NaOX give the haloform (iodoform yellow ppt) and a carboxylate. Aldol condensation: aldehydes and ketones having alpha hydrogen undergo reaction in the presence of dilute alkali to form beta-hydroxy aldehydes (aldols) or ketones, which dehydrate on heating to give alpha,beta-unsaturated carbonyls. Cross aldol. Cannizzaro reaction: aldehydes with no alpha hydrogen (HCHO, benzaldehyde) undergo disproportionation in conc. alkali to give an alcohol and the salt of a carboxylic acid. Electrophilic substitution in benzaldehyde occurs at the meta position. Uses: formaldehyde (formalin, 40% solution) for preservation; Bakelite; acetone as solvent; vanillin and benzaldehyde in perfumes. Carboxylic acids: prepared by oxidation of alcohols and aldehydes, hydrolysis of nitriles and amides, Grignard reagent with CO2, and oxidation of alkylbenzenes. Properties: high boiling points due to dimer formation through hydrogen bonding. Acidity: Ka; electron-withdrawing groups increase acidity (ClCH2COOH > CH3COOH); benzoic acid is stronger than acetic acid; formic acid (Ka 1.77 x 10^-4) is stronger than acetic acid. Reactions: formation of esters (Fischer esterification with alcohol and conc. H2SO4), acid chlorides (SOCl2, PCl5), anhydrides, amides; reduction with LiAlH4 to alcohols; decarboxylation (soda lime gives alkane; Kolbe electrolysis); Hell-Volhard-Zelinsky reaction (alpha halogenation with Cl2/Br2 and red phosphorus); ring substitution in benzoic acid occurs at meta position. Test: carboxylic acids liberate CO2 with sodium bicarbonate. Uses: acetic acid in vinegar (5-8%), benzoic acid as a preservative, soaps from higher fatty acids and nylon-6,6 from adipic acid.
## Deep dive: Aldehydes and ketones
Nucleophilic addition is the characteristic reaction. Tollens' and Fehling's tests distinguish aldehydes; the iodoform test detects methyl ketones.
## Deep dive: Name reactions
Aldol needs alpha hydrogen; Cannizzaro needs none. Clemmensen and Wolff-Kishner reduce C=O to CH2.
## Deep dive: Carboxylic acids
Acids are strongly hydrogen bonded and acidic; electron-withdrawing groups increase acidity. Esterification and HVZ reaction are key.
