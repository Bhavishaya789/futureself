# Alcohols, Phenols and Ethers
minutes: 45
yt: class 12 chemistry alcohols phenols ethers lucas test kolbe reimer tiemann williamson
@study
Alcohols contain an -OH on an sp3 carbon; phenols contain -OH on an sp2 carbon of benzene; ethers have an oxygen bonded to two carbons (R-O-R). Classification of alcohols: primary, secondary and tertiary; monohydric, dihydric (glycol), trihydric (glycerol). Preparation of alcohols: hydration of alkenes (acid catalysed, Markovnikov), hydroboration-oxidation (anti-Markovnikov), reduction of aldehydes and ketones using NaBH4 or LiAlH4 (also catalytic H2), carboxylic acids and esters with LiAlH4, and Grignard reagents with carbonyl compounds (HCHO gives primary alcohols, other aldehydes give secondary, ketones give tertiary alcohols). Manufacture of ethanol: fermentation of sugar by zymase of yeast; invertase converts sucrose to glucose and fructose. Methanol is made from water gas (CO + 2H2 at 200-300 atm, 573 K with ZnO-Cr2O3 catalyst). Physical properties: higher boiling points than comparable hydrocarbons and ethers due to hydrogen bonding; solubility decreases with alkyl chain; boiling point order primary > secondary > tertiary. Chemical reactions: (a) acidity of alcohols: weaker than water; order methanol > primary > secondary > tertiary; react with sodium to give alkoxide and hydrogen; (b) reaction with HX (Lucas test: HCl/ZnCl2: tertiary gives turbidity immediately, secondary in 5 minutes, primary on heating); (c) dehydration with conc. H2SO4: at 443 K gives ethene (alkene), at 413 K gives ethoxyethane (ether); (d) oxidation: primary alcohols give aldehydes with PCC and carboxylic acids with KMnO4 or K2Cr2O7; secondary give ketones; tertiary resist oxidation; (e) esterification with acids. Phenols: preparation from haloarenes (Dow process), benzenesulphonic acid, diazonium salts and cumene (cumene process: cumene oxidation to cumene hydroperoxide and treatment with dilute acid gives phenol and acetone). Phenol is more acidic than alcohols because of resonance stabilisation of the phenoxide ion; electron-withdrawing groups (nitro) increase acidity (2,4,6-trinitrophenol or picric acid is strongly acidic), electron-donating groups decrease it. Reactions of phenols: electrophilic substitution (OH is ortho-para directing and activating): nitration (dilute HNO3 gives o- and p-nitrophenol; conc. gives picric acid), halogenation (bromine water gives 2,4,6-tribromophenol white precipitate), Kolbe's reaction (sodium phenoxide with CO2 at 400 K, 4-7 atm gives salicylic acid, the starting material for aspirin), Reimer-Tiemann reaction (phenol, CHCl3 and NaOH gives salicylaldehyde), reaction with zinc dust gives benzene; oxidation of phenol with chromic acid gives benzoquinone; coupling; FeCl3 test (violet colour). Ethers: preparation by dehydration of alcohols and Williamson synthesis (alkyl halide + sodium alkoxide; SN2; primary alkyl halides are best as tertiary give alkenes). Properties: lower boiling points than alcohols as no hydrogen bonding; cleavage by HI (excess hot) gives alkyl iodides; with an unsymmetrical ether the smaller alkyl group gives the alkyl iodide (SN2) unless a tertiary group gives SN1; anisole undergoes Friedel-Crafts reactions (ortho-para directing). Uses: methanol as a solvent and fuel; ethanol in medicine; denatured alcohol; phenol in antiseptics and Bakelite; diethyl ether as an anaesthetic.
## Deep dive: Alcohols
Prepared by hydration, reduction and Grignard reactions. They show hydrogen bonding, acidity and oxidation behaviour depending on class. Lucas test distinguishes them.
## Deep dive: Phenols
More acidic than alcohols; ring is activated for electrophilic substitution. Kolbe and Reimer-Tiemann reactions are key.
## Deep dive: Ethers
Williamson synthesis uses SN2. Ethers are cleaved by HI.
