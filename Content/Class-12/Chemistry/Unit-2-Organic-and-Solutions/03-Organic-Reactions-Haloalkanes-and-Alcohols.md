# Haloalkanes, alcohols and key organic reactions
minutes: 25

@study
**SN2:** one step, backside attack, inversion of configuration, rate = k[RX][Nu], favoured by primary halides and strong nucleophiles. **SN1:** two steps via a carbocation, racemisation, rate = k[RX], favoured by tertiary halides and polar protic solvents. Order of SN1 reactivity: tertiary > secondary > primary.
**Alcohols:** oxidation of a primary alcohol gives an aldehyde then a carboxylic acid; a secondary alcohol gives a ketone; a tertiary alcohol resists oxidation. **Lucas test** (ZnCl2/HCl): tertiary reacts immediately, secondary in about 5 min, primary does not react at room temperature. Dehydration with conc. H2SO4 at 443 K gives an alkene (Saytzeff rule: more substituted alkene).

## Moderate
P: Distinguish primary, secondary and tertiary alcohols with the Lucas test.
A: Tertiary turbid immediately; secondary in about 5 min; primary no turbidity at room temperature.
R: 2 | Tertiary and secondary
R: 1 | Primary
## High
P: Compare SN1 and SN2 mechanisms.
A: SN1 two-step with carbocation, racemisation, tertiary, polar protic; SN2 one-step, inversion, primary, polar aprotic.
R: 2 | SN1
R: 2 | SN2
## Advanced
P: Predict the major product of dehydrating 2-butanol and justify it.
A: But-2-ene by Saytzeff's rule, as the more substituted alkene is more stable.
R: 2 | But-2-ene
