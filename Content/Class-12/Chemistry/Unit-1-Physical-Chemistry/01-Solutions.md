A solution is a homogeneous mixture of two or more components. The component present in the largest amount is the solvent and the other is the solute. Concentration terms: mass percentage = (mass of component / total mass) x 100; volume percentage; mass by volume percentage; parts per million (ppm) = (number of parts of component / total parts) x 10^6; mole fraction x_A = n_A / (n_A + n_B), and the sum of mole fractions is 1; molarity M = moles of solute / volume of solution in litres (depends on temperature); molality m = moles of solute / mass of solvent in kg (independent of temperature). Solubility: the maximum amount of solute that dissolves in a given amount of solvent at a given temperature; like dissolves like. Henry's law: the partial pressure of a gas in the vapour phase is proportional to its mole fraction in the solution: p = K_H x. Higher K_H means lower solubility; K_H increases with temperature, so gas solubility decreases as temperature rises. Applications: soda water bottles are sealed under high pressure; scuba divers suffer from bends (use helium-diluted air); anoxia at high altitude. Vapour pressure of a liquid solution; Raoult's law: the partial vapour pressure of each volatile component is directly proportional to its mole fraction: p_A = p_A^0 x_A, and total pressure p = p_A^0 x_A + p_B^0 x_B. For a solution of a non-volatile solute, the relative lowering of vapour pressure (p^0 - p)/p^0 = x_B (mole fraction of solute). Ideal solutions obey Raoult's law over the entire range; enthalpy of mixing is zero and volume change is zero (n-hexane and n-heptane; bromoethane and chloroethane). Non-ideal solutions show positive deviation (A-B interactions weaker than A-A and B-B; ethanol and acetone; Delta H mixing positive; Delta V positive) or negative deviation (A-B interactions stronger; chloroform and acetone; nitric acid and water; Delta H negative). Azeotropes are binary mixtures having the same composition in liquid and vapour phase and boiling at constant temperature: minimum boiling azeotropes (positive deviation; ethanol-water 95.5% ethanol) and maximum boiling azeotropes (negative deviation; nitric acid 68% and water). Colligative properties depend on the number of solute particles and not on their nature: (1) relative lowering of vapour pressure, (2) elevation of boiling point: Delta T_b = K_b m, where K_b is the molal elevation constant (K_b water = 0.52 K kg/mol), (3) depression of freezing point: Delta T_f = K_f m (K_f water = 1.86 K kg/mol) and (4) osmotic pressure. Molar mass from them: M_B = K_b x 1000 x w_B / (Delta T_b x w_A) for boiling point and similarly. Osmosis: the flow of solvent through a semipermeable membrane from pure solvent to solution (or lower to higher concentration). Osmotic pressure Pi = C R T = (n/V) R T; the molar mass M = w R T / (Pi V). Isotonic solutions have the same osmotic pressure (0.9% NaCl is isotonic with blood); hypertonic and hypotonic solutions cause shrinking or swelling of cells. Reverse osmosis occurs when a pressure larger than osmotic pressure is applied to the solution side and is used in desalination. Abnormal molar masses: association (e.g. ethanoic acid dimerises in benzene) decreases colligative effect, dissociation (KCl in water) increases it. van't Hoff factor i = normal molar mass / abnormal molar mass = observed colligative property / calculated; i = 1 + (n-1) alpha for dissociation; modified: Delta T_b = i K_b m, Delta T_f = i K_f m, Pi = i C R T. Examples: i for NaCl = 2, K2SO4 = 3, glucose = 1.
## Deep dive: Concentration and Henry's law
Molality is temperature independent; molarity is not. Gas solubility rises with pressure and falls with temperature.
## Deep dive: Raoult's law
Ideal solutions obey Raoult's law; non-ideal ones deviate positively or negatively. Azeotropes cannot be separated by fractional distillation.
## Deep dive: Colligative properties
They depend on number of particles. The van't Hoff factor corrects for association and dissociation.
