@study
Chemical kinetics is the study of rates of chemical reactions and the factors affecting them and the mechanism. The rate of a reaction is the change in concentration of a reactant or product per unit time: for R -> P, average rate = -Delta[R]/Delta t = +Delta[P]/Delta t; instantaneous rate is dx/dt. For aA + bB -> cC + dD, rate = -(1/a) d[A]/dt = -(1/b) d[B]/dt = (1/c) d[C]/dt. Units of rate: mol L-1 s-1. Factors affecting rate: concentration of reactants, temperature, catalyst, surface area and nature of reactants, and radiation. Rate law: rate = k [A]^x [B]^y, where x and y are determined experimentally; k is the rate constant. Order of reaction is the sum of the exponents x + y, determined experimentally and can be zero, fractional or whole. Molecularity is the number of reacting species colliding simultaneously in an elementary step; it is always a whole number (1, 2, 3) and cannot be zero or fractional; it applies only to elementary reactions. Units of k: for order n, (mol L-1)^(1-n) s-1. Zero order: rate = k, [A] = [A]0 - kt; units of k are mol L-1 s-1; t1/2 = [A]0/2k. First order: rate = k[A]; integrated: ln([A]0/[A]) = kt, or k = (2.303/t) log([A]0/[A]); units s-1; t1/2 = 0.693/k, independent of initial concentration. Examples of first-order reactions: decomposition of N2O5, radioactive decay, hydrogenation of ethene; pseudo first order reactions: hydrolysis of ethyl acetate (acid catalysed), inversion of cane sugar; these seem first order since one reactant is in large excess. Determination of order: initial rate method, integrated rate method, graphical (plot of log[A] vs t is a straight line with slope = -k/2.303 for first order). Temperature dependence: the rate generally doubles for every 10 degrees rise (temperature coefficient 2 to 3). The Arrhenius equation: k = A e^(-Ea/RT); where A is the Arrhenius (frequency or pre-exponential) factor and Ea is the activation energy: ln k = ln A - Ea/RT; log k = log A - Ea/(2.303 RT); a plot of ln k vs 1/T is a straight line with slope -Ea/R; log(k2/k1) = (Ea/(2.303 R)) ((T2 - T1)/(T1 T2)). Activation energy is the minimum extra energy that reactants must have to form the activated complex (threshold energy - average energy of reactants); lower activation energy gives faster reactions. The fraction of molecules with energy equal to or greater than Ea is e^(-Ea/RT). Collision theory: reaction requires collisions with proper orientation and energy greater than the threshold: rate = P Z_AB e^(-Ea/RT), where P is the steric (probability) factor. A catalyst provides an alternate pathway of lower activation energy; it does not change Delta H or the equilibrium constant. Mechanism: complex reactions occur in steps; the slowest step is the rate-determining step; the order depends on the slowest step. Example: 2NO2 + F2 -> 2NO2F: rate = k[NO2][F2]. Radioactive decay is first order.
## Deep dive: Rate and order
Rate laws are determined experimentally. Order is not equal to stoichiometry. Molecularity applies to elementary steps.
## Deep dive: Integrated rate laws
Zero and first-order reactions have distinct plots and half-lives. First order half-life is constant.
## Deep dive: Temperature and catalyst
The Arrhenius equation gives activation energy. Catalysts lower Ea without changing equilibrium.
