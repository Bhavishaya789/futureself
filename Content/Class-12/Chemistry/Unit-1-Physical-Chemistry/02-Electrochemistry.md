@study
Electrochemistry studies the relationship between electrical energy and chemical reactions. A galvanic (voltaic) cell converts chemical energy into electrical energy through a spontaneous redox reaction: the Daniell cell has Zn|Zn2+ || Cu2+|Cu; the zinc anode undergoes oxidation, and the copper cathode undergoes reduction; a salt bridge (KCl or KNO3 in agar) maintains electrical neutrality. The cell is represented as Zn(s)|Zn2+(aq) || Cu2+(aq)|Cu(s). Electrode potential is measured against the standard hydrogen electrode (SHE), which is assigned 0.00 V. E cell = E cathode - E anode. Standard electrode potentials: Zn2+/Zn = -0.76 V; Cu2+/Cu = +0.34 V; so E cell of Daniell cell = 1.10 V. A more positive reduction potential means a stronger oxidising agent; a lower value means stronger reducing agent. The Nernst equation: E cell = E0 cell - (RT/nF) ln Q; at 298 K, E cell = E0 - (0.0591/n) log Q. At equilibrium E cell = 0 and E0 = (0.0591/n) log Kc. Gibbs energy: Delta G0 = -nF E0 cell; Delta G0 = -RT ln K. Faraday constant F = 96,500 C/mol (96487). Conductance G = 1/R (siemens); resistivity rho, conductivity kappa = 1/rho = G (l/A) = G G*, where G* is the cell constant (l/A). Molar conductivity Lambda_m = kappa x 1000 / C (S cm2 mol-1) where C is in mol/L. Strong electrolytes: Lambda_m increases slowly with dilution: Debye-Huckel-Onsager equation Lambda_m = Lambda_m0 - A sqrt(C). Weak electrolytes: Lambda_m rises steeply on dilution because the degree of dissociation increases and cannot be extrapolated to zero concentration. Kohlrausch's law of independent migration of ions: Lambda_m0 = lambda+0 + lambda-0 (the limiting molar conductivity of an electrolyte is the sum of the contributions of its cation and anion). Degree of dissociation alpha = Lambda_m / Lambda_m0 and K_a = C alpha^2 / (1 - alpha). Electrolysis: Faraday's first law: the mass deposited m = Z I t; the amount of substance deposited is proportional to the quantity of charge passed; second law: masses deposited by the same charge are proportional to their equivalent weights. 1 mole of electrons = 1 faraday = 96500 C. Products of electrolysis depend on the nature of the electrolyte, electrode, and overpotential: electrolysis of molten NaCl gives Na and Cl2; aqueous NaCl gives H2 at the cathode and Cl2 at the anode with NaOH formed. Batteries: primary (Dry cell/Leclanche cell: Zn anode, graphite cathode with MnO2 and NH4Cl, EMF 1.5 V; mercury cell used in watches, hearing aids; constant 1.35 V) and secondary (lead storage battery: Pb anode and PbO2 cathode in 38% H2SO4, 12 V, discharge gives PbSO4 and water; nickel-cadmium cell). Fuel cells convert the energy of combustion of fuels such as hydrogen directly into electricity: H2-O2 fuel cell with an alkaline KOH electrolyte, used in Apollo space programme, efficiency about 70%, pollution-free; anode: 2H2 + 4OH- -> 4H2O + 4e-; cathode: O2 + 2H2O + 4e- -> 4OH-. Corrosion is an electrochemical phenomenon: rusting of iron; at the anode Fe -> Fe2+ + 2e-; at the cathode O2 + 4H+ + 4e- -> 2H2O; Fe2+ oxidised to Fe3+ forms Fe2O3.xH2O (rust). Prevention: barrier protection by painting, galvanisation (zinc coating) and cathodic protection with a more reactive metal such as Mg.
## Deep dive: Galvanic cells and Nernst equation
A spontaneous redox reaction generates EMF. The Nernst equation links potential to concentration. Delta G = -nFE.
## Deep dive: Conductance
Molar conductivity rises on dilution. Kohlrausch law gives limiting molar conductivities of weak electrolytes.
## Deep dive: Electrolysis, batteries, corrosion
Faraday's laws quantify electrolysis. Batteries and fuel cells give practical power. Corrosion is an electrochemical process.
