# Coordination Compounds

@study
Coordination compounds contain a central metal atom or ion bonded to a fixed number of ligands (ions or molecules) by coordinate bonds; examples K4[Fe(CN)6], [Cu(NH3)4]SO4, [Ni(CO)4]. Double salts such as Mohr's salt (FeSO4.(NH4)2SO4.6H2O), carnallite and potash alum dissociate completely into simple ions in water, but complexes do not lose identity. Werner's theory (1893, Nobel Prize 1913): metals show two types of valence: primary valence (oxidation state; ionisable; satisfied by negative ions) and secondary valence (coordination number; non-ionisable; satisfied by neutral molecules or negative ions); secondary valences have a fixed spatial arrangement: octahedral (CN 6), tetrahedral and square planar (CN 4). Terms: coordination entity (the central atom plus ligands in square brackets), central atom/ion, ligands (monodentate: Cl-, NH3, H2O, CN-; bidentate: ethane-1,2-diamine (en), oxalate; polydentate: EDTA, which is hexadentate; ambidentate: NO2- (nitro N or nitrito O) and SCN- (thiocyanato S or isothiocyanato N)), coordination number (the number of ligand donor atoms bonded to the metal), coordination sphere, counter ions, oxidation number and homoleptic (only one kind of donor group) and heteroleptic complexes. Chelate: a ligand binding through two or more donor atoms to form ring structures; chelates are more stable. Nomenclature (IUPAC): cation named first; ligands named in alphabetical order before the metal; anionic ligands end in -o (chlorido, cyanido, oxalato), neutral ligands retain names except aqua (H2O), ammine (NH3), carbonyl (CO) and nitrosyl (NO); prefixes di, tri, tetra for simple ligands and bis, tris for complex ones; the oxidation state of the metal is in Roman numerals in brackets; for anionic complexes the metal name ends in -ate (ferrate, cuprate, argentate, aurate, plumbate, stannate). Example: [Co(NH3)6]Cl3 is hexaamminecobalt(III) chloride. K3[Fe(CN)6] is potassium hexacyanidoferrate(III). Isomerism: structural (linkage, coordination, ionisation, hydrate, solvate) and stereoisomerism (geometrical: cis-trans in [Pt(NH3)2Cl2] square planar and in [Co(NH3)4Cl2]+ octahedral; fac-mer in MA3B3; optical isomerism in octahedral complexes such as [Co(en)3]3+ and cis-[CoCl2(en)2]). Square planar MA4 and tetrahedral complexes do not show geometrical isomerism. Bonding: Valence bond theory (VBT) uses hybridisation: octahedral d2sp3 (inner orbital, low spin, strong field ligands) or sp3d2 (outer orbital, high spin, weak field ligands); tetrahedral sp3; square planar dsp2. [Ni(CO)4] is tetrahedral and diamagnetic (Ni(0) d10 sp3); [Ni(CN)4]2- is square planar and diamagnetic (dsp2); [NiCl4]2- is tetrahedral and paramagnetic; [Fe(CN)6]4- is diamagnetic d2sp3; [FeF6]3- is paramagnetic sp3d2. Crystal field theory (CFT): ligands are point charges that split the d orbitals; in octahedral fields, the t2g (lower; dxy, dyz, dzx) and eg (higher; dx2-y2, dz2) sets are separated by Delta_o; strong field ligands (large Delta_o > P, the pairing energy) give low spin; weak field gives high spin. The spectrochemical series: I- < Br- < SCN- < Cl- < F- < OH- < C2O4^2- < H2O < NCS- < edta4- < NH3 < en < CN- < CO. In tetrahedral complexes, Delta_t = (4/9) Delta_o, so they are always high spin. Colour comes from d-d transitions of the complementary colour absorbed. Importance: haemoglobin (Fe), chlorophyll (Mg), vitamin B12 (Co), cisplatin (anticancer), EDTA in hardness estimation, extraction of silver and gold by cyanide complexes, Ni purified by Mond process, and qualitative analysis (Ni-DMG red precipitate).
## Deep dive: Werner and nomenclature
Primary and secondary valences give oxidation state and coordination number. IUPAC naming lists ligands alphabetically and states the oxidation number.
## Deep dive: Isomerism and bonding
Geometrical and optical isomerism arise in octahedral and square planar complexes. VBT uses hybridisation; CFT uses d-orbital splitting.
## Deep dive: Importance
Complexes occur in biology, medicine, extraction and analysis.
