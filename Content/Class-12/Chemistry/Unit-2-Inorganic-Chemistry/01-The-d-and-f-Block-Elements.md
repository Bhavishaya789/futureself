# The d- and f-Block Elements
minutes: 45
yt: class 12 chemistry d block f block transition elements lanthanoids actinoids KMnO4 K2Cr2O7
@study
The d-block elements are those in which the last electron enters the d-orbital; they occupy groups 3 to 12 and include the 3d, 4d, 5d and 6d series. Transition elements have partly filled d-orbitals in their atoms or ions; zinc, cadmium and mercury (group 12) are not considered transition elements because they have fully filled d10 configuration in the atom and ions. General electronic configuration (n-1)d1-10 ns1-2. Properties: metallic, high melting points (except Zn, Cd and Hg, which are low), because of strong metallic bonding by unpaired d-electrons; high enthalpies of atomisation; the melting points rise to a maximum at about d5 (Cr, Mo, W) and then fall; atomic radii decrease along a series then remain nearly constant owing to the d-electron shielding; lanthanoid contraction makes 4d and 5d radii of the second and third series similar (Zr and Hf). Ionisation enthalpies increase along the series. Variable oxidation states because (n-1)d and ns electrons have comparable energies; Mn shows +2 to +7. Stability of higher oxidation states in oxides and fluorides; lower oxidation states in ionic compounds. Standard electrode potentials: E0 Cu2+/Cu is positive (+0.34) as the sum of enthalpy of atomisation, ionisation and hydration is unfavourable. Magnetic properties: paramagnetism due to unpaired electrons; magnetic moment mu = sqrt(n(n+2)) BM where n is the number of unpaired electrons. Coloured ions due to d-d transitions: Ti3+, Cu2+ (blue) and Ni2+ (green); Sc3+, Zn2+ and Ti4+ are colourless as they have empty or fully filled d orbitals. Complex formation is due to small size, high charge and availability of vacant d orbitals. Catalytic properties: Fe in Haber process, V2O5 in the contact process, Ni in hydrogenation. Interstitial compounds (TiC, Fe3H) are hard and high-melting. Alloys: brass (Cu-Zn), bronze (Cu-Sn) and steel. Important compounds: potassium dichromate K2Cr2O7 prepared from chromite ore FeCr2O4 by fusion with sodium carbonate in air to form sodium chromate (yellow), acidified to sodium dichromate (orange), then treated with KCl; in acidic solution it is a strong oxidising agent: Cr2O7^2- + 14H+ + 6e- -> 2Cr3+ + 7H2O; chromate-dichromate equilibrium depends on pH: yellow CrO4^2- in basic and orange Cr2O7^2- in acidic medium. Potassium permanganate KMnO4 is prepared from pyrolusite MnO2 by fusion with KOH in air to give green K2MnO4, which is oxidised electrolytically or by disproportionation in acid to purple KMnO4. In acid: MnO4- + 8H+ + 5e- -> Mn2+ + 4H2O (equivalent weight M/5); in neutral or faintly alkaline: MnO4- + 2H2O + 3e- -> MnO2 + 4OH-. Oxidises Fe2+ to Fe3+, oxalate to CO2, SO2 and H2S. The f-block elements: lanthanoids (4f series, atomic numbers 58 to 71; Ce to Lu) and actinoids (5f series, 90 to 103; Th to Lr). Lanthanoids: general configuration [Xe]4f1-14 5d0-1 6s2; common oxidation state +3 (Ce +4, Eu +2); lanthanoid contraction is the steady decrease in atomic and ionic radii with increasing atomic number due to poor shielding by 4f electrons; consequences: similarity of 4d and 5d elements, difficulty in separation of lanthanoids, and decrease in basicity of hydroxides. Misch metal (lanthanoid 95% with iron 5%, traces of S, C, Ca, Al) is used for making flints for lighters. Actinoids are radioactive, with a wide range of oxidation states (+3 to +7); less shielding by 5f electrons. Uses: Mg-Ce alloys.
## Deep dive: General properties
Transition metals show variable oxidation states, coloured ions, magnetism, catalysis and complex formation. Lanthanoid contraction affects the third series.
## Deep dive: K2Cr2O7 and KMnO4
Both are strong oxidising agents in acid. Their preparations and half reactions are important.
## Deep dive: f-block
Lanthanoids mostly show +3 state and contraction; actinoids are radioactive with many oxidation states.
