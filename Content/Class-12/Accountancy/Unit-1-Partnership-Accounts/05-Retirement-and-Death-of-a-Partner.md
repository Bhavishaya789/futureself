# Retirement and Death of a Partner
minutes: 45
yt: class 12 accountancy retirement death partner gaining ratio settlement joint life policy
@study
Retirement of a partner is withdrawal from the firm; under Section 32 a partner may retire with the consent of all partners, in accordance with an agreement or, in a partnership at will, by giving notice. On retirement the firm is reconstituted and the continuing partners continue the business. The retiring partner is entitled to his capital, share in reserves and accumulated profits, share in revaluation profit, share of goodwill, interest on capital and salary up to the date of retirement, and share of profit up to the date of retirement. Adjustments: (1) new profit sharing ratio, (2) gaining ratio = New ratio - Old ratio, (3) goodwill, (4) revaluation, (5) reserves, (6) joint life policy, (7) settlement of the amount due, (8) adjustment of capitals. New ratio: if not given, the retiring partner's share is divided among the continuing partners in the old ratio or as agreed (so new ratio is their old ratio), or equally, or in the specified proportion of acquisition. Gaining ratio: continuing partners gain and compensate the retiring partner for goodwill. Goodwill entry: Remaining partners' Capital A/cs Dr (in gaining ratio); To Retiring Partner's Capital A/c (for his share of goodwill). If goodwill already appears in the books it is written off in the old ratio. Revaluation Account: gain or loss shared by all partners (including the retiring partner) in the old ratio. Reserves and accumulated profits are credited to all partners in the old ratio. Joint life policy (JLP): the firm insures the lives of all partners; premium is charged to P&L; at retirement or death the policy is realised at its surrender value; the policy account is shown in the balance sheet at surrender value; if the policy is shown at surrender value, no extra entry, and if shown at premium paid, the policy is transferred to partners in the old ratio. When the death of a partner occurs, the firm receives the policy amount: Bank A/c Dr; To Joint Life Policy A/c; the policy reserve is transferred to partners' capital. Settlement of the amount due: (a) paid in lump sum in cash, (b) paid in instalments with interest on the unpaid balance, (c) transferred to loan account on which interest at 6 percent (or as agreed) per annum is payable (the retiring partner's loan); (d) part cash and part loan. If the deed says that the amount is to be paid in instalments with interest, the interest is calculated on the outstanding balance. Death of a partner: the legal representatives of the deceased are entitled to his capital, reserves, goodwill share, interest on capital and share of profit up to the date of death. Share of profit up to date of death is calculated on the basis of time (proportionate to the period) or sales (turnover basis), or last year's profits. Executor's Account is opened; the amount due is transferred to the Executor's A/c. Interest on drawings up to date of death is charged. Deceased partner's drawings are deducted. Capital adjustment: continuing partners' capitals may be adjusted in the new ratio. Deficit is brought in cash or the amount is paid. The profit and loss suspense account is used for profit up to date of death: Profit and Loss Suspense A/c Dr; To Deceased Partner's Capital A/c.
## Deep dive: Retirement
The retiring partner is paid his capital, reserves, goodwill and revaluation share. Continuing partners gain the retiring share and compensate him.
## Deep dive: Death
The legal representative receives the amount due. Profit up to death is computed on time or turnover basis. A suspense account may be used.
## Deep dive: Settlement
The amount may be paid at once or in instalments, or converted to a loan with interest. A joint life policy provides funds for payment.
