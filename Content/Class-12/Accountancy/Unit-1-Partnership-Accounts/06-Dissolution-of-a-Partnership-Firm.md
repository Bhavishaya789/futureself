# Dissolution of a Partnership Firm
minutes: 45
yt: class 12 accountancy dissolution realisation account order of payment insolvency
@study
Dissolution of a partnership means the partnership between all partners ends; dissolution of the firm means the business is closed, its assets sold, liabilities paid and the surplus distributed. Section 39 of the Partnership Act defines dissolution of the firm as the dissolution of partnership between all partners. Modes of dissolution: by agreement (Section 40), compulsory dissolution (Section 41; when all partners or all but one become insolvent, or the business becomes unlawful), on the happening of certain contingencies (Section 42; expiry of term, completion of the venture, death of a partner, insolvency of a partner), by notice in a partnership at will (Section 43) and by the court (Section 44; for lunacy, permanent incapacity, misconduct, persistent breach, transfer of interest, continuous losses, or on just and equitable grounds). Settlement of accounts (Section 48): losses, including deficiency of capital, are paid first out of profits, next out of capital and lastly by the partners individually in their profit-sharing ratio. Assets, including any sum contributed by the partners to make up deficiencies of capital, are applied in this order: (1) in paying the debts of the firm to third parties; (2) in paying each partner proportionately what is due to him on account of advances as distinguished from capital; (3) in paying each partner proportionately what is due to him on account of capital; (4) the residue, if any, is divided among the partners in the profit sharing ratio. Realisation Account: prepared to record the sale of assets and settlement of liabilities and to find the profit or loss on realisation. Debit side: all assets (other than cash, bank and fictitious assets) at book values, liabilities paid, realisation expenses paid by the firm. Credit side: liabilities (external) at book values, amounts realised from assets, assets taken over by partners. Profit or loss on realisation is transferred to partners' capital accounts in the profit sharing ratio. Accounts not transferred to Realisation: Capital Accounts, Current Accounts, Partners' loan, Reserves and P&L balance and fictitious assets; these are settled through partners' accounts. Reserves and accumulated profits are transferred to partners' capital accounts in the profit sharing ratio. Partner's loan is paid before capital. Partner's loan is transferred to his capital account if adjusted so. A partner who takes an asset is debited with the value. If a partner agrees to pay a liability, his capital is debited. Expenses of realisation borne by a partner: the partner's capital is credited with the commission or expenses, and the firm is charged only if the deed says so. Unrecorded assets: if sold, credited in the Realisation Account. Unrecorded liabilities: if paid, debited. Deficiency of capital: a partner with a debit balance of capital must bring in cash. If a partner is insolvent, the loss is shared by the solvent partners in the capital ratio (Garner v Murray rule) — not the profit sharing ratio — and capital accounts are used. Garner v Murray applies in the absence of agreement, and the insolvent partner's deficiency is borne by the solvent partners in the ratio of their last agreed capitals (before dissolution, after adjusting for profits and losses). The cash book shows the final settlement.
## Deep dive: Modes and law
Dissolution can be by agreement, compulsion, contingency, notice or court. Section 48 sets the order of payment. Losses come first from profit, then capital and then partners.
## Deep dive: Realisation account
It records sales of assets and payment of liabilities. Its result is shared in the profit ratio. Partner accounts are closed afterward.
## Deep dive: Insolvent partner
The Garner v Murray rule makes solvent partners share the deficiency in the capital ratio. It does not use the profit ratio.

A: When a partner is insolvent his deficiency is borne by solvent partners in the ratio of their capitals before dissolution.
