# Accounting for Partnership Firms: Fundamentals
minutes: 45
yt: class 12 accountancy partnership fundamentals deed capital accounts interest on drawings
@study
A partnership is the relation between persons who have agreed to share the profits of a business carried on by all or any of them acting for all (Indian Partnership Act, 1932, Section 4). Features: two or more persons (minimum 2; maximum 50 under the Companies Act 2013 and rules), agreement, business, profit sharing, mutual agency and unlimited liability. The partnership deed is the written agreement; it generally contains the firm's name, nature of business, capital contributions, profit sharing ratio, interest on capital and drawings, salary or commission to partners, interest on loans and rules for admission, retirement and settlement of accounts. If the deed is silent, the provisions of the Partnership Act apply under Section 13: no interest on capital; no salary or commission; profits shared equally; interest on a partner's loan at 6 percent per annum; and no interest on drawings. Fixed capital method: capital account remains unchanged except for additions or withdrawals of capital, and a separate current account records interest, salary, drawings and profit share. Fluctuating capital method: all adjustments are made directly in the capital account. Profit and Loss Appropriation Account shows the distribution of profit: it is debited with interest on capital, partner's salary, commission and transfer to reserve, and credited with net profit from the P&L account and interest on drawings. Interest on capital is calculated on opening capital (or on capital after adjustment as per the deed) at the agreed rate. Interest on drawings is a gain to the firm; its calculation depends on the date and amount of drawings. If drawings are made at the beginning of each month, the average period is 6.5 months; at the end of each month 5.5 months; in the middle of each month 6 months; and for equal amounts at the beginning of each quarter 7.5 months, at the end 4.5 months. Interest on drawings = total drawings x rate/100 x average months/12. Product method is used where amounts differ. Guarantee of minimum profit to a partner: the deficiency is borne by the other partners in a given ratio or by one partner. Past adjustments: where an omission is found after accounts are closed, a rectification entry through the Adjustment Account is passed rather than reopening the accounts; the net effect is shared among partners in the old ratio. Partner's loan is not capital, so interest on it is a charge against profit, not an appropriation. Interest on capital is an appropriation if the firm has profit; if there is a loss, no interest on capital is payable unless the deed so provides (and it is then a charge). Salary is an appropriation unless stated as a charge. Partners' capital accounts: debit side drawings, interest on drawings, losses; credit side opening balance, interest on capital, salary and profit share. Admission to the Act: the firm is not a separate legal entity from partners.
## Deep dive: Partnership Act provisions
Section 4 defines partnership; Section 13 gives default rules. Equal sharing of profit, no interest on capital, 6 percent on loan. The deed overrides defaults.
## Deep dive: Capital accounts
Fixed capital keeps capital constant with a current account for other items. Fluctuating capital adjusts everything in the capital account. Choose the method per the deed.
## Deep dive: Appropriation account
It distributes profit among partners after interest, salary and reserve. Interest on drawings is credited. Past adjustments are passed through an adjustment account.

## Formulas
Interest on capital = Capital x Rate/100 x Time
Interest on drawings = Drawings x Rate/100 x Avg months/12
Avg period: monthly start = 6.5; end = 5.5; middle = 6 months
Quarterly start = 7.5; end = 4.5 months
Default (Sec 13): equal profit; loan interest 6%; no salary
