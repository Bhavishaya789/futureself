# Analysis of Financial Statements
minutes: 45
yt: class 12 accountancy analysis financial statements comparative common size tools
@study
Analysis of financial statements is the process of identifying the financial strengths and weaknesses of a firm by establishing relationships between items of the balance sheet and the statement of profit and loss. Interpretation is explaining the meaning of the analysed data. Objectives: to assess profitability, liquidity, solvency and efficiency; to forecast and to compare. Users: management, investors, creditors, employees, government and researchers. Tools: (1) Comparative statements, (2) Common size statements, (3) Ratio analysis, (4) Cash flow analysis and (5) Trend analysis. Comparative statements show the figures of two or more years side by side, with absolute change (increase or decrease) and percentage change. Percentage change = (Absolute change / Base year amount) x 100. The earlier year is the base year. If the base is zero or negative, the percentage cannot be calculated. Comparative balance sheet and comparative statement of profit and loss. Common size statements express each item as a percentage of a common base: in the balance sheet the base is the total of assets (or total of equity and liabilities); in the statement of profit and loss the base is revenue from operations (net sales). Common size % = (Item / Base) x 100. Advantages: enables inter-firm and intra-firm comparison and shows structure. Trend analysis shows the direction of change using index numbers; Trend % = (Current year figure / Base year figure) x 100, with base year = 100. Limitations of financial analysis: based on historical data, ignores price level changes, ignores qualitative factors, accounting policies differ across firms, and a single year's data is not enough. Horizontal analysis compares data over time; vertical analysis compares items within a single year. Intra-firm comparison compares the firm over years; inter-firm comparison compares with other firms. Types of analysts: internal (management, using internal records) and external (outsiders, using published reports). Short-term and long-term analysis. Analysis for creditors focuses on solvency and liquidity; for investors, profitability and returns; for employees, stability. Financial statements are interim or annual. Importance of accuracy. The comparison of a company with the industry average, using benchmarks. Window dressing manipulates figures to look better. Steps in analysis: determine the purpose, collect the data, select the tool, compute and interpret. Examples: increase in revenue from Rs 10 lakh to Rs 12 lakh is an absolute change of Rs 2 lakh and a percentage change of 20 percent. Consistency in format aids comparison.
## Deep dive: Tools
Comparative statements show change; common size shows structure; ratios show relationships; trend shows direction. Each needs a base.
## Deep dive: Computation
Percentage change uses the earlier year as base. Common size uses total assets or revenue. Trend uses a base year of 100.
## Deep dive: Limits
Analysis relies on past data and accounting policies. Qualitative factors and price changes are ignored.

## Formulas
Absolute change = Current - Previous
% change = (Absolute change / Base year) x 100
Common size % = Item / Base x 100
Base: balance sheet = total assets; P&L = revenue
Trend % = Current / Base year x 100
