# Accounting Ratios
minutes: 45
yt: class 12 accountancy accounting ratios liquidity solvency activity profitability
@study
An accounting ratio expresses the relationship between two accounting figures. Ratio analysis helps in understanding liquidity, solvency, activity and profitability. Classification: (1) Liquidity ratios, (2) Solvency ratios, (3) Activity or turnover ratios and (4) Profitability ratios. Liquidity ratios: Current ratio = Current assets / Current liabilities (ideal 2:1); Quick or liquid ratio = Quick assets / Current liabilities (ideal 1:1), where quick assets = current assets - inventories - prepaid expenses. Solvency ratios: Debt-equity ratio = Long-term debt (or Debt) / Equity (Shareholders' funds) (ideal 2:1); Total assets to debt ratio = Total assets / Long-term debt; Proprietary ratio = Shareholders' funds / Total assets; Interest coverage ratio = Net profit before interest and tax / Interest on long-term debt. Activity ratios: Inventory turnover ratio = Cost of revenue from operations / Average inventory (times); average inventory = (Opening + closing) / 2; Trade receivables turnover ratio = Net credit revenue from operations / Average trade receivables (times); Trade payables turnover = Net credit purchases / Average trade payables; Working capital turnover = Revenue from operations / Working capital; Fixed assets turnover = Revenue from operations / Net fixed assets; Collection period = 365 / receivable turnover; Payment period = 365 / payables turnover. Profitability ratios: Gross profit ratio = (Gross profit / Revenue from operations) x 100; Operating ratio = (Operating cost / Revenue from operations) x 100, where operating cost = cost of revenue from operations + operating expenses; Operating profit ratio = 100 - Operating ratio; Net profit ratio = (Net profit / Revenue from operations) x 100; Return on investment (ROI) = (Profit before interest and tax / Capital employed) x 100; Return on equity; Earnings per share = (Net profit after tax - preference dividend) / Number of equity shares; Price-earning ratio = Market price per share / EPS; Dividend per share. Capital employed = Shareholders' funds + Non-current liabilities, or Net fixed assets + Working capital. Working capital = Current assets - Current liabilities. Cost of revenue from operations = Opening inventory + Purchases + Direct expenses - Closing inventory. Gross profit = Revenue from operations - Cost of revenue from operations. Interpretation: A high current ratio suggests liquidity but may signal idle assets; a high debt-equity ratio signals higher risk; a high inventory turnover shows efficient sales; a high receivables turnover shows quick collection. Effect of transactions on ratios: for example, if current assets and liabilities equal, issuing shares for cash raises the current ratio. Limitations: ratios are based on past data, different accounting policies, no qualitative factors, and need comparison with norms. Working capital positive indicates current assets exceed current liabilities. Net profit means profit before tax less tax as per the question. Quick assets exclude inventories and prepaid. Current liabilities include proposed dividend. Ratios can be expressed as a pure ratio, a percentage or in times. Return on investment uses PBIT. Net profit ratio uses profit after tax, as per the question.
## Deep dive: Liquidity and solvency
Current and quick ratios show short-term ability. Debt-equity and proprietary ratios show long-term strength.
## Deep dive: Activity
Turnover ratios show how fast inventory and receivables move. Higher turnover generally means efficiency.
## Deep dive: Profitability
Gross, operating and net ratios use revenue as base. ROI uses capital employed. Interpretation needs comparison.

## Formulas
Current ratio = CA / CL
Quick ratio = (CA - Inventory - Prepaid) / CL
Debt-equity = Long-term debt / Shareholders funds
Inventory turnover = Cost of revenue / Avg inventory
Receivables turnover = Net credit revenue / Avg receivables
Gross profit % = GP / Revenue x 100
Operating ratio = (Cost of revenue + Operating exp) / Revenue x 100
Net profit % = NP / Revenue x 100
ROI = PBIT / Capital employed x 100
EPS = (PAT - Pref. dividend) / Equity shares
