# Cash Flow Statement
minutes: 45
yt: class 12 accountancy cash flow statement AS 3 operating investing financing
@study
A cash flow statement shows the inflows and outflows of cash and cash equivalents during a period, classified into operating, investing and financing activities. It is prepared as per Accounting Standard 3 (AS 3). Cash includes cash in hand and demand deposits with banks; cash equivalents are short-term, highly liquid investments (maturity up to three months) readily convertible into known amounts of cash. Objectives: to show the sources and uses of cash, to assess the ability to generate cash and to plan. Operating activities are the principal revenue-producing activities of the enterprise and activities other than investing and financing. Examples of inflows: cash receipts from customers; outflows: payments to suppliers, employees, taxes. Investing activities are acquisition and disposal of long-term assets and investments not included in cash equivalents: purchase and sale of fixed assets, purchase and sale of investments, loans given and repaid, interest and dividends received. Financing activities change the size and composition of owners' capital and borrowings: issue of shares and debentures, redemption, buy-back, payment of dividends, repayment of loans, and interest paid. Under AS 3, interest and dividends received and paid for non-financial enterprises: interest paid is a financing activity, and interest and dividends received are investing activities; dividend paid is a financing activity. Income tax paid is shown under operating activities. Methods of reporting operating activities: direct method (gross cash receipts and payments) and indirect method (adjusting net profit before tax for non-cash and non-operating items). Indirect method steps: Net profit before tax and extraordinary items (net profit as per the statement of profit and loss + tax + transfer to reserve + proposed dividend, if applicable); add non-cash and non-operating expenses: depreciation, amortisation of intangible assets and preliminary expenses written off, interest on borrowings, loss on sale of fixed assets, provision for doubtful debts; subtract non-operating income: dividend and interest received, profit on sale of fixed assets or investments; this gives operating profit before working capital changes; then adjust for changes in current assets and current liabilities: increase in current assets (except cash and cash equivalents) is deducted; decrease is added; increase in current liabilities is added; decrease is deducted; this gives cash generated from operations; deduct income tax paid to get net cash from operating activities. Cash flow from investing activities: purchase of fixed assets (outflow), sale of fixed assets (inflow), purchase and sale of investments, interest and dividends received (inflow). Cash flow from financing activities: proceeds from issue of shares and debentures (inflow), redemption and repayment (outflow), interest paid and dividend paid (outflow). Net increase or decrease in cash = Operating + Investing + Financing. Opening balance + net change = closing balance of cash and cash equivalents. Transactions not involving cash flows: purchase of assets by issue of shares, bonus shares, conversion of debentures, depreciation, and transfer between cash and bank or cash equivalents. Extra-ordinary items are shown separately. Format: A Cash flows from operating activities; B investing; C financing; net increase; add opening; closing.
## Deep dive: Classification
Cash flows are grouped into operating, investing and financing. AS 3 guides classification of interest and dividends.
## Deep dive: Operating activities
The indirect method adjusts profit for non-cash and non-operating items and for working capital changes. Tax paid is deducted.
## Deep dive: Investing and financing
Investing covers fixed assets and investments. Financing covers shares, debentures, loans, interest paid and dividends.

- Tax = Net cash from operating

Closing = Opening + Net change
