# Financial Statements of a Company
minutes: 45
yt: class 12 accountancy financial statements company balance sheet schedule iii statement of profit and loss
@study
Financial statements are the final outcome of the accounting process, prepared to show the financial position and performance of a business. Under Section 129 of the Companies Act 2013, a company must prepare financial statements in the form given in Schedule III: the Balance Sheet, the Statement of Profit and Loss, the Cash Flow Statement (for companies other than certain small ones), the Statement of Changes in Equity and notes. Features of financial statements: they are historical, based on accounting concepts, show interim and final data and are prepared for stakeholders. Objectives: to provide information about financial position, performance and cash flows. The Balance Sheet shows assets, liabilities and equity at a point in time. Part I Equity and Liabilities: (1) Shareholders' funds: share capital, reserves and surplus (capital reserve, capital redemption reserve, securities premium, debenture redemption reserve, general reserve, surplus i.e. balance in the statement of profit and loss), and money received against share warrants; (2) Share application money pending allotment; (3) Non-current liabilities: long-term borrowings (debentures, bonds, term loans), deferred tax liabilities (net), other long-term liabilities (trade payables long-term), and long-term provisions (provision for employee benefits); (4) Current liabilities: short-term borrowings (bank overdraft, cash credit, loans repayable on demand), trade payables (creditors and bills payable), other current liabilities (current maturities of long-term debt, interest accrued, unpaid dividend, calls in advance, outstanding expenses) and short-term provisions (provision for tax, proposed dividend, provision for employee benefits). Part II Assets: (1) Non-current assets: property, plant and equipment and intangible assets (goodwill, patents, trademarks), capital work-in-progress, intangible assets under development, non-current investments, long-term loans and advances, deferred tax assets and other non-current assets (preliminary expenses to the extent not written off); (2) Current assets: current investments, inventories (raw material, work-in-progress, finished goods, stock-in-trade, stores and spares, loose tools), trade receivables (debtors and bills receivable), cash and cash equivalents (cash in hand, bank balance, cheques in hand, fixed deposits with maturity up to three months), short-term loans and advances (advance tax, prepaid expenses, loans to employees) and other current assets (accrued income, advances for supply of goods). Current means expected to be realised or settled within 12 months or the operating cycle. Statement of Profit and Loss: Revenue from operations (sale of products and services, other operating revenues, less excise duty), other income (interest, dividends, profit on sale of fixed assets), total revenue, expenses (cost of materials consumed, purchases of stock-in-trade, changes in inventories of finished goods, work-in-progress and stock-in-trade, employee benefit expenses, finance costs, depreciation and amortisation, other expenses), profit before tax, tax expense (current and deferred) and profit for the period. Earnings per share is disclosed. Notes to accounts give detail. Vertical or horizontal format; Schedule III uses a vertical format. Balance Sheet as at; Statement of Profit and Loss for the year ended. Contingent liabilities are disclosed in notes. Prior period items are shown separately. Format adds comparative figures for the previous year.
## Deep dive: Statements
Schedule III prescribes the balance sheet and profit and loss format. The cash flow statement and notes complete the set.
## Deep dive: Balance sheet
Equity and liabilities and assets are classified into current and non-current. Shareholders' funds include share capital and reserves.
## Deep dive: Profit and loss
Revenue and other income are matched with expenses. Tax is deducted to find profit for the period.
