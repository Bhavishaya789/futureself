# Issue and Redemption of Debentures
minutes: 45
yt: class 12 accountancy debentures issue redemption debenture redemption reserve sinking fund
@study
A debenture is a written acknowledgement of a debt by a company, usually under its common seal, containing a promise to pay interest at a fixed rate and to repay the principal on a specified date. Debenture holders are creditors, not owners. Types: secured (mortgage) and unsecured (naked); redeemable and irredeemable; convertible and non-convertible; registered and bearer. Interest on debentures is a charge against profit, payable whether or not there is profit. Tax is deducted at source (TDS) on interest. Issue: debentures can be issued for cash, for consideration other than cash (to vendors) and as collateral security. Issue at par, at premium or at discount. Premium on issue is a capital profit credited to Securities Premium and is not a revenue profit. Discount on issue is a capital loss, debited to Discount on Issue of Debentures A/c and written off, usually in the year of issue from securities premium or the statement of profit and loss over the life of debentures. Redeemable at par, premium or discount. Premium on redemption is a loss, accounted at the time of issue by debiting Loss on Issue of Debentures A/c and crediting Premium on Redemption of Debentures A/c (a liability). Journal entries: Debenture Application and Allotment A/c Dr; To Debentures A/c; To Securities Premium. Debentures issued as collateral security: only a memorandum or a note, unless the loan is defaulted; Debentures Suspense A/c Dr; To Debentures A/c, and interest is not payable on collateral debentures; the balance sheet shows the loan with a note and the collateral security. Section 71 of the Companies Act, 2013: a company may issue debentures with the option to convert into shares. Debenture Redemption Reserve (DRR): a company issuing debentures must create DRR out of profits; as per Rule 18 of the Companies (Share Capital and Debentures) Rules, 2014, listed companies are exempt, as are NBFCs and housing finance companies, and for others DRR is 10 percent of the value of outstanding debentures; investment in specified securities (called debenture redemption investment) of not less than 15 percent of debentures maturing in the year must be made by 30 April. Methods of redemption: (a) lump sum at maturity, (b) in instalments (annual drawings by lot), (c) purchase in the open market (own debentures; cancel or keep as investment), and (d) conversion into shares or new debentures. Redemption out of capital or profits. Sinking fund method: a fixed amount is set aside every year and invested outside; the amount grows with interest; Sinking Fund A/c, Sinking Fund Investment A/c. Own debentures purchased: Own Debentures A/c Dr; interest on own debentures is income. Gain or loss on cancellation transferred to Capital Reserve. Conversion: Debentures A/c Dr; To Share Capital A/c; To Securities Premium. Redemption entries: Debentures A/c Dr; To Debenture holders A/c; Debenture holders A/c Dr; To Bank A/c. DRR is transferred to General Reserve after redemption. Interest on debentures: Interest A/c Dr; To Debenture holders A/c; TDS Payable A/c.
## Deep dive: Debentures
Debentures are a loan with fixed interest. Interest is payable regardless of profit. Debenture holders are creditors.
## Deep dive: Issue
Debentures may be issued at par, premium or discount. Premium is a capital profit. Collateral issue is only a memorandum entry.
## Deep dive: Redemption
Redemption can be lump sum, instalments, open market or conversion. DRR and investment requirements protect holders. Sinking fund accumulates money.
## Hindi saransh
Rinpatra: nirgaman, byaj, mochan; rinpatra mochan sanchit kosh (DRR) aur sinking fund vidhi.
> Remember: interest on debentures is a charge on profit; DRR is created out of profit.

## Formulas
Interest = Face value x Rate/100 x Time
Premium on issue: capital profit (Securities Premium)
Discount on issue: capital loss (write off)
DRR (non-exempt) = 10% of outstanding debentures
Investment = at least 15% of debentures maturing in year

## Moderate
P: Differentiate between shares and debentures.
A: Shares represent ownership and carry dividend; debentures represent a loan and carry interest.

## High
P: 1,000 debentures of Rs 100 issued at 5 percent discount. Find discount.
A: Discount = 1000 x 100 x 5% = Rs 5,000, a capital loss debited to Discount on Issue of Debentures.

## Advanced
P: Explain the Debenture Redemption Reserve.
A: A reserve created out of profit to redeem debentures, usually 10 percent of outstanding debentures for non-exempt companies.
