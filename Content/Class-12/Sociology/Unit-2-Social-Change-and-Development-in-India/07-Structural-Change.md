# Structural Change

@study
Social change is the alteration of social structure and culture over time. Changes can be evolutionary (gradual) or revolutionary (rapid), and may be structural (affecting institutions such as the family and economy) or cultural. Causes include environmental, technological, economic, political and cultural factors. The Industrial Revolution in England in the late eighteenth century, with the steam engine of James Watt, the spinning jenny and the factory system, was a structural change that created industrial capitalism, wage labour and cities. The French Revolution of 1789 was a political revolution that spread ideas of liberty, equality and fraternity. Sociologists theorised the new society. Karl Marx saw capitalism as based on class conflict between bourgeoisie and proletariat, driven by the economy. Max Weber stressed rationalisation, bureaucracy and the role of ideas such as the Protestant ethic. Emile Durkheim analysed the division of labour and the shift from mechanical solidarity to organic solidarity, and warned of anomie. Urbanisation is the growth of towns and cities, with migration, slums, anonymity and diversity; sociologists like Weber and Simmel studied city life. In colonial India, the East India Company and later the Crown established new institutions: the railways (1853), telegraph, postal system, modern education and the factory, bringing urbanisation in port cities like Bombay, Madras and Calcutta, a market economy, and new classes. Colonial rule changed agrarian structures through the Permanent Settlement (1793), commercialised agriculture and dismantled handicrafts. After independence, planned development, public sector industry, land reforms and the Green Revolution transformed India. Technology such as the printing press and telegraph, and now the internet, speeds up change. Structural change in India includes industrialisation, urbanisation and the growth of a middle class, while many traditional institutions persist.
## Deep dive: Types and causes
Evolutionary or revolutionary, structural or cultural; technological, economic and political causes.
## Deep dive: Classical thinkers
Marx on class conflict, Weber on rationalisation, Durkheim on division of labour and anomie.
## Deep dive: India
Colonial railways, towns and commercialisation, and post-independence planning transformed structure.
