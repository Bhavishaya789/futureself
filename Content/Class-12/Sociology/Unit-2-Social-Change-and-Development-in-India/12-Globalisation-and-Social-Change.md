# Globalisation and Social Change
minutes: 40
yt: class 12 sociology globalisation social change outsourcing BPO glocalisation global village transnational corporations consumption culture
@study
Globalisation is the growing interconnection of economies, cultures and societies across the world. It is not new, but the present phase after 1991 is faster, based on information technology, communication and capital flows. Economic globalisation includes trade liberalisation, the role of the WTO (1995), transnational corporations (TNCs), global finance, outsourcing and business process outsourcing (BPO), as in call centres in Bangalore and Gurgaon. India’s reforms of 1991 (liberalisation, privatisation and globalisation) opened the economy, and the IT industry, services and exports grew. Global communication includes mobile phones, satellite television and the internet, which compress time and space (David Harvey), making the world a global village (Marshall McLuhan). Cultural change takes two forms: homogenisation, the spread of a uniform global culture of fast food, brands and music, and glocalisation (Roland Robertson), where global products adapt to local cultures, as with McDonald’s offering vegetarian items in India. Globalisation also affects work: employment in services grew, flexible and contract work spread, and women entered new jobs such as call centres, though under night shifts. Consumption patterns changed with malls, brands and credit. Farmers face competition and volatile world prices; small producers and artisans have lost, while some export crops have gained. Migration and remittances link families across nations. Media and the internet spread new ideas, and social media connect people. Critics point to inequality, job insecurity, cultural homogenisation and dominance of TNCs. Supporters point to growth, jobs, technology and wider choices. In sociological terms globalisation affects class, caste, gender and the state. The state’s role has changed from regulator to facilitator, but it has not disappeared. Globalisation, hence, brings change that is uneven and contested.
## Deep dive: Economic globalisation
Liberalisation, WTO, TNCs, outsourcing and BPO changed India’s economy after 1991.
## Deep dive: Culture
Homogenisation and glocalisation; global village and time-space compression.
## Deep dive: Impacts
Growth and jobs alongside inequality, insecurity and pressure on farmers and artisans.
