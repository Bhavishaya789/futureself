# Social Movements

@study
A social movement is a sustained collective effort to bring about or resist social change, using organisation, ideology and leadership, often outside formal politics. Features include collective action, an organised leadership, a common ideology and a continuing effort. Theories include relative deprivation (frustration from a gap between expectation and reality) and resource mobilisation (the role of organisation and resources). Movements are classified as redemptive (changing individuals), reformative (changing structures gradually) and revolutionary (overthrowing the social order). Old social movements, such as the workers’ and peasant movements, focus on class and economic demands and are linked to parties; new social movements, such as environmental, women’s and Dalit movements, are about identity, ecology and rights. Peasant movements include the Champaran Satyagraha (1917), Kheda (1918), the Bardoli Satyagraha (1928) led by Vallabhbhai Patel, the Tebhaga movement in Bengal (1946-47), the Telangana movement (1946-51) and the Naxalbari uprising of 1967; the All India Kisan Sabha was formed in 1936 under Swami Sahajanand Saraswati. Tribal movements demand land, forest and identity; the Jharkhand movement led to the state in 2000. Dalit movements include the Mahad Satyagraha, Ambedkar’s conversion to Buddhism in 1956, and the Dalit Panthers (1972). Women’s movements began with reform in the nineteenth century and in modern times campaigned against dowry, rape (the Mathura case of 1972 and amendments of 1983), domestic violence, and for property rights; the Vishakha guidelines of 1997 led to the Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace Act 2013, the Domestic Violence Act 2005 and the Hindu Succession (Amendment) Act 2005; the Criminal Law Amendment of 2013 followed the Delhi protests of 2012, and the Women’s Reservation Act 2023 reserved one-third of seats in the Lok Sabha and state assemblies. Environmental movements include Chipko (1973), Narmada Bachao Andolan (1985) and Silent Valley in Kerala (1970s-80s). Movements shape law, policy and public opinion, but face repression, co-option and internal division. Social movements are essential to democracy because they voice marginalised concerns.
## Deep dive: Concepts
Collective, organised, ideological efforts; relative deprivation and resource mobilisation; redemptive, reformative and revolutionary types.
## Deep dive: Movements
Peasant, tribal, Dalit, women’s and environmental movements shaped India’s politics and laws.
## Deep dive: Impact
Movements led to laws such as the 2013 harassment Act and 2005 property rights, and to states like Jharkhand.

## Moderate
P: What is a social movement?
A: A sustained collective effort, usually organised, to bring about or resist social change.

## High
P: Distinguish old and new social movements.
A: Old movements, such as workers’ and peasant movements, focus on class and economic demands, while new ones, such as environmental and women’s movements, focus on identity, ecology and rights.

R: 2 | Identity
