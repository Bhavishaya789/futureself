# Mass Media and Communications

@study
Mass media are means of communication that reach large audiences: print (newspapers, magazines), electronic (radio, television, cinema) and digital (internet and social media). They are one-way or interactive, have wide reach and shape opinion, culture and politics. The printing press, associated with Gutenberg around 1440, spread ideas and literacy. In India, the first newspaper, Hicky’s Bengal Gazette, began in 1780; Raja Rammohan Roy launched Sambad Kaumudi in 1821; the press became a tool of nationalism with leaders such as Tilak (Kesari and Mahratta), Gandhi (Young India, Harijan) and Ambedkar (Mooknayak, 1920). The colonial state used the Vernacular Press Act of 1878 under Lytton to restrict Indian language papers, and it was repealed in 1882. Radio began with the Indian Broadcasting Company in 1927 and was named All India Radio in 1936; private FM radio began from 1999 onwards. Television began in India as an experimental service in Delhi on 15 September 1959, Doordarshan expanded after 1975 with the SITE experiment and colour came in 1982 with the Asian Games. Satellite and cable television arrived after 1991 with Star TV and CNN, ending the state monopoly. Prasar Bharati, the public broadcaster, was set up under the Prasar Bharati Act 1990 and became operational in 1997. The first Indian feature film was Raja Harishchandra (1913) by Dadasaheb Phalke, and the first talkie was Alam Ara (1931). The internet and smartphones created social media and citizen journalism. Freedom of the press flows from Article 19(1)(a) of the Constitution, subject to reasonable restrictions. Media influence democracy by informing citizens, but issues such as paid news, ownership concentration, sensationalism and TRP competition affect quality. Media convergence blurs boundaries among platforms. The digital divide means that access is unequal. Media are also shaped by market forces and advertising, and shape consumption and culture.
## Deep dive: Media growth
From the 1780 press to radio, television, satellite channels and social media.
## Deep dive: Press and nationalism
Newspapers spread nationalism; the Vernacular Press Act of 1878 tried to curb them.
## Deep dive: Media and democracy
Article 19(1)(a) protects the press; paid news, ownership and the digital divide are concerns.
