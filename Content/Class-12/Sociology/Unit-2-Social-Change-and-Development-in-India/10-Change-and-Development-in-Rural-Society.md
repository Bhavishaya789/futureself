# Change and Development in Rural Society
minutes: 40
yt: class 12 sociology rural society agrarian structure land reforms green revolution zamindari MGNREGA bonded labour migration
@study
About two-thirds of Indians live in rural areas and depend on agriculture, which is the main source of livelihood. The agrarian structure includes landowners, tenants, sharecroppers and agricultural labourers, linked to caste: upper and dominant castes usually own land, while Dalits and landless castes work as labourers. Under the British, the Permanent Settlement (1793) made zamindars owners, the Ryotwari and Mahalwari systems taxed cultivators, high land revenue and commercialisation led to indebtedness. Village society had the jajmani system and rural indebtedness to moneylenders. After independence, land reforms aimed at equity and productivity: abolition of zamindari and intermediaries in the 1950s, tenancy reforms, ceilings on landholding and consolidation of holdings. Success was uneven, with better results in Kerala and West Bengal, while land ceiling surplus was small. Vinoba Bhave’s Bhoodan movement (1951) sought voluntary land gifts. The Green Revolution of the 1960s used high yielding variety seeds, fertilisers and irrigation, increased wheat and rice output in Punjab, Haryana and western Uttar Pradesh, but widened regional and class inequalities and caused ecological costs like falling water tables. Rural labour changed: the Bonded Labour System (Abolition) Act of 1976 outlawed bondage; the Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act 2005 guarantees 100 days of wage employment. Commercialisation, mechanisation, contract farming and migration to cities and the Gulf have altered rural life; women do much farm work but own little land. Globalisation and market liberalisation exposed farmers to price fluctuations and input costs, and indebtedness and distress have been linked with farmers’ suicides. Rural-urban linkages, non-farm employment and remittances shape villages. The state tried to support rural development through the Public Distribution System, Minimum Support Price and credit.
## Deep dive: Agrarian structure
Landowners, tenants and labourers linked to caste; colonial settlements brought indebtedness.
## Deep dive: Reforms
Zamindari abolition, tenancy reform, land ceilings, Bhoodan, the Green Revolution and MGNREGA.
## Deep dive: Contemporary change
Commercialisation, migration, distress and non-farm work reshape villages.
