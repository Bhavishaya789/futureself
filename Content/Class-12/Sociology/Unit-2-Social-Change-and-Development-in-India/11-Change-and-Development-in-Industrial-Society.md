# Change and Development in Industrial Society
minutes: 40
yt: class 12 sociology industrial society industrialisation trade unions informal sector liberalisation labour public sector tata steel
@study
Industrialisation means the shift to factory production, mechanised work and wage labour. In colonial India industries developed unevenly: cotton textile mills in Bombay and Ahmedabad, jute mills in Calcutta, and the Tata Iron and Steel Company set up in 1907 at Sakchi (later Jamshedpur) by Jamsetji Tata’s family as a swadeshi enterprise. After independence the state led industrialisation through planning, public sector enterprises in steel, power and heavy industry, and the Industrial Policy Resolution of 1956 under a mixed economy, with licences, import substitution and protection. Industrial policy changed after the 1991 reforms, which reduced licensing, opened up to foreign investment and liberalised imports. Industrial workers are in the organised (formal) sector with job security and benefits, and in the larger unorganised or informal sector, which employs about 90 per cent of the workforce, with low wages and no security. Women work in industries like textiles and garments, often at lower pay. Contract labour and casualisation have grown. Workers organised into trade unions: the Trade Unions Act 1926 recognised unions, and the All India Trade Union Congress (1920) was the first central union. Strikes such as the Bombay textile strike of 1982-83 led by Datta Samant showed workers’ militancy and the decline of mills. The Industrial Disputes Act 1947 and the Factories Act 1948 regulate relations and conditions. Small-scale and cottage industries are important in employment, and many reserved items were de-reserved after 1991. Migration brought workers to industrial cities, where chawls and slums housed them. Industrial development raises issues of pollution and safety, as in the Bhopal gas tragedy of December 1984, and of child labour, regulated by the Child Labour Act of 1986. Special Economic Zones were created under the Act of 2005. Globalisation increased outsourcing and flexible labour and weakened unions. Industrial society also changes family life, class and work culture.
## Deep dive: Industrial development
Colonial mills, Tata Steel 1907 and post-independence public sector led by planning.
## Deep dive: Workers
Formal and informal sectors; unions, strikes and laws like the Industrial Disputes Act 1947.
## Deep dive: After 1991
Liberalisation, contract labour and outsourcing changed work and weakened unions.
