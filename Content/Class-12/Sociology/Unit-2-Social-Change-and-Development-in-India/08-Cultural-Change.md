# Cultural Change

@study
Cultural change refers to changes in values, beliefs, rituals and practices. In India it is seen through Sanskritisation, westernisation, modernisation and secularisation. M. N. Srinivas introduced Sanskritisation (the adoption of upper caste customs by lower castes) and westernisation (change in lifestyle, dress, food and ideas under British influence). Modernisation is the broader process of adopting modern ideas and institutions such as science, rationality, democracy and equality. Secularisation is the decline in the influence of religion on public life, though in India religion is also visible in public life. The nineteenth century saw social reform movements that questioned social evils and tried to reinterpret tradition. The Brahmo Samaj (1828, Raja Rammohan Roy) opposed sati, idol worship and supported women’s education; sati was banned in 1829 by Lord William Bentinck. The Prarthana Samaj (1867) worked in Maharashtra. The Arya Samaj (1875, Dayanand Saraswati) used the slogan Back to the Vedas and promoted education and widow remarriage. The Ramakrishna Mission (1897, Vivekananda) combined spirituality and service. The Aligarh Movement of Sir Syed Ahmad Khan (the Mohammedan Anglo-Oriental College, 1875) promoted modern education among Muslims. The Singh Sabha (1873) renewed Sikh identity. Jyotiba Phule’s Satyashodhak Samaj (1873) fought the caste order, Sri Narayana Guru’s SNDP (1903) uplifted the Ezhavas in Kerala, and Periyar’s Self-Respect Movement (1925) challenged Brahmin dominance in Tamil Nadu. Legislative reforms included the Widow Remarriage Act (1856) and the Age of Consent Act (1891). Reformers used print, schools and associations, and debated tradition and modernity. Reform movements had common features: they were responses to colonial modernity, valued rational thinking, were mostly led by the educated middle class, and often reinterpreted tradition. Their limitation was that their reach was often limited to the urban middle class. Media and education continue cultural change today.
## Deep dive: Concepts
Sanskritisation, westernisation, modernisation and secularisation describe cultural change in India.
## Deep dive: Reform movements
Brahmo Samaj, Arya Samaj, Aligarh, Singh Sabha, Satyashodhak Samaj, SNDP and Self-Respect Movement.
## Deep dive: Laws and features
Sati ban 1829, Widow Remarriage Act 1856; reforms reinterpreted tradition and used modern means.
