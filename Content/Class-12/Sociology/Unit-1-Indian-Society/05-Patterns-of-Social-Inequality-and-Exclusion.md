# Patterns of Social Inequality and Exclusion
minutes: 40
yt: class 12 sociology social inequality exclusion untouchability dalit adivasi OBC women disability atrocities act reservation
@study
Social inequality is the unequal distribution of resources and opportunities among groups, and social exclusion is the denial of full participation in society. Caste-based discrimination appears in untouchability, with segregation, humiliation, exploitation and violence. Scheduled Castes (Dalits) are listed under Article 341. Constitutional safeguards include Article 15 (no discrimination), Article 16(4) (reservation in jobs), Article 17 (abolition of untouchability), reservation in education and legislatures, and laws such as the Untouchability (Offences) Act 1955, renamed the Protection of Civil Rights Act in 1976, and the Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes (Prevention of Atrocities) Act 1989. Reformers fought caste: Jyotiba Phule (Gulamgiri 1873, Satyashodhak Samaj 1873), B. R. Ambedkar (Annihilation of Caste 1936, Mahad Satyagraha 1927, the Poona Pact 1932), Periyar and Gandhi (who used the term Harijan). Adivasis or Scheduled Tribes, notified under Article 342, suffer displacement due to mining and dams, loss of forests and poverty; the Fifth and Sixth Schedules protect their areas, and the Forest Rights Act 2006 recognises forest rights. Other Backward Classes (OBCs) were studied by the Kaka Kalelkar Commission (1953) and the Mandal Commission (1980), which recommended 27 per cent reservation, implemented in 1990. Women face inequality through patriarchy in work, wages, property and safety; laws include the Hindu Succession (Amendment) Act 2005 and the Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act 2005. Persons with disabilities are excluded by attitudes and lack of access; the Persons with Disabilities Act 1995 was replaced by the Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act 2016. Manual scavenging was prohibited by law. Inequality and exclusion are social not natural, and combine caste, class and gender.
## Deep dive: Caste and untouchability
Article 17, the Protection of Civil Rights Act and the Atrocities Act 1989 fight untouchability.
## Deep dive: Adivasis and OBCs
Adivasis face displacement and forest loss; Mandal Commission recommended 27 per cent OBC reservation.
## Deep dive: Gender and disability
Patriarchy and inaccessibility exclude women and persons with disabilities; laws protect their rights.
