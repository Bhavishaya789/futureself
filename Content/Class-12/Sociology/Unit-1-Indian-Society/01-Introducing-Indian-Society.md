# Introducing Indian Society
minutes: 40
yt: class 12 sociology introducing indian society colonialism nationalism social reform unity in diversity AR Desai Srinivas
@study
Indian society is studied through its history, especially the colonial period, which shaped modern India’s economy, institutions and identity. Colonialism was a system of political and economic domination by Britain; it changed land revenue systems (the Permanent Settlement of 1793 under Cornwallis, the Ryotwari system and the Mahalwari system), ruined traditional handicrafts, and built railways (first run in 1853 from Bombay to Thane), the telegraph, post and modern education. It also created new classes: an English-educated middle class that became the base of nationalism. The colonial state conducted the census, and the first systematic census was in 1881, which classified people by caste and religion and affected identities. Nationalism emerged as a response to colonial rule; A. R. Desai’s Social Background of Indian Nationalism (1948) is a Marxist analysis that sees nationalism as arising from the material conditions created by colonialism, such as new communications, education, press and capitalism. The Indian National Congress was founded in 1885. Social reform movements such as those of Raja Rammohan Roy (who campaigned against sati, abolished in 1829), Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar (widow remarriage), Jyotiba Phule and Dr B. R. Ambedkar questioned social evils. Communalism developed as the use of religious identity for political ends, linked to colonial policies of divide and rule and to the 1947 Partition. Indian sociology grew with scholars such as G. S. Ghurye (Caste and Race in India), D. P. Mukerji, A. R. Desai and M. N. Srinivas (Sanskritisation, dominant caste, village studies). Nehru’s Discovery of India (1946) described unity in diversity, the idea that India’s many languages, religions and regions share a common cultural fabric. Sociology also studies how modern ideas such as secularism, democracy and citizenship were absorbed.
## Deep dive: Colonialism
Land revenue changes, railways, education and a new middle class remade Indian society.
## Deep dive: Nationalism and reform
A. R. Desai explained nationalism through material conditions; reformers challenged caste and gender evils.
## Deep dive: Unity in diversity
Nehru’s Discovery of India describes a shared cultural fabric across diverse communities.
