# The Challenges of Cultural Diversity
minutes: 40
yt: class 12 sociology challenges of cultural diversity communalism secularism linguistic states national integration minority rights
@study
India has great cultural diversity: many languages, religions, castes, tribes and regions. Diversity is a source of strength but also poses challenges such as regionalism, communalism, ethnic conflict and the integration of minorities. The Indian ideal of unity in diversity needs the state to balance recognition of identity with national unity. Community, nation and state are distinct: a nation is a large community with a sense of unity, a state is a political and legal body, and India is a nation-state with a plural society. Language is a key identity; the Eighth Schedule recognises 22 languages after the 2003 amendment, and the States Reorganisation Act of 1956 formed states on linguistic lines, after the 1953 Andhra movement, which proved that linguistic identity was compatible with national unity. The three-language formula and Hindi as official language with English as associate language remain debated. The census of 2011 showed the religious composition: about 79.8 per cent Hindus, 14.2 per cent Muslims, 2.3 per cent Christians, 1.7 per cent Sikhs, 0.7 per cent Buddhists and 0.4 per cent Jains. Minority rights are protected by Articles 29 and 30, which protect minorities’ culture and right to run educational institutions. Communalism is an ideology that uses religious identity for political mobilisation and sees other religious groups as opposed; it has produced riots, as in the Partition of 1947, the anti-Sikh violence of 1984, the Babri Masjid demolition and riots in 1992-93, and Gujarat in 2002. Secularism in India means equal respect for all religions by the state, not a strict separation as in the West. Ethnic and regional conflicts such as Kashmir, Punjab and the Northeast were addressed by accords and statehood. Role of the state includes protecting minority rights, ensuring equal treatment and promoting dialogue. National integration is promoted by shared symbols, education and constitutional values. A diverse society needs a sense of citizenship above narrower identities.
## Deep dive: Diversity and language
Linguistic states and the Eighth Schedule recognised language identity without breaking unity.
## Deep dive: Communalism and secularism
Communalism politicises religion; Indian secularism means equal respect for all faiths.
## Deep dive: Minority rights
Articles 29 and 30 protect minority culture and educational institutions.
