# The Market as a Social Institution
minutes: 40
yt: class 12 sociology market social institution adam smith marx commodification weekly haat traditional trading communities consumption
@study
Sociologists see the market not only as an economic mechanism but as a social institution embedded in culture, caste, kinship and power. Adam Smith (The Wealth of Nations, 1776) described the market as guided by an invisible hand of self-interest, with the division of labour and free competition. Karl Marx saw capitalism as a social system in which workers sell labour power for wages, with the owner appropriating surplus value, and described commodity fetishism, the way social relations between people appear as relations between things. Weber and others studied how culture shapes economic life. Markets existed in traditional forms: the weekly haat or periodic tribal market is a social meeting place as well as a trading point. Caste and trade were linked: communities such as the Marwaris, the Nakarattar Chettiars and the Gujarati Banias were traditional trading castes who used kinship networks, trust and credit; the Marwaris rose in the colonial period in Calcutta. Gift exchange studied by Malinowski (the Kula ring of the Trobriand Islands) and Mauss shows that non-market exchange builds social bonds. Colonialism transformed markets: it integrated India in the world economy, as an exporter of raw cotton, opium and indigo and importer of manufactured goods, and caused the decline of handicrafts. Commodification means that things like labour, land and even culture become things for sale. After 1991 liberalisation, the market expanded with malls, brands and consumer credit. Consumption is socially significant: Bourdieu argued that tastes mark social class and form cultural capital, and the culture of consumption creates status through goods. Globalisation links local markets to global ones, and the informal sector and street vendors continue. The sociological view shows that the market is not neutral and that economic action depends on social relations.
## Deep dive: Perspectives
Smith’s invisible hand, Marx’s capitalism and surplus value, and the sociological idea that markets are embedded in society.
## Deep dive: Traditional markets
Weekly haats and trading castes like the Marwaris and Chettiars show social bases of exchange.
## Deep dive: Change
Colonialism, liberalisation and consumer culture transformed markets and commodified more of life.
