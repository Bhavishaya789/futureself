# Social Institutions: Continuity and Change
minutes: 40
yt: class 12 sociology social institutions caste family marriage kinship tribe sanskritisation jajmani
@study
Social institutions are established ways of organising social life, including caste, tribe, family, marriage and kinship. Caste is a system of hierarchical, hereditary groups. Ghurye listed six features: segmental division, hierarchy, restrictions on feeding and social intercourse, civil and religious disabilities and privileges, lack of unrestricted choice of occupation and restrictions on marriage (endogamy). Varna is the fourfold classification (Brahmin, Kshatriya, Vaishya, Shudra) and jati is the actual local caste group. Concepts of purity and pollution (Dumont) underlie hierarchy. Colonialism altered caste through the census, land revenue and law. Post-independence, Article 17 abolished untouchability, but caste has changed rather than disappeared: M. N. Srinivas developed the concepts of Sanskritisation (a lower caste adopting upper caste rituals to rise in status, in his study of the Coorgs, 1952), westernisation and the dominant caste (numerically strong, owning land, with high political power). The jajmani system bound service castes to landowners in hereditary exchange. Caste is now linked with politics, and Beteille noted the weakening of caste in some areas of life. A tribe is a community with a common territory, language and culture, usually without a stratified caste structure; Scheduled Tribes are listed under Article 342, forming about 8.6 per cent of the population in 2011. Tribes are classified by their location, language and livelihood, and Particularly Vulnerable Tribal Groups need special care. The family is the basic unit; types include nuclear and joint families, patriarchal and matriarchal, patrilineal and matrilineal (the Khasi and Nair were matrilineal). Marriage forms: monogamy, polygamy (polygyny and polyandry), endogamy (marriage within a group), exogamy (outside it), hypergamy (marrying upwards) and hypogamy. Kinship ties are of blood (consanguineal) and marriage (affinal). Changes are shown by laws: the Hindu Marriage Act 1955, the Special Marriage Act 1954, the Dowry Prohibition Act 1961, the Child Marriage Restraint Act 1929 and the Prohibition of Child Marriage Act 2006.
## Deep dive: Caste
Hierarchical hereditary groups; Ghurye’s six features; Srinivas’s Sanskritisation and dominant caste.
## Deep dive: Tribe
A community of common territory, language and culture; Scheduled Tribes listed under Article 342.
## Deep dive: Family and marriage
Nuclear and joint, patrilineal and matrilineal; endogamy, exogamy, hypergamy and laws of reform.
