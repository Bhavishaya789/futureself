# Transport and Communication
minutes: 45
yt: class 12 geography transport communication roads railways waterways pipelines suez panama trans siberian internet
@study
Transport is the movement of people, goods and services from one place to another; communication is the transfer of information. Modes of transport: land (roads, railways, pipelines), water (inland waterways, sea routes and oceanic routes) and air. Roads are the most used for short and medium distances: metalled and unmetalled; the density of roads varies; the major national road networks are in North America and Europe; the Trans-Canada Highway (the longest national highway, 7821 km), the Pan-American Highway (connects Alaska to Chile) and the Trans-Siberian highway. Railways are suited for long distances and bulk goods: the Trans-Siberian Railway (Moscow to Vladivostok, about 9,332 km, the longest double-tracked electrified railway), the Canadian Pacific Railway (Vancouver to Montreal, 1886), the Union Pacific railway, and the Trans-Australian railway. Pipelines carry liquids and gases such as crude oil, natural gas and water; they have low running cost; the world has pipelines connecting oil fields to refineries and ports. Water transport has the cheapest cost for heavy goods: inland waterways: the Great Lakes-St Lawrence Seaway, the Rhine and Danube waterways, the Mississippi, and the Ganga in India. Sea routes: the Suez Canal (opened 1869 by Ferdinand de Lesseps, linking the Mediterranean Sea at Port Said with the Red Sea at Suez; shortens the Europe-Asia route) and the Panama Canal (opened 1914, linking the Atlantic Ocean with the Pacific Ocean; saves distance between the east and west coast of USA). Major ocean routes: the North Atlantic route (busiest), Mediterranean-Indian Ocean route, Cape of Good Hope route, South Atlantic route and North Pacific route. Air transport is the fastest and most costly; Europe, North America and Asia have dense air traffic; the busiest air route is the North Atlantic; global hubs include London, New York, Singapore. Communication: from early couriers and telegraph to telephone, radio, television, satellites, mobile phones and the Internet; satellite communication allows global links; the Internet and e-commerce have revolutionised communication and trade. Cyberspace. Time space convergence. Gateways and ports.
## Deep dive: Modes
Roads, railways, pipelines, waterways and airways each suit different goods and distances.
## Deep dive: Canals
The Suez and Panama canals shortened sea routes dramatically.
## Deep dive: Communication
Satellites, mobile phones and the Internet connect the world instantly and reshape trade.
