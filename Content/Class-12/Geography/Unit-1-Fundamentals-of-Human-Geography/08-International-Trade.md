# International Trade
minutes: 45
yt: class 12 geography international trade balance of trade WTO GATT regional trade blocs
@study
International trade is the exchange of goods and services among countries. Trade within a country is called internal or domestic trade. Exports are goods sent out and imports are goods brought in. Balance of trade is the difference between exports and imports; a favourable (surplus) balance occurs when exports exceed imports; an unfavourable (deficit) balance when imports exceed exports. Basis of trade: difference in national resources, population factors (size, cultural factors, and standard of living), stage of economic development (agricultural countries export raw materials and import manufactured goods), extent of foreign investment, and transport. Trade is of two types: bilateral (between two countries) and multilateral (among many countries). Trade in volume is dominated by manufactured goods but earlier by primary goods; major items include crude oil, minerals, machinery and cars. Trade in services is increasing. Free trade versus protection. World Trade Organisation (WTO): established on 1 January 1995, headquartered in Geneva, replacing GATT (General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade of 1947); it sets rules for trade and settles disputes. Regional trade blocs: European Union (EU), ASEAN, NAFTA/USMCA, SAARC/SAFTA, OPEC (cartel of oil exporters), MERCOSUR, APEC, BRICS. Benefits: market access and economic cooperation; problems: disputes and dependence. Gateways to international trade: ports such as Rotterdam, Singapore, Shanghai, Mumbai; dock; hinterland. Free ports. Tariffs and quotas are trade barriers. Trade between developed and developing countries has often been unequal. Trade and environment. The case for trade: comparative advantage, specialisation, access to goods and healthier competition. Reasons for trade restrictions: protecting infant industries. E commerce is a growing mode of trade. Case studies: Trade in oil, spices (historically), and software.
## Deep dive: Concepts
Exports, imports and the balance of trade measure a nation's trading position.
## Deep dive: Institutions
The WTO (successor to GATT, 1995, Geneva) regulates trade; regional trade blocs promote regional cooperation.
## Deep dive: Bases
Differences in resources, population, development stage and transport create trade.
