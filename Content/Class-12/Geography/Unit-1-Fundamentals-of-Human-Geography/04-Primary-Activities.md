# Primary Activities
minutes: 45
yt: class 12 geography primary activities hunting gathering pastoralism agriculture mining plantation shifting cultivation
@study
Primary activities are those that depend on the use of earth's resources such as land, water, vegetation, building materials and minerals: hunting, gathering, pastoral activities, fishing, forestry, mining and quarrying, and agriculture. People who work in them are called red-collar workers because of their outdoor, rural work. Hunting and gathering: the oldest economic activity. Gathering is practised in the cold high latitudes (northern Canada, northern Eurasia, southern Chile) and in low latitudes (Amazon basin, tropical Africa, northern Australia and the interior of Southeast Asia); it requires little capital and low technology and gives low yield per person. Pastoralism: nomadic herding is the rearing of animals by moving from place to place for fodder, e.g. in Sahara, Central Asia, Tibet, and the Arctic; transhumance is seasonal movement between pastures at different altitudes or latitudes, e.g. the Gujjars and Bakarwals of the Himalayas; commercial livestock rearing is ranching in the temperate grasslands. Agriculture: subsistence agriculture (crops are mostly consumed by the farmer's family) includes primitive subsistence farming (shifting cultivation or slash and burn, known as jhumming in northeast India, milpa in Central America, ladang in Malaysia and Indonesia, roça in Brazil; and nomadic herding), and intensive subsistence agriculture (wet rice cultivation dominant in monsoon Asia, and dry crop cultivation where rainfall is low). Commercial agriculture: extensive commercial grain cultivation (wheat in the prairies of North America, Pampas of Argentina, Steppes of Eurasia), mixed farming (Europe), dairy farming (Denmark, New Zealand), Mediterranean agriculture (citrus fruits, grapes, olives), market gardening and horticulture (Netherlands), plantation agriculture (tea, coffee, cocoa, rubber, sugarcane, bananas) and co-operative and collective farming. Mining: factors affecting mining: size and grade of deposit, accessibility, and technology and capital; methods are surface or open-cast mining (cheap, quick) and underground or shaft mining (for deep deposits, more risky). Fishing: major fishing grounds are the continental shelf areas (Grand Banks off Newfoundland, North Sea and the Northwest Pacific); Japan, China and Peru are leading countries. Forestry. Red collar workers.
## Deep dive: Hunting and herding
Gathering and nomadic herding are low technology activities; transhumance is seasonal vertical movement.
## Deep dive: Agriculture
Subsistence farming includes shifting cultivation and intensive farming; commercial farming includes plantations, grain and dairy farming.
## Deep dive: Mining and fishing
Open-cast and shaft mining depend on deposit depth; the continental shelf has rich fishing grounds.
