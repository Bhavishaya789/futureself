# Human Geography: Nature and Scope
minutes: 45
yt: class 12 geography human geography nature scope determinism possibilism neo determinism ratzel semple vidal
@study
Human geography studies the relationship between humans and the physical environment, the spatial distribution of human phenomena, and the way people and places interact. Definitions: Friedrich Ratzel defined it as the synthesis of physical geography and the study of human societies; Ellen C. Semple defined it as the study of the changing relationship between the unresting man and the unstable earth; Paul Vidal de la Blache defined it as the study of the relationship between the earth and the human beings it supports, a conception resulting from a more synthetic knowledge of the earth and its inhabitants. Nature: it studies the interrelationship between physical environment and socio-cultural environment made by humans. Naturalisation of humans: early humans were slaves of nature and adapted to it, worshipping its forces. Humanisation of nature: with technology, humans modified nature and created the cultural landscape. Environmental determinism (Ratzel and the Greek scholars) held that nature controls human activities; possibilism (Vidal de la Blache, Febvre) held that nature gives possibilities and humans choose among them; neo-determinism or stop-and-go determinism (Griffith Taylor) is a middle path: neither red lights (absolute determinism) nor green lights (absolute possibilism), but humans should follow the signals of nature and proceed with caution. Time-space compression has reduced distances through transport and communication technology. Fields and sub-fields: social geography, urban geography, political geography, population geography, settlement geography, economic geography (resource, agriculture, industry, marketing, tourism, international trade), historical geography, cultural geography, behavioural geography, medical geography and gender geography. Approaches in the growth of the discipline: early exploration and description, regional analysis, areal differentiation, spatial organisation (quantitative revolution, 1950s-60s), humanistic, radical and behavioural approaches (1970s), and post-modernism in the 1990s. Human geography is related to other social sciences such as sociology, economics, anthropology and political science. Scope: study of population, resources, development, settlements, activities and their location.
## Deep dive: Definitions
Ratzel, Semple and Vidal de la Blache each stressed the relationship between humans and the earth.
## Deep dive: Determinism and possibilism
Nature controls (determinism) versus humans choose (possibilism); neo-determinism is the middle path.
## Deep dive: Fields
Human geography has many sub-fields, from social and urban to medical and behavioural geography.
