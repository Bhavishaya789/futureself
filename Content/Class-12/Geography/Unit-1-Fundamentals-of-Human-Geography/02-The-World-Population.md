# The World Population
minutes: 45
yt: class 12 geography world population distribution density growth demographic transition composition
@study
The study of population is the study of the number, distribution, growth and composition of people. Distribution: the world population is unevenly distributed; about 90 percent lives on about 10 percent of the land. Density of population is the number of persons per square kilometre. Arithmetic density is total population divided by total area; physiological density is population divided by net cultivable area; agricultural density is the number of agricultural workers per unit of cultivated land. Factors influencing distribution: (1) geographical factors: availability of water, landforms (plains attract settlement, mountains do not), climate (extreme climates repel), and soils (fertile alluvial soils attract); (2) economic factors: minerals, urbanisation and industrialisation; (3) social and cultural factors: religious or cultural importance, and political factors such as government policy. Population growth is the change in the number of people over time; growth rate is expressed as a percentage. Natural increase = births - deaths; actual growth = births - deaths + in-migration - out-migration. Positive growth occurs when births exceed deaths or when people migrate in; negative growth when deaths exceed births or people migrate out. Components of change: fertility (birth rate), mortality (death rate) and migration. Doubling time is the number of years a population takes to double. Trends: the world population was about 1 billion around 1804 and has risen rapidly since the Industrial Revolution and advances in medicine. Demographic transition theory describes three stages: Stage 1 high fertility and high mortality, slow growth; Stage 2 high fertility with declining mortality, rapid growth; Stage 3 low fertility and low mortality, slow or zero growth. Population control: family planning, awareness, education and health care. Population composition covers age-sex structure (shown by age-sex pyramids), sex ratio, literacy, occupational structure, and rural urban composition. Sex ratio is the number of females per 1000 males. Age groups: children (0-14), working age (15-59) and aged (60+). Dependency ratio. Malthus argued that population grows faster than food supply.
## Deep dive: Distribution and density
Water, landforms, climate and soils plus economic and cultural factors decide where people live.
## Deep dive: Growth
Growth = natural increase plus net migration; demographic transition has three stages.
## Deep dive: Composition
Age-sex pyramids, sex ratio, literacy and occupation describe the quality of population.
