# Secondary Activities
minutes: 45
yt: class 12 geography secondary activities manufacturing industries location weber classification high technology
@study
Secondary activities change raw materials into more valuable products: manufacturing, processing and construction. People who work in them are called blue-collar workers. Manufacturing is the production of goods through processing of raw materials; it is the backbone of economic development and reduces dependence on agriculture. Factors affecting location: market, raw materials, labour supply, sources of energy, transport and communication, government policy, agglomeration economies and cost of land. Weber's least cost theory: industries are located where the total cost of production is minimum, considering transport cost, labour cost and agglomeration. Classification by size: household or cottage industries, small scale industries, large scale industries. By inputs or raw materials: agro-based (cotton, woollen, jute, silk, rubber, sugar, tea, coffee, edible oil), mineral-based (iron and steel, cement, aluminium, machine tools, petrochemicals), chemical-based (petrochemical, salt, fertilisers, plastic), forest-based (pulp and paper, furniture, lac), and animal-based (leather, woollen). By output: basic or key industries (iron and steel) and consumer industries (sugar, soap). By ownership: public sector (e.g. SAIL, BHEL), private sector (TISCO, Reliance), joint sector (Oil India), cooperative sector. Footloose industries are not tied to raw materials or markets, e.g. IT industry and diamond cutting. Industrial regions: Ruhr in Germany, Great Lakes in the USA, Mumbai-Pune and Hugli in India. High technology industry is characterised by research and development, a highly skilled workforce, and automation; examples are Silicon Valley (California) and Silicon Plateau (Bengaluru). Technopolis. Industrial pollution and conservation. Manufacturing in world regions: Europe, North America, East Asia. Cotton textile, iron and steel, and fertilisers are major examples. Agglomeration economies are benefits of locating near other firms. Industrial landscape and urbanisation.
## Deep dive: Location
Raw materials, market, labour, power and transport decide where industries locate; Weber stressed least cost.
## Deep dive: Classification
Industries are classified by size, raw material, output and ownership.
## Deep dive: High technology
High-tech and footloose industries depend on skills and research rather than raw materials.
