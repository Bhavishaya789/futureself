# Human Settlements
minutes: 45
yt: class 12 geography human settlements rural urban patterns functional classification megacity conurbation
@study
A human settlement is a cluster of dwellings of any type or size where people live; it may be permanent or temporary. Settlements are classified as rural or urban on the basis of size, occupation and functions: rural settlements are small and depend largely on primary activities like agriculture; urban settlements are larger and have secondary and tertiary activities. Rural-urban differences also include density and way of life. Patterns of rural settlements: compact or nucleated (houses built close together, e.g. in fertile plains), dispersed or isolated (scattered houses in hilly or forest areas), and sub-types by shape: linear (along a road, railway or river), rectangular, circular, star-like (roads converging), T-shaped, Y-shaped, cross-shaped, double village. Factors: water supply, terrain, climate, defence, culture. Rural settlements have problems of inadequate infrastructure, poor sanitation and water. Urban settlements are classified by size: town (a small urban settlement), city (a larger urban centre of at least one lakh people in Indian usage), million city (one million), conurbation (a continuous urban area formed by the merging of towns and cities, a term coined by Patrick Geddes), megalopolis (a super metropolitan region, term used by Jean Gottman for the northeastern urban region of the USA), and megacity (over 10 million). Functional classification of towns: administrative (capital cities like Delhi, Washington D.C., Canberra), trading and commercial centres, industrial (Pittsburgh, Jamshedpur), mining (Broken Hill, Dhanbad), military or garrison (cantonment towns), cultural or religious (Varanasi, Jerusalem, Mecca), residential, transport hubs (ports like Rotterdam), recreational and tourist (Miami, Shimla). Urban pattern and structure; urbanisation is the rise of the share of population living in towns; world urban population is growing and exceeding rural; problems: overcrowding, slums, pollution, traffic and shortage of water; solutions include planning, smart growth. Rural-urban continuum and rurban areas. Primate city. Satellite towns. The first city with a million people was London around 1800. Cities such as Tokyo and Delhi are among the most populous.
## Deep dive: Rural patterns
Compact, dispersed, linear, rectangular, star-like and other patterns arise from site and cultural factors.
## Deep dive: Urban size
Towns, cities, million cities, conurbations, megalopolises and megacities rank settlements by size.
## Deep dive: Functions
Towns may be administrative, industrial, mining, cultural, transport or tourist centres.
