# Planning and Sustainable Development
minutes: 45
yt: class 12 geography planning sustainable development target area hill area indira gandhi canal bharmaur niti aayog brundtland
@study
Planning is the process of deciding how to use resources to achieve development goals. Types: sectoral planning (for sectors like agriculture, industry), and regional or spatial planning (for areas that lag behind). India began planning in 1951 through five-year plans by the Planning Commission, which was replaced by the NITI Aayog in 2015. Target area planning: concentrates on backward areas, e.g. Command Area Development, Drought Prone Area Programme (DPAP) and Hill Area Development Programme. Hill area development programme started in the Fifth Five Year Plan (1974) for 15 districts of Uttar Pradesh, Himachal Pradesh, Assam, West Bengal and Tamil Nadu; the Western Ghats Development programme (1981) and the Tribal sub-plan (1974) are also notable. Drought prone area programme covers semi-arid regions, with watershed development and afforestation. Bharmaur region of Himachal Pradesh: an example of tribal area development; Gaddis, a tribal community, live here; its Integrated Tribal Development Project started in 1974; it improved literacy and increased female literacy; roads, schools and health were developed. Sustainable development, as defined by the Brundtland Commission in Our Common Future (1987), is development that meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs. It involves economic, social and environmental dimensions. Indira Gandhi Canal Command Area (earlier known as the Rajasthan Canal) is among the largest canal projects, with water from the Sutlej-Beas (Harike barrage) flowing to western Rajasthan; two stages; Stage I is in Ganganagar, Hanumangarh and Bikaner (northern part) and Stage II covers Bikaner, Jaisalmer, Barmer and Jodhpur; its Command Area Development includes land levelling, afforestation, shelter belts and pasture development, and cropping pattern reform; problems include waterlogging and salinity due to excess irrigation; recommended sustainability measures: proper use of water, afforestation and pasture development, eco-friendly farming and crop diversification. Concepts: economic disparities, balanced regional development.
## Deep dive: Planning
Sectoral and regional planning aim to reduce disparities; target area planning focuses on backward areas.
## Deep dive: Case studies
The Indira Gandhi Canal and Bharmaur show planning in a desert and in a tribal hill region.
## Deep dive: Sustainability
Sustainable development meets present needs without harming future generations.
