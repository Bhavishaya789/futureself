# India: Population and Migration
minutes: 45
yt: class 12 geography india population census 2011 density sex ratio literacy migration push pull
@study
According to Census 2011, the population of India was 1,210.9 million (about 17.5 percent of the world population), with a density of 382 persons per sq km. Uttar Pradesh is the most populous state; Sikkim and Lakshadweep have the smallest populations among states and UTs; Bihar has the highest density among states (1106 per sq km) while Arunachal Pradesh has the lowest (17); among UTs, NCT of Delhi has the highest density. The decadal growth rate during 2001-2011 was 17.7 percent (slowest since 1951-61). Growth rate was highest in Dadra and Nagar Haveli among UTs and Meghalaya among states; Kerala and Nagaland recorded low growth. Sex ratio is the number of females per 1000 males: India's sex ratio in 2011 was 943; Kerala had the highest (1084) and Haryana the lowest among states (879); Daman and Diu had the lowest among UTs. Child sex ratio (0-6 years) was 919. Literacy: 74.04 percent in 2011 (male 82.14, female 65.46); Kerala had the highest literacy (93.91 percent) and Bihar the lowest (63.82 percent). Age composition: children 0-14, working age 15-59, aged 60 and above; India has a young population with a large working age share. Rural-urban composition: about 68.8 percent rural and 31.2 percent urban in 2011. Occupational structure: primary (agriculture and mining), secondary and tertiary; a high share depends on agriculture. Adolescents (10-19 years) form about 21 percent. Population problems: high birth rate, poverty, low literacy. Population policy: Family Planning started in 1952; the National Population Policy 2000 aims to bring down the total fertility rate to replacement level and achieve stable population; Adolescents as targets. Migration is the movement of people from one place to another. Types: internal (within the country) and international (outside); interstate and intrastate. Streams: rural-rural (mostly women migrate after marriage), rural-urban, urban-urban, urban-rural. Causes: push factors (poverty, unemployment, natural disasters, poor living conditions) and pull factors (better employment, education, health and services). Consequences: economic (remittances), demographic (age and sex selectivity), social (cultural change, integration) and environmental (slums, pressure on cities, depletion of resources). Remittances from abroad are a big source of foreign exchange.
## Deep dive: Census 2011
Population of 1210.9 million, density 382, sex ratio 943, literacy 74.04 percent.
## Deep dive: Composition
Young age structure, mostly rural, with high dependence on agriculture.
## Deep dive: Migration
Push and pull factors drive rural-rural, rural-urban and other streams, with economic and social consequences.
