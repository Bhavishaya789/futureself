# India: Human Settlements
minutes: 45
yt: class 12 geography india human settlements rural clustered dispersed urban towns metropolitan functional classification
@study
In India, settlements are classified as rural or urban. Rural settlements are found in villages. Types of rural settlements: clustered settlements (compact, closely built houses, in the fertile plains of north India, Ganga plain and the fertile alluvial tracts), semi-clustered settlements (partly clustered, found in Gujarat plains and parts of Rajasthan), hamleted settlements (the settlement is divided into several units physically separated but with a common name, found in Ganga plain, Chhattisgarh and lower valleys of the Himalayas), and dispersed or isolated settlements (small hamlets or single houses on hill slopes, in the Himalayan, northeastern, Western Ghats and dry areas such as Rajasthan). Factors for rural settlement patterns: water availability, relief, defence and culture. Urban settlements: the Census defines urban places as all places with a municipality, corporation, cantonment board or notified town area committee (statutory towns) and all other places which satisfy these criteria: a minimum population of 5000, at least 75 percent male working population in non-agricultural pursuits and a density of at least 400 persons per sq km (census towns). Urban centres are grouped by population into six classes: Class I (1,00,000 and above), II (50,000-99,999), III (20,000-49,999), IV (10,000-19,999), V (5,000-9,999) and VI (below 5,000). A million city has over 1 million, and a megacity has more than 10 million. Evolution of towns: ancient (Varanasi, Prayag, Pataliputra, Madurai), medieval (Delhi, Hyderabad, Jaipur, Lucknow: forts and temples), modern (British: Mumbai, Chennai, Kolkata as colonial ports and Chandigarh as a planned city). Functional classification: administrative towns and cities (Chandigarh, New Delhi, Bhopal), industrial towns (Mumbai, Salem, Coimbatore, Bhilai), transport cities (Kandla, Kochi, Visakhapatnam), commercial towns (Kolkata, Satna), mining towns (Raniganj, Jharia, Ankleshwar), garrison cantonment towns (Ambala, Jalandhar, Mhow, Babina), educational towns (Roorkee, Pilani, Varanasi), religious and cultural towns (Varanasi, Madurai, Puri, Amritsar, Ajmer), tourist towns (Nainital, Udhagamandalam, Mount Abu). Problems of urban settlements: overcrowding, slums, water shortage, lack of electricity, transport, sewerage, pollution and crime. Rural settlements face problems of poor infrastructure and sanitation. Population by size; The 2011 census shows 31.16 percent urban.
## Deep dive: Rural types
Clustered, semi-clustered, hamleted and dispersed settlements relate to terrain and water.
## Deep dive: Urban definition
Statutory towns have municipalities; census towns satisfy 5000, 75 percent non-agri male workers and 400 density.
## Deep dive: Functions and problems
Towns serve administrative, industrial, transport, mining, military and religious functions and face crowding and slums.
