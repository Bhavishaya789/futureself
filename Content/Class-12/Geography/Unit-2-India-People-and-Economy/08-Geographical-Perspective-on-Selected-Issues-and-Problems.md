# Geographical Perspective on Selected Issues and Problems
minutes: 45
yt: class 12 geography environmental pollution urban waste slums land degradation delhi yamuna
@study
This chapter looks at four major problems from a geographical perspective: environmental pollution, urban waste disposal, urbanisation and slums, and land degradation. Environmental pollution is the contamination of the environment by harmful substances. Types: air pollution (from vehicles, industries, burning of fuels; affects health and causes acid rain, global warming and ozone depletion), water pollution (from industrial effluents, sewage and agricultural run-off; affects rivers like the Ganga and Yamuna), soil pollution, noise pollution and solid waste. Causes include rapid urbanisation, industrialisation and population growth. Control: cleaner fuels such as CNG, emission standards, treatment plants, afforestation, public awareness. Ganga Action Plan was started in 1985 to clean the river; the Yamuna Action Plan followed. The Namami Gange programme is a more recent mission. Pollutants from industry and cities flow to rivers. Urban waste disposal: solid waste from households, commercial establishments and industries (municipal solid waste); growing amounts due to consumption; problems: unscientific disposal in open dumps, health hazards, groundwater contamination by leachate, and air pollution from burning. Solutions: segregation at source, recycling, composting, waste to energy and sanitary landfills; the three Rs reduce, reuse and recycle. Urbanisation and slums: the rapid growth of cities leads to slums, unauthorised colonies and informal settlements for the urban poor; slums are densely crowded areas with poor housing, inadequate water and sanitation, and are located near factories, railway lines and open drains. Problems of slums: health, crime, exploitation of residents, substance abuse and child labour. Government approaches: slum improvement, Jawaharlal Nehru National Urban Renewal Mission, housing for all and in-situ rehabilitation. Land degradation: deterioration of land quality due to soil erosion, waterlogging, salinisation, deforestation, overgrazing and mining; areas such as Punjab, Haryana and Rajasthan face waterlogging and salinisation; wastelands need reclamation. Remedies: afforestation, check dams, contour ploughing, shelter belts, crop rotation, organic manure. Sustainable use of land. Education, awareness, and policy.
## Deep dive: Pollution
Urban and industrial growth pollute air, water and soil; clean fuels and treatment plants help.
## Deep dive: Waste and slums
Solid waste and slums grow with urbanisation; segregation, recycling and housing improvement are solutions.
## Deep dive: Land degradation
Erosion, waterlogging and salinity degrade land; afforestation and soil conservation restore it.
