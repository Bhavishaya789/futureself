# Manufacturing Industries
minutes: 45

@study
Manufacturing is the backbone of development: it reduces dependence on agriculture, creates jobs, earns foreign exchange and removes regional disparities. The contribution of manufacturing to GDP in India is about 17 percent (2011-12), well below the 25 percent target in the National Manufacturing Policy 2011. Location factors: raw material, labour, capital, power, market, transport and government policy. Industries are classified by raw material (agro-based, mineral-based), role (basic and consumer), capital investment (small scale: investment up to a limit), ownership (public, private, joint, cooperative) and bulk and weight of raw material and finished goods. Agro-based industries: cotton textiles (Mumbai, Ahmedabad; first mill at Fort Gloster near Kolkata in 1818, and the first successful mill at Mumbai in 1854; Mumbai was called the Cottonopolis of India; India is a large exporter of yarn), jute textiles (West Bengal, along the Hugli; the first mill in 1855 at Rishra), sugar (shifting from north India to Maharashtra and Karnataka due to higher sucrose content and a longer crushing period; cooperative mills), silk and woollen. Mineral-based industries: iron and steel (the basic industry; first successful plant at Jamshedpur by TISCO in 1907; others: IISCO at Burnpur, Bhilai, Rourkela, Durgapur, Bokaro, Visakhapatnam, Salem, Bhadravati; the plants are near raw materials, mostly Chotanagpur plateau), aluminium (bauxite; Odisha, Kerala, Uttar Pradesh), cement (limestone, silica, gypsum and coal; the first plant in Chennai in 1904), and chemical industries such as fertilisers (nitrogenous, phosphatic and potash; Green Revolution demand), petrochemicals and pharmaceuticals. Knowledge-based industries: information technology (Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Noida, Pune, Chennai), electronics and software parks. Industrial regions: Mumbai-Pune, Hugli, Bengaluru-Tamil Nadu, Gujarat, Chotanagpur, Vishakhapatnam-Guntur, Gurugram-Delhi-Meerut, Kollam-Thiruvananthapuram. Industrial pollution: air, water, noise; control measures include treating effluents, filters and recycling of water, use of cleaner fuels. Special Economic Zones (SEZs). Make in India (2014).
## Deep dive: Importance
Manufacturing creates jobs, adds value and promotes growth.
## Deep dive: Major industries
Cotton textiles, jute, sugar, iron and steel, fertilisers, cement and IT are key industries.
## Deep dive: Regions and pollution
Clusters concentrate in industrial regions; effluent control is vital.
