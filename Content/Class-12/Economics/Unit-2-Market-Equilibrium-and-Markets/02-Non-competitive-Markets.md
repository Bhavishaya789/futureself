# Non-competitive Markets
minutes: 45
yt: class 12 economics monopoly monopolistic competition oligopoly price discrimination collusion
@study
Non competitive (imperfect) markets include monopoly, monopolistic competition and oligopoly. Monopoly: a single seller, no close substitutes and barriers to entry (patents, legal licence, control over resources, economies of scale). The monopolist is a price maker. Its demand (AR) curve slopes downward and MR lies below AR; MR is twice as steep as AR for a straight line. Equilibrium: MC = MR with MC cutting MR from below; the firm can earn supernormal profit even in the long run because of barriers. Price discrimination is charging different prices for the same good to different buyers (first, second, third degree). Natural monopoly occurs where economies of scale make one firm cheapest; public utilities. Monopolistic competition: a large number of sellers, differentiated products (brands), free entry and exit, selling costs (advertising), and each firm has some control over price; the demand curve is downward sloping and relatively elastic; short run supernormal profit or loss; long run normal profit through entry and exit, with price equal to AC but above MC and output below the minimum AC point (excess capacity). Examples: toothpaste, soaps, restaurants, clothes. Oligopoly: a few large firms, interdependence in decisions, barriers to entry, product homogeneous or differentiated, price rigidity (kinked demand curve) and non-price competition. Collusion: firms agree on price or output, as a cartel (e.g. OPEC); collusion is illegal in many countries; non-collusive oligopoly leads to price wars. Duopoly: two sellers. Comparison: perfect competition has P=MC and efficient allocation, monopoly has P>MC and deadweight loss, and monopolistic competition has excess capacity. Features compared in the table: number of firms, product, entry, price control, selling cost and demand elasticity. Government regulation: competition law and the Competition Commission of India (CCI) prevent anti-competitive practices.
## Deep dive: Monopoly
One seller with barriers to entry faces a downward sloping demand curve and maximises profit at MC = MR.
## Deep dive: Monopolistic competition
Many firms sell differentiated products; advertising matters and long run profit is normal.
## Deep dive: Oligopoly
A few interdependent firms; kinked demand explains rigid prices; collusion forms cartels.

## Advanced
P: Explain the kinked demand curve.
A: Firms expect rivals to match price cuts but not price rises, so demand is elastic above the kink and inelastic below it, making price rigid.
R: 2 | Price cut matched
R: 2 | Price rise ignored
