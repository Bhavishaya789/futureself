# Market Equilibrium
minutes: 45
yt: class 12 economics market equilibrium demand supply shift excess demand excess supply
@study
Market equilibrium occurs when quantity demanded equals quantity supplied at a price called the equilibrium price; the quantity is the equilibrium quantity. At a price above equilibrium there is excess supply (surplus), which pushes the price down; at a price below there is excess demand (shortage), which pushes the price up. The market clears at equilibrium. Effects of shifts in demand with fixed supply: an increase in demand raises both the equilibrium price and quantity; a decrease lowers both. Shifts in supply with fixed demand: an increase in supply lowers the price and raises the quantity; a decrease raises the price and lowers the quantity. Simultaneous shifts: both demand and supply increase: the quantity rises, price may rise, fall or stay depending on relative changes; demand increases and supply decreases: price rises, quantity uncertain, and so on. Numerical problems: solve Qd = a - bP and Qs = c + dP by equating. For the firm with a fixed number of firms, equilibrium is determined by market forces; with free entry and exit the number of firms changes in the long run. Price ceiling at below equilibrium creates shortage; floor above equilibrium creates surplus. Applications: minimum support price in agriculture creates surplus stocks, rent control reduces housing supply. Consumer surplus is the difference between what a consumer is willing to pay and the price paid; producer surplus is the difference between the price received and the minimum acceptable price. Efficiency: equilibrium maximises total surplus. Other things equal (ceteris paribus), a change in price causes a movement along the curve, not a shift. Demand and supply schedules, curves and the market clearing mechanism show the invisible hand of Adam Smith.
## Deep dive: Equilibrium
Equilibrium price equates demand and supply. Excess supply pushes price down; excess demand pushes it up.
## Deep dive: Shifts
Demand rightwards raises price and quantity; supply rightwards lowers price but raises quantity.
## Deep dive: Control
A binding ceiling creates shortage; a binding floor creates surplus.
