# Theory of Consumer Behaviour
minutes: 45
yt: class 12 economics consumer equilibrium utility indifference curve budget line demand elasticity
@study
Utility is the want satisfying power of a commodity. Total utility (TU) is the sum of utility from all units; marginal utility (MU) is the addition to TU from one more unit: MU = change in TU / change in quantity, or MUn = TUn - TUn-1. Law of diminishing marginal utility: as consumption of a good increases, MU from each additional unit falls, other things equal; TU rises at a decreasing rate while MU is positive, is maximum when MU is zero and falls when MU is negative. Consumer equilibrium in the one commodity case: MU of the good (in money terms) equals the price: MUx/Px = MU of money. In the two commodity case: MUx/Px = MUy/Py. Indifference curve approach: an indifference curve shows combinations of two goods that give equal satisfaction; an indifference map is a set of curves, with higher curves indicating more satisfaction. Properties: slope downward, convex to the origin (diminishing marginal rate of substitution, MRS), do not intersect, higher curve gives higher utility. MRS is the amount of one good the consumer gives up for one more unit of the other. Budget set: all bundles affordable with income M at prices P1 and P2: P1x1 + P2x2 <= M. The budget line P1x1 + P2x2 = M has slope -P1/P2 (the price ratio); a rise in income shifts it parallel outward; a price change rotates it. Consumer equilibrium (IC approach): where the budget line is tangent to the highest attainable indifference curve; MRS = P1/P2, and the IC is convex at that point. Demand: the quantity a consumer is willing and able to buy at a price in a given time. Law of demand: other things equal, price and quantity demanded are inversely related. Demand curve slopes downward due to the substitution effect, income effect and diminishing marginal utility. Determinants: price, income, prices of related goods (substitutes and complements), tastes and expectations. Normal goods: demand rises with income; inferior goods: demand falls with income. Movement along the curve is a change in quantity demanded (price changes); shift of the curve is a change in demand (other factors). Price elasticity of demand Ed = (percentage change in quantity demanded) / (percentage change in price); percentage method; total expenditure method: if price falls and expenditure rises, Ed>1; if unchanged, Ed=1; if it falls, Ed<1. Types: perfectly elastic (infinite), perfectly inelastic (zero), unitary, greater and less than one. Determinants: availability of substitutes, nature of the good (necessity versus luxury), proportion of income spent, time period and number of uses.
## Deep dive: Utility
Diminishing marginal utility explains the downward sloping demand curve. A consumer is in equilibrium when MUx/Px = MUy/Py.
## Deep dive: Indifference curves
Equilibrium is the tangency of the budget line and an indifference curve, where MRS equals the price ratio.
## Deep dive: Demand and elasticity
Demand falls as price rises; elasticity measures responsiveness, measured by percentage or total expenditure method.
