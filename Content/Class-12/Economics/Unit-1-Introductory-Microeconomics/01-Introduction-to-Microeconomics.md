# Introduction to Microeconomics
minutes: 45
yt: class 12 economics microeconomics scarcity opportunity cost production possibility curve central problems
@study
Economics studies how individuals, firms and governments choose to use scarce resources having alternative uses to satisfy unlimited wants. Microeconomics studies individual units (consumers, firms, markets); macroeconomics studies aggregates such as national income, inflation and unemployment. Scarcity arises because resources are limited while wants are unlimited, so choice is unavoidable. Opportunity cost is the value of the next best alternative given up. Economic activities: production, consumption and distribution. Positive economics describes what is; normative economics says what ought to be. Central problems of an economy: what to produce, how to produce and for whom to produce. What to produce concerns the choice and quantity of goods (consumer goods or capital goods); how to produce concerns the technique (labour intensive or capital intensive); for whom concerns distribution of income. Economic systems: capitalist (market economy; private ownership; profit motive; price mechanism), socialist (state ownership; planning) and mixed (both sectors; India). Production Possibility Frontier (PPF) or curve shows the maximum combinations of two goods an economy can produce with given resources and technology. Properties: slopes downward (more of one means less of the other), concave to the origin because the marginal opportunity cost (marginal rate of transformation, MRT) increases as more of a good is produced. Points on the curve: efficient full employment; points inside: unemployment or inefficiency; points outside: unattainable now. A rightward shift of the PPF results from growth of resources or improvement of technology; a leftward shift from loss of resources. Marginal opportunity cost = loss of output of one good / gain of output of the other. Factors of production: land (rent), labour (wages), capital (interest) and entrepreneurship (profit). Goods: free versus economic goods, consumer versus capital goods, final versus intermediate goods. Assumptions of ceteris paribus (other things equal).
## Deep dive: Scarcity and choice
Limited resources against unlimited wants force choice, and every choice has an opportunity cost.
## Deep dive: Central problems
Every economy must decide what, how and for whom to produce.
## Deep dive: PPF
The PPF is concave because the opportunity cost of producing more of a good rises; shifts show growth in resources or technology.
