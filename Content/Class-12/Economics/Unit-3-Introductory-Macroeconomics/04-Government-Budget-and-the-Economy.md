# Government Budget and the Economy
minutes: 45
yt: class 12 economics government budget revenue expenditure fiscal deficit primary deficit taxes
@study
The government budget is an annual statement of estimated receipts and expenditure of the government for a financial year (April 1 to March 31), presented under Article 112 of the Constitution; the Union Budget is presented by the Finance Minister. Objectives: reallocation of resources, redistribution of income and wealth, economic stability and growth, and management of public enterprises. Components: revenue account and capital account. Revenue receipts neither create a liability nor reduce assets: tax revenue (direct taxes such as income tax and corporation tax, indirect taxes such as GST and customs) and non-tax revenue (interest, dividends, fees, fines). Capital receipts create a liability or reduce assets: borrowings, recovery of loans and disinvestment (sale of shares of public sector enterprises). Revenue expenditure does not create assets (salaries, interest payments, subsidies, grants); capital expenditure creates assets or reduces liabilities (roads, buildings, loans given, repayment of debt). Planned and non-planned expenditure (older classification); developmental and non-developmental. Balanced budget: receipts = expenditure; surplus: receipts > expenditure; deficit: expenditure > receipts. Revenue deficit = revenue expenditure - revenue receipts. Fiscal deficit = total expenditure - total receipts excluding borrowing = borrowings; it shows the borrowing requirement. Primary deficit = fiscal deficit - interest payments; it shows the borrowing needed for expenditure other than interest. Effective revenue deficit. Measures of deficit: a high revenue deficit means the government is dissaving. Deficit financing: borrowing, printing money; consequences: inflation, debt burden and crowding out. Fiscal policy: taxation, expenditure and public debt. Taxes: direct (burden cannot be shifted) versus indirect (burden can be shifted); progressive, proportional and regressive taxes. GST replaced many indirect taxes in 2017. The Fiscal Responsibility and Budget Management (FRBM) Act 2003 targets deficit reduction. Subsidy, public goods (non-rival and non-excludable), merit goods and externalities justify government spending. Debt: internal and external; debt service. Budget multiplier.
## Deep dive: Receipts and expenditure
Revenue items do not alter assets or liabilities; capital items do. Both receipts and expenditure are split this way.
## Deep dive: Deficits
Revenue deficit, fiscal deficit and primary deficit measure different gaps; fiscal deficit equals borrowing.
## Deep dive: Fiscal policy
The government uses taxes, spending and borrowing to allocate resources, redistribute income and stabilise the economy.
