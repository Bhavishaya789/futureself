# Open Economy Macroeconomics
minutes: 45

@study
An open economy trades goods, services and assets with other countries. Balance of payments (BoP) is a systematic record of all economic transactions between residents of a country and the rest of the world in a year. Components: the current account records trade in goods (visible items, balance of trade), trade in services (invisible items), income and transfers; the capital account records capital flows: foreign direct investment (FDI), foreign portfolio investment (FPI), external borrowing and assistance, and changes in reserves. Balance of trade (BOT) = exports of goods - imports of goods; a surplus is favourable and a deficit unfavourable. Current account deficit shows excess of imports (including invisibles) over exports. BoP always balances in accounting terms because of double entry book keeping; autonomous items (taken for profit, independent of BoP position) versus accommodating items (taken to settle a deficit); BoP deficit = autonomous receipts less than payments, financed by selling foreign exchange reserves or borrowing. Foreign exchange: foreign currencies; the exchange rate is the price of one currency in terms of another. Systems: fixed exchange rate (the government fixes the rate, the Bretton Woods system until 1971; devaluation and revaluation), flexible or floating (determined by demand and supply in the foreign exchange market; appreciation and depreciation), and managed floating (the central bank intervenes; the system India uses). Determination: the demand for foreign exchange comes from imports, foreign travel, gifts and investment abroad and slopes downward; supply comes from exports, foreign investment inflows and remittances and slopes upward; equilibrium where they meet. A rise in the exchange rate (more rupees per dollar) is a depreciation of the rupee, making exports cheaper and imports dearer; appreciation does the opposite. Nominal exchange rate versus real exchange rate = e x P*/P. Purchasing power parity. Foreign exchange reserves held by the RBI. Devaluation is a deliberate lowering of the rate in a fixed system. Merits of flexible rate: automatic adjustment; demerits: instability. Speculation. Spot and forward markets.
## Deep dive: Balance of payments
The current account holds trade, services, income and transfers; the capital account holds investment and borrowing.
## Deep dive: Exchange rate
The rate is determined by demand and supply of foreign exchange; depreciation makes exports cheaper and imports costlier.
## Deep dive: Systems
Fixed, flexible and managed float differ in central bank intervention; India follows managed floating.

P: Differentiate between BoT and BoP.

## Advanced
P: Explain the effect of rupee depreciation.
A: More rupees per dollar make Indian exports cheaper and imports costlier, so exports tend to rise and imports to fall.
