# Indigo
minutes: 45

@study
Indigo by Louis Fischer, from his book The Life of Mahatma Gandhi, describes Gandhi's first satyagraha in India, in Champaran, Bihar, in 1917. At the Congress session in Lucknow in December 1916, a poor peasant named Rajkumar Shukla from Champaran asked Gandhi to come to his district, where peasants were forced under the tinkathia system to grow indigo on three twentieths (3/20) of their land for their British landlords. When the Germans invented synthetic indigo, the landlords demanded compensation from peasants to release them from the contract, and the peasants now wanted their money back. Shukla followed Gandhi to Cawnpore, Ahmedabad and Calcutta, and finally Gandhi went with him to Patna, where he was received at the house of Rajendra Prasad, whose servants took him for a peasant. In Muzaffarpur, Gandhi met Professor J. B. Kripalani and lawyers who explained the situation, and then proceeded to Motihari. The commissioner, Morehead, ordered him to leave Tirhut; Gandhi refused and was summoned to appear in court. Thousands of peasants gathered around the courthouse; the government was baffled. Gandhi pleaded guilty, saying he disobeyed out of a higher duty, the voice of conscience; the judge postponed the sentence, and finally the case against Gandhi was withdrawn. Gandhi, assisted by lawyers including Rajendra Prasad, Brij Kishore, Mazharul Haq and others, collected the evidence from thousands of peasants. The Lieutenant Governor invited him and set up an inquiry commission with Gandhi as a member. Gandhi agreed to a settlement in which the landlords refunded only 25 percent of the money they had illegally extracted; he accepted it, saying the amount was not important but that the landlords had surrendered part of their prestige. Within a few years the British planters left, and indigo cultivation vanished. Gandhi also engaged in social work: schools, health work, and he insisted that people be self-reliant; he told C. F. Andrews not to stay, saying that the battle should be fought by Indians themselves, so that the peasants would learn courage and self-reliance. Themes: civil disobedience, non-violence, courage, self-reliance, leadership, justice for the poor. The title refers to the dye plant and the colonial exploitation tied to it.
## Deep dive: Champaran
The tinkathia system forced peasants to grow indigo; Gandhi investigated, was ordered to leave, defied the order and won an inquiry.
## Deep dive: Methods
Non-violent civil disobedience, collecting evidence and winning public support made the government yield.
## Deep dive: Self-reliance
Gandhi told Andrews to leave because freedom must be earned by Indians; this freed the peasants from fear.
