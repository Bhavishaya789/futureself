# Deep Water
minutes: 45
yt: class 12 english flamingo deep water william douglas swimming fear ymca summary
@study
Deep Water is an extract from Of Men and Mountains by William Douglas, a US Supreme Court Justice. It describes his fear of water and how he overcame it. As a child of about three or four, he went with his father to the beach in California; the waves knocked him down and he was frightened by the experience; he felt choked, terrified and watched his world fade. This childhood experience left him with an abiding fear of water. At the age of ten or eleven, he decided to learn to swim at the Yakima YMCA pool. One day he went alone, and a big boy, thinking it a joke, threw him into the deep end. He sank to the bottom, struggled, and decided to spring upward from the bottom to the surface; he planned to jump with a surge and use the strength of his legs, but he came up with water in his mouth and could not move forward. He was panicked, he swallowed water, lost consciousness, then woke to find the boy and lifeguard had pulled him out. The experience left him with a terrifying fear of water; it kept him away from canoeing, fishing and swimming, and made him feel that he had missed out on the pleasure of camping and the outdoors. At first he thought of a way out and hired an instructor who trained him over several months: the instructor held a belt around his waist and a rope through a pulley, and asked him to put his face in the water, blow bubbles, kick and stretch his legs. Gradually he learned to swim in the pool using the instructor's training, overcoming his fear bit by bit; but still the terror returned when he swam alone in the lake, until he conquered it fully in Lake Wentworth, New Hampshire. At Lake Wentworth, he swam across the lake and felt the terror return in the middle but he screamed to himself, told his legs to swim and finally reached the shore; he realised that I had conquered fear; he learned that the only thing to fear is fear itself, a line of Roosevelt which he recalls. The theme: fear, determination, will power, perseverance and the training required to overcome it. Style: autobiographical, vivid descriptions of panic, suspense, short sentences in moments of danger.
## Deep dive: The fear
A near drowning at the YMCA pool created an enduring terror of water that reached into adult life.
## Deep dive: The training
A patient instructor used a rope and belt, with drills in breathing, kicking and floating, building confidence step by step.
## Deep dive: The lesson
Will power and practice overcome fear; the only thing to fear is fear itself.
