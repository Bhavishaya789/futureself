# The Rattrap
minutes: 45
yt: class 12 english flamingo the rattrap selma lagerlof summary edla willmansson ironmaster
@study
The Rattrap by Selma Lagerlof (Swedish Nobel laureate) tells of a peddler who made and sold small rattraps of wire. As his business did not support him, he also begged and stole to survive. One day he thought that the whole world was a rattrap: it offers riches, joy, shelter and food as bait, and once someone is tempted to touch the bait, it closes in and everything ends. A kindly old crofter gives him shelter for the night and entertains him with stories and a game of cards; he mentions the 30 kronor he received from the creamery for his cow's milk and shows the peddler the money. In the morning when the crofter left for work, the peddler stole the money from the leather pouch hanging on the window frame, believing himself clever. Fleeing, he went into the forest, lost his way, and realised that he was caught in a rattrap himself, like a rat, wandering round and round. In the evening he heard hammering from an ironworks, found a blacksmith who allowed him to sleep by the furnace, and met the ironmaster, who mistook him for an old regimental comrade, Captain von Stahle. He invited him home, but the peddler refused. The ironmaster's daughter Edla Willmansson persuaded him to come for Christmas; the peddler, who feared being arrested, was warmly welcomed; Edla treated him with kindness, even when the ironmaster discovered that he was not the captain. When the ironmaster threatened to call the sheriff, Edla insisted he be allowed to stay for Christmas, saying that we should extend a kindness in the holiday season. The peddler left, taking a gift of Christmas present: a suit of clothes. Later Edla received a rattrap in a package from the peddler with 30 kronor and a letter in which he wrote that he had been raised from a rat to a captain by her kindness, and returned what he had stolen. He signed himself as Captain von Stahle. Themes: human goodness, kindness and trust transform a person; the world is a rattrap of temptation; Christmas spirit and compassion; individuals can reform; the distinction between appearance and reality. Style: allegory, irony, symbolism, third person narrative, humour.
## Deep dive: The rattrap metaphor
The world is a rattrap: baits of riches, shelter and food lure people to their ruin; the peddler himself is caught.
## Deep dive: Edla
Her kindness, understanding and trust draw out the peddler's goodness; she is unlike her pragmatic father.
## Deep dive: Transformation
The peddler returns the stolen money and signs as Captain von Stahle; kindness reforms.
