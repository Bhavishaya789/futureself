# Poets and Pancakes
minutes: 45

@study
Poets and Pancakes is an extract from the memoir My Years with Boss by Asokamitran, who worked at Gemini Studios in Madras (now Chennai), a leading Indian film studio of the 1940s and 1950s, owned by S. S. Vasan, whom the staff called the Boss. The title joins two ideas: Pancake, a brand of make-up used in huge quantities in the studio's make-up department, and the poets who were drawn to the studio. The make-up department was crowded with people from different regions, and the narrator describes them with gentle satire. The emblem of Gemini was a pair of twins blowing bugles, and the studio's gate carried it. The narrator describes several characters: the Legal Adviser, a lawyer in a khadi dhoti who seldom had any real legal work; and Kothamangalam Subbu, Vasan's talented right-hand man, called Number Two, a poet who headed the story department and wrote verse. The story department was a group of writers who looked after the scripts. The studio had visitors: a group from the Moral Re-Armament (MRA) movement staged shows there, and later the English poet Stephen Spender, editor of the magazine Encounter, visited; the studio staff did not know who he was, and the narrator, who was an ordinary employee, had to explain English poetry to the staff. The extract is a humorous account of how the film world, busy with commerce, treated art, poets and writers. Themes: art versus commerce, the gap between creative people and the film industry, and humour. Style: anecdotal, ironic first person narration with satire.

A busy film studio whose departments and staff are described with gentle satire.
## Deep dive: The Boy and the Legal Adviser
Characters like the Boy (Subbu) and the Legal Adviser show the odd mix of art and bureaucracy in the studio.
## Deep dive: Poets and film
The visits of MRA and of a poet show how little the film world understood poetry.
