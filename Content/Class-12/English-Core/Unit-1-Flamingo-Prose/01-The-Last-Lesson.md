# The Last Lesson
minutes: 45
yt: class 12 english flamingo the last lesson alphonse daudet summary franz m hamel
@study
The Last Lesson by Alphonse Daudet is set in Alsace, a French region that Prussia had taken after the Franco-Prussian War of 1870-71. The narrator, a schoolboy named Franz, is late for school and tempted to skip it and play outside on a bright, warm morning. On his way he passes the town hall, where a crowd reads the bulletin board that has carried bad news for two years. The blacksmith Wachter tells him not to hurry because he will get to school in time. At school, instead of the usual noise, there is silence. M. Hamel wears his best green coat, a frilled shirt and a black silk cap, which he wore only on inspection days and prize days. The back benches are occupied by village elders including old Hauser with his three-cornered hat, the former mayor and the postmaster. M. Hamel announces that an order from Berlin says only German will be taught in the schools of Alsace and Lorraine, and that this is his last French lesson. Franz is shocked and regrets that he had neglected his lessons; he realises he can hardly write French and that his books, which seemed a nuisance, are now like old friends. M. Hamel tells the class that the blame lies with everyone, parents who preferred to send children to work and himself who sent them to water his flowers and gave them a holiday to go fishing. He says that French is the most beautiful, clearest and most logical language, and that when a people are enslaved, as long as they hold fast to their language it is as if they had the key to their prison. The lesson on grammar is followed by reading, writing (copy slips reading France, Alsace), and a lesson in history. The old Hauser spells out the letters with the children, weeping. At the church clock striking twelve and the Prussian trumpets sounding, M. Hamel stands pale, tries to speak but chokes, takes chalk and writes in large letters Vive la France, then makes a gesture that school is dismissed. Themes: linguistic imperialism, patriotism, regret over lost opportunities and the value of education and mother tongue. Style: simple first person narrative, symbolism (the bulletin board, the green coat), irony (the pigeons that may be made to coo in German). Teacher and student relationship changes from fear to respect.
## Deep dive: Language and freedom
The story shows that language is bound to identity; losing it means losing freedom. M. Hamel calls it the key to the prison.
## Deep dive: Characters
Franz changes from a careless boy to a repentant learner; M. Hamel from stern teacher to an emotional patriot.
## Deep dive: Symbols
The bulletin board, the formal dress of M. Hamel and the Vive la France message symbolise loss, respect and resistance.
