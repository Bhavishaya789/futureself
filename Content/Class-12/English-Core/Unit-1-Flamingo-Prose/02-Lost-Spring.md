# Lost Spring
minutes: 45

@study
Lost Spring: Stories of Stolen Childhood by Anees Jung has two parts. Part I: Sometimes I find a Gold Coin describes Saheb-e-Alam, a ragpicker boy from Dhaka whose family left their home when storms swept away their fields and homes. He lives in Seemapuri, on the outskirts of Delhi, in a squatters settlement with about 10,000 ragpickers who came from Bangladesh in 1971, with no identity other than ration cards for food. For them, garbage is gold: it is a means of survival and for children it is wrapped in wonder (sometimes finding a rupee or even a ten rupee note in the heap). Saheb wears discarded tennis shoes and walks barefoot though he dreams of playing tennis, as the narrator remarks that she likes to see him play at the club. The writer offers to start a school, and he asks eagerly whether she will; the promise remains unkept. Saheb later finds work at a tea stall for 800 rupees and all the meals, but loses his carefree freedom: the steel canister he carries is heavier than the plastic bag he used to carry, and he is no longer his own master. Part II: I want to Drive a Motor Car describes Mukesh in Firozabad, the centre of the glass-blowing industry, where families have worked on bangles for generations. Children and parents work in dark hutments with high temperatures, unsafe furnaces, and poor ventilation, losing their eyesight. The caste system and the exploitation by middlemen, policemen, bureaucrats and politicians keep them trapped; the bangle makers say it is their karma (destiny) to be bangle makers. A girl named Savita is described as one who sits with her mother welding pieces of coloured glass into circles, and she is unaware that the bangles symbolise an Indian woman's suhaag. Mukesh insists on being his own master and dreams of becoming a motor mechanic, saying he will go to the garage which is a distance away from his home; the writer comments that dreams are in his eyes though bangle makers are afraid to dream. The theme: poverty, child labour, exploitation, lost childhood, the burden of tradition and the denial of the right to education. The title Lost Spring signifies the childhood that is stolen. Anees Jung's style is journalistic, reportage with description, irony and compassion. Related law: the Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education Act 2009 and the prohibition of child labour.
## Deep dive: Saheb
His life shows the poverty of migrant ragpickers; garbage is both survival and a dream, and employment ends his freedom.
## Deep dive: Mukesh and Firozabad
Generations of glass bangle makers are trapped by tradition, caste and middlemen, yet Mukesh dares to dream.
## Deep dive: Theme
Childhood is stolen by poverty and exploitation, and the state and society fail to protect it.
