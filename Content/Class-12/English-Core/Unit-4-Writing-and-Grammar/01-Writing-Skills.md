# Writing Skills
minutes: 45
yt: class 12 english core writing skills notice invitation reply letter article report speech advertisement format
@study
The Class 12 English Core writing section tests short and long compositions in set formats. Notice: a short formal announcement written in a box with the heading NOTICE, the name of the issuing organisation, the date, a title, the body (event, date, time, venue, contact) and the writer's name and designation; the limit is about 50 words; use passive voice and impersonal style. Poster and advertisement: a catchy slogan, key details, contact information, and an eye-catching layout; classified advertisements are compact and use abbreviations. Invitation and reply: formal invitations are in the third person with an impersonal style, giving host, occasion, date, time, venue and RSVP; replies accept or decline in the same third person style; informal invitations are in the first person. Letters: formal letters have sender's address, date, receiver's address, subject line, salutation (Sir or Madam), body, and complimentary close (Yours faithfully for Sir or Madam; Yours sincerely when a name is known) and signature. Types: letter to the editor (on a social problem or public issue; the writer addresses the editor, gives a heading, states the problem with facts and suggests solutions), job application with a CV or bio-data (covering letter and resume with career objective, education, experience, skills, references), business letters (letter of enquiry, order, complaint, reply to complaint), and letters seeking information from institutions. Article: 150-200 words with a title, a byline, an introduction, a body of two to three paragraphs and a conclusion; types: descriptive, argumentative and informative. Report: a factual account of an event with a title, reporter name and place and date line; it is written in the past tense and the passive voice, in a formal style; types: event reports and newspaper reports. Speech: begin with a salutation to the audience, introduce the topic, give arguments, appeal and end with thanks; use spoken style, rhetorical questions. Debate: for or against motion. Tips: plan and revise, follow word limits, use paragraphs, avoid slang and errors, and use cohesive devices such as therefore, moreover and however. Marking: format, content, expression and accuracy.
## Deep dive: Formats
Each form has its own layout and style; marks are given for format, content, fluency and accuracy.
## Deep dive: Letters
Formal letters need the sender's address, date, receiver's address, subject, salutation, body and complimentary close.
## Deep dive: Articles and reports
Articles have a headline, byline and structured paragraphs; reports use the past tense and passive voice.
