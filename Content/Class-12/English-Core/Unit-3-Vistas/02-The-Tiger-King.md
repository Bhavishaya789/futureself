# The Tiger King

@study
The Tiger King by Kalki (R. Krishnamurthy) is a satire on the vanity and arrogance of rulers and on blind belief in prophecy. The Maharaja of Pratibandapuram, Jung Jung Bahadur, was given the title the Tiger King after astrologers predicted, when he was only ten days old, that he would die by a tiger. The infant Maharaja himself declared that he would kill a hundred tigers to prove the astrologers wrong. When he became king, he began to hunt tigers; the Dewan and other officials praised him and made arrangements. He killed seventy tigers, and the state ran short of tigers; the Maharaja threatened officials who failed him. To meet his target he married into a princess's family that was famed for its tigers, and he continued until he had killed ninety nine. The hundredth tiger was hard to find; when at last an old, weak tiger was found, the Maharaja shot at it and missed, but the tiger fell dead from fright, and the Maharaja thought he had killed the hundredth tiger. His court celebrated, but a tiger in the story turned out to be the cause of his end: a wooden toy tiger bought for the Maharaja's three year old son as a birthday gift had a splinter that pierced his hand; the wound turned septic and the Maharaja died. The prophecy had come true in an unexpected way. Themes: the irony of fate, arrogance of power, absurdity of blind faith, cruelty to wildlife. Style: satire, exaggeration, humour and dramatic irony.
## Deep dive: Prophecy
The Maharaja is told he will be killed by a tiger, and so he wages war on tigers.
## Deep dive: Satire
The story mocks royal vanity, flatterers and the abuse of power.
## Deep dive: Irony
The hundredth tiger is only killed by fright; the Maharaja dies from a wooden toy tiger.
## Hindi saransh
The Tiger King: Maharaja Jung Jung Bahadur ki kahani, jo vyang aur niyati ki vidambana dikhati hai.
> Remember: a toy tiger, not a real one, kills the Tiger King.
