# The Third Level

@study
The Third Level by Jack Finney is a fantasy about a man who finds a hidden level at Grand Central Station in New York that leads into the past. The narrator, Charley, tells his psychiatrist friend Sam Weiner that Grand Central has a third level in addition to the two that everybody knows. One night, taking a wrong turn, he found himself on a level with old fashioned gas lamps, wooden ticket windows and men and women in clothes of the 1890s: it was the year 1894. He tried to buy tickets for Galesburg, Illinois, the quiet town of his childhood, to escape the world of worry and tension, but the clerk refused his modern money, which was larger than the notes then in use. Charley rushed out to exchange his money for old currency, but the amount he could manage was too little, and when he returned he could not find the third level again. Sam, the psychiatrist, called it a waking dream, a wish fulfilment of a man who wants to escape modern life. Charley's wife Louisa was worried by his search, but Sam later began to believe him. Charley and Sam both collected old currency, and Sam, a stamp collector, vanished. Later Charley found among his stamp collection a first day cover dated July 18, 1894, posted from Galesburg, Illinois, and addressed to him. It was a letter from Sam, saying he had found the third level, bought a ticket with old money, and settled in Galesburg, a pleasant, quiet place, and urged Charley and Louisa to join him. Themes: escape from the stress and insecurity of modern life, nostalgia for a peaceful past, fantasy and reality. Style: first person narration, conversational tone.
## Deep dive: The third level
An imaginary level of Grand Central leading to the year 1894 offers escape from modern stress.
## Deep dive: Sam and Charley
Sam first explains the third level as wishful dreaming, but then goes to the past himself, leaving a letter.
## Deep dive: Theme
The story is about escape from war, tension and insecurity to a quiet past.
