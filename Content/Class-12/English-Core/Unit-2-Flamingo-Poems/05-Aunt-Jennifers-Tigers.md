# Aunt Jennifer’s Tigers
minutes: 45
yt: class 12 english flamingo poem aunt jennifers tigers adrienne rich analysis
@study
Aunt Jennifer's Tigers is a poem by the American poet and feminist Adrienne Rich (1929-2012), written in 1951. It is a poem of three stanzas, written in rhyming couplets. Aunt Jennifer embroiders tigers on a panel: they prance across the screen, bright topaz denizens of a world of green. They do not fear the men beneath the tree; they pace in sleek chivalric certainty. The tigers are bold, confident, free and fearless, in sharp contrast to Aunt Jennifer, who is timid, fearful and tired, and whose fingers flutter through her wool as she works. She finds it hard to pull the ivory needle because her wedding ring sits heavily on her finger, the massive weight of Uncle's wedding band, which stands for the burden of marriage and male domination. When Aunt Jennifer is dead, her terrified hands will lie still, still ringed with ordeals she was mastered by, but the tigers she made will go on prancing, proud and unafraid. Themes: the oppression of women in marriage and patriarchy, the longing for freedom, the power of art and creativity as an escape and a legacy, contrast between the real and the imagined. Symbols: tigers (freedom, strength, courage, the free self she cannot be), the wedding band (oppression, male dominance), the needle (her creative effort). Devices: contrast, symbolism, imagery, metaphor, enjambment, rhymed couplets (aabb), irony in the tigers' fearlessness. Tone: sympathetic and critical. Message: women's creativity and the desire for freedom survive their suppression.
## Deep dive: Aunt Jennifer
Timid and burdened by marriage; she makes tigers that she can never be.
## Deep dive: Tigers
Symbols of freedom, strength and fearlessness; they remain proud after she dies.
## Deep dive: Ring
The wedding band symbolises male domination and the weight of marriage.
