# My Mother at Sixty-six
minutes: 45
yt: class 12 english flamingo poem my mother at sixty six kamala das analysis
@study
My Mother at Sixty-six is a poem by Kamala Das (also known as Madhavikutty and Kamala Surayya), a major Indian poet writing in English and Malayalam. The speaker is driving from her parents' home in Cochin to the Cochin airport to catch a flight to Delhi. Her mother sits beside her, sleeping or dozing with her mouth open. The speaker looks at her mother's face, which is pale and ashen like that of a corpse, and the sight makes her heart ache with the thought of losing her. To escape the painful thought she looks outside the car: she sees young trees which seem to be sprinting and merry children spilling out of their homes, contrasting with her mother's old age, that is, the vitality of youth against the decay of age. After the security check at the airport, she stands a few yards away from her mother and looks again at her face, which is pale, wan, like a late winter's moon. She feels the same old familiar ache and childhood fear of losing her mother. She puts on a smile and says goodbye with the words see you soon, Amma, hiding her pain, hoping that her mother will be fine. Themes: ageing, the fear of separation and death, mother daughter love, the contrast between old age and youth, helplessness. Poetic devices: simile (ashen like that of a corpse; pale as a late winter's moon), metaphor, personification (sprinting trees), imagery (visual), contrast, enjambment, repetition of the word smile. The poem is written in free verse with a conversational tone. The pale moon of winter stands for waning life and the smile conceals the pain.
## Deep dive: The scene
The car journey and airport parting set the poet's fear of losing her ageing mother against the energy of nature and children.
## Deep dive: Images
The corpse-like face and the late winter moon show age; sprinting trees and children show life.
## Deep dive: The smile
The poet's forced smile and goodbye hide her pain and hope for another meeting.
