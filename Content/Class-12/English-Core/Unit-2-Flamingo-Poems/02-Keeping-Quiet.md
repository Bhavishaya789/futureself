# Keeping Quiet
minutes: 45
yt: class 12 english flamingo poem keeping quiet pablo neruda analysis
@study
Keeping Quiet is a poem by the Chilean poet Pablo Neruda (translated into English), a Nobel laureate for Literature in 1971. The poet asks us to count up to twelve and then keep still. For once on earth, let us not speak in any language and not move our arms so much. It would be an exotic moment without rush, without engines, when all of us would be together in a sudden strangeness. The fishermen in the cold sea would not harm the whales, and the man gathering salt would look at his hurt hands. People who prepare green wars, wars with gas and fire, victories with no survivors, would put on clean clothes and walk about with their brothers in the shade, doing nothing. What the poet wants is not total inactivity: life is what it is about; he does not want to deal with death. He says that if we were not so single-minded in pursuing our aims and for once did nothing, a huge silence might interrupt this sadness of never understanding ourselves and of threatening ourselves with death. Perhaps the earth can teach us as when everything seems dead and later proves to be alive. Now the poet will count up to twelve and the reader should keep quiet while he goes. Themes: introspection, peace, brotherhood, the futility of war, the destruction of nature, the need for self-examination. Devices: repetition of counting, imagery of fishermen and whales (conservation), personification, irony (people can be at peace by doing nothing), contrast between activity and stillness, free verse, direct address in plural form (let us). Tone: persuasive, calm and yet urgent.
## Deep dive: Stillness
A moment of stillness will help humans recognise their unity and rethink war, greed and harm to nature.
## Deep dive: Not death
The silence he asks for is not death or inaction; it is a pause for introspection that leads to renewal, as in the earth in winter.
## Deep dive: Images
The whales, salt gatherer and war makers show harm to nature and fellow humans.
