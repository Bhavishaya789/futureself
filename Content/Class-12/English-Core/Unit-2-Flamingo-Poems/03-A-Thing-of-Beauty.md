# A Thing of Beauty
minutes: 45

@study
A Thing of Beauty is an extract from the long poem Endymion (1818) by the English Romantic poet John Keats. The poem begins with the idea that a thing of beauty is a joy for ever: its loveliness increases and never passes into nothingness; it will keep a bower of quiet for us and a sleep full of sweet dreams, health and quiet breathing. Therefore, every morning we are wreathing a flowery band to bind us to the earth, in spite of despondence, of the inhuman dearth of noble natures and of the gloomy days, all the unhealthy and dark ways that are cut for our search. Yet some shape of beauty moves away the pall from our dark spirits. Such are the sun, the moon, trees old and young, sprouting a shady boon for simple sheep, and daffodils in the green world they live in; and clear rills that for themselves make a cooling covert against the summer's heat; the mid-forest brake rich with a sprinkling of fair musk-rose blooms. Also there is the grandeur of the dooms we have imagined for the mighty dead; all lovely tales that we have heard or read: an endless fountain of immortal drink, pouring unto us from the heaven's brink. The poet says that beauty is our companion and a source of consolation in a world of suffering, and its power is not limited to the moment but also lives on in memory. Nature, tales of heroes and imagination feed the human spirit. Themes: the lasting power of beauty, nature as a healer, imagination and consolation in a troubled world. Devices: metaphor (flowery band; endless fountain of immortal drink), alliteration (sweet dreams, health and quiet breathing), personification (spirits), imagery of nature, rhyming couplets (heroic couplets) in iambic pentameter, rich diction. Tone: lyrical and celebratory. Keats also believed that beauty is truth.
## Deep dive: Beauty lasts
Beauty never fades into nothingness; it gives lasting joy, peaceful sleep and sweet dreams.
## Deep dive: Consolation
Nature, heroic stories and imagination are bonds to the earth that console us amid sorrow and dark days.
## Deep dive: Images
Sun, moon, trees, daffodils, rills and musk-rose blooms show the beauty of nature that heals.
