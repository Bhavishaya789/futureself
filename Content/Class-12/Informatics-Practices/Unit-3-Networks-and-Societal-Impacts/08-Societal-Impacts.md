# Societal Impacts: Digital Footprint, Cyber Safety, IPR and E-waste
minutes: 45
yt: class 12 informatics practices societal impacts digital footprint cyber crime it act ipr plagiarism e waste
@study
Digital technology has social and ethical impacts. A digital footprint is the trail of data left by online activity. An active footprint is data given deliberately, like posts or forms, while a passive footprint is collected without the user acting, like cookies or IP logs. Netiquette is courteous online behaviour. Cyberbullying is harassment online. Cyber crime includes hacking, phishing (fraudulent emails or sites to steal data), identity theft, ransomware, spam and eavesdropping. Safety measures include strong passwords, two-factor authentication, firewalls, antivirus and caution with links. The Information Technology Act, 2000, amended in 2008, is the main cyber law of India, recognising electronic records and digital signatures and defining cyber offences. Intellectual Property Rights (IPR) protect creations of the mind: copyright covers original literary, artistic and software works; patents protect inventions; trademarks protect brand identifiers. Plagiarism is presenting someone else’s work as one’s own. Software licences include proprietary and Free and Open Source Software (FOSS); the GNU General Public License and Creative Commons licences allow sharing under conditions. Electronic waste, e-waste, is discarded electronic devices, and it contains hazardous materials such as lead and mercury; the 3Rs of reduce, reuse and recycle apply. The digital divide is the gap between those with and without access to technology. E-governance delivers government services online, with examples like Aadhaar and DigiLocker. Health issues include eye strain and repetitive strain injury, and ergonomics helps. Data protection and privacy are growing concerns.
## Deep dive: Digital footprint and safety
Active and passive footprints persist; strong passwords and caution protect users.
## Deep dive: Cyber law and IPR
The IT Act 2000 defines cyber offences, and copyright, patents and trademarks protect creations.
## Deep dive: E-waste and divide
E-waste needs safe disposal, and the digital divide shows unequal access to technology.
