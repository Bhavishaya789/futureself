# Differential Equations
minutes: 45
yt: class 12 maths differential equations order degree variable separable homogeneous linear integrating factor
@study
A differential equation contains an independent variable, a dependent variable and derivatives. The order is the order of the highest derivative, and the degree is the power of the highest-order derivative after the equation is made free of radicals and fractions in the derivatives, defined only if it is a polynomial in the derivatives. For example, (d2y/dx2) + (dy/dx)^3 = 0 has order 2 and degree 1. A general solution contains arbitrary constants equal in number to the order, and a particular solution is obtained by giving values to the constants using initial conditions. To form a differential equation from a family of curves with n arbitrary constants, differentiate n times and eliminate the constants. Methods of solving first order, first degree equations: variable separable form dy/dx = f(x) g(y), solved by separating variables and integrating, i.e. integral of dy/g(y) = integral of f(x) dx + C. A homogeneous equation dy/dx = F(y/x) is solved by the substitution y = vx, so dy/dx = v + x dv/dx, which separates variables. A function f(x, y) is homogeneous of degree n if f(kx, ky) = k^n f(x, y). A linear first order equation has the form dy/dx + P y = Q, where P and Q are functions of x only. Its integrating factor is IF = e^(integral of P dx), and the solution is y (IF) = integral of Q (IF) dx + C. Another form is dx/dy + P1 x = Q1 with P1 and Q1 functions of y, with IF = e^(integral of P1 dy). Examples: dy/dx + y = e^(-x) has IF e^x. Population growth dP/dt = kP gives P = P0 e^(kt), radioactive decay and Newton’s law of cooling follow similar forms. Always check the order, degree and type before choosing a method.
## Deep dive: Order and degree
Order is the highest derivative order; degree is its power when the equation is polynomial in derivatives.
## Deep dive: Solving methods
Variable separable, homogeneous (y = vx) and linear (integrating factor) forms cover first-order equations.
## Deep dive: Applications
Growth, decay and cooling problems lead to simple first-order equations.
