# Integrals

@study
Integration is the inverse of differentiation. If d/dx F(x) = f(x), then the indefinite integral of f is F(x) + C, where C is the constant of integration. Standard integrals: x^n integrates to x^(n+1)/(n+1) + C for n not equal to -1; 1/x integrates to ln of modulus x + C; e^x to e^x; a^x to a^x/ln a; sin x to -cos x; cos x to sin x; sec^2 x to tan x; cosec^2 x to -cot x; sec x tan x to sec x; cosec x cot x to -cosec x; 1/(1 + x^2) to tan inverse x; 1/sqrt(1 - x^2) to sin inverse x; 1/(x sqrt(x^2 - 1)) to sec inverse x. Further: tan x integrates to ln of modulus sec x, cot x to ln of modulus sin x, sec x to ln of modulus (sec x + tan x), cosec x to ln of modulus (cosec x - cot x). Standard forms: 1/(x^2 - a^2) gives (1/2a) ln of modulus((x - a)/(x + a)), 1/(a^2 - x^2) gives (1/2a) ln of modulus((a + x)/(a - x)), 1/(x^2 + a^2) gives (1/a) tan inverse(x/a), and 1/sqrt(a^2 - x^2) gives sin inverse(x/a). Methods include substitution, integration by parts, and partial fractions. Integration by parts: integral of u v dx = u times integral of v dx minus integral of (u′ times integral of v dx) dx; choose u by ILATE (inverse, logarithmic, algebraic, trigonometric, exponential). The special result: integral of e^x (f(x) + f′(x)) dx = e^x f(x) + C. Partial fractions are used for rational functions by splitting into simpler fractions. The definite integral from a to b of f(x) dx is the limit of a sum and equals F(b) - F(a), the second fundamental theorem. The first fundamental theorem says d/dx of the integral from a to x of f(t) dt equals f(x). Properties: the integral from a to b equals minus the integral from b to a; the integral from a to b equals the sum of integrals over [a, c] and [c, b]; the integral from a to b of f(x) dx equals the integral from a to b of f(a + b - x) dx; the integral from 0 to a of f(x) dx equals the integral from 0 to a of f(a - x) dx; the integral from -a to a of f(x) dx is 2 times the integral from 0 to a for even f and 0 for odd f. Substitution changes limits for definite integrals.
## Deep dive: Indefinite integrals
Standard forms, substitution, parts (ILATE) and partial fractions give antiderivatives.
## Deep dive: Definite integrals
The fundamental theorem gives F(b) - F(a); properties simplify integrals.
## Deep dive: Symmetry
Even functions double the half-interval integral and odd functions give zero over symmetric limits.
