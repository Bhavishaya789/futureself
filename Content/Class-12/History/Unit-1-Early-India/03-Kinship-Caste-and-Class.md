# Kinship, Caste and Class: Early Societies
minutes: 45
yt: class 12 history theme 3 kinship caste and class mahabharata critical edition varna jati manusmriti
@study
This theme examines social norms and the Mahabharata. The Mahabharata, traditionally ascribed to Vyasa, was composed over centuries from about 500 BCE and attained about 100,000 verses. A critical edition was prepared at the Bhandarkar Oriental Research Institute, Pune, under V.S. Sukthankar between 1919 and 1966. It narrates a conflict between the Kauravas and Pandavas over the kingdom of Hastinapura. Kinship rules in Brahmanical texts favoured patriliny, with property passing from father to sons. Women were expected to marry outside their gotra (exogamy), which was assigned from about 1000 BCE. The Dharmasutras and Dharmashastras, including the Manusmriti, laid down norms of marriage and eight forms of marriage were listed. Endogamy within the jati and polygyny were practised, and rare polyandry appears in the case of Draupadi. Inscriptions of the Satavahanas show names that include the mother, a matronymic, as in Gotami-putra. The varna order placed Brahmanas, Kshatriyas, Vaishyas and Shudras in sequence and assigned duties to each, with those outside called untouchables or Chandalas. Texts such as the Manusmriti described these rules, and the Buddhist tradition challenged birth-based claims. Historians study the Mahabharata using many methods, noting later interpolations and changes. Characters such as Ekalavya and Hidimba illustrate attitudes towards forest peoples and outsiders. Gift-giving, dana, and different rules for property of women (stridhana) show ideals. The Chinese Buddhist pilgrim Fa-Hsien noted the lives of Chandalas.
## Deep dive: Mahabharata as a source
The epic grew over centuries, so historians separate its layers and treat it as a source for ideals more than events.
## Deep dive: Kinship and marriage
Patriliny, exogamy within gotras and eight forms of marriage shaped Brahmanical norms.
## Deep dive: Varna and jati
Texts such as the Manusmriti ranked society by varna and marginalised Chandalas; Buddhist ideas questioned this.
