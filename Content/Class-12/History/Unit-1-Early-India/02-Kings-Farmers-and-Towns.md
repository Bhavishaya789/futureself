# Kings, Farmers and Towns: Early States and Economies
minutes: 45
yt: class 12 history theme 2 kings farmers and towns mahajanapadas mauryas ashoka inscriptions
@study
Around the sixth century BCE, early states emerged in the Ganga plains. Sixteen Mahajanapadas were recorded, among them Magadha, Kosala, Vajji, Kuru, Panchala, Avanti and Gandhara. Magadha grew powerful through iron tools, fertile land, elephants and rivers; its capitals included Rajagriha and later Pataliputra. Rulers such as Bimbisara, Ajatashatru and Mahapadma Nanda expanded it. The Mauryan empire was founded by Chandragupta Maurya about 321 BCE and reached its peak under Ashoka. Sources include the Arthashastra, the Indica of the Greek ambassador Megasthenes, and inscriptions. Ashoka, after the Kalinga war about 261 BCE, adopted dhamma and appointed dhamma mahamattas. His edicts in Brahmi and Kharosthi were deciphered by James Prinsep in 1838. Administration at the centre involved the king, provincial governors and officials. Later states included the Satavahanas, Guptas and Pandyas. Prashastis such as the Prayaga Prashasti, composed by Harishena for Samudragupta, praised rulers, while the Junagadh inscription records the work of Rudradaman. Agricultural expansion used iron ploughs, irrigation and transplantation of paddy. Land grants to Brahmans and officials spread in the early centuries CE. Towns grew as centres of trade, crafts and administration. Mathura, Ujjayini, Arikamedu and Pataliputra were important. Punch-marked silver coins and later gold coins show an expanding money economy. Guilds or shrenis organised craftspeople. Roman trade brought gold to south India, as at Arikamedu.
## Deep dive: Mahajanapadas and Magadha
Magadha rose through resources, strong rulers and strategic capitals; the Mauryas then built an empire.
## Deep dive: Ashoka and dhamma
After Kalinga, Ashoka promoted dhamma through edicts and special officials, aiming at social harmony.
## Deep dive: Towns and trade
Coins, guilds, crafts and trade networks, including Roman links, supported urban growth.
