@study
The Great Stupa at Sanchi in Madhya Pradesh survived because it escaped the attention of treasure hunters and was protected by the begums of Bhopal, Shah Jehan Begum and her successor Sultan Jehan Begum, who funded its preservation. John Marshall wrote its major study. The stupa, associated with the emperor Ashoka in the third century BCE, enshrines relics of the Buddha. It was a mound of earth later covered with bricks and stone, with a railing, gateways (toranas) and sculptures narrating Jataka stories. The period around the sixth century BCE saw new thinkers. Siddhartha Gautama, the Buddha, was born at Lumbini, attained enlightenment at Bodh Gaya, gave his first sermon at Sarnath and died at Kushinagar. His teaching rests on the Four Noble Truths and the Eightfold Path leading to nirvana, and he rejected the idea of a permanent soul. Mahavira, the twenty-fourth Tirthankara of Jainism, stressed ahimsa. The Ajivikas were another school. Upanishadic thinkers debated the atman and Brahman. Buddhism later split into Theravada and Mahayana; the latter celebrated the Bodhisattva and image worship. Monasteries, viharas, and chaitya halls were built. Alongside, devotional Hinduism grew with worship of Vishnu and Shiva. Vishnu was believed to take avatars, and temples with a garbhagriha emerged; examples include the Dashavatara Temple at Deogarh from the Gupta period and the rock-cut Kailashnatha Temple at Ellora. Historians use sculpture, inscriptions and texts, but must understand local traditions to read them correctly.
## Deep dive: Sanchi and preservation
Sanchi survived through luck and care by the begums of Bhopal; its gateways narrate Jataka stories.
## Deep dive: Buddhism and Jainism
Buddhist teachings stress the Four Noble Truths and the Eightfold Path; Jainism stresses ahimsa.
## Deep dive: Temples and devotion
Devotional worship of Vishnu and Shiva led to temple building with a sanctum, garbhagriha.
