# Bricks, Beads and Bones: The Harappan Civilisation
minutes: 45

@study
The Harappan or Indus Valley Civilisation flourished in the north-west of the subcontinent. Its Mature phase is dated roughly 2600 to 1900 BCE, preceded by Early Harappan cultures and followed by Late Harappan ones. Harappa, in modern Pakistan, was first noticed by Charles Masson in the 1820s; its systematic excavation began in 1921 under Daya Ram Sahni, while Mohenjodaro was excavated from 1922 under Rakhal Das Banerji. Alexander Cunningham, the first Director-General of the Archaeological Survey of India, had earlier visited Harappa; John Marshall, Director-General from 1902, publicised the discovery in 1924. Mortimer Wheeler later introduced stratigraphic methods. Major sites include Harappa, Mohenjodaro, Chanhudaro, Kalibangan, Lothal, Banawali, Rakhigarhi and Dholavira. Many settlements were divided into a smaller, higher citadel and a larger, lower town. Mohenjodaro had the Great Bath, a large tank made of baked bricks lined with bitumen. Drains were covered, and houses were built around courtyards. Bricks followed a standard ratio of 1:2:4. Granaries, standard weights made of chert, seals of steatite with an undeciphered script, and beads of carnelian show organised production and trade. Copper came from Khetri in Rajasthan, lapis lazuli from Shortughai in Afghanistan, and shell from Nageshwar and Balakot. Contacts with Mesopotamia, which called the region Meluhha, are attested; Dilmun (Bahrain) and Magan (Oman) were intermediaries. Craft production involved bead-making at Chanhudaro and Lothal. Farmers grew wheat, barley, pulses, peas, sesame and cotton; ploughs are shown by terracotta models and a field furrow at Kalibangan. Social differences are debated: burials show few differences, but some goods were rare. Archaeologists infer religion from seals, figurines and structures; the so-called Pashupati seal and the Mother Goddess figurines are interpreted with caution. The civilisation declined around 1900 BCE, with proposed causes including climate change, river shifts and deforestation.
## Deep dive: Planning and architecture
Citadel and lower town, covered drains, baked-brick houses and the Great Bath point to strong civic planning.
## Deep dive: Trade and craft
Seals, weights, beads, copper and lapis lazuli show long-distance trade with Mesopotamia and Central Asia.
## Deep dive: Sources and interpretation
Archaeologists interpret seals, burials, figurines and structures, but the script is undeciphered, so conclusions stay tentative.
