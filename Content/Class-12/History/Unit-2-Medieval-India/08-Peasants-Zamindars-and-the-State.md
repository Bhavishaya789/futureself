# Peasants, Zamindars and the State: Agrarian Society and the Mughal Empire
minutes: 45
yt: class 12 history theme 8 peasants zamindars and the state ain i akbari abul fazl mughal land revenue
@study
In the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries, about 85 percent of the population lived in villages. The chief source for agrarian history is the Ain-i-Akbari, compiled by Abul Fazl in 1598 as part of the Akbarnama. It records revenue, crops, and social groups. Peasants were called raiyat or muzarian. There were khud-kashta peasants, who cultivated in their own village, and pahi-kashta, who cultivated land elsewhere. Land was abundant and labour scarce, and cultivation expanded with new crops from the Americas such as maize, chilli and tobacco, alongside jins-i-kamil or perfect crops like cotton and sugarcane. Villages had panchayats headed by the muqaddam or mandal, and jati panchayats regulated caste. The jajmani relationship tied artisans to peasants through service. Zamindars held hereditary rights and collected revenue; they often had forts and armed retainers and were intermediaries between peasants and the state. The land revenue system aimed at assessment (jama) and collection (hasil). Todar Mal, Akbar’s revenue minister, introduced the dahsala system based on average yields and prices over ten years. Measurement (zabt) was used in many regions; Akbar’s revenue was often one-third of the produce. Silver flowed into India through trade, supporting the money economy. Officials such as the amin, patwari and qanungo recorded land, and jagirdars held revenue assignments tied to their mansabs. Pastoralists and forest peoples like the Bhils also interacted with the state. Peasants sometimes resisted through flight and revolt.
## Deep dive: Village society
Peasants, panchayats and the jajmani system shaped village life under Mughal rule.
## Deep dive: Zamindars and revenue
Zamindars collected revenue as intermediaries, while the state assessed and collected it through officials.
## Deep dive: Sources
The Ain-i-Akbari gives statistics, but it reflects the view of the imperial court.
