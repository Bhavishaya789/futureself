# Through the Eyes of Travellers: Perceptions of Society
minutes: 45
yt: class 12 history theme 5 through the eyes of travellers al biruni ibn battuta bernier kitab ul hind
@study
Travellers left accounts of the subcontinent that historians use with care. Al-Biruni, born in Khwarizm in Central Asia, came to India with the army of Mahmud of Ghazni, and wrote the Kitab-ul-Hind in Arabic around the eleventh century. He learned Sanskrit, studied texts such as Patanjali, and was critical of the caste system, comparing it with social divisions elsewhere. He wrote about astronomy, religion, and customs. Ibn Battuta, from Tangier in Morocco, travelled widely and reached India in 1333. He served as a qazi at the court of Muhammad bin Tughlaq in Delhi. His account, the Rihla, written in Arabic and compiled around 1355, describes cities, the postal system (the horse post uluq and the foot post dawa), and practices like sati. He found Indian cities thriving and the agrarian economy productive. Francois Bernier, a French physician, lived in India between 1656 and 1668 and was associated with Dara Shikoh and later a Mughal noble. His Travels in the Mogul Empire compared India with Europe and argued that the absence of private property in land harmed Mughal agriculture, a view modern historians question. Other travellers include Abd al-Razzaq, who visited Vijayanagara, and Peter Mundy, Jean-Baptiste Tavernier and Niccolao Manucci. Travellers observed trade, cities, crafts, women and slavery, but wrote from their own backgrounds, so their accounts reflect biases. Historians cross-check with other sources.
## Deep dive: Al-Biruni
Al-Biruni used Sanskrit sources to write the Kitab-ul-Hind and criticised the caste system.
## Deep dive: Ibn Battuta
Ibn Battuta described Delhi, cities and the postal system in the Rihla after serving the Tughlaq court.
## Deep dive: Bernier
Bernier compared India with Europe and argued crown ownership of land caused decline, a claim historians dispute.
