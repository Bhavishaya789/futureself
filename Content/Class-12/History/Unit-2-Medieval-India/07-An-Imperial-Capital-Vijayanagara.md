# An Imperial Capital: Vijayanagara
minutes: 45
yt: class 12 history theme 7 vijayanagara hampi krishnadeva raya amara nayaka mahanavami dibba
@study
Vijayanagara, meaning city of victory, was the capital of an empire founded in 1336 by Harihara and Bukka of the Sangama dynasty on the Tungabhadra river in Karnataka. Its ruins at Hampi were brought to attention in 1800 by Colin Mackenzie, a British engineer and antiquarian, who prepared the first survey map. Hampi was declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1986. The empire reached its peak under Krishnadeva Raya of the Tuluva dynasty, who ruled from 1509 to 1529. Rulers called themselves raya and shared power with the amara-nayakas, military chiefs who held land (amaram) in return for troops and tribute. The empire was defeated at the Battle of Talikota in 1565 by the combined forces of the Deccan sultanates, after which the capital declined. The city was spread over a sacred centre along the river and a royal centre in the south-west. The royal centre contained about sixty temples and palaces, including the Lotus Mahal, the Mahanavami Dibba, the Hazara Rama Temple and elephant stables. The Virupaksha temple and Vitthala temple, with its stone chariot, are major religious structures. Water was managed through tanks and canals, and fortification walls enclosed cultivated land. Foreign visitors, including Abd al-Razzaq from Persia, Domingo Paes and Duarte Barbosa from Portugal, and Fernao Nuniz, described the city, its markets and festivals such as Mahanavami. Rulers depended on trade in horses and spices through ports on the west coast.
## Deep dive: Rise and fall
Founded in 1336, Vijayanagara peaked under Krishnadeva Raya and fell after Talikota in 1565.
## Deep dive: Amara-nayaka system
Military chiefs held land in return for troops and tribute, strengthening and also straining the empire.
## Deep dive: Capital city
The sacred and royal centres, temples, tanks and fortifications show planning and royal ritual.
