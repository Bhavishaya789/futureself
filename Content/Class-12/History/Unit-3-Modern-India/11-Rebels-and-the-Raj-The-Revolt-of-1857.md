# Rebels and the Raj: The Revolt of 1857
minutes: 45
yt: class 12 history theme 11 rebels and the raj revolt of 1857 mangal pandey rani lakshmibai bahadur shah zafar
@study
The Revolt of 1857 began at Meerut on 10 May 1857 when sepoys rose against their officers, marched to Delhi and proclaimed the Mughal emperor Bahadur Shah Zafar as their leader. It spread through north and central India, including Lucknow, Kanpur, Jhansi and Bihar. Causes included the British policy of annexation: the Doctrine of Lapse devised by Lord Dalhousie annexed states such as Satara and Jhansi, and Awadh was annexed in 1856 on grounds of misgovernment, deposing Nawab Wajid Ali Shah. The Subsidiary Alliance by Wellesley had earlier weakened states. Economic and social discontent included heavy revenue, loss of zamindari rights, and interference with customs. The immediate trigger was the introduction of the Enfield rifle with greased cartridges, rumoured to contain cow and pig fat, which offended Hindu and Muslim sepoys. Mangal Pandey of Barrackpore had acted in March 1857. Leaders included Rani Lakshmibai of Jhansi, Nana Sahib at Kanpur, Begum Hazrat Mahal at Lucknow, Kunwar Singh in Bihar and Tatya Tope. The rebels used rumours, prophecies and symbols such as the chapati. The British under Governor-General Canning suppressed the revolt; Delhi was recaptured in September 1857, and Bahadur Shah was exiled to Rangoon. The Government of India Act of 1858 transferred power from the Company to the Crown, and Queen Victoria’s proclamation promised respect for rights and religions. Historians use official reports, rebel proclamations, letters, paintings and memoirs, which carry different perspectives.
## Deep dive: Causes
Annexation, the Doctrine of Lapse, revenue pressures and the greased cartridge combined to cause the revolt.
## Deep dive: Leaders and spread
Bahadur Shah, Rani Lakshmibai, Nana Sahib, Begum Hazrat Mahal and Kunwar Singh led resistance in different regions.
## Deep dive: Aftermath
The Company’s rule ended in 1858; the Crown took over and promised religious non-interference.
