# Mahatma Gandhi and the Nationalist Movement: Civil Disobedience and Beyond
minutes: 45
yt: class 12 history theme 13 mahatma gandhi nationalist movement champaran non cooperation dandi quit india
@study
Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi returned from South Africa in 1915 and was guided by Gopal Krishna Gokhale. In 1916 he spoke at the Banaras Hindu University opening. His early satyagrahas were at Champaran in 1917 for indigo peasants, at Ahmedabad in 1918 for mill workers, with Anasuya Sarabhai, and at Kheda in 1918 for peasants hit by crop failure. The Rowlatt Act of 1919 allowed detention without trial, and Gandhi called a nationwide hartal. On 13 April 1919 General Dyer fired on a crowd at Jallianwala Bagh in Amritsar. Gandhi linked the Khilafat cause with swaraj and launched the Non-Cooperation Movement in 1920, calling for boycott of courts, schools, councils and foreign cloth. After a mob burned a police station at Chauri Chaura in February 1922, he withdrew the movement. The Lahore session of 1929 under Jawaharlal Nehru adopted the goal of Purna Swaraj, and 26 January 1930 was observed as Independence Day. The Salt Satyagraha began with the Dandi March on 12 March 1930 from Sabarmati to Dandi, where he broke the salt law on 6 April; it began the Civil Disobedience Movement. The Gandhi-Irwin Pact of 1931 led Gandhi to the Second Round Table Conference. The Poona Pact of 1932 between Gandhi and B.R. Ambedkar settled on reserved seats instead of separate electorates for the depressed classes. The Government of India Act 1935 led to provincial elections in 1937. The Cripps Mission of 1942 failed, and the Quit India Movement was launched on 8 August 1942 with the call Do or Die. Historians use Gandhi’s writings, newspaper reports, police records and private letters to study the movement.
## Deep dive: Early satyagrahas
Champaran, Ahmedabad and Kheda established Gandhi as a mass leader before 1920.
## Deep dive: Mass movements
Non-Cooperation, Civil Disobedience and Quit India mobilised diverse groups across India.
## Deep dive: Compromises
The Gandhi-Irwin Pact and Poona Pact show negotiation alongside protest.
