# Colonialism and the Countryside: Exploring Official Archives
minutes: 45
yt: class 12 history theme 10 colonialism and the countryside permanent settlement ryotwari santhals deccan riots
@study
After the Battle of Plassey in 1757 and the grant of diwani of Bengal, Bihar and Orissa in 1765, the East India Company sought a regular revenue. The Permanent Settlement of 1793, introduced by Lord Cornwallis in Bengal, fixed the revenue to be paid by zamindars permanently. Zamindars who failed to pay on time lost their estates under the sunset law, so many estates were auctioned. Jotedars, rich peasants, gained influence in villages. The Company expanded cultivation into hill areas. The Paharias of the Rajmahal hills lived by shifting cultivation and hunting, and were disturbed by settlers; Santhals, who came as cultivators, were settled in the Damin-i-Koh area in 1832, but were exploited by moneylenders and revolted in 1855-56 under Sido and Kanhu. In western India, the Ryotwari system, introduced by Thomas Munro in Madras and also in Bombay, settled revenue directly with the cultivator, with periodic revision. In north India, the Mahalwari system, devised by Holt Mackenzie in 1822, settled revenue with village communities. The American Civil War (1861-65) cut cotton supplies to Britain and caused a cotton boom in the Deccan, followed by a crash and heavy debt. In 1875 peasants in Poona and Ahmednagar attacked sahukars, an event called the Deccan Riots. The Deccan Riots Commission and the Deccan Agriculturists Relief Act of 1879 followed. Official archives such as revenue records, survey reports and Buchanan’s surveys are used by historians, but must be read for official bias.
## Deep dive: Revenue settlements
Permanent Settlement, Ryotwari and Mahalwari systems differed in who paid revenue and how often it was revised.
## Deep dive: Tribal and peasant revolts
Paharias, Santhals and Deccan peasants resisted exploitation by settlers, zamindars and moneylenders.
## Deep dive: Archives
Official records are useful but reflect the colonial viewpoint, so historians read them with care.
