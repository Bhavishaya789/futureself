# Framing the Constitution: The Beginning of a New Era
minutes: 45
yt: class 12 history theme 15 framing the constitution constituent assembly ambedkar objectives resolution
@study
The Constituent Assembly was formed in 1946 under the Cabinet Mission plan, with members elected indirectly by provincial assemblies, and met first on 9 December 1946. Rajendra Prasad was elected its president. The Assembly at first had about 300 members; after Partition it was reduced to about 299. The Muslim League members from areas that became Pakistan left, and Dr. B.R. Ambedkar was elected chairman of the Drafting Committee in August 1947. B.N. Rau served as the constitutional adviser. Jawaharlal Nehru moved the Objectives Resolution on 13 December 1946, which declared India an independent sovereign republic and promised justice, equality and freedom; it was adopted on 22 January 1947. The Assembly took two years, eleven months and eighteen days, with eleven sessions. The Constitution was adopted on 26 November 1949 and came into effect on 26 January 1950. Members debated key issues: separate electorates, which were opposed by many members after Partition; the powers of the Centre and states, with a strong Centre favoured; language, with the Munshi-Ayyangar formula settling on Hindi in Devanagari script as the official language with English continuing for fifteen years; and fundamental rights, which Sardar Patel and others discussed in committees. Women members included Hansa Mehta and Durgabai Deshmukh, and Jaipal Singh spoke for adivasis. The Assembly drew on the Government of India Act 1935 and models from other countries. Historians use Constituent Assembly Debates to understand the thinking behind the Constitution.
## Deep dive: Making of the Assembly
Elected indirectly in 1946, the Assembly met on 9 December 1946 with Rajendra Prasad as president.
## Deep dive: Key debates
Separate electorates, federalism, language and rights were hotly debated before compromises were reached.
## Deep dive: Outcome
The Constitution was adopted on 26 November 1949 and came into force on 26 January 1950.
