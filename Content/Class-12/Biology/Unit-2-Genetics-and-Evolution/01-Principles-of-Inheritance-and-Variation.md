# Principles of Inheritance and Variation
minutes: 45
yt: class 12 biology inheritance mendel linkage sex determination pedigree chromosomal disorders
@study
Genetics is the study of inheritance and variation. Gregor Mendel (1822 to 1884) conducted hybridisation experiments on garden pea (Pisum sativum) for seven years and discovered the laws of inheritance; he studied seven pairs of contrasting characters (stem height, flower colour, flower position, pod shape, pod colour, seed shape and seed colour). Alleles are alternative forms of a gene. A monohybrid cross between pure tall (TT) and dwarf (tt) gives all tall F1 (Tt); selfing F1 gives F2 with a phenotypic ratio of 3:1 and genotypic ratio of 1:2:1. Law of dominance: characters are controlled by factors; where two dissimilar factors are present, only one dominant expresses. Law of segregation: alleles separate during gamete formation so that a gamete carries only one allele; this is the law of purity of gametes. Test cross: crossing F1 or an unknown dominant phenotype with a homozygous recessive parent, giving 1:1 if heterozygous. Back cross is crossing with either parent. Dihybrid cross: round yellow (RRYY) x wrinkled green (rryy) gives 9:3:3:1 in F2; Law of independent assortment: when two pairs of traits are combined, segregation of one pair is independent of the other. Chromosomal theory of inheritance (Sutton and Boveri, 1902): chromosomes are vehicles of genes. Deviations from Mendel: incomplete dominance (snapdragon or Antirrhinum, 1:2:1 phenotypic ratio), codominance (ABO blood group alleles IA and IB), multiple alleles (IA, IB, i; three alleles but an individual has any two), pleiotropy (one gene affecting multiple traits, such as phenylketonuria), polygenic inheritance (skin colour, height). Blood groups: IAIA or IAi: A; IBIB or IBi: B; IAIB: AB; ii: O. Sex determination: XX-XY in humans and Drosophila (male heterogametic); XX-XO in grasshopper; ZW-ZZ in birds (female heterogametic). In humans, sex is determined by the sperm. Linkage: genes on the same chromosome are inherited together; Morgan studied Drosophila (yellow-bodied, white-eyed female x brown-bodied, red-eyed male) and found linkage and recombination; strength of linkage depends on the distance; Alfred Sturtevant used recombination frequency to map genes. Mutation: sudden heritable change; point mutation includes sickle-cell anaemia (substitution of Glu by Val in beta globin at the sixth position, a GAG to GUG change). Mendelian disorders: autosomal recessive (sickle-cell anaemia, phenylketonuria, thalassaemia), X-linked recessive (haemophilia, red-green colour blindness) and autosomal dominant (Huntington's disease, myotonic dystrophy). Haemophilia: a heterozygous female is a carrier. Thalassaemia is a quantitative defect in globin chain synthesis (alpha in chromosome 16, beta in chromosome 11). Chromosomal disorders: Down syndrome (trisomy 21; 47 chromosomes), Klinefelter syndrome (XXY; 47), Turner syndrome (XO; 45). Pedigree analysis is used to trace traits in families. Pedigree symbols: square male, circle female, shaded affected. Variation arises from mutation, recombination and gene flow.
## Deep dive: Mendel's laws
Dominance, segregation and independent assortment explain inheritance. Test cross reveals genotype. Dihybrid cross gives 9:3:3:1.
## Deep dive: Beyond Mendel
Incomplete dominance, codominance, multiple alleles, pleiotropy and polygenic inheritance extend the laws. ABO blood groups show three of these.
## Deep dive: Linkage, sex and disorders
Linked genes recombine less often. Sex is determined by chromosomes. Mendelian and chromosomal disorders can be traced by pedigrees.
