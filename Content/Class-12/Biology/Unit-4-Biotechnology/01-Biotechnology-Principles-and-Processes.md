# Biotechnology: Principles and Processes
minutes: 45
yt: class 12 biology biotechnology principles processes recombinant DNA restriction enzymes PCR vectors
@study
Biotechnology is the use of living organisms or their products for human welfare. The European Federation of Biotechnology (EFB) defines it as the integration of natural science and organisms, cells, parts thereof and molecular analogues for products and services. The two core techniques are genetic engineering (altering the genetic material of organisms) and bioprocess engineering (maintenance of sterile conditions to allow growth of the desired microbe/eukaryotic cell in large quantities). Stanley Cohen and Herbert Boyer (1972) constructed the first recombinant DNA by linking an antibiotic resistance gene with a native plasmid of Salmonella typhimurium. Tools of rDNA technology: (1) Restriction enzymes: molecular scissors; Hind II was the first restriction endonuclease (isolated by Smith and Wilcox); EcoRI (from Escherichia coli RY 13) recognises the palindromic sequence GAATTC and cuts between G and A, creating sticky ends. Exonucleases remove nucleotides from the ends; endonucleases cut at specific positions within the DNA. Restriction enzymes were discovered by Arber; Werner Arber, Hamilton Smith and Daniel Nathans got the Nobel Prize. Palindromes read the same on both strands in the 5' to 3' direction. DNA ligase joins the cut fragments. (2) Cloning vectors: plasmids (circular extrachromosomal DNA), bacteriophages and Ti plasmid of Agrobacterium. Features of a vector: origin of replication (ori), selectable marker, cloning sites and small size. pBR322 was the first vector: ori, antibiotic resistance genes ampR and tetR, rop gene and unique cloning sites: BamHI, SalI, PstI, EcoRI, ClaI and HindIII. Selectable markers: ampicillin, chloramphenicol, tetracycline or kanamycin resistance. Insertional inactivation: in the pUC vectors, insertion of foreign DNA into the lacZ gene (beta-galactosidase) inactivates it; recombinant colonies are white and non-recombinants are blue in the presence of a chromogenic substrate. (3) Competent host: DNA is a hydrophilic molecule and cannot pass through membranes; the bacterial cells are made competent by treating with a specific concentration of divalent cations such as calcium, followed by heat shock at 42 degrees Celsius. Other methods: microinjection, biolistics (gene gun, using gold or tungsten particles) and disarmed pathogen vectors. Processes: isolation of DNA (lysozyme for bacteria, cellulase for plants, chitinase for fungus; RNase removes RNA, protease removes proteins; the DNA precipitates in chilled ethanol as fine threads), cutting by restriction enzymes, gel electrophoresis (agarose gel; DNA is negatively charged and moves towards the anode; ethidium bromide staining under UV light shows orange bands), elution, amplification by PCR (Kary Mullis; steps: denaturation at 94 degrees C, annealing with primers, and extension by Taq polymerase from Thermus aquaticus, which is thermostable), insertion into the vector, transformation, selection and expression. Bioreactors: stirred-tank reactors with an agitator system, an oxygen delivery system, foam control, temperature and pH control, and sampling ports; sparged stirred tank bioreactor. Downstream processing: separation and purification of the product, formulation with preservatives and clinical trials.
## Deep dive: Tools
Restriction enzymes cut at palindromic sites and ligase joins fragments. Vectors carry the gene and have ori and selectable markers.
## Deep dive: Process
DNA is isolated, cut, run on gels, ligated into a vector and transformed into competent cells. PCR amplifies DNA with Taq polymerase.
## Deep dive: Scale-up
Bioreactors provide controlled conditions. Downstream processing purifies the product.

## High
P: Explain insertional inactivation.
A: Foreign DNA inserted into lacZ stops beta-galactosidase so recombinant colonies stay white while non-recombinants turn blue.

R: 2 | Insert
