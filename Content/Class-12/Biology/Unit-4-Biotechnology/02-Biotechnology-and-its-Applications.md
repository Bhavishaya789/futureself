# Biotechnology and its Applications

@study
Applications of biotechnology are in agriculture, medicine, environment and industry. Agriculture: GM (genetically modified) crops are produced to make crops more tolerant to abiotic stresses (cold, drought, salt, heat), reduce reliance on chemical pesticides (pest-resistant crops), reduce post-harvest losses, increase efficiency of mineral usage and enhance the nutritional value (Golden Rice, rich in Vitamin A). Bt toxin is produced by Bacillus thuringiensis; cry genes code for proteins that kill certain insects: cryIAc and cryIIAb control cotton bollworms, and cryIAb controls corn borer. The Bt toxin is present as an inactive protoxin; in the insect gut, the alkaline pH solubilises it and it binds to midgut epithelial cells creating pores that cause swelling and lysis, killing the insect. Bt cotton is a GM crop in India. RNA interference (RNAi): silencing of a specific mRNA due to a complementary dsRNA molecule that binds to and prevents translation; used against the nematode Meloidogyne incognita infecting tobacco roots, by producing sense and antisense RNA in the host to form dsRNA. Medicine: genetically engineered insulin: insulin consists of two short polypeptide chains, A and B (21 and 30 amino acids), linked by disulphide bridges; in humans it is synthesised as pro-insulin with a C peptide that is removed on maturation. In 1983, Eli Lilly produced two DNA sequences corresponding to A and B chains of human insulin and introduced them in E. coli to produce them separately, then combined by disulphide bonds (Humulin). Gene therapy: the first clinical gene therapy was given in 1990 to a four-year-old girl with adenosine deaminase (ADA) deficiency, which is caused by deletion of the ADA gene; lymphocytes from the patient's blood are grown in culture, a functional ADA cDNA (using a retroviral vector) is introduced and returned to the patient; permanent cure requires introducing genes from marrow cells. Molecular diagnosis: PCR to detect HIV, ELISA (antigen-antibody interaction) and DNA or RNA probe labelled with radioactive molecule (autoradiography) detect mutations. Transgenic animals: animals whose DNA has been manipulated to possess and express a foreign gene; mice, rats, rabbits, pigs, sheep, cows and fish are made. Uses: normal physiology and development, study of disease, biological products (alpha-1-antitrypsin for emphysema; the first transgenic cow Rosie (1997) produced human alpha-lactalbumin-enriched milk; 2.4 grams per litre), vaccine safety (polio vaccine tested in transgenic mice) and chemical safety testing (toxicity). Ethical issues: GEAC (Genetic Engineering Approval Committee) makes decisions regarding the validity of GM research and safety of introducing GM organisms for public services. Biopiracy: the use of bio-resources by multinational companies and other organisations without proper authorisation from the countries and people concerned; examples are patents on basmati rice, turmeric and neem; India has passed the Indian Patents Bill. Cry genes. Bioremediation. Biowar.
## Deep dive: Agriculture
GM crops resist pests and stress. Bt cotton uses cry genes. RNAi silences genes in pests.
## Deep dive: Medicine
Recombinant insulin and gene therapy treat disease. PCR and ELISA diagnose it. Transgenic animals make biological products.
## Deep dive: Ethics
GEAC regulates GM organisms. Biopiracy raises concerns about patents on traditional resources.
