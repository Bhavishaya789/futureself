# Ecosystem

@study
An ecosystem is a functional unit of nature where living organisms interact among themselves and with the surrounding physical environment. Ecosystem categories: terrestrial (forest, grassland, desert) and aquatic (pond, wetland, lake, river, estuary). Structure: species composition and stratification (vertical distribution of different species occupying different levels, such as trees on top, shrubs, herbs). Pond ecosystem: abiotic components (water, light, temperature, inorganic and organic substances), producers (phytoplankton, algae, macrophytes), consumers (zooplankton, fish) and decomposers (bacteria, fungi). Productivity: primary production is the amount of biomass or organic matter produced per unit area over a time period by plants during photosynthesis. Gross primary productivity (GPP) is the rate of production of organic matter during photosynthesis; net primary productivity (NPP) = GPP - R (respiration losses). Secondary productivity is the rate of formation of new organic matter by consumers. Primary productivity depends on the plant species, environmental factors and nutrient availability. Annual NPP of the whole biosphere is about 170 billion tons (dry weight) of organic matter; the productivity of oceans is only 55 billion tons though they cover 70 percent of the area, because of limited light penetration. Decomposition: fragmentation by detritivores (earthworm), leaching, catabolism by bacterial and fungal enzymes, humification (accumulation of humus, a dark amorphous substance resistant to microbial action, acting as a reservoir of nutrients) and mineralisation. Decomposition is oxygen-requiring; rate is controlled by chemical composition of detritus (lignin and chitin slow; nitrogen and sugars fast) and climatic factors (temperature and soil moisture; warm and moist environment favours it, low temperature and anaerobiosis inhibit it). Energy flow: the sun is the source; only 2 to 10 percent of the PAR (photosynthetically active radiation, 50 percent of incident solar radiation) is captured by plants. Producers, primary consumers (herbivores), secondary consumers (carnivores) and tertiary consumers form trophic levels. Food chains: the grazing food chain (GFC) begins with producers and the detritus food chain (DFC) begins with dead organic matter; in aquatic ecosystems the GFC is the major conduit of energy flow, whereas in terrestrial ecosystems the DFC is. The 10 percent law (Lindeman): only about 10 percent of energy is transferred from one trophic level to the next. Food web is a network of interconnecting food chains. Ecological pyramids: pyramid of numbers, biomass and energy; the pyramid of energy is always upright; the pyramid of numbers and biomass can be inverted (biomass pyramid inverted in the sea; numbers pyramid inverted in a tree ecosystem). Limitations: it does not account for the same species at two trophic levels and saprophytes. Ecological succession: gradual and fairly predictable change in the species composition of a given area. Primary succession starts on a barren area (bare rock) with pioneer species; the pioneer in a lithosere is lichens; the seral stages lead to a climax community. Hydrarch succession takes place in wetter areas, and xerarch in dry areas. Secondary succession begins in areas where natural biotic communities have been destroyed, such as abandoned farm lands, burned or cut forests; faster. Nutrient cycling: carbon cycle (about 71 percent of carbon is dissolved in oceans; atmosphere has 1 percent; fossil fuels; the human activities add CO2), phosphorus cycle (no gaseous exchange; rocks are the reservoir), and nutrient cycling is gaseous or sedimentary. Ecosystem services: purification of air and water, mitigation of droughts and floods, nutrient cycling, pollination; Robert Costanza and colleagues estimated their value at US$33 trillion a year.
## Deep dive: Productivity and decomposition
GPP minus respiration gives NPP. Decomposition recycles nutrients through fragmentation, leaching, catabolism, humification and mineralisation.
## Deep dive: Energy flow
Energy flows one way through trophic levels with 10 percent transfer. Grazing and detritus food chains form food webs. Pyramids show the structure.
## Deep dive: Succession and cycles
Succession leads to a climax community. Carbon and phosphorus cycle through reservoirs. Ecosystem services have great value.
