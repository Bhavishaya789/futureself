# Microbes in Human Welfare
minutes: 45
yt: class 12 biology microbes human welfare fermentation sewage biogas biofertiliser
@study
Microbes are present everywhere and are beneficial in household products, industrial products, sewage treatment, energy generation and as biocontrol agents and biofertilisers. Household: Lactobacillus (lactic acid bacteria, LAB) converts milk to curd; it increases vitamin B12 and checks disease-causing microbes in the stomach. Dough for idli and dosa is fermented by bacteria; the puffed-up appearance of bread is due to CO2 produced by the yeast Saccharomyces cerevisiae (baker's yeast). Toddy is made by fermentation of sap from palms. Cheese: Swiss cheese has large holes due to a large amount of CO2 produced by the bacterium Propionibacterium sharmanii; Roquefort cheese is ripened by growing a specific fungus (Penicillium roqueforti). Industrial products: fermentors (bioreactors) in industry. Beverages: Saccharomyces cerevisiae is used for fermenting malted cereals and fruit juices to produce wine, beer, whisky, brandy and rum (distillation gives whisky, brandy and rum; wine and beer are not distilled). Antibiotics: Alexander Fleming discovered penicillin from Penicillium notatum in 1928; Ernest Chain and Howard Florey established its effectiveness and the three got the Nobel Prize in 1945. Organic acids: Aspergillus niger (citric acid), Acetobacter aceti (acetic acid), Clostridium butylicum (butyric acid) and Lactobacillus (lactic acid). Enzymes: lipases in detergents, pectinases and proteases to clarify bottled juices, streptokinase produced by Streptococcus (clot buster, used to remove clots in the blood vessels of patients with myocardial infarction). Bioactive molecules: cyclosporin A (immunosuppressive agent in organ transplants; from the fungus Trichoderma polysporum), statins (blood cholesterol lowering agent; Monascus purpureus). Sewage treatment: primary treatment (physical removal of floating debris and grit by sedimentation, producing primary sludge and effluent) and secondary or biological treatment (the primary effluent is passed into aeration tanks where aerobic microbes form flocs; BOD (biochemical oxygen demand) reduces; the effluent is then passed into a settling tank where bacterial flocs sediment as activated sludge; part is used as inoculum and the remaining is passed into anaerobic sludge digesters, where anaerobic bacteria produce biogas (methane, hydrogen sulphide and carbon dioxide)). BOD measures organic matter; the greater the BOD, the more polluted the water. The Ganga Action Plan and Yamuna Action Plan. Biogas: methanogens (Methanobacterium) in the rumen of cattle and in anaerobic sludge produce methane; the starting material is cattle dung (gobar); the biogas plant has a digester and a gas holder; the technology was developed in India by IARI and KVIC. Biocontrol agents: Ladybird for aphids, dragonflies for mosquitoes, Bacillus thuringiensis (Bt) for butterfly caterpillars, Trichoderma for plant pathogens, and Baculoviruses (Nucleopolyhedrovirus) for insects. Biofertilisers: bacteria (Rhizobium symbiotic with legume roots, Azospirillum and Azotobacter free-living nitrogen fixers), fungi (mycorrhiza such as Glomus, which absorb phosphorus), and cyanobacteria (Anabaena, Nostoc, Oscillatoria in paddy fields; Azolla with Anabaena).
## Deep dive: Household and industrial
Microbes make curd, bread, cheese, wine and antibiotics. Fermentors scale production. Enzymes and bioactive molecules have medical uses.
## Deep dive: Sewage and biogas
Primary and secondary treatment lower BOD. Methanogens in sludge digesters make biogas.
## Deep dive: Biocontrol and biofertilisers
Microbial agents replace chemicals for pests and fertilisers. Rhizobium, mycorrhiza and cyanobacteria improve soil fertility.
