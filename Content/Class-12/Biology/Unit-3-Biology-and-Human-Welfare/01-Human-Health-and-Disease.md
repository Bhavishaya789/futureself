# Human Health and Disease
minutes: 45
yt: class 12 biology human health disease immunity AIDS cancer drugs vaccination
@study
Health is a state of complete physical, mental and social well-being. Disease types: infectious (communicable) and non-infectious. Common diseases: typhoid (Salmonella typhi; Widal test), pneumonia (Streptococcus pneumoniae and Haemophilus influenzae), common cold (rhino viruses), malaria (Plasmodium; female Anopheles mosquito; Plasmodium falciparum causes malignant malaria; haemozoin released on rupture of RBCs causes fever and chill), amoebiasis (Entamoeba histolytica), ascariasis (Ascaris), filariasis (Wuchereria; elephantiasis), ringworm (Microsporum, Trichophyton, Epidermophyton) and dengue and chikungunya (Aedes mosquitoes). Immunity: innate immunity (barriers: physical, physiological, cellular and cytokine barriers) and acquired immunity (specific; primary and secondary responses; memory). Cells: B-lymphocytes produce antibodies (humoral immunity); T-lymphocytes mediate cell-mediated immunity. An antibody (immunoglobulin) has four peptide chains: two light and two heavy (H2L2); types are IgA, IgM, IgE, IgG. Active immunity is produced when the host is exposed to antigens (infection or vaccine) and takes time; passive immunity is by readymade antibodies (colostrum IgA; tetanus antitoxin). Vaccination uses weakened or inactivated pathogens, creating memory; Edward Jenner introduced vaccination for smallpox. Recombinant DNA technology has produced a hepatitis B vaccine from yeast. Allergy is an exaggerated response to allergens; IgE, histamine and serotonin are released from mast cells; antihistamine and adrenaline treat. Autoimmunity: the body attacks self cells (rheumatoid arthritis). The lymphoid organs: primary (bone marrow and thymus) and secondary (spleen, lymph nodes, tonsils, Peyer's patches, MALT). Organ transplant rejection occurs due to the immune system. AIDS (Acquired Immuno Deficiency Syndrome) is caused by HIV, a retrovirus with RNA genome. Transmission: sexual contact, contaminated blood and needles, and from infected mother to child; not by touch or sharing food. HIV infects helper T-cells (CD4 cells); in the macrophages the viral RNA gets converted to DNA by reverse transcriptase; the immunity falls and opportunistic infections such as tuberculosis occur. Diagnosis by ELISA. Treatment: anti-retroviral therapy, which is not a cure. NACO and NGOs spread awareness; National AIDS Control Organisation. Cancer: uncontrolled cell division; contact inhibition is lost; normal cells are checked by contact inhibition; benign tumours remain localised, while malignant tumours spread by metastasis. Carcinogens: physical (X-rays, gamma rays, UV), chemical (tobacco smoke) and biological (oncogenic viruses with cellular oncogenes or proto-oncogenes). Detection: biopsy, radiography, CT, MRI and antibodies against cancer-specific antigens. Treatment: surgery, radiation therapy, chemotherapy and immunotherapy with biological response modifiers such as alpha-interferon. Drugs and alcohol abuse: opioids (heroin or smack from morphine of Papaver somniferum, binds to opioid receptors), cannabinoids (marijuana, hashish, charas, ganja from Cannabis sativa), coca alkaloids (cocaine from Erythroxylum coca), and atropine and datura. Adolescence and drug abuse: peer pressure, curiosity and stress. Prevention: education, counselling and professional help.
## Deep dive: Pathogens and diseases
Bacteria, viruses, protozoa, helminths and fungi cause disease. Vectors transmit malaria and dengue. Hygiene and vector control prevent them.
## Deep dive: Immunity
Innate barriers and acquired B and T cell responses protect us. Vaccines build memory. Allergy and autoimmunity are errors of immunity.
## Deep dive: AIDS, cancer and drugs
HIV destroys helper T cells. Cancer arises from uncontrolled growth and metastasis. Drug abuse harms health and needs prevention and counselling.

## Advanced
P: Explain how HIV causes AIDS.
A: HIV enters helper T cells, reverse transcribes its RNA to DNA, multiplies and destroys them, so immunity falls and opportunistic infections occur.

R: 2 | Reverse transcriptase
