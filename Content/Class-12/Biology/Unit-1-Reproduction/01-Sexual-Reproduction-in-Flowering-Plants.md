# Sexual Reproduction in Flowering Plants

@study
The flower is the reproductive organ of angiosperms. The stamen has an anther and filament; the anther is usually bilobed and dithecous (two lobes, four microsporangia). Microsporangium wall layers from outside: epidermis, endothecium, middle layers and tapetum; the tapetum nourishes the developing pollen grains. Microsporogenesis: pollen mother cells (2n) undergo meiosis to form tetrads of microspores (n). The pollen grain (male gametophyte) has a two-layered wall: the outer exine made of sporopollenin, one of the most resistant organic materials, with germ pores, and the inner intine of cellulose and pectin. A mature pollen grain has a vegetative cell and a generative cell which divides into two male gametes. The pistil (carpel) has stigma, style and ovary. The ovule has funicle, hilum, integuments, micropyle, nucellus and chalaza. Megasporogenesis: the megaspore mother cell (2n) undergoes meiosis to form four megaspores (n), of which three degenerate and one functional megaspore develops into the female gametophyte (embryo sac). The typical embryo sac is monosporic, 7-celled and 8-nucleate: the egg apparatus (an egg and two synergids with filiform apparatus), three antipodal cells and a central cell with two polar nuclei. Pollination is the transfer of pollen grains from the anther to the stigma. Types: autogamy, geitonogamy (same plant, different flower; genetically like autogamy but ecologically cross-pollination) and xenogamy (genetically different plants). Agents: wind (anemophily), water (hydrophily), insects (entomophily), birds and bats. Outbreeding devices: dichogamy, herkogamy, self-incompatibility, dioecy and unisexual flowers. Cleistogamous flowers never open and are always autogamous. Pollen-pistil interaction: compatible pollen germinates to form a pollen tube. Double fertilisation is unique to angiosperms: one male gamete fuses with the egg (syngamy) to form the zygote, and the other fuses with the two polar nuclei (triple fusion) to form the primary endosperm nucleus (3n). Post-fertilisation: endosperm develops before the embryo; types include free-nuclear, cellular and helobial; coconut water is free-nuclear endosperm. Embryo development: zygote, proembryo, globular, heart-shaped and mature embryo. A dicot embryo has two cotyledons, epicotyl and hypocotyl; the monocot embryo has one cotyledon (scutellum in grasses), coleoptile and coleorhiza. Seed develops from ovule and fruit from ovary; ovule integuments become seed coat (testa and tegmen). Perisperm is the persistent nucellus (black pepper, beet). Albuminous seeds retain endosperm (wheat, castor); non-albuminous seeds do not (pea, groundnut). Apomixis is the formation of seeds without fertilisation; polyembryony is more than one embryo in a seed (citrus, mango). Parthenocarpy forms seedless fruit (banana). Pollen banks, emasculation and bagging are used in plant breeding. Pollen allergy comes from Parthenium. Pollen is rich in nutrients.
## Deep dive: Male and female gametophytes
The anther produces pollen by microsporogenesis; the ovule produces the embryo sac by megasporogenesis. The embryo sac has seven cells and eight nuclei.
## Deep dive: Pollination and fertilisation
Pollination transfers pollen to the stigma. Double fertilisation produces zygote and endosperm. Triple fusion gives the 3n endosperm.
## Deep dive: Seed and fruit
The zygote forms the embryo, the ovule forms the seed and the ovary forms the fruit. Endosperm may persist or be consumed.
