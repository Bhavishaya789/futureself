Humans are sexually reproducing and viviparous. The male reproductive system: a pair of testes in the scrotum (2 to 2.5 degrees Celsius lower than body temperature for spermatogenesis), epididymis, vas deferens, ejaculatory duct, urethra, penis and accessory glands (seminal vesicles, prostate and bulbourethral glands). Each testis has about 250 compartments called testicular lobules, each containing one to three seminiferous tubules lined with spermatogonia (male germ cells) and Sertoli cells (nourish sperm); Leydig (interstitial) cells outside the tubules produce androgens. Spermatogenesis: spermatogonia (2n) divide mitotically; primary spermatocyte (2n) undergoes meiosis I to form two secondary spermatocytes (n) and meiosis II to form four spermatids, which transform into sperms by spermiogenesis. GnRH from the hypothalamus stimulates the pituitary to release LH and FSH; LH acts on Leydig cells to secrete androgens (testosterone); FSH acts on Sertoli cells. A sperm has a head (with acrosome containing hydrolytic enzymes and nucleus), neck, middle piece (mitochondria) and tail. Semen is seminal plasma plus sperm; about 200 to 300 million sperm are released in one ejaculation, and at least 60 percent must be normal and motile for fertility. The female reproductive system: ovaries, oviducts (fallopian tubes: infundibulum, ampulla, isthmus), uterus (perimetrium, myometrium, endometrium), cervix, vagina, external genitalia, and mammary glands. Oogenesis begins during embryonic development; oogonia form primary oocytes that get arrested at prophase I; at puberty about 60,000 to 80,000 remain; a primary follicle becomes a secondary follicle, a tertiary follicle and then a mature Graafian follicle; the secondary oocyte (arrested at metaphase II) is released at ovulation; meiosis II is completed only after sperm entry, producing the ovum and a second polar body. The menstrual cycle lasts about 28 days; ovulation occurs on about day 14. Phases: menstrual (days 1 to 5), follicular or proliferative, ovulatory and luteal or secretory. LH surge triggers ovulation. The corpus luteum secretes progesterone; if no fertilisation, it degenerates and menstruation begins. Menarche is the first menstruation and menopause the cessation at 50 years. Fertilisation occurs in the ampulla; the sperm acrosomal enzymes (hyaluronidase) dissolve the zona pellucida; entry of a sperm induces the cortical reaction that blocks polyspermy. The zygote undergoes cleavage to form a morula (8 to 16 cells), then the blastocyst with trophoblast and inner cell mass; the trophoblast implants in the endometrium. Placenta is formed by chorionic villi and uterine tissue, and is connected to the embryo by the umbilical cord; it facilitates exchange of oxygen, nutrients and waste and acts as an endocrine tissue producing hCG, hPL, oestrogen and progesterone; relaxin is produced in later stages. The inner cell mass differentiates into the three germ layers: ectoderm, mesoderm and endoderm. After one month of pregnancy the heart is formed; by the end of the second month limbs and digits develop; by the end of the first trimester most major organ systems are formed. Gestation is about nine months. Parturition is induced by a complex neuroendocrine mechanism involving cortisol, oestrogen and oxytocin. Lactation: colostrum, the first milk, contains antibodies (IgA) vital for the new-born's immunity.
## Deep dive: Male and female systems
Testes make sperm and testosterone; ovaries make ova and hormones. Accessory glands and ducts support gamete transport.
## Deep dive: Gametogenesis and cycle
Spermatogenesis yields four sperms per cell; oogenesis yields one ovum. The menstrual cycle is controlled by FSH, LH, oestrogen and progesterone.
## Deep dive: Fertilisation to birth
Fertilisation in the ampulla is followed by cleavage and implantation. The placenta supports the foetus. Parturition is hormone controlled.
