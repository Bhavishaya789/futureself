# Reproductive Health
minutes: 45
yt: class 12 biology reproductive health contraception STDs infertility MTP ART IVF
@study
Reproductive health means total well-being in all aspects of reproduction: physical, emotional, behavioural and social. India was among the first countries to initiate action plans and programmes at the national level to attain total reproductive health; the family planning programmes were initiated in 1951 and are now called the Reproductive and Child Health Care (RCH) programmes. Components: sex education in schools, awareness of problems related to adolescence and sex-related abuse, the use of contraception, awareness of STDs, antenatal and postnatal care, and prevention of infertility. Statutory bodies: Amniocentesis for sex determination is banned (the Pre-Conception and Pre-Natal Diagnostic Techniques Act, 1994) to check female foeticide, though it is permitted to detect genetic disorders. Population explosion: the population grew from about 350 million at independence to over 1.2 billion by 2011. Contraception: an ideal contraceptive should be user-friendly, easily available, effective and reversible, with no side-effects. Natural or traditional methods: periodic abstinence (avoiding coitus from day 10 to 17 of the cycle), withdrawal or coitus interruptus and lactational amenorrhoea (absence of menstruation during lactation, effective up to six months). Barrier methods: condoms (for male and female), diaphragms, cervical caps and vaults. Condoms also protect against STDs and AIDS. Intra-uterine devices (IUDs): non-medicated (Lippes loop), copper releasing (CuT, Cu7, Multiload 375) and hormone-releasing (Progestasert, LNG-20). Copper ions suppress sperm motility and fertilising capacity; hormone releasing IUDs make the uterus unsuitable for implantation. Oral contraceptives: pills such as Saheli (non-steroidal, once a week) and progestogen-oestrogen combinations; they inhibit ovulation and implantation and alter cervical mucus. Emergency contraception: administration of progestogens or IUDs within 72 hours of coitus. Surgical methods (sterilisation): vasectomy in males and tubectomy in females. Medical termination of pregnancy (MTP) was legalised in 1971; it is safe up to 12 weeks of pregnancy, and the MTP Amendment Act, 2021 extends the upper limit to 24 weeks for certain categories. Sexually transmitted infections (STIs) include gonorrhoea, syphilis, genital herpes, chlamydiasis, genital warts, trichomoniasis, hepatitis-B and HIV/AIDS. Hepatitis-B, genital herpes and HIV are viral; the first three, namely gonorrhoea, syphilis and chlamydiasis, are curable with antibiotics. Prevention: avoid sex with unknown or multiple partners, use condoms and seek early diagnosis. Infertility: inability to conceive despite unprotected intercourse for a year. Assisted reproductive technologies (ART): In vitro fertilisation (IVF) followed by embryo transfer (ET) in a laboratory with zygote or early embryos up to 8 blastomeres transferred into the fallopian tube (ZIFT) and embryos with more than 8 blastomeres into the uterus (IUT). Gamete intra-fallopian transfer (GIFT) transfers ova into the fallopian tube. Intracytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI) injects a sperm directly into an ovum. Artificial insemination (AI) and IUI transfers semen into the vagina or uterus. Test tube baby: the first was Louise Brown in 1978; India's first was born in 1986. Adoption is another option.
## Deep dive: Reproductive health
It includes physical, emotional and social well-being. Sex education, safe motherhood and STI awareness are key. RCH programmes address them.
## Deep dive: Contraception
Methods include natural, barrier, IUD, pills and surgical. Each has merits and limits. Condoms protect against STIs.
## Deep dive: MTP, STIs and ART
MTP is legal under limits. STIs are prevented by safe behaviour. ART such as IVF and ICSI help infertile couples.
