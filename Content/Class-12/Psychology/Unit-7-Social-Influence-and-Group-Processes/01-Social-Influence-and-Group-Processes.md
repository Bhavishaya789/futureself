# Social Influence and Group Processes
minutes: 40
yt: class 12 psychology social influence group processes conformity obedience milgram asch groupthink leadership conflict
@study
A group is two or more people who interact, share goals, norms and a sense of belonging. Groups form to satisfy needs for security, status, affiliation, goals and identity. Characteristics include norms (expected behaviour), roles, status, cohesiveness and shared goals. Types are primary or secondary, formal or informal, in-groups and out-groups. Tuckman’s stages of group formation are forming, storming, norming, performing and adjourning. Social influence includes conformity (changing behaviour to match the group), compliance (yielding to a request) and obedience (following orders). Asch’s 1951 line judgement experiment showed conformity to a unanimous majority. Milgram’s 1963 obedience experiment, in which participants gave what they thought were electric shocks up to 450 volts, found that about 65 per cent went to the maximum, showing the power of authority. Group polarisation is the strengthening of the average group view after discussion; groupthink (Janis) is poor decision making due to pressure for consensus. Social loafing and social facilitation affect performance. Leadership is influencing others towards goals; Lewin identified autocratic, democratic and laissez-faire styles in 1939; trait theories say leaders have certain qualities; Fiedler’s contingency model says effectiveness depends on fit between style and situation; transformational leaders inspire change. Crowds are temporary collections; a mob is an emotional, violent crowd, and deindividuation (Zimbardo) is loss of self-awareness in groups. Intergroup conflict can arise from competition: Sherif’s Robbers Cave experiment (1954) showed that competition created hostility and superordinate goals reduced it. Social identity theory of Tajfel explains in-group favouritism and out-group bias. Conflict resolution methods include contact, superordinate goals, negotiation, mediation and the GRIT (graduated reciprocation) approach. Cooperation and competition are the two basic orientations.
## Deep dive: Group basics
Norms, roles, status and cohesiveness; Tuckman’s stages from forming to adjourning.
## Deep dive: Social influence
Conformity (Asch), obedience (Milgram), polarisation and groupthink.
## Deep dive: Leadership and conflict
Lewin’s styles, Fiedler’s model; Sherif’s Robbers Cave shows how superordinate goals reduce conflict.
