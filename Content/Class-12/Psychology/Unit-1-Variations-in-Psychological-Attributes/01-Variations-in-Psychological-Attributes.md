# Variations in Psychological Attributes

@study
Individual differences are the ways in which people differ from one another in traits and abilities. Intelligence is the global capacity to act purposefully, think rationally and deal effectively with the environment, in Wechsler’s definition. Intelligence tests began with Binet and Simon in 1905; the mental age (MA) is the level of mental development and the intelligence quotient is IQ = (MA / CA) x 100, where CA is chronological age. Terman revised the test as the Stanford-Binet in 1916, and Wechsler developed scales such as WAIS and WISC, which use deviation IQ. IQ scores follow a normal curve with mean 100 and standard deviation 15; about 2 per cent score above 130 (gifted) and below 70 (intellectual disability). Intellectual disability is graded mild (50-69), moderate (35-49), severe (20-34) and profound (below 20). Theories of intelligence include Spearman’s two-factor theory (general factor g and specific factors s), Thurstone’s seven primary mental abilities, Guilford’s structure of intellect (operations, contents, products), Gardner’s multiple intelligences (linguistic, logical-mathematical, spatial, musical, bodily-kinaesthetic, interpersonal, intrapersonal and naturalistic), Sternberg’s triarchic theory (componential or analytical, experiential or creative, contextual or practical) and the PASS model of Das, Kirby and Jarman (planning, attention-arousal, simultaneous and successive processing). Emotional intelligence, proposed by Salovey and Mayer in 1990 and popularised by Goleman, is the ability to perceive, understand, manage and use emotions. Aptitude is potential to acquire a skill. Creativity is producing novel and appropriate ideas; Guilford separated divergent thinking (fluency, flexibility, originality, elaboration) from convergent thinking, and the stages of creativity are preparation, incubation, illumination and verification. Psychological tests must be reliable, valid, standardised, and have norms; they may be verbal, non-verbal or performance tests, such as Raven’s Progressive Matrices and Kohs Block Design. Culture shapes intelligence, so tests should be as culture-fair as possible.
## Deep dive: Intelligence and IQ
IQ = MA/CA x 100; scores form a normal curve with mean 100 and SD 15.
## Deep dive: Theories
Spearman g, Thurstone, Guilford, Gardner’s eight intelligences, Sternberg’s triarchic and PASS.
## Deep dive: Creativity and testing
Divergent thinking and four stages; tests must be reliable, valid and standardised.
