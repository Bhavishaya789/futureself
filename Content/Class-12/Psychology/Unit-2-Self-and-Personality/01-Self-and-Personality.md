# Self and Personality

@study
The self is a person’s awareness of their own existence and identity. William James separated the I (the knower) from the Me (the known), and Cooley proposed the looking-glass self. Self-concept is the organised set of beliefs about oneself, self-esteem is the evaluation of one’s worth, and Bandura’s self-efficacy is belief in one’s ability to succeed in a task. Self-regulation is controlling one’s behaviour. In the Indian view the self is atman, the true inner self, beyond the body and mind. Personality is the relatively stable pattern of characteristics that makes a person unique; Allport defined it as the dynamic organisation within the individual of psychophysical systems that determine unique adjustment. Type approaches classify people into categories: Hippocrates’s four humours (sanguine, phlegmatic, choleric, melancholic), Sheldon’s somatotypes, Jung’s introvert and extrovert, Friedman and Rosenman’s Type A, B and C, and the Indian vata, pitta and kapha and the gunas sattva, rajas and tamas. Trait approaches describe personality through traits: Allport’s cardinal, central and secondary traits, Cattell’s 16 source traits (16PF), Eysenck’s extraversion, neuroticism and psychoticism, and the Big Five of Costa and McCrae (openness, conscientiousness, extraversion, agreeableness, neuroticism). Freud’s psychodynamic theory has the id (pleasure principle), the ego (reality principle) and the superego (morality), with psychosexual stages oral, anal, phallic, latency and genital, and defence mechanisms such as repression, denial, projection, displacement, rationalisation, reaction formation, sublimation and regression. Jung proposed the collective unconscious and Adler the inferiority complex. Behaviourists like Skinner see personality as learned responses. Humanistic theorists: Rogers stressed the self, unconditional positive regard and the fully functioning person; Maslow stressed self-actualisation. Assessment uses self-report inventories, projective techniques such as the Rorschach inkblot test (ten cards), the Thematic Apperception Test of Murray and Morgan (pictures and a blank card), and the sentence completion test, plus behavioural analysis, interview and observation.
## Deep dive: Self
James’s I and Me, self-concept, self-esteem and Bandura’s self-efficacy.
## Deep dive: Theories of personality
Type, trait, psychodynamic, behavioural and humanistic approaches.
## Deep dive: Assessment
Self-report inventories and projective tests like the Rorschach and TAT.
