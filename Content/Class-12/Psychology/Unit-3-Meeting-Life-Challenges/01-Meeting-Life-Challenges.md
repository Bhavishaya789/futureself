# Meeting Life Challenges

@study
Stress is a pattern of responses to stimulus events (stressors) that disturb equilibrium and exceed coping ability. Lazarus and Folkman describe stress as a transaction between the person and environment, with primary appraisal (is it a threat?) and secondary appraisal (can I cope?). Hans Selye (1936) described the General Adaptation Syndrome in three stages: alarm reaction, resistance and exhaustion. Eustress is positive, motivating stress; distress is harmful. Sources of stress include life events (the Holmes and Rahe Social Readjustment Rating Scale, 1967), daily hassles, traumatic events, physical, psychological and social stressors. Effects include physiological (adrenaline and cortisol release, raised heart rate, illness), emotional (anxiety, depression), cognitive (poor concentration) and behavioural effects, and burnout, a state of physical, emotional and mental exhaustion. Learned helplessness (Seligman) and locus of control (Rotter) relate to stress. Coping is the effort to manage demands: Endler and Parker describe task-oriented coping (problem-focused), emotion-oriented coping and avoidance-oriented coping. Stress management techniques include relaxation such as Jacobson’s progressive muscle relaxation, meditation and yoga, biofeedback, creative visualisation, cognitive behavioural techniques including Meichenbaum’s stress inoculation training, and exercise. Stress-resistant factors include hardiness (Kobasa: commitment, control and challenge), positive attitude, social support and assertiveness. Positive health is more than absence of disease; WHO defines health as complete physical, mental and social well-being. Life skills, defined by WHO as abilities for adaptive and positive behaviour, number ten: self-awareness, empathy, critical thinking, creative thinking, decision making, problem solving, effective communication, interpersonal relationships, coping with stress and coping with emotions. Healthy lifestyle includes balanced diet, regular exercise, adequate sleep and avoiding smoking and alcohol.
## Deep dive: Stress and GAS
Stress arises from appraisal; Selye’s GAS has alarm, resistance and exhaustion.
## Deep dive: Coping and management
Task, emotion and avoidance coping; relaxation, meditation, biofeedback and stress inoculation help.
## Deep dive: Positive health
Hardiness, support and ten WHO life skills promote well-being.
