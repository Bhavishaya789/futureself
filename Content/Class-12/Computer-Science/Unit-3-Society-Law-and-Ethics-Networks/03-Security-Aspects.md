# Security Aspects
minutes: 45
yt: class 12 computer science cyber security malware firewall cryptography phishing cyber law IT act
@study
Cyber security protects systems, networks and data from attack. Threats: malware (malicious software): virus (attaches to files and needs a host program to spread), worm (self replicating over networks without a host), trojan (looks useful but hides harm), ransomware (encrypts files and demands payment), spyware (secretly collects information), adware (shows unwanted adverts), keylogger (records keystrokes) and botnets. Attacks: phishing (fake emails or sites to steal credentials), pharming (redirecting to fake sites), spoofing, eavesdropping or sniffing, man in the middle, denial of service (DoS) and distributed DoS (DDoS) flooding a server, SQL injection, brute force and password guessing, and social engineering. Protection: antivirus software, firewall (filters incoming and outgoing traffic by rules), HTTPS with SSL/TLS, strong passwords (long, mixed characters, unique), two factor authentication, regular updates and patches, backups, cautious clicking, and not installing pirated software. Cryptography: encryption converts plaintext to ciphertext and decryption reverses it; symmetric encryption uses one shared key (AES); asymmetric encryption uses a public and a private key (RSA); digital signature verifies sender and integrity. Cookies are small files that store site data. Intellectual property rights: copyright, patent, trademark; plagiarism is presenting others' work as your own; open source and free software, licences such as GPL and Creative Commons. Cyber crime and law: hacking (white hat, black hat, grey hat), identity theft, cyber bullying and online fraud are offences; the IT Act 2000 (amended 2008) is India's cyber law and it recognises digital signatures and electronic records; Section 66 deals with computer related offences. E-waste: discarded electronics should be recycled, reused or disposed of through authorised channels. Digital footprint: the trail of data left online. Netiquette: good online behaviour. Health: ergonomics and screen time.
## Deep dive: Malware
Virus needs a host, worm spreads alone, trojan disguises, ransomware encrypts and spyware spies.
## Deep dive: Defence
Layered defence uses firewall, antivirus, updates, strong passwords, two factor authentication and encryption.
## Deep dive: Law and ethics
IT Act 2000 covers cyber crime; copyright, patents and trademarks protect intellectual property.
