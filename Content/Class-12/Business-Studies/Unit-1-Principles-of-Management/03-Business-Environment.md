# Business Environment
minutes: 45
yt: class 12 business studies business environment economic political legal social technological demonetisation LPG
@study
Business environment is the sum total of all individuals, institutions and other forces that are outside the control of a business enterprise but may affect its performance. Features: totality of external forces, specific and general forces, inter-relatedness, dynamic, uncertainty, relativity and complexity. Importance: identification of opportunities and getting first mover advantage, identification of threats and early warning signals, tapping useful resources, coping with rapid changes, assisting in planning and policy formulation, improving performance and image building. Dimensions: economic, social, technological, political and legal. Economic environment: economic conditions, policies and system, such as interest rates, inflation, fiscal and monetary policy, the Planning Commission (replaced by NITI Aayog in 2015). Social: customs, values, expectations of society; Technological: innovations, scientific improvements; Political: political stability, government attitudes and policies; Legal: laws such as the Consumer Protection Act, the Companies Act, and the Competition Act. Economic reforms in India since 1991: the New Industrial Policy 1991 (LPG: liberalisation, privatisation, globalisation). Liberalisation: end of licence-permit raj, freedom to import and export, abolition of licensing for most industries, increase in the number of industries open to private sector, reduced tariffs and the freedom to fix prices. Privatisation: transfer of ownership of public sector enterprises to private; disinvestment (selling a part of the equity), and the Department of Disinvestment was set up. Globalisation: integration of the Indian economy with the world economy; outsourcing of services; the World Trade Organisation (WTO; successor to GATT in 1995). Impact of government policy changes on business and industry: increasing competition, more demanding customers, rapidly changing technological environment, necessity for change, need for developing human resources, market orientation and loss of budgetary support to the public sector. Demonetisation: on 8 November 2016, the Government of India declared that Rs 500 and Rs 1000 notes were no longer legal tender to curb black money, corruption and counterfeit currency; the new Rs 500 and Rs 2000 notes were issued. Impact: digital payments increased and the cash dependence reduced. SWOT and PESTLE analysis help in assessing the environment. Environmental scanning is the process of monitoring. Business environment includes micro environment (customers, suppliers, competitors, intermediaries) and macro environment (general forces). Make in India, Digital India and GST (Goods and Services Tax, introduced from 1 July 2017) are recent reforms.
## Deep dive: Concept and importance
Business environment includes external forces beyond control. It helps identify opportunities and threats and improve planning.
## Deep dive: Dimensions
Economic, social, technological, political and legal factors shape business. Each affects decisions differently.
## Deep dive: Reforms
Liberalisation, privatisation and globalisation changed Indian business. Demonetisation and GST are later changes.
