# Organising
minutes: 45
yt: class 12 business studies organising structure functional divisional delegation decentralisation formal informal
@study
Organising is the process of defining and grouping activities and establishing authority relationships among them. Steps: (1) identification and division of work, (2) departmentalisation (grouping of jobs into departments on the basis of functions or products), (3) assignment of duties and (4) establishing reporting relationships. Importance: specialisation, clarity in working relationships, optimum utilisation of resources, adaptation to change, effective administration, development of personnel and expansion and growth. Organisation structure is the framework within which managerial and operating tasks are performed. Functional structure: grouping of jobs of similar nature under a department (production, marketing, finance). Advantages: specialisation, control over functions, efficiency, training is easier and reduced duplication. Disadvantages: functional objectives may be overemphasised, difficult to coordinate, and conflict among departments. Suitable for large firms with diverse activities but a single product line. Divisional structure: grouping on the basis of products or markets such as the Reliance divisions. Advantages: performance can be assessed, quick decision making, facilitates expansion and gives a chance for development of managers; disadvantages: conflict over allocation of funds, duplication of activities and increased cost, and competition among divisions. Formal organisation: the structure of well-defined jobs, each bearing a measure of authority and responsibility; it follows the rules, is created by top management and has a clear chain; the advantages: fixing of responsibility, unity of command and the certainty of tasks; disadvantages: delay in action due to formality, limited scope for personal initiative and ignores human relations. Informal organisation: a network of social relationships arising spontaneously due to personal interactions among employees; it has no written rules, it is not deliberately created and develops within the formal structure. Advantages: fast spread of information, fulfils social needs and supports formal structure; disadvantages: spread of rumours, resistance to change and pressure for conformity. Delegation of authority: the downward transfer of authority from a superior to a subordinate. Elements: authority (the right to give orders), responsibility (the obligation to perform; it cannot be delegated, because it flows upward) and accountability (answerability for the final outcome; it cannot be delegated). Authority flows downward and responsibility flows upward. Importance: effective management, employee development, motivation, facilitation of growth, better coordination and quicker decisions. Decentralisation: systematic delegation of authority at all levels of management; centralisation is concentration of decision-making at the top. Difference: delegation is a process of sharing authority between a superior and a subordinate, while decentralisation is a policy decision to share authority at all levels; delegation is compulsory in the process, while decentralisation is optional; the two are complementary: decentralisation is the extension of delegation to the lowest level. Importance of decentralisation: develops initiative among subordinates, develops managerial talent, quick decision-making, relief to top management, facilitates growth and better control. Span of management is the number of subordinates reporting to one superior.
## Deep dive: Process and structure
Organising divides work and sets authority relations. Functional and divisional structures suit different firms.
## Deep dive: Formal and informal
The formal structure is deliberate; the informal arises spontaneously. Both coexist and support each other.
## Deep dive: Delegation
Authority is delegated, but responsibility and accountability remain with the superior. Decentralisation extends delegation systematically.
