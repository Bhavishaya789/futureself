# Planning
minutes: 45
yt: class 12 business studies planning importance limitations steps types of plans
@study
Planning is deciding in advance what to do, when to do it, how to do it and who is to do it. It bridges the gap between where we are and where we want to go. Features: planning focuses on achieving objectives, is a primary function, is pervasive, is continuous, is futuristic, involves decision making and is a mental exercise. Importance: planning provides direction, reduces the risk of uncertainty, reduces overlapping and wasteful activities, promotes innovative ideas, facilitates decision making and establishes standards for controlling. Limitations: planning leads to rigidity, may not work in a dynamic environment, reduces creativity, involves huge costs, is a time-consuming process and does not guarantee success. Planning process: (1) setting objectives, (2) developing premises (assumptions about the future: internal or external; tangible or intangible), (3) identifying alternative courses of action, (4) evaluating alternative courses, (5) selecting an alternative, (6) implementing the plan and (7) follow-up action. Types of plans (single-use and standing): Objectives (end results), Strategy (comprehensive plan for achieving organisational objectives; three dimensions: determining long-term objectives, adopting a course of action and allocating resources; includes the environment), Policy (general statements guiding thinking and action in decision making; a standing plan that provides the framework within which action takes place), Procedure (the chronological sequence of steps to be taken to perform a task; standing plan), Method (the way in which a task has to be performed; the prescribed way), Rule (specific statement that tells what is to be done; no discretion), Programme (a detailed statement about a project covering objectives, policies, procedures, rules, tasks, human and physical resources), Budget (statement of expected results expressed in numerical terms). Difference: a policy gives direction for thinking, a rule is rigid and must be followed; a procedure is a sequence of steps whereas a method is a single way of performing one step. Plans are made at all levels; planning is done by managers at all levels but top management sets strategy. Planning premises affect the quality of plans. Single-use plans are programmes, budgets and projects; standing plans are policies, procedures, methods and rules. Planning and controlling are inseparable: planning without control is meaningless and control without planning is blind. Planning is a primary function because other functions follow it. Planning is based on forecasting.
## Deep dive: Concept and importance
Planning provides direction and reduces risk. It sets standards for control. It is a continuous and pervasive function.
## Deep dive: Process
The planning process runs from objectives to follow-up. Premises and alternatives are key steps.
## Deep dive: Types of plans
Objectives, strategy, policy, procedure, method, rule, programme and budget differ in scope and rigidity.
