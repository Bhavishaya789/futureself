# Staffing
minutes: 45
yt: class 12 business studies staffing recruitment selection training human resource management
@study
Staffing is the process of finding the right people for the right job: it includes recruitment, selection, placement, training, development, appraisal and compensation of personnel. Human resource management is the broader term; it covers staffing. Importance: obtaining competent personnel, higher performance, continuous survival and growth, optimum utilisation of human resources, job satisfaction and morale. Staffing is a function of all managers, and a part of the human resource department in large organisations. The Staffing process: (1) estimating manpower requirements (workload analysis and workforce analysis), (2) recruitment, (3) selection, (4) placement and orientation (induction), (5) training and development, (6) performance appraisal and (7) promotion and career planning and compensation. Recruitment is the process of searching for prospective employees and stimulating them to apply for jobs. Sources: internal (transfers, promotions) and external (direct recruitment, casual callers, advertisement, employment exchanges, placement agencies and management consultants, campus recruitment, recommendations of employees, labour contractors, web publishing and e-recruitment). Internal sources: transfer (shifting an employee from one job to another without change in status or pay) and promotion (shifting to a higher position with higher pay, status and responsibilities). Merits of internal recruitment: motivation, simple, economical, stability. Demerits: no new talent, limits the choice and may lead to rivalry. External sources: fresh talent, wider choice, competitive spirit; demerits: expensive, time consuming and demoralising for existing staff. Selection is the process of choosing from the pool of applicants the right candidate; steps: preliminary screening, selection tests (intelligence, aptitude, personality, trade or proficiency and interest tests), employment interview (structured and unstructured), reference and background checks, selection decision, medical examination, job offer and contract of employment. Recruitment is positive (attracting), while selection is negative (rejecting unsuitable). Training is the process of increasing the knowledge and skills of an employee for doing a particular job; development is the growth of an employee in all respects, concerned with the overall growth of managers. Training is job-oriented and short term; development is career oriented, long term. Importance of training: benefits to the organisation (systematic learning, higher productivity and less wastage) and employees (better career opportunities, higher earnings and more satisfaction). Methods: on-the-job (apprenticeship programmes, coaching, job rotation, internship training) and off-the-job (classroom lectures/conferences, films, case studies, computer modelling, vestibule training and programmed instruction). Induction is the introduction of a new employee to the organisation. Vestibule training provides training in a duplicate workplace. Internship training is a joint programme with educational institutions. Apprenticeship programmes provide both theoretical and practical training.
## Deep dive: Staffing process
Staffing runs from manpower planning to compensation. Recruitment and selection fill positions with suitable people.
## Deep dive: Recruitment and selection
Recruitment attracts applicants from internal and external sources. Selection filters by tests, interviews and checks.
## Deep dive: Training
Training improves skills for a job; development builds overall growth. On-the-job and off-the-job methods are used.
