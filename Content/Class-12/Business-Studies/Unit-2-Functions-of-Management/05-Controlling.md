# Controlling
minutes: 45
yt: class 12 business studies controlling importance process techniques budgetary control ROI PERT CPM
@study
Controlling is the management function of ensuring that activities are performed as per plans by comparing actual performance with standards and taking corrective action where deviations are found. It involves setting standards, measuring performance, comparing actual with standard, analysing deviations and taking corrective action. Features: controlling is an end function, a pervasive function, a forward-looking function, a continuous activity and has a close relation with planning (planning without controlling is meaningless and controlling without planning is blind). Importance: accomplishing organisational goals, judging accuracy of standards, making efficient use of resources, improving employee motivation, ensuring order and discipline and facilitating coordination in action. Limitations: difficulty in setting quantitative standards, little control on external factors, resistance from employees (for example, to surveillance by cameras) and costly affair. Process of controlling: (1) setting performance standards (quantitative or qualitative), (2) measurement of actual performance (personal observation, sample checking, performance reports), (3) comparing actual performance with standards, (4) analysing deviations (critical point control: the focus on key result areas, which are critical to the success; management by exception: attention only to significant deviations; the range of deviation is accepted) and (5) taking corrective action. Techniques of managerial control: Traditional techniques: personal observation; statistical reports; breakeven analysis (the level of output at which there is no profit or loss; BEP = Fixed cost / (Selling price per unit - Variable cost per unit)) and budgetary control (a technique of managerial control in which all operations are planned in advance in the form of budgets and actual results are compared with budgetary standards; budgets are of types: sales, production, cash, material, capital expenditure and research and development). Modern techniques: return on investment (ROI = Net income / Total investment; a useful technique to measure the overall performance of a business and its units), ratio analysis (liquidity, solvency, profitability and turnover ratios), responsibility accounting (a system of accounting in which different sections, divisions and departments of an organisation are set up as responsibility centres: cost centre, revenue centre, profit centre and investment centre), management audit (systematic appraisal of the overall performance of the management), PERT (Programme Evaluation and Review Technique) and CPM (Critical Path Method) — network techniques used for planning, scheduling and controlling large projects: steps are to define activities, develop the network, estimate time, identify the critical path (the longest path), and revise. PERT uses three time estimates (optimistic, pessimistic and most likely), suitable for projects where time estimates are uncertain; CPM uses a single time estimate, suitable for projects where activities are routine and where cost is also a factor. Management information system (MIS) is a computer-based information system that provides information and support for effective decision making. Controlling and planning: planning is the base. Standards should be flexible and quantifiable.
## Deep dive: Concept and process
Controlling compares actual with standards and corrects deviations. Critical point control and management by exception focus attention.
## Deep dive: Techniques
Traditional techniques include observation, reports, breakeven analysis and budgets. Modern techniques include ROI, ratio analysis, responsibility accounting, PERT and CPM.
## Deep dive: Planning and control
They are inseparable. Plans give standards; control checks results.
