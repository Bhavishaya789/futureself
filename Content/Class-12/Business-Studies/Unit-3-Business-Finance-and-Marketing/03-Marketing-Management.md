# Marketing Management
minutes: 45
yt: class 12 business studies marketing management marketing mix product branding pricing promotion
@study
Marketing is a social and managerial process by which individuals and groups obtain what they need and want through creating, offering and exchanging products of value with others. Marketing involves the creation of exchange, satisfaction of needs and wants, and profit. Features: needs and wants, creating a market offering, customer value, exchange mechanism. Marketing management is the planning, organising, directing and controlling of marketing activities. Philosophies: production concept, product concept, selling concept, marketing concept and societal marketing concept. Marketing vs selling: marketing focuses on customer needs and satisfaction with profit through satisfaction, selling focuses on the product and profit through sales volume. Marketing mix: the set of marketing tools used to pursue marketing objectives: product, price, place and promotion (the 4 Ps). Product is a bundle of benefits, a good, service or idea; classification: consumer goods (convenience: staples, impulse and emergency; shopping; specialty) and industrial goods (raw materials, installations, supplies and services). Branding: the process of giving a name, term, sign or symbol; brand name, trade mark (a brand or part of it that is legally registered). Benefits of branding to the customer: helps in identification of products, ensures quality, helps in status; to the marketer: differentiation, advertising and promotion programme, price differential, and goodwill building. Features of a good brand name: short and simple, easy to spell, pronounce and recognise, suggestive of the product's benefits, distinctive and adaptable, capable of being registered and protected legally. Packaging: the activity of designing and producing a container or wrapper; levels: primary (immediate container), secondary (additional layer), transportation packaging. Functions: product identification, product protection, convenience, promotion. Labelling: the display of information on or with the package; functions: describes the product and specifies its contents, identification of the product or brand, grading of products, helps in promotion of products and provides information required by law. Price: the amount of money paid for a product; factors affecting price determination: product cost, utility and demand, extent of competition in the market, government and legal regulations, pricing objectives and marketing methods used. Pricing objectives: obtaining market share leadership, surviving in a competitive market, attaining product quality leadership, maximising current profit. Physical distribution and place: channels of distribution: direct (zero level), one-level (retailer), two-level (wholesaler and retailer) and three-level (agent). Role of channels: to bridge the gap between producer and consumer. Functions of the middlemen. Components of physical distribution: order processing, transportation, warehousing, inventory control and packaging. Promotion mix: advertising (paid, impersonal communication through mass media), personal selling (oral presentation to prospective buyers), sales promotion (short-term incentives to encourage purchase, such as rebates, discounts, refunds, product combinations, quantity gift, instant draw and scratch card, full finance @ 0%, sampling, contests) and publicity (non-paid; part of public relations), and public relations. Advertising objections: adds to cost, undermines social values, confuses buyers, encourages the sale of inferior products, and some advertisements are in bad taste. Advantages: reach and builds the brand image. AIDA model. Personal selling: personal interaction. Difference: advertising is impersonal, personal selling is face to face. Marketing mix also influences consumer behaviour.
## Deep dive: Marketing and mix
Marketing meets customer needs through the mix of product, price, place and promotion. Marketing focuses on satisfaction while selling focuses on volume.
## Deep dive: Product
Branding, packaging and labelling support the product. Good brand names are simple and legally protectable.
## Deep dive: Price, place and promotion
Price depends on cost, demand and competition. Channels deliver goods; promotion informs and persuades.
