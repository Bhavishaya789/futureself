# Financial Management
minutes: 45
yt: class 12 business studies financial management capital structure fixed capital working capital dividend
@study
Financial management is concerned with the optimal procurement as well as the usage of finance. Role: financial planning, financing, investment and control; it helps in determining financial needs, ensuring adequate funds at the least cost, maximising shareholders' wealth and managing risk. Objectives: wealth maximisation (maximise the market value of shares) is preferred over profit maximisation because it considers risk, time value of money and the interest of shareholders. Three decisions: investment decision, financing decision and dividend decision. Investment decision (capital budgeting) is the decision on how the firm's funds are invested in different assets; long-term capital budgeting decisions are about fixed assets, with factors affecting: cash flows of the project, the rate of return, the investment criteria involved and risk. Short-term investment decision is working capital management. Financing decision: the decision about the proportion of debt and equity; it depends on the cost of funds, risk and flotation cost. Factors affecting financing decisions: cost, risk, floatation cost, cash flow position, control considerations, state of capital markets, fixed operating cost and the tax rate. Dividend decision: how much of profit is distributed as dividends and how much is retained. Factors affecting dividend decision: amount of earnings, stability of earnings, stability of dividends, growth opportunities, cash flow position, shareholders' preference, taxation policy, stock market reaction, access to capital market and legal constraints. Financial planning is the preparation of a financial blueprint of an organisation's future operations; its objectives are to ensure availability of funds and to avoid excess funds; importance: ensures adequate funds, helps in avoiding business shocks and surprises, helps in co-ordinating various business functions, reduces waste, duplication of efforts and gaps in planning, and links present with future. Capital structure is the mix of owners' funds (equity share capital, preference share capital, retained earnings) and borrowed funds (debentures, loans). Financial leverage or trading on equity is the use of fixed-cost debt in the capital structure to increase the return to equity shareholders; when ROI exceeds the cost of debt, the earning per share rises. Debt-equity ratio = Debt / Equity. Factors affecting capital structure: cash flow position, interest coverage ratio (ICR = EBIT / Interest), debt service coverage ratio, return on investment, cost of debt, tax rate, cost of equity, floatation costs, risk consideration, flexibility, control, regulatory framework, stock market conditions, capital structure of companies in the same industry. Fixed capital is the investment in long-term assets; factors affecting the choice: nature of business, scale of operations, choice of technique, technology upgradation, growth prospects, diversification, financing alternatives and level of collaboration. Working capital is the capital required for day-to-day operations: Gross working capital = Total current assets; Net working capital = Current assets - Current liabilities. Factors affecting working capital requirements: nature of business, scale of operations, business cycle, seasonal factors, production cycle, credit allowed, credit availability, operating efficiency and the level of competition. Cost of capital and risk are important in decision making.
## Deep dive: Decisions
Investment, financing and dividend decisions aim to maximise shareholders' wealth. Each depends on cost, risk and returns.
## Deep dive: Capital structure
The mix of debt and equity affects risk and returns. Trading on equity raises EPS when ROI exceeds interest cost.
## Deep dive: Fixed and working capital
Fixed capital funds long-term assets; working capital funds day-to-day operations. Many factors decide requirements.
