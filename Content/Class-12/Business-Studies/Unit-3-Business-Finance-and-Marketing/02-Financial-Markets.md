# Financial Markets
minutes: 45
yt: class 12 business studies financial markets money market capital market stock exchange SEBI
@study
A financial market is a market for the creation and exchange of financial assets. Functions: mobilisation of savings and channelling them into the most productive uses, facilitating price discovery, providing liquidity to financial assets and reducing the cost of transactions. Types: money market and capital market. Money market is a market for short-term funds (up to one year), with the Reserve Bank of India as the regulator and no physical location; instruments: Treasury bills (T-bills; issued by the RBI on behalf of the Government of India, zero coupon, in denominations of Rs 25,000 with maturities of 14, 91, 182 and 364 days), Commercial paper (CP; introduced in 1990; unsecured promissory note issued by large, creditworthy companies with maturity from 15 days to one year), Call money (for one day; notice money 2 to 14 days; the interest rate is called the call rate), Certificate of deposit (CD; unsecured negotiable short-term instruments issued by commercial banks and financial institutions; introduced in 1989), and Commercial bill (a bill of exchange used to finance the working capital needs of business firms). Capital market is a market for long-term funds with the SEBI as the regulator; it consists of primary market and secondary market. Primary market (new issues market): deals with the issue of new securities for the first time; methods of floating new issues: public issue through prospectus (IPO and FPO), offer for sale (shares offered to the public by existing owners through intermediaries such as issuing houses or stockbrokers), private placement (allotment of securities to institutional investors and selected individuals), rights issue (the existing shareholders have the right to subscribe in proportion), and e-IPO (online through the stock exchange). Secondary market (stock exchange): where existing securities are bought and sold; functions: providing liquidity and marketability, pricing of securities, safety of transactions, contributing to economic growth, spreading of equity cult and providing scope for speculation. Stock exchange is an institution which provides a platform for buying and selling of existing securities. Stock exchanges in India: the Bombay Stock Exchange (BSE; 1875; oldest in Asia) and the National Stock Exchange (NSE; 1992; recognised as a stock exchange in 1993; started trading in 1994; the first demutualised electronic exchange). OTCEI (Over the Counter Exchange of India; 1992) and the Interconnected Stock Exchange of India. Trading procedure: select a broker; open a demat account with a depository (NSDL and CDSL) and a trading account; placing an order; executing the order; settlement (T+1 now; originally rolling settlement of T+2). Demat: dematerialisation (conversion of physical shares into electronic form), depository (an institution holding securities in electronic form; NSDL (1996) and CDSL (1999)). Depository participant (DP) is an agent of the depository. SEBI (Securities and Exchange Board of India) was established on 12 April 1992 under the SEBI Act 1992; objectives: to regulate stock exchanges and the securities industry to promote their orderly functioning, to protect the rights and interests of investors and to prevent trading malpractices. Functions: regulatory (registration of brokers, regulation of stock exchanges, prohibition of insider trading and fraudulent practices), development (investor education, training of intermediaries) and protective. Sensex is the index of the BSE of 30 companies; Nifty is the index of NSE of 50 companies.
## Deep dive: Money market
It deals in short-term instruments such as T-bills, commercial paper, call money and CDs under RBI regulation.
## Deep dive: Capital market
The primary market issues new securities; the secondary market trades existing ones. SEBI regulates it.
## Deep dive: Trading
Trading needs a broker, demat and trading accounts. NSDL and CDSL are depositories. Sensex and Nifty are indices.

A: Money market deals in funds up to one year regulated by RBI; capital market in long-term funds regulated by SEBI.
