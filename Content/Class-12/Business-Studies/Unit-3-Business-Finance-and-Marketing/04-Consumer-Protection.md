# Consumer Protection
minutes: 45
yt: class 12 business studies consumer protection act 2019 rights responsibilities redressal
@study
A consumer is a person who buys goods or avails any service for a consideration for personal use. The Consumer Protection Act, 2019 replaced the Consumer Protection Act, 1986 and came into effect in July 2020. Importance of consumer protection: from the consumer's point of view (consumer ignorance, unorganised consumers, widespread exploitation) and from the business point of view (long-term interest, social responsibility, moral justification, government intervention, survival and growth). Ways and means of consumer protection: efforts of business (self-regulation, chambers of commerce, business associations), consumer awareness, consumer organisations and non-governmental organisations (voluntary consumer associations; Consumer Guidance Society of India) and government (legislation). Legal framework: Consumer Protection Act (CPA), Sale of Goods Act 1930, Indian Contract Act 1872, Essential Commodities Act 1955, Agricultural Produce (Grading and Marketing) Act 1937, Prevention of Food Adulteration Act 1954 (now Food Safety and Standards Act, 2006), Standards of Weights and Measures Act 1976, Trade Marks Act 1999, Competition Act 2002, and the Bureau of Indian Standards Act 2016 (BIS; ISI mark for industrial products; Agmark for agricultural products; Hallmark for jewellery). Consumer rights (CPA 2019, Section 2(9)): (1) right to safety, (2) right to be informed, (3) right to choose, (4) right to be heard, (5) right to seek redressal and (6) right to consumer awareness. Consumer responsibilities: be aware of various goods and services, buy only standardised goods, learn about the risks associated with products, read labels carefully, assert yourself to ensure a fair deal, be honest in your dealings, ask for a cash memo, use the product as per instructions, be conscious of your rights and file a complaint in an appropriate consumer forum, and protect the environment. Who can file a complaint: a consumer, any recognised consumer association, one or more consumers with the same interest, the Central or State Government, the Central Consumer Protection Authority, or legal heir or representative of a deceased consumer. Redressal: Consumer Disputes Redressal Agencies: District Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission (up to Rs 1 crore), State Commission (above Rs 1 crore up to Rs 10 crore) and National Commission (above Rs 10 crore). Appeals: from District to State, from State to National and from National to the Supreme Court (within 30 days). Reliefs available to the consumer: to remove the defect in goods, replace the goods, refund the price, pay compensation for loss or injury, discontinue unfair trade practices, withdraw hazardous goods, issue corrective advertisement, and pay punitive damages and cost of the litigation. Central Consumer Protection Authority (CCPA) regulates matters relating to violation of consumer rights, unfair trade practices and false or misleading advertisements. Product liability: the liability of the manufacturer, service provider or seller for harm caused by a defective product. E-commerce is covered; mediation cells are set up. National Consumer Day is observed on 24 December (the date the CPA 1986 was enacted) and World Consumer Rights Day on 15 March. Fee: the complaints to be filed in writing with the registered post; for claims up to Rs 5 lakh there is no fee. Consumer protection and unfair trade practices.
## Deep dive: Rights and responsibilities
Consumers have six rights and corresponding responsibilities. Awareness and assertiveness are essential.
## Deep dive: Redressal
District, State and National commissions handle disputes by value. Reliefs include repair, replacement, refund and compensation.
## Deep dive: Law
The Consumer Protection Act, 2019 created the CCPA and covers product liability and e-commerce. Other laws protect consumers too.
