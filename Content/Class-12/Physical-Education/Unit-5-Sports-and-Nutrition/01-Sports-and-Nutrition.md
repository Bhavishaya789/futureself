# Sports and Nutrition
minutes: 40
yt: class 12 physical education sports and nutrition balanced diet macro micro nutrients carbohydrates proteins fats vitamins pre competition diet
@study
Nutrition is the process by which the body takes in and uses food. A balanced diet gives all nutrients in the right proportion to meet the needs of the body. Macronutrients are needed in large amounts: carbohydrates (4 kcal per gram, the main fuel), proteins (4 kcal per gram, for growth and repair) and fats (9 kcal per gram, concentrated energy). Micronutrients are needed in small amounts: vitamins and minerals. Vitamins A, D, E and K are fat soluble; vitamins B complex and C are water soluble. Deficiency diseases include night blindness (vitamin A), rickets (vitamin D), scurvy (vitamin C), beriberi (vitamin B1), goitre (iodine) and anaemia (iron). Calcium builds bones and teeth, iron forms haemoglobin, and sodium and potassium keep fluid balance. Water, about 60 per cent of body weight, regulates temperature and carries nutrients. Fibre or roughage is a non-nutritive component that aids digestion. Nutritive components give energy and growth; non-nutritive components such as fibre, water and colour agents do not give energy. Food intake and energy balance affect weight: healthy weight is judged by BMI, which equals weight in kg divided by height in metres squared. Pre-competition meals should be light, easy to digest and eaten about three to four hours before, with carbohydrates. During competition fluids and quick energy such as glucose are useful. After competition, carbohydrates and proteins restore glycogen and repair muscle. Eating disorders such as anorexia and bulimia harm athletes. Myths include that more protein always means more muscle, and that supplements replace food. Food intolerance and allergy need care.
## Deep dive: Nutrients
Carbohydrates and fats give energy, proteins build and repair, vitamins and minerals protect and regulate.
## Deep dive: Balanced diet
The right amounts of all nutrients support growth, performance and healthy weight measured by BMI.
## Deep dive: Sports meals
Pre-competition meals are light with carbohydrates; post-competition meals restore glycogen and repair tissue.
