# Training in Sports
minutes: 40
yt: class 12 physical education training in sports strength endurance speed flexibility coordination circuit training fartlek interval
@study
Sports training is a planned, systematic process to improve performance. Its aim is the development of motor qualities through load, overload and recovery. Strength is of three kinds: maximum strength (the greatest force in one effort), explosive strength (fast force, or power) and strength endurance (repeated force over time). Methods are isometric (muscle length does not change), isotonic (muscle length changes) and isokinetic (constant speed). Endurance is aerobic (with oxygen) or anaerobic (without enough oxygen), and its methods are continuous running, interval training (work and recovery intervals) and fartlek (speed play, a Swedish method with varying pace over natural ground). Speed has reaction, movement and acceleration abilities; acceleration runs and pace runs develop it. Flexibility is active (using own muscles) or passive (with outside help), and methods include static stretching, ballistic stretching and PNF (proprioceptive neuromuscular facilitation). Coordinative abilities are orientation, differentiation, adaptation, rhythm, reaction, balance and coupling. Circuit training uses a series of stations done in turn, developed by Morgan and Adamson at the University of Leeds in 1953. Principles of training include overload, progression, specificity, individuality, reversibility and variety. Adaptation occurs during recovery, and supercompensation is the rise above the previous level. A warm-up prepares the body and a cool-down helps recovery. Periodisation divides the year into preparatory, competitive and transition periods.
## Deep dive: Motor qualities
Strength, endurance, speed, flexibility and coordination are developed by specific methods.
## Deep dive: Methods
Isometric, isotonic and isokinetic exercises; continuous, interval and fartlek runs; static, ballistic and PNF stretches.
## Deep dive: Planning
Overload, progression, recovery and periodisation turn load into adaptation and peak form.
