# Biomechanics and Sports
minutes: 40
yt: class 12 physical education biomechanics newton laws levers equilibrium friction projectile axes planes
@study
Biomechanics applies the laws of mechanics to living bodies and sport. Newton’s first law, the law of inertia, says a body stays at rest or in uniform motion unless acted on by a force; a footballer sliding after stopping and a runner continuing after the finish line show it. The second law says force equals mass times acceleration, F = ma; a harder kick gives a bigger acceleration. The third law says every action has an equal and opposite reaction; a sprinter pushes the ground back and the ground pushes forward. Levers have an effort, a load and a fulcrum. In a first class lever the fulcrum lies between effort and load, as in nodding the head; in a second class lever the load lies between fulcrum and effort, as in raising the heels; in a third class lever the effort lies between fulcrum and load, as in the biceps curl, the most common type in the body. Equilibrium is a state of balance: static when at rest and dynamic when moving. It is stable, unstable or neutral. Stability increases with a lower centre of gravity, a larger base of support, a heavier body and a line of gravity falling within the base. Friction opposes motion; static, sliding and rolling friction matter, and spikes and grip raise useful friction while lubrication reduces harmful friction. A projectile follows a curved path under gravity; the optimum angle of release for maximum range at the same height is 45 degrees. Planes divide the body: sagittal (left and right), frontal (front and back) and transverse (upper and lower), and movements occur around axes. Movement terms include flexion, extension, abduction, adduction, rotation and circumduction.
## Deep dive: Newton’s laws
Inertia, F = ma and action reaction explain starts, kicks, throws and landings.
## Deep dive: Levers
First, second and third class levers differ in the position of the fulcrum, load and effort.
## Deep dive: Equilibrium and projectiles
A low centre of gravity and wide base add stability; 45 degrees gives the maximum range.
