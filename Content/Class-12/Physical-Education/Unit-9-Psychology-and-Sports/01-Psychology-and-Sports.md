# Psychology and Sports
minutes: 40

@study
Sports psychology studies the mind of athletes and its effect on performance. Personality is the sum of the characteristics that make a person unique. Carl Jung classified people as introverts and extroverts; introverts are shy and inward looking, extroverts are outgoing and sociable. Sheldon’s body type theory links body build and temperament: endomorphic (round, soft; relaxed and sociable), mesomorphic (muscular; energetic and assertive) and ectomorphic (thin; reserved and thoughtful). The Big Five traits are openness, conscientiousness, extraversion, agreeableness and neuroticism. Motivation is the inner drive that directs behaviour; intrinsic motivation comes from within, for enjoyment, and extrinsic motivation comes from rewards and recognition. Techniques to raise motivation include goal setting, rewards, praise, varied training and a good coach. Exercise adherence means sticking to a regular exercise programme; it improves with enjoyment, social support, convenient time and place, and realistic goals, and falls with injury, lack of time and boredom. Aggression is behaviour intended to harm. Hostile aggression aims to hurt another, while instrumental aggression is used to achieve a goal, and assertive behaviour is firm but within the rules. Psychological attributes include self-esteem (how one values oneself), mental imagery (rehearsing performance in the mind, internal or external), self-talk (positive statements to oneself) and goal setting. SMART goals are Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Realistic and Time bound. Relaxation and concentration help control anxiety.
## Deep dive: Personality
Jung’s introvert and extrovert, Sheldon’s body types and the Big Five describe personality.
## Deep dive: Motivation and adherence
Intrinsic and extrinsic motives, goals and support keep athletes training.
## Deep dive: Mental skills
Self-esteem, imagery, self-talk and SMART goals improve performance and control aggression.
