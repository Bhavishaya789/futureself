# Children and Women in Sports
minutes: 40
yt: class 12 physical education children and women in sports motor development postural deformities female athlete triad
@study
Children go through physical, motor, mental and social development. Motor development is the progressive change in movement skills, and it depends on age, nutrition, heredity, environment and practice. Exercise guidelines for children recommend at least 60 minutes of moderate to vigorous physical activity daily, with strengthening activities three days a week. Regular activity improves growth, fitness, bone health, posture, concentration and confidence, while a sedentary lifestyle causes obesity and diabetes. Postural deformities include knock knees (the knees touch while the ankles are apart), bow legs (the knees are apart while the ankles touch), flat foot (the arch of the foot is flattened), round shoulders, kyphosis (excess forward curve of the upper back), lordosis (excess inward curve of the lower back) and scoliosis (sideways curve of the spine). Corrective exercises include stretching, strengthening, yoga and, where needed, medical advice. Women face special concerns in sports. Female athlete triad refers to the combination of disordered eating, amenorrhoea (missed menstrual cycles) and osteoporosis (weak bones). Causes include low energy intake and heavy training; prevention includes balanced diet, adequate calcium and iron, rest and counselling. Menstrual dysfunction and iron deficiency anaemia are common. Women’s participation is influenced by social attitudes, safety, facilities, lack of role models and family support. Measures to promote it include equal opportunity, women coaches, scholarships, safe facilities and media coverage. Pregnancy and post-pregnancy exercise should follow medical advice.
## Deep dive: Children’s activity
Sixty minutes of daily activity supports growth and fitness and prevents lifestyle disease.
## Deep dive: Postural deformities
Knock knees, bow legs, flat foot, kyphosis, lordosis and scoliosis are corrected by exercises and care.
## Deep dive: Women in sports
The female athlete triad and social barriers need nutrition, counselling and supportive policies.
