# Nuclei
minutes: 40
yt: class 12 physics nuclei nuclear size mass defect binding energy radioactivity fission fusion half life
@study
The nucleus consists of protons and neutrons (nucleons); the neutron was discovered by Chadwick in 1932. Atomic number Z is the number of protons, mass number A is the number of nucleons and neutron number is N = A - Z. Isotopes have the same Z but different A, isobars have the same A, and isotones have the same N. The atomic mass unit is 1 u = 1.66 x 10^-27 kg = 931.5 MeV/c^2. Nuclear radius R = R0 A^(1/3) with R0 = 1.2 fm, so nuclear density is about 2.3 x 10^17 kg/m^3 and independent of A. Mass defect is the difference between the sum of nucleon masses and the nuclear mass, and binding energy is mass defect times c^2. The binding energy per nucleon rises to a maximum of about 8.75 MeV near iron (A about 56) and then falls, so fusion of light nuclei and fission of heavy nuclei both release energy. Nuclear force is short range, strong, charge independent and saturated. Radioactivity was discovered by Becquerel in 1896. Alpha particles are helium nuclei, beta particles are electrons or positrons, gamma rays are photons. The decay law is N = N0 e^(-lambda t); half life T = 0.693/lambda; mean life is 1/lambda = 1.44 T. Activity R = lambda N, in becquerel (one decay per second); one curie is 3.7 x 10^10 Bq. In alpha decay Z falls by 2 and A by 4; in beta minus decay a neutron changes to a proton with an antineutrino; in beta plus decay a proton changes to a neutron with a neutrino. Fission, discovered by Hahn and Strassmann in 1938, splits a heavy nucleus like U-235 into two with about 200 MeV energy and neutrons that sustain a chain reaction; reactors use moderators and control rods. Fusion powers the Sun and needs very high temperature.
## Deep dive: Nuclear structure
R = R0 A^(1/3); constant density; mass defect gives binding energy E = delta m c^2.
## Deep dive: Radioactivity
N = N0 e^(-lambda t); half life 0.693/lambda; activity lambda N.
## Deep dive: Fission and fusion
Binding energy per nucleon peaks near iron; fission and fusion each release energy.
