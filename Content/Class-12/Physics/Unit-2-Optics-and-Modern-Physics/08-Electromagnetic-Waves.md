# Electromagnetic Waves

@study
Maxwell found that a changing electric field also produces a magnetic field and introduced the displacement current Id = epsilon0 d(phi_E)/dt, which makes Ampere’s law consistent. Maxwell’s four equations are Gauss’s law for electricity, Gauss’s law for magnetism, Faraday’s law and the Ampere-Maxwell law. They predict electromagnetic waves, whose speed in vacuum is c = 1/sqrt(mu0 epsilon0) = 3 x 10^8 m/s, equal to the speed of light, so light is electromagnetic. Hertz produced and detected the waves in 1887. EM waves are transverse: E and B are perpendicular to each other and to the direction of travel, in phase, with E0/B0 = c. They need no medium, carry energy and momentum, and exert radiation pressure. In a medium, v = 1/sqrt(mu epsilon) and n = c/v. The energy density is shared equally between E and B, and the intensity is I = (1/2) epsilon0 E0^2 c. The spectrum in order of decreasing wavelength is radio waves, microwaves, infrared, visible, ultraviolet, X-rays and gamma rays. Radio waves are used in communication and are produced by oscillating circuits. Microwaves are used in radar and microwave ovens (water molecules resonate near 2.45 GHz) and are produced by klystrons and magnetrons. Infrared waves come from hot bodies, are used in remote controls, physiotherapy and the greenhouse effect, and are called heat waves. Visible light spans about 400 nm to 700 nm. Ultraviolet waves come from the Sun and are used in sterilisation and LASIK, and the ozone layer absorbs most of them. X-rays, discovered by Roentgen in 1895, are produced when fast electrons strike a metal target and are used in medicine and security. Gamma rays come from nuclei and are used in cancer treatment.
## Deep dive: Displacement current
A changing electric field acts like a current Id = epsilon0 dphi_E/dt and generates a magnetic field.
## Deep dive: Wave properties
Transverse, in phase, E/B = c, carrying energy and momentum; speed 1/sqrt(mu0 epsilon0).
## Deep dive: Spectrum
Radio, microwave, infrared, visible, ultraviolet, X-ray and gamma rays differ in wavelength, source and use.
