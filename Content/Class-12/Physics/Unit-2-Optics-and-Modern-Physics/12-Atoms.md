Thomson’s plum pudding model pictured the atom as a positive sphere with electrons embedded in it. Geiger and Marsden, under Rutherford, scattered alpha particles from thin gold foil. Most passed straight through, a few were deflected through large angles and about one in 8000 turned back, so the positive charge and nearly all the mass lie in a tiny nucleus of size about 10^-15 m, compared with the atom’s 10^-10 m. Rutherford proposed the nuclear atom in 1911. The distance of closest approach for an alpha particle of kinetic energy K is r0 = (1/4 pi epsilon0) 2Ze^2/K. Rutherford’s model could not explain stability (an orbiting electron should radiate and spiral in) or the line spectra. Bohr’s model (1913) assumes stationary orbits with no radiation, quantised angular momentum mvr = nh/2 pi, and emission or absorption of a photon when an electron jumps, h nu = Ei - Ef. For hydrogen-like atoms, r_n = n^2 a0/Z with a0 = 0.529 angstrom, and E_n = -13.6 Z^2/n^2 eV. The ground state is -13.6 eV, so the ionisation energy is 13.6 eV; the first excited state is -3.4 eV. Kinetic energy is +13.6/n^2 eV and potential energy is -27.2/n^2 eV. Speed v_n is proportional to Z/n (about 2.19 x 10^6 m/s for n = 1) and time period grows as n^3. The Rydberg formula is 1/lambda = R(1/n1^2 - 1/n2^2) with R = 1.097 x 10^7 per metre. The spectral series are Lyman (n1 = 1, ultraviolet), Balmer (n1 = 2, visible), Paschen (n1 = 3, infrared), Brackett (n1 = 4) and Pfund (n1 = 5). De Broglie explained Bohr’s rule by standing waves, 2 pi r = n lambda. Bohr’s theory fails for multi-electron atoms and does not explain fine structure or intensities.
## Deep dive: Rutherford model
Alpha scattering gave a tiny, dense, positive nucleus; the model could not explain stability or spectra.
## Deep dive: Bohr model
Quantised orbits mvr = nh/2 pi, r_n = n^2 a0, E_n = -13.6/n^2 eV, and photon h nu = Ei - Ef.
## Deep dive: Spectral series
Lyman, Balmer, Paschen, Brackett and Pfund correspond to final levels 1 to 5.
