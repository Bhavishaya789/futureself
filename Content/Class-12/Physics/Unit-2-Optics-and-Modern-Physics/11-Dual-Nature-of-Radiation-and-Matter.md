# Dual Nature of Radiation and Matter
minutes: 40
yt: class 12 physics dual nature photoelectric effect einstein equation de broglie davisson germer work function stopping potential
@study
The photoelectric effect is the emission of electrons from a metal surface when light of sufficient frequency falls on it. Hertz and Hallwachs observed it and Lenard studied it. The experimental facts: for a given frequency, the photocurrent is proportional to intensity; there is a threshold frequency nu0 below which no emission occurs, however intense the light; the maximum kinetic energy of photoelectrons depends on frequency, not intensity; and emission is instantaneous. The stopping potential V0 is the reverse voltage that stops the fastest electrons, so eV0 = KEmax. Classical wave theory failed to explain threshold, intensity independence and instant emission. Einstein (1905) proposed that light consists of quanta (photons), with E = h nu = hc/lambda, where h = 6.63 x 10^-34 J s; hc = 1240 eV nm. Einstein’s photoelectric equation is KEmax = h nu - phi0, where phi0 = h nu0 is the work function. A graph of V0 against nu is a straight line of slope h/e, with intercept on the frequency axis equal to nu0. Millikan verified it in 1916. A photon has momentum p = h/lambda and energy but zero rest mass. De Broglie (1924) proposed that matter has wave nature: lambda = h/p = h/mv. For an electron accelerated through V volts, lambda = 12.27/sqrt V angstrom. For a particle of kinetic energy K, lambda = h/sqrt(2mK). Davisson and Germer (1927) confirmed electron diffraction from a nickel crystal. Matter waves are not electromagnetic. The Heisenberg principle limits simultaneous knowledge of position and momentum. Photocells are used in alarms, automatic doors and light meters.
## Deep dive: Photoelectric effect
Threshold frequency, intensity-proportional current, instantaneous emission and KE depending on frequency.
## Deep dive: Einstein’s equation
KEmax = h nu - phi0 and eV0 = h nu - phi0; the V0 versus nu graph has slope h/e.
## Deep dive: Matter waves
De Broglie lambda = h/mv; electrons diffract in the Davisson-Germer experiment.
