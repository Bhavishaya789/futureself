# Wave Optics
minutes: 40
yt: class 12 physics wave optics huygens interference young double slit diffraction polarisation brewster malus
@study
Wave optics treats light as a wave. A wavefront is the locus of points in the same phase. Huygens’s principle says every point on a wavefront is a source of secondary wavelets, and the new wavefront is the envelope; it explains reflection and refraction. Interference is the superposition of two coherent waves. Constructive interference occurs for path difference n lambda and destructive for (n + 1/2) lambda. Young’s double slit experiment (1801) gives fringes of equal width beta = lambda D/d on a screen at distance D with slit separation d. The intensity is I = 4 I0 cos^2(phi/2). For amplitudes a1 and a2, Imax/Imin = ((a1 + a2)/(a1 - a2))^2. Sources must be coherent: constant phase difference from the same source. Diffraction is bending of light around obstacles or through narrow openings of size comparable to the wavelength. In single slit diffraction, minima occur at a sin theta = n lambda, the central maximum has angular width 2 lambda/a and is the brightest and widest, and its linear width is 2 lambda D/a. The Fresnel distance is a^2/lambda. Polarisation shows that light is a transverse wave. Unpolarised light passing a polaroid is reduced to half intensity. Malus’s law states I = I0 cos^2 theta for polarised light through an analyser. Brewster’s law gives the polarising angle with tan i_B = n, at which reflected and refracted rays are at 90 degrees. Polaroids reduce glare and are used in sunglasses, cameras and 3D glasses. Longitudinal waves such as sound cannot be polarised.
## Deep dive: Huygens and interference
Secondary wavelets explain reflection and refraction; coherent waves interfere with beta = lambda D/d.
## Deep dive: Diffraction
Single slit minima at a sin theta = n lambda; the central maximum is widest and brightest.
## Deep dive: Polarisation
Malus I = I0 cos^2 theta and Brewster tan i_B = n show light is transverse.
