# Ray Optics and Optical Instruments
minutes: 40
yt: class 12 physics ray optics mirror lens refraction total internal reflection prism microscope telescope eye
@study
Ray optics treats light as straight rays. For spherical mirrors, 1/v + 1/u = 1/f with f = R/2 and magnification m = -v/u, using the Cartesian sign convention. Refraction obeys Snell’s law, n1 sin i = n2 sin r, with refractive index n = c/v. Total internal reflection needs light going from a denser to a rarer medium at an angle above the critical angle C, where sin C = 1/n; it is used in optical fibres, prisms in binoculars and the sparkle of diamond, and causes mirages. Refraction at a spherical surface gives n2/v - n1/u = (n2 - n1)/R. For a thin lens, 1/v - 1/u = 1/f and the lens maker’s formula is 1/f = (n - 1)(1/R1 - 1/R2); magnification m = v/u. Power P = 1/f in dioptre, and thin lenses in contact give P = P1 + P2. A prism deviates light; for a thin prism delta = (n - 1)A, and for a prism n = sin((A + Dm)/2)/sin(A/2) at minimum deviation. Dispersion splits white light because n depends on wavelength; red bends least and violet most. The blue sky is due to Rayleigh scattering, proportional to 1/lambda^4, and red sunsets occur as blue is scattered away. A simple microscope gives m = 1 + D/f with D = 25 cm. A compound microscope gives m = (L/fo)(1 + D/fe) approximately (L is tube length). An astronomical telescope in normal adjustment has m = fo/fe and length fo + fe. Reflecting telescopes avoid chromatic aberration and allow large mirrors. In the eye, myopia is corrected by a concave lens, hypermetropia by a convex lens, presbyopia by bifocals and astigmatism by cylindrical lenses.
## Deep dive: Mirrors and lenses
1/v + 1/u = 1/f for mirrors; 1/v - 1/u = 1/f for lenses; lens maker 1/f = (n-1)(1/R1 - 1/R2).
## Deep dive: Total internal reflection
Dense to rare above the critical angle with sin C = 1/n gives fibre optics and diamond sparkle.
## Deep dive: Instruments
Microscope and telescope magnifications and eye defects with corrective lenses.
