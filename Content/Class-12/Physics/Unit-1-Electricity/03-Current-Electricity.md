# Current Electricity
minutes: 40
yt: class 12 physics current electricity ohm law drift velocity resistivity kirchhoff wheatstone potentiometer cells
@study
Electric current is the rate of flow of charge, I = Q/t, in ampere. In a conductor, free electrons drift with drift velocity v_d = eE tau/m, and I = n e A v_d. Ohm’s law says V = IR for a conductor at constant temperature. Resistance R = rho L / A, where resistivity rho depends on the material and temperature, not on size. For metals resistivity rises with temperature (alpha positive), for semiconductors it falls, and alloys like manganin and constantan have a very small temperature coefficient. Conductivity sigma = 1/rho, and mobility mu = v_d/E. Resistors in series add, R = R1 + R2; in parallel 1/R = 1/R1 + 1/R2. A cell has emf E and internal resistance r; terminal voltage V = E - Ir, and the current is I = E/(R + r). Cells in series add emf and internal resistance; identical cells in parallel keep emf and reduce internal resistance. Kirchhoff’s laws: the junction rule (sum of currents at a node is zero, conservation of charge) and the loop rule (sum of potential changes around a loop is zero, conservation of energy). A Wheatstone bridge is balanced when P/Q = R/S, with no galvanometer current. The metre bridge applies this principle. A potentiometer measures emf without drawing current from the cell, and compares emfs by balance lengths: E1/E2 = l1/l2. Electrical power P = VI = I^2 R = V^2/R, and energy is Pt; the kilowatt hour is 3.6 x 10^6 J. Heating effect is H = I^2 R t. Colour code gives resistor values.
## Deep dive: Drift and Ohm
I = neAv_d; V = IR; R = rho L/A and resistivity depends on temperature.
## Deep dive: Cells and circuits
V = E - Ir; Kirchhoff’s laws and the Wheatstone bridge solve networks.
## Deep dive: Potentiometer and power
The potentiometer compares emfs by balance length; P = VI = I^2R.
