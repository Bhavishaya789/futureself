# Electric Charges and Fields

@study
Electric charge is a basic property of matter and is of two kinds, positive and negative. Like charges repel and unlike charges attract. Charge is conserved (it can neither be created nor destroyed in an isolated system), quantised (q = ne, with e = 1.6 x 10^-19 C) and additive. Bodies can be charged by friction, conduction and induction. Coulomb’s law gives the force between two point charges: F = k q1 q2 / r^2, with k = 1/(4 pi epsilon0) = 9 x 10^9 N m^2 C^-2, where epsilon0 = 8.85 x 10^-12 C^2 N^-1 m^-2. The force acts along the line joining the charges and obeys superposition. The electric field is the force per unit positive test charge, E = F/q, with unit N/C or V/m; for a point charge E = kq/r^2. Field lines begin on positive and end on negative charges, never cross, and are closer where the field is stronger. An electric dipole is a pair of equal and opposite charges separated by 2a, with dipole moment p = q x 2a pointing from negative to positive. On the axis, E = 2kp/r^3 (for r much greater than a) along p; on the equatorial line, E = kp/r^3 opposite to p. In a uniform field a dipole feels torque tau = pE sin theta and has potential energy U = -pE cos theta. Electric flux is phi = E A cos theta, in N m^2/C. Gauss’s law states that the total flux through a closed surface equals q enclosed divided by epsilon0. Applications: an infinite line charge gives E = lambda/(2 pi epsilon0 r); an infinite plane sheet gives E = sigma/(2 epsilon0); near a charged conductor E = sigma/epsilon0; a thin spherical shell gives E = kq/r^2 outside and zero inside. Charge on a conductor lies on its outer surface, and the field inside a conductor is zero.
## Deep dive: Coulomb’s law and field
F = kq1q2/r^2 and E = kq/r^2 describe forces and fields of point charges and obey superposition.
## Deep dive: Dipole
Dipole moment p = 2aq; axial field 2kp/r^3, equatorial field kp/r^3; torque pE sin theta.
## Deep dive: Gauss’s law
Flux through a closed surface equals q enclosed / epsilon0, giving fields of lines, sheets and shells.
