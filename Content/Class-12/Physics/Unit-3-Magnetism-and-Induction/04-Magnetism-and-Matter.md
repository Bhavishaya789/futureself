# Magnetism and Matter

@study
A bar magnet has two poles of equal strength, and isolated magnetic poles (monopoles) do not exist. A freely suspended magnet points north-south. Magnetic field lines form closed loops, run from north to south outside the magnet and south to north inside, never cross, and are closer where the field is stronger. A current loop behaves as a magnetic dipole with moment m = NIA in A m^2. For a bar magnet, the axial field is B = (mu0/4pi) 2m/r^3 and the equatorial field is B = (mu0/4pi) m/r^3, opposite to m. In a uniform field the torque is mB sin theta and the potential energy is -mB cos theta. Gauss’s law for magnetism says the net flux through any closed surface is zero. Earth’s magnetism is described by three elements: declination (angle between magnetic and geographic meridians), dip or inclination (angle of the total field with the horizontal) and the horizontal component B_H. B_H = B cos delta, B_V = B sin delta and tan delta = B_V/B_H. At the magnetic equator the dip is zero; at the poles it is 90 degrees. Magnetisation M is magnetic moment per unit volume, H is magnetising field, B = mu0 (H + M), susceptibility chi = M/H and relative permeability mu_r = 1 + chi. Diamagnetic substances have small negative chi and are weakly repelled; paramagnetic substances have small positive chi, obey Curie’s law chi = C/T and are weakly attracted; ferromagnetic substances have large positive chi because of domains and lose ferromagnetism above the Curie temperature. Superconductors are perfectly diamagnetic. Hysteresis is the lag of B behind H; the loop gives retentivity and coercivity. Soft iron has low retentivity and coercivity, suiting electromagnets and transformer cores; steel has high values, suiting permanent magnets.
## Deep dive: Bar magnet
Field lines are closed loops; axial field 2m mu0/4pi r^3 and equatorial half as large and opposite.
## Deep dive: Earth’s magnetism
Declination, dip and horizontal component describe the field; tan dip = B_V/B_H.
## Deep dive: Magnetic materials
Dia, para and ferromagnets differ in susceptibility; hysteresis guides material choice.

Vertical component | B_V = B sin delta
Dip | tan delta = B_V / B_H
Total field | B = sqrt(B_H^2 + B_V^2)

## Advanced
P: Why are soft iron and steel chosen for different uses?
A: Soft iron has low retentivity and coercivity and small hysteresis loss, so it suits electromagnets and transformer cores; steel has high retentivity and coercivity so it holds magnetism as a permanent magnet.
R: 2 | Soft iron low loss
R: 2 | Steel high coercivity
R: 2 | Cores
