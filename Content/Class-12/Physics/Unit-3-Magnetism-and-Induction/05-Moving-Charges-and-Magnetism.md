# Moving charges and magnetism

@study
A charge q moving with velocity v in field B feels F = q v B sin theta. Biot-Savart gives B = (mu0/4 pi) I dl x r / r^3; a long straight wire gives B = mu0 I/(2 pi r), a circular loop centre B = mu0 I/(2R), a solenoid B = mu0 n I. A current-carrying wire in a field feels F = B I L sin theta; a loop has torque tau = N I A B sin theta.
## Deep dive: force on charges and currents
The magnetic force on a charge is F = q (v x B), magnitude q v B sin theta, perpendicular to both v and B, so it does no work. In a uniform field a charge moves in a circle when v is perpendicular to B: radius r = m v/(q B), period T = 2 pi m/(q B) (independent of speed), frequency f = qB/(2 pi m) (cyclotron frequency). If v has a component along B the path is a helix with pitch p = v cos theta T. With both E and B (velocity selector) the charge moves straight when q E = q v B, i.e. v = E/B. A straight wire of length L carrying current I in field B feels F = B I L sin theta; two parallel wires carrying currents I1 and I2 at distance d attract (same direction) or repel with force per unit length mu0 I1 I2/(2 pi d), which defines the ampere.
## Deep dive: field due to currents
Biot-Savart law: dB = (mu0/4 pi) I dl sin theta / r^2. Long straight wire: B = mu0 I/(2 pi r). Centre of a circular loop of radius R: B = mu0 I/(2R); N turns: mu0 N I/(2R). On the axis of a loop: B = mu0 I R^2/(2 (R^2 + x^2)^(3/2)). Ampere circuital law: closed integral of B . dl = mu0 I_enclosed. Solenoid: B = mu0 n I inside (n turns per metre), almost zero outside; toroid: B = mu0 N I/(2 pi r). Semicircular arc of radius R at its centre: B = mu0 I/(4R).
## Deep dive: torque, galvanometer and magnetic materials
A rectangular loop of N turns, area A, current I in field B has torque tau = N I A B sin theta = m x B where m = N I A is the magnetic dipole moment; potential energy U = -m . B. The moving-coil galvanometer has deflection proportional to current: N I A B = k phi, so current sensitivity phi/I = N A B/k. A galvanometer converts to an ammeter with a small shunt S = G Ig/(I - Ig) in parallel, and to a voltmeter by a large series resistance R = V/Ig - G. Materials: diamagnetic (weakly repelled, chi negative), paramagnetic (weakly attracted, chi small positive, Curie law chi proportional to 1/T), ferromagnetic (strongly attracted, hysteresis, Curie temperature). Bar magnet: B on the axis = (mu0/4 pi) 2m/r^3 and on the equator (mu0/4 pi) m/r^3 for r >> length. Earth field: horizontal component BH = B cos delta, vertical component B sin delta.

Q: What is the force on a moving charge in a magnetic field?
A: F = q v B sin theta.
Q: Does the magnetic force do work on a charge?
A: No, it is perpendicular to the velocity.
Q: What path does a charge follow when v is perpendicular to B?
A: A circle.
Q: What is the radius of the circular path?
A: m v/(q B).
Q: What is the cyclotron frequency?
A: q B/(2 pi m).
Q: What is the Lorentz force?
A: F = q (E + v x B).
Q: What is a velocity selector?
A: A crossed E and B field region that lets only charges with v = E/B pass undeflected.
Q: State the Biot-Savart law.
A: dB = (mu0/4 pi) I dl sin theta/r^2.
Q: What is the field of a long straight wire?
A: mu0 I/(2 pi r).
Q: State Ampere's circuital law.
A: The line integral of B around a closed loop equals mu0 times the enclosed current.
Q: What is the field inside a solenoid?
A: mu0 n I.
Q: What is the field outside an ideal solenoid?
A: Almost zero.
Q: What is the force between two parallel currents in the same direction?
A: Attraction.
Q: How is the ampere defined?
A: Through the force per unit length between two parallel current-carrying wires.
Q: What is the magnetic dipole moment of a loop?
A: m = N I A.
Q: What is the torque on a current loop?
A: tau = m B sin theta.
Q: How is a galvanometer converted to an ammeter?
A: By connecting a small shunt resistance in parallel.
Q: How is a galvanometer converted to a voltmeter?
A: By connecting a large resistance in series.
Q: What are diamagnetic materials?
A: Materials weakly repelled by a magnet.
Q: What are paramagnetic materials?
A: Materials weakly attracted by a magnet.
Q: What are ferromagnetic materials?
A: Materials strongly attracted by a magnet, showing hysteresis.
Q: What is the Curie temperature?
A: The temperature above which a ferromagnet becomes paramagnetic.
Q: What is magnetic hysteresis?
A: The lag of magnetisation behind the magnetising field.
Q: What is the SI unit of magnetic field?
A: Tesla.
