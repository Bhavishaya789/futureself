@study
One mole contains 6.022 x 10^23 particles; n = mass/M; molarity M = moles/litre; molality m = moles/kg solvent; mole fraction x = n_i/n_total. Colligative properties: DTb = Kb m, DTf = Kf m, pi = C R T, relative lowering of vapour pressure = x_solute (Raoult). Van t Hoff factor i modifies each. Titration: N1 V1 = N2 V2 and M1 V1 n2 = M2 V2 n1.
## Deep dive: mole concept
One mole is the amount of substance containing 6.022 x 10^23 entities (Avogadro number N_A). Moles n = mass/molar mass = number of particles/N_A = volume at STP/22.4 L. Percentage composition and empirical formula: divide percentages by atomic masses, then by the smallest to get the simplest ratio; molecular formula = empirical formula x n where n = molar mass/empirical mass. Stoichiometry: balance the equation, convert masses to moles, use mole ratios; the limiting reagent is the reactant that gives the least product. Percentage yield = (actual/theoretical) x 100. Equivalent mass = molar mass/n-factor.
## Deep dive: concentration terms and colligative properties
Molarity M = moles of solute/volume (L); molality m = moles of solute/mass of solvent (kg) (independent of temperature); normality N = gram equivalents/volume (L) = M x n-factor; mole fraction x_A = n_A/(n_A + n_B); ppm = mass of solute/mass of solution x 10^6. Dilution: M1 V1 = M2 V2. Raoult law: P_A = x_A P_A^0; total P = sum x_i P_i^0. Relative lowering of vapour pressure (P^0 - P)/P^0 = x_solute. Elevation of boiling point DTb = i Kb m, depression of freezing point DTf = i Kf m, osmotic pressure pi = i C R T. Van t Hoff factor i = observed colligative property/calculated: i > 1 for dissociation (NaCl about 2), i < 1 for association (acetic acid in benzene). Molar mass from colligative data M = Kf w2 x 1000/(DTf w1).
## Deep dive: titrations
At the equivalence point gram equivalents of acid = gram equivalents of base: N1 V1 = N2 V2; with molarity M1 V1 n2 = M2 V2 n1 for a reaction a A + b B. Redox titrations: KMnO4 in acid medium has n-factor 5 (Mn7+ to Mn2+), K2Cr2O7 has n-factor 6, oxalic acid 2, Fe2+ 1. Indicators: phenolphthalein (pH 8.3 to 10) for strong base titrations, methyl orange (3.1 to 4.4) for strong acid. Back titration is used for insoluble or volatile substances. Hardness of water is expressed in ppm of CaCO3.

Q: What is Avogadro's number?
A: 6.022 x 10^23.
Q: What is a mole?
A: The amount of substance containing 6.022 x 10^23 elementary entities.
Q: What is molarity?
A: Moles of solute per litre of solution.
Q: What is molality?
A: Moles of solute per kilogram of solvent.
Q: Which is independent of temperature, molarity or molality?
A: Molality.
Q: What is the mole fraction?
A: The ratio of the moles of one component to the total moles.
Q: What is normality?
A: Gram equivalents of solute per litre of solution.
Q: What is the limiting reagent?
A: The reactant that is completely consumed first.
Q: State Raoult's law.
A: The partial vapour pressure of a component is its mole fraction times its vapour pressure in the pure state.
Q: What are colligative properties?
A: Properties that depend only on the number of solute particles.
Q: Name the four colligative properties.
A: Relative lowering of vapour pressure, boiling point elevation, freezing point depression and osmotic pressure.
Q: What is the van't Hoff factor?
A: The ratio of observed to calculated colligative property.
Q: What is osmosis?
A: The flow of solvent through a semipermeable membrane towards the higher solute concentration.
Q: What is reverse osmosis?
A: Solvent flow from the solution to the pure solvent under pressure larger than osmotic pressure.
Q: What is an isotonic solution?
A: Solutions with the same osmotic pressure.
Q: What is an azeotrope?
A: A constant-boiling mixture that cannot be separated by distillation.
Q: What is the n-factor of KMnO4 in acidic medium?
A: 5.
Q: What is the n-factor of K2Cr2O7?
A: 6.
Q: What is the equivalence point?
A: The point at which the reactants have reacted in chemically equivalent amounts.
Q: Which indicator is used for a strong acid and weak base titration?
A: Methyl orange.
Q: What is a primary standard?
A: A stable, pure substance of known composition used to prepare standard solutions.
Q: What does a positive deviation from Raoult's law mean?
A: Weaker solute-solvent interactions than in the pure components.
