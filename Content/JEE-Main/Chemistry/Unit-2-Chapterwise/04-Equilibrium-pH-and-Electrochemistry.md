@study
Kc = [products]/[reactants] with stoichiometric powers; Kp = Kc (RT)^Dn. pH = -log [H+], pH + pOH = 14, Ka Kb = Kw = 10^-14. Henderson: pH = pKa + log [salt]/[acid]. Ksp of AxBy = x^x y^y s^(x + y). Electrochemistry: E_cell = E_cathode - E_anode, Nernst E = E0 - (0.0591/n) log Q, DG = -nFE, Faraday m = Z I t.
## Deep dive: chemical equilibrium
For aA + bB <=> cC + dD, Kc = [C]^c [D]^d/([A]^a [B]^b); Kp = Kc (RT)^Dn with Dn = (c + d) - (a + b). The reaction quotient Q is compared with K: Q < K forward shift, Q > K backward shift, Q = K equilibrium. Reversing the reaction inverts K; multiplying coefficients by n raises K to the nth power; adding reactions multiplies K values. Le Chatelier: adding reactant shifts forward; raising pressure favours fewer gas moles; heat favours the endothermic direction (K changes only with temperature, van t Hoff: log (K2/K1) = DH (T2 - T1)/(2.303 R T1 T2)). Degree of dissociation alpha: for A <=> 2B at total pressure P, Kp = 4 alpha^2 P/(1 - alpha^2).
## Deep dive: ionic equilibrium
Water: Kw = [H+][OH-] = 10^-14 at 25 C; pH = -log [H+]; pH + pOH = 14. Strong acid: [H+] = C. Weak acid: [H+] = sqrt (Ka C) = C alpha. Ka Kb = Kw for a conjugate pair. Buffer: pH = pKa + log ([salt]/[acid]) (Henderson-Hasselbalch); buffer capacity is maximal when [salt] = [acid]. Salt hydrolysis: for a weak acid-strong base salt pH = 7 + (1/2) (pKa + log C). Solubility product Ksp: for AB, s = sqrt (Ksp); for AB2 or A2B, s = (Ksp/4)^(1/3). Precipitation occurs when the ionic product exceeds Ksp; the common ion effect lowers solubility. Indicator pH range = pKin +- 1.
## Deep dive: electrochemistry
A galvanic cell converts chemical energy to electrical energy; anode (oxidation, negative), cathode (reduction, positive). E0_cell = E0_cathode - E0_anode; DG0 = -n F E0 and DG0 = -2.303 R T log K, giving log K = n E0/0.0591 at 25 C. Nernst equation: E = E0 - (0.0591/n) log Q. Concentration cell: E = (0.0591/n) log (C2/C1). Faraday laws: mass deposited m = (M/(n F)) I t; one faraday = 96500 C. Conductance: conductivity kappa = G (l/A), molar conductivity Lambda_m = kappa x 1000/C; Kohlrausch: Lambda_m0 = lambda+ + lambda-. Batteries: lead storage, Leclanche, Ni-Cd, fuel cell (H2-O2 with 2H2 + O2 -> 2H2O). Corrosion is electrochemical rusting of iron.

Q: What is Le Chatelier's principle?
A: A system at equilibrium shifts to oppose any change imposed on it.
Q: What is the equilibrium constant?
A: The ratio of product to reactant concentrations, each raised to its stoichiometric coefficient, at equilibrium.
Q: What is the reaction quotient?
A: The same ratio as K but at any instant.
Q: What is the relation between Kp and Kc?
A: Kp = Kc (RT)^Dn.
Q: Does a catalyst change K?
A: No, it only changes the rate of reaching equilibrium.
Q: What changes the value of K?
A: Only temperature.
Q: What is the ionic product of water at 25 C?
A: 10^-14.
Q: What is pH?
A: The negative log of the hydrogen ion concentration.
Q: What is the pH of a neutral solution at 25 C?
A: 7.
Q: What is a buffer?
A: A solution that resists a change in pH on adding small amounts of acid or base.
Q: State the Henderson-Hasselbalch equation.
A: pH = pKa + log ([salt]/[acid]).
Q: What is the common ion effect?
A: Reduced solubility or ionisation caused by adding an ion already present.
Q: What is the solubility product?
A: The product of ion concentrations in a saturated solution, each raised to its coefficient.
Q: When does a precipitate form?
A: When the ionic product exceeds Ksp.
Q: What is hydrolysis of a salt?
A: The reaction of the salt ions with water.
Q: What is a Lewis acid?
A: An electron pair acceptor.
Q: What is a Bronsted-Lowry acid?
A: A proton donor.

A: Oxidation.

Q: What is conductivity?

Q: State Kohlrausch's law.
