Photoelectric effect: K_max = h f - phi, h = 6.63 x 10^-34 J s. de Broglie wavelength lambda = h/(m v). Bohr model for hydrogen: E_n = -13.6/n^2 eV, r_n = 0.529 n^2 angstrom, v_n = 2.19 x 10^6/n m/s; spectral lines 1/lambda = R (1/n1^2 - 1/n2^2). Radioactivity N = N0 e^(-lambda t), half-life T = 0.693/lambda; mass defect gives binding energy E = Dm c^2 = 931.5 Dm (MeV).
## Deep dive: photoelectric effect and matter waves
Einstein equation: h f = phi + K_max, where phi = h f_0 is the work function and f_0 the threshold frequency. K_max = e V_s (stopping potential). The photocurrent is proportional to intensity (for f > f_0); K_max depends only on f, not on intensity. Photon energy E = h f = h c/lambda = 1240/lambda(nm) eV; momentum p = h/lambda. de Broglie wavelength lambda = h/p = h/(m v) = h/sqrt (2 m K); for an electron accelerated through V volts, lambda = 12.27/sqrt (V) angstrom; for a particle in thermal equilibrium lambda = h/sqrt (3 m k T). Davisson-Germer experiment confirmed electron diffraction.
## Deep dive: atoms and spectra
Rutherford alpha scattering shows a small dense nucleus of radius R = R0 A^(1/3) with R0 = 1.2 fm. Bohr postulates: angular momentum m v r = n h/(2 pi); energy E_n = -13.6 Z^2/n^2 eV; radius r_n = 0.529 n^2/Z angstrom; speed v_n = 2.19 x 10^6 Z/n m/s; time period T_n proportional to n^3/Z^2. Emitted photon energy for a transition n2 -> n1: E = 13.6 Z^2 (1/n1^2 - 1/n2^2) eV, with 1/lambda = R Z^2 (1/n1^2 - 1/n2^2), R = 1.097 x 10^7 m^-1. Series: Lyman (n1 = 1, UV), Balmer (n1 = 2, visible), Paschen (n1 = 3, IR), Brackett (n1 = 4), Pfund (n1 = 5). Number of lines for transitions from the nth level to the ground state is n(n - 1)/2. Ionisation energy of hydrogen is 13.6 eV.
## Deep dive: nuclei and radioactivity
Mass number A, atomic number Z; neutrons N = A - Z. Mass defect Dm = Z m_p + (A - Z) m_n - M; binding energy B = Dm x 931.5 MeV; binding energy per nucleon peaks near A = 56 (about 8.8 MeV) which explains fission of heavy and fusion of light nuclei. Radioactive decay law: N = N0 e^(-lambda t); half-life T = ln 2/lambda = 0.693/lambda; mean life tau = 1/lambda = 1.44 T; activity A = lambda N, SI unit becquerel (1 curie = 3.7 x 10^10 Bq). After n half-lives N = N0/2^n. Alpha decay reduces A by 4 and Z by 2; beta-minus raises Z by 1; gamma changes neither. Q value of a reaction = (mass of reactants - mass of products) x 931.5 MeV. Fission of U-235 releases about 200 MeV; fusion powers stars.

Q: State Einstein's photoelectric equation.
A: h f = phi + K_max.
Q: What is the work function?
A: The minimum energy needed to remove an electron from a metal surface.
Q: What is the threshold frequency?
A: The minimum frequency of light that can cause photoemission.
Q: What is the stopping potential?
A: The reverse potential that stops the fastest photoelectrons.
Q: Does K_max depend on the intensity of light?
A: No, only on frequency.
Q: What is the de Broglie wavelength?
A: h/(m v).
Q: What did the Davisson-Germer experiment show?
A: The wave nature of electrons.
Q: What is Rutherford's model?
A: A tiny dense positive nucleus with electrons orbiting around it.
Q: What is the Bohr quantisation condition?
A: m v r = n h/(2 pi).
Q: What is the ground state energy of hydrogen?
A: -13.6 eV.
Q: What is the ionisation energy of hydrogen?
A: 13.6 eV.
Q: Which series of hydrogen lies in the visible region?
A: The Balmer series.
Q: Which series lies in the ultraviolet?
A: The Lyman series.
Q: What is the Rydberg constant?
A: 1.097 x 10^7 per metre.
Q: What is a mass defect?
A: The difference between the mass of the nucleons and the mass of the nucleus.
Q: What is binding energy?
A: The energy needed to separate a nucleus into its nucleons.
Q: Where is binding energy per nucleon maximum?
A: Near A = 56 (iron).
Q: What is half-life?
A: The time in which half the nuclei decay.
Q: What is the relation between half-life and mean life?
A: Mean life = 1.44 x half-life.
Q: What is activity?
A: The number of decays per second.
Q: What is the SI unit of activity?
A: Becquerel.
Q: What changes in alpha decay?
A: Mass number falls by 4 and atomic number by 2.
Q: What changes in beta-minus decay?
A: Atomic number rises by 1 with the mass number unchanged.
Q: What powers the Sun?
A: Nuclear fusion.
Q: What is nuclear fission?
A: The splitting of a heavy nucleus into lighter nuclei with release of energy.
