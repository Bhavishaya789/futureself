@study
Coulomb force F = k q1 q2/r^2; field E = k q/r^2; potential V = k q/r; potential energy U = k q1 q2/r. Gauss law: flux = q_enc/e0 giving E = sigma/e0 near a conductor. Capacitance C = e0 A/d, energy U = (1/2) C V^2; series 1/C = sum 1/Ci, parallel C = sum Ci; a dielectric multiplies C by K.
## Deep dive: charges, fields and potential
Coulomb law F = k q1 q2/r^2 with k = 1/(4 pi e0) = 9 x 10^9 N m^2/C^2; the force is along the line joining the charges (repulsive for like charges). Electric field E = F/q = k Q/r^2 for a point charge. Dipole of moment p = q (2a): axial field 2 k p/r^3, equatorial field k p/r^3, torque p E sin theta, potential energy -p E cos theta. Potential V = k Q/r; potential difference V_AB = -integral E . dl; E = -dV/dr. Potential energy of two charges U = k q1 q2/r; of a system of charges it is the sum over pairs. Work done in moving a charge q through potential difference V is q V.
## Deep dive: Gauss law and conductors
Gauss law: closed-surface flux = q_enc/e0. Field of an infinite line charge E = lambda/(2 pi e0 r); of an infinite sheet sigma/(2 e0); just outside a conductor sigma/e0; inside a conductor zero. For a uniformly charged spherical shell: E = 0 inside, k Q/r^2 outside; for a solid insulating sphere E = k Q r/R^3 inside. All the charge of a conductor resides on its outer surface, and the surface is an equipotential. The potential of a shell is k Q/R on and inside the surface. Electrostatic energy density u = (1/2) e0 E^2.
## Deep dive: capacitors
Capacitance C = Q/V; parallel plate C = e0 A/d; with a dielectric of constant K filling the gap C = K e0 A/d; a conducting slab of thickness t increases C to e0 A/(d - t); a dielectric slab gives C = e0 A/(d - t + t/K). Energy stored U = (1/2) C V^2 = Q^2/(2C) = (1/2) Q V. Series: 1/C = 1/C1 + 1/C2 (same charge); parallel: C = C1 + C2 (same voltage). Two capacitors C1 (charged to V1) and C2 (uncharged) joined together share charge: common potential V = C1 V1/(C1 + C2) and energy loss (1/2) C1 C2 V1^2/(C1 + C2). If a battery stays connected V is constant; if disconnected Q is constant when the plate separation or dielectric changes.

Q: State Coulomb's law.
A: The force between two point charges is proportional to the product of the charges and inversely proportional to the square of the distance.
Q: What is the SI unit of electric field?
A: N/C or V/m.
Q: What is an electric dipole?
A: Two equal and opposite charges separated by a small distance.
Q: What is the dipole moment?
A: The product of charge and separation, p = q (2a).
Q: State Gauss's law.
A: The electric flux through a closed surface is q_enc/e0.
Q: What is the electric field inside a conductor?
A: Zero.
Q: What is the field due to an infinite sheet of charge?
A: sigma/(2 e0).
Q: What is the electric field just outside a conductor?
A: sigma/e0.
Q: What is the field inside a charged spherical shell?
A: Zero.
Q: What is the potential inside a charged shell?
A: Constant, equal to the surface potential.
Q: What is an equipotential surface?
A: A surface on which the potential is the same everywhere.
Q: Why can field lines never cross?
A: Because the field would have two directions at the crossing point.
Q: What is capacitance?
A: The ratio of charge stored to potential difference, Q/V.
Q: What is the unit of capacitance?
A: Farad.
Q: What is the energy stored in a capacitor?
A: (1/2) C V^2.
Q: What is a dielectric?
A: An insulating material that increases the capacitance by reducing the effective field.
Q: What happens to the capacitance when a dielectric is inserted?
A: It increases by the factor K.
Q: What is the energy density of the electric field?
A: (1/2) e0 E^2.
Q: How do capacitors combine in series?
A: 1/C = 1/C1 + 1/C2 + ...
Q: How do capacitors combine in parallel?
A: C = C1 + C2 + ...
Q: What is dielectric strength?
A: The maximum field a dielectric can withstand before breakdown.
Q: What is the unit of permittivity?
A: C^2 N^-1 m^-2 or F/m.
Q: What is polarisation?
A: Dipole moment per unit volume of a dielectric.
