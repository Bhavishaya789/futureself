@study
Equations of motion v = u + at, s = ut + (1/2) a t^2, v^2 = u^2 + 2as; projectile range R = u^2 sin 2 theta/g, maximum height H = u^2 sin^2 theta/(2g), time of flight T = 2 u sin theta/g. Newton second law F = ma, friction f = mu N, circular motion a = v^2/r, banking tan theta = v^2/(r g), and conservation of momentum in collisions.
## Deep dive: motion in a straight line and projectiles
For constant acceleration use v = u + a t, s = u t + (1/2) a t^2, v^2 = u^2 + 2 a s, and the distance in the nth second s_n = u + a (2n - 1)/2. For free fall use a = g downward. Area under a v-t graph is displacement; slope of an x-t graph is velocity. Relative motion: v_AB = v_A - v_B; river-boat problems resolve velocities along and across the stream (minimum time when the boat heads straight across; shortest path when it heads upstream at sin theta = v_r/v_b). Projectile motion from the ground: horizontal speed u cos theta constant, vertical speed u sin theta - g t; time of flight T = 2 u sin theta/g; maximum height H = u^2 sin^2 theta/(2 g); range R = u^2 sin 2 theta/g, maximum at 45 degrees; ranges at theta and (90 - theta) are equal; H = R tan theta/4 at... in general R/H = 4/tan theta. The trajectory y = x tan theta - g x^2/(2 u^2 cos^2 theta) is a parabola.
## Deep dive: Newton's laws, friction and circular motion
Newton second law F_net = m a; weight apparent in a lift: N = m (g + a) accelerating up and m (g - a) accelerating down. For connected bodies (Atwood machine) a = (m1 - m2) g/(m1 + m2) and tension T = 2 m1 m2 g/(m1 + m2). Friction: static f <= mu_s N, kinetic f = mu_k N; on an incline the body slides when tan theta > mu_s, and a = g (sin theta - mu cos theta). Angle of repose = tan^-1 mu. Circular motion: centripetal acceleration a = v^2/r = omega^2 r; a car on a level road is safe when v <= sqrt (mu r g); on a banked road with no friction tan theta = v^2/(r g); at the top of a vertical circle the minimum speed is sqrt (g r) and at the bottom sqrt (5 g r) to complete the circle on a string.
## Deep dive: momentum and collisions
Linear momentum p = m v; impulse J = F Dt = Dp. In an isolated system momentum is conserved. In a perfectly elastic collision kinetic energy is also conserved; for masses m1 and m2 with m2 at rest, v1 = (m1 - m2) u/(m1 + m2) and v2 = 2 m1 u/(m1 + m2). Coefficient of restitution e = (v2 - v1)/(u1 - u2): e = 1 elastic, e = 0 perfectly inelastic (bodies stick). Rocket propulsion: thrust = u (dm/dt). For a variable mass system use F = d(mv)/dt. Centre of mass x_cm = sum m_i x_i / sum m_i; it moves as if all external forces act on the total mass.
