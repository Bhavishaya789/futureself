**Key formulas and rules**
- **Spotting errors**: checks subject verb agreement
- **Fill in the blanks**: tests prepositions and vocabulary
- **Active to passive**: moves the object to subject position
- **Direct to indirect**: changes pronouns and tenses
- **One word substitution**: condenses a phrase
- **Idioms**: have figurative meanings

**Facts to remember**
- Spotting errors checks subject verb agreement.
- Fill in the blanks tests prepositions and vocabulary.
- Active to passive moves the object to subject position.
- Direct to indirect changes pronouns and tenses.
- One word substitution condenses a phrase.
- Idioms have figurative meanings.
- Synonyms have similar meanings.
- Antonyms have opposite meanings.
- Cloze test needs context.
- Sentence improvement replaces the underlined part.
- Para jumbles need a logical order.
- Articles a, an and the.
- Tenses show time of action.
- Prepositions show relation between words.

**Exam approach.** Write the formula first, substitute with units, then check the order of magnitude. In Govt Exam, speed comes from recognising the standard pattern of each chapter, so practise the numerical types below until they are automatic. Revise this chapter in three passes: the formula sheet, the facts above, then the generated numerical questions with a timer.

**How to revise.** Read the formula list and the facts once, then close the page and write them from memory. Attempt the generated questions in sets of ten, mark each answer, and note the reason for every miss. Questions in this chapter repeat a small number of patterns with changed numbers or wording, so after two or three sets you should recognise each pattern at a glance. Keep a short error log of units, signs and exceptions, and read it again the day before the test.
